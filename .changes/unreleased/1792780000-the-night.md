The night sky is now worked out from the real sky on the map's date. The moon is drawn at its true size, a little larger near its closest point in the month and smaller at its furthest. It shows its real phase, with the lit side facing the sun, the dark seas on its face and a faint earthshine on the dark part of a crescent. A rising moon is orange and turns white as it climbs. Moonlight follows the phase, so the snow under a full moon is clearly brighter than under a quarter moon, and a night with no moon is dark apart from the starlight and the lamps. The stars come out as the twilight fades and are fewer under a bright moon. They twinkle more and grow dimmer near the horizon, and they turn with the sky clock. The Milky Way sits where it really is for that date and hour. In thin cloud the moon has a corona and a halo, and a faint airglow lies along the horizon.
