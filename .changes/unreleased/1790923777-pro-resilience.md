title: You ski like a professional — and you only fall when you can't stay up
---

The skier no longer falls off landings he comes down on his skis for: crooked, rolled onto an edge or slapped down on the tips, he rides them away. He goes down only when the body physically can't stay up — landing on his side, back or head, the tips spearing the snow, a trunk square in front of his skis, an edge catching while he slides sideways, or a landing harder than any legs can hold. A trunk taken on the shoulder at speed knocks him aside instead of throwing him. Every near fall now shows on the skier: arms flung out after a hard landing, a shoulder knocked back off a tree, a hand put down to the snow, a wobble over an edge that bit — with a word on the HUD when it was close. The rivals are each dealt their own toughness, so one of them rides out what throws another.
