# Skiing

The skier model (`engine/game/skier.ts`, with `suspension.ts`, `snow.ts`, `poles.ts`, `flight.ts`, `chassis.ts` and `collision.ts`; the shared numbers in `engine/game/defs/tuning.ts` and the pair of skis in `defs/skis.ts`) is a rigid body on the snow — the skier and his skis as one — integrated semi-implicitly at 120 Hz. Every force is summed in `stepSkier`, each from the module that owns its model, and each model says where its numbers come from at the function that implements it. **There is no MODE.** A launch is a skier whose stations have all left the snow; a landing is one whose stations came back; a fall is a body lying on its side. Each is read off the same forces every other step is made of, and the events say so after the fact.

Every number below is quoted with its unit as `TUNING` or `SKIS` states it; the measured ones at the end come from skiing the real engine on the synthetic maps the tests use (`npm run ride`).

## The pair (`defs/skis.ts`)

SIX pairs of skis, each a real CLASS and an answer to a kind of snow rather than a point on one scale (`SKI_CATALOG`, in the order the ski card turns through them — best all-round first; `skisById`). Each is named for an animal of the high country that moves the way it skis, and every number is kept inside the class's measured band, never a make and a model:

|                          | Chamois (`SKIS`) — all-mountain | Swift — slalom | Chough — giant slalom | Eagle — downhill | Marmot — powder | Hare — park    |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------- | -------------- | --------------------- | ---------------- | --------------- | -------------- |

| waist / tip / tail       | 88 / 130 / 114 mm               | 66 / 122 / 104 | 68 / 104 / 88         | 66 / 100 / 86    | 116 / 142 / 130 | 90 / 122 / 120 |
| sidecut radius           | 18 m                            | 13 m           | 27 m                  | 45 m             | 22 m            | 17 m           |

| most edge (`edgeMax`)    | 1.05 rad (60°)                  | 1.15           | 1.15                  | 1.1              | 0.95            | 1.0            |

Every pair carries a `skierMass` = 80 kg skier in his kit, standing `cogHeight` = 1.0 m over the snow (skier and skis together, the body frame's origin), his boots `stance` = 0.3 m apart, his hips able to hang `hipReach` = 0.35 m inside a turn, a full tuck dropping him `crouchDrop` = 0.3 m, his poles reaching `poleReach` = 0.9 m ahead and pushing `polePush` = 260 N a plant. A racer's tuck is 0.25–0.35 m² of drag area and a recreational skier's half-tuck 0.45–0.6; standing tall on a piste 0.6–0.9.

`topSpeed` is a DOCUMENTED EXPECTATION, not an input: the physics delivers it, `tests/skier_test.ts` and `tests/catalog_test.ts` hold every pair to within a tenth of it, and the bot reads it (`limits.ts`'s `topSpeedOf`) to know what flat out is. It is quoted in a tuck down `TOP_SPEED_PITCH` = π/9 (a 20° groomed schuss — a red piste's steep pitch, the steepest a groomer holds a straight line down for long), and it is where the air's drag on `cdATuck` meets the slope's pull less the base's friction (`terminalSpeed`): `v = √(2·m·g·(sin θ − crr·cos θ) / (ρ·C_dA))`, 117 km/h on the Chamois.

**The Chamois is the reference pair.** Every shared number in `TUNING` was tuned on it, and `footprint.ts` prices every other pair's skis as multipliers on the shared snow model that read exactly 1 for it:

- _the pressure_ is the whole weight over the two skis' length by their waist. The rest sink in powder goes as `(p / p₀)^footprint.floatExp` (0.8) and the planing speed as `√(p / p₀)` — the wide powder ski sinks least and comes up onto the top soonest, the narrow race skis sink most — and the same sink prices the powder drag, the work of compacting the snow (Bekker);
- _the edge_ holds the groomer as `1 + edgeFlex · (flex − flex₀)` (0.5) and `1 − edgeRocker · (rocker − rocker₀)` (0.35): a stiff ski holds its whole length on the edge, a soft one its middle, and a rockered tip is lifted off the snow before the edge starts — the downhill ski bites hardest, the powder ski least;
- _the base_ holds powder as the waist to `baseFloat` (0.8), and a rockered tip floats and steers there, `1 + rockerFloat · (rocker − rocker₀)` (0.3);
- _the roll onto an edge_ goes as the inverse of `(L / L₀) · (W / W₀)^waistRate · (1 + flexRate · (flex − flex₀))` (0.5, 0.4): a short narrow ski is the quickest edge to edge (the slalom ski), a long stiff one the slowest (the downhill ski) — the waist because the boot stands that much further from the edge it is tipped onto;
- _a soft ski lands softer_: the harsh speed goes as `1 + flexHarsh · (flex₀ − flex)` (0.25).

The exponents are tuning values, bounded by the measurements above rather than read off a paper; `make ride ARGS="--skis all"` is what they are judged by.

The inertia is a tall body over a pair of rods (`inertiaOf`): on the Chamois 26 kg·m² in pitch, 9 in yaw and 28 in roll — which is why a skier turns far more readily than he pitches, and why every hand on the body below is stated on the reference pair and scaled by this one's inertia about that axis.

## Where the skis meet the snow, and the legs (`suspension.ts`)

SIX STATIONS: each ski's tip, its middle under the boot and its tail (`STATIONS`, at 0.38 of the length ahead of the boot, 0 and 0.32 behind, bearing a quarter, a half and a quarter of the ski's load). Each station is a spring-damper — THE LEG — cast down THE SNOW'S OWN NORMAL from under the hips: along the ski's line by its station (the body's forward axis, so a body pitched over its tips loads them), across the snow by half the stance (the ground's own across, square to the skis), and down from the CoG — the raycast vehicle, whose contact is wherever that ray meets the snow's SUPPORT (`snow.ts`), found by Newton's method along the ray. **The stance stands on the snow and the leg reaches from the hips**: a skier's legs are two, and his skis stay on the snow whatever his body does above them, so the body's roll is its INCLINATION into a turn and never a ski lifted. Cast from a point rolled over with the body along its own axis — the sibling games' sled — a 0.3 m stance under a body laid 30° over was one ski 0.15 m off the snow, and every turn-in a skier falling over his inside ski.

The REST LOAD each station carries comes off the geometry rather than being authored — each ski half the weight, its stations by their shares — and each attachment is placed so its spring sits at exactly its rest sag with the CoG at `cogHeight`. So a skier put down on flat snow stands at the height the spec says (`tests/skier_test.ts`): the Chamois stands 0.988 m over the groomer (1.0 less the corduroy's 1.2 cm of sink) with his knees bent 6 cm, and the six loads sum to his 868 N.

The legs: `legs.rate` = 7500 N/m per ski (the three stations of one ski share it), sagging about 6 cm at rest — about 1.8 Hz in heave — with `bump` = 500 N·s/m folding and `rebound` = 900 straightening (a knee is always softer folding than straightening: a landing taken in one bob), over `travel` = 0.45 m. The damper reads THE COMPRESSION'S OWN CHANGE against the same surface as the spring, so a crease in the snow is a crease in both; over the last `BOTTOM_ZONE` = 30 % of the stroke the bump damping rises to three times its own (the muscle a skier braces a landing with), and past the travel a HYSTERETIC stop at `STOP_RATE` = 12 times the leg's rate loads fully and hands back `STOP_RELEASE` = 20 % of it on the way out — a knee at the end of its bend swallows a slam rather than springing off it. THE TUCK FOLDS THE LEGS: the stations hang `crouchDrop · crouch` higher, and the body is moved down by the change along its own up as it folds (kinematically — a tuck let go at 80 km/h on loaded springs fired the sibling model half a metre into the air), so a crouched skier sits lower on legs that still carry all of him and has less leg left for a landing.

Two FUSES, which are not models: no station may push more than `MAX_LOAD` = 15 times its rest load, and no body rate may exceed `MAX_SPIN` = 25 rad/s. A station is read only while the body's up points at least `PROBE_MIN_DOWN` = 0.25 of the way at the sky — a skier on his side has no legs under him.

## The snow (`snow.ts`)

**The sink** is the planing hull's draft carried straight over. Snow under a moving ski has less time to yield the faster it is crossed, so the support rises with speed:

```
sink = powderSink · depth · scale · exp(−(v / planeSpeed)²) · (1 − packed) + packedSink · packed
```

with `powderSink` = 0.22 m (a pair on 40 cm of fresh snow, buried to the boot tops at rest), `packedSink` = 0.012 m (a groomed piste's centimetre of corduroy), `planeSpeed` = 6 m/s — a third of the rest sink at the planing speed and a few centimetres by 30 km/h, the moment a skier feels the tips come up — `scale` the pair's own `Footprint.sink` (and `endSink` = 0.7 of it at a tip or a tail: the ski is a beam and its ends ride up), the planing speed scaled by `Footprint.plane`, and `packed` the level's `packedAt` under the station. Each station's support follows the sink the speed asks for over `sinkLag` = 0.25 s.

**The snow dial.** A free ride may ask for shallower or deeper powder (`SNOW_DIAL`, 0.25–2.5; the start card's SNOW row asks for it as a depth of loose snow, read back through `snowCoverOf`, `snow.cover` = 0.4 m at the ordinary dial): `GameState.snowDepth` multiplies `powderSink` wherever it is read, and the groomer's cut is left alone.

**DEEP SNOW** (`snow.ts`'s header). Past the ordinary depth — up to a metre of fresh snow, `deep.full` = 2.5 on the dial, a maritime winter's usual by March — the powder is BOTTOMLESS, and everything it adds grows by that share (`bottomlessOf`: none at the ordinary depth, where every race is skied and nothing below changes by a bit): _the snow gives under load_, `(L / L₀)^give` with `deep.give` = 0.8 (Bekker's exponent for fresh snow is near one), read no further than `loadMax` = 2.5 and never past `deep.compact` = 0.72 of the loose layer; _it stays pressed_, a support coming back up only as fast as the ski moves onto snow nobody pressed (`settleShare`, all of it by `deep.settle` = 2 m/s); _it planes later_, the planing speed going as the dial to `deep.plane` = 0.1; _the body ploughs_, the knees each over a shin's `0.12` m paying `deep.belly` = 0.5 of the plough's law on whatever untouched powder stands over them (to `bellyMax` = 0.5 m) — so tips held up out of the snow pay none of it, and leaning back is how a skier climbs onto the top; and _it turns with the weight_: the roll the skier holds loses `skier.deepHold` = 0.7 of itself and THE SOFT SIDE GIVES, a moment of `skier.deepTip` = 2 times his weight at his CoG height per radian of roll off the snow's plane, fully while the skis sit down at their rest sink and `deepPlaning` = half of it planing on top, only once he is moving (over `deepTipFrom` = 1.5 to `deepTipFull` = 4 m/s).

**The new snow.** Where the sky snows (R19), the fall at a moment is `snowAt(level, t)` in `engine/game/snowfall.ts`, a pure function of the map and the clock, and it LAYS SNOW: `GameState.fresh` grows at `freshRate` = 8 cm an hour at a fall of 1, and `freshBury` = 0.18 m of it buries the groomer's feel outright — every station, the hull, a stood skier and a thrown body read the surface through `packedUnder`, and `depthUnder` deepens the dial by it.

**The resistance**, per station, along its line of travel, faded out below `DRAG_FADE` = 0.3 m/s so a skier at rest is not rocked through zero:

- _the base's friction_: `crrPacked` = 0.035 of the load on the groomer (0.03–0.05 on cold hard snow) or `crrPowder` = 0.07 in fresh snow;
- _the plough_: `plough` = 70 N per metre of ski width per metre of sink per (m/s)², in powder only and only at the TIP (the mid and the tail run in the furrow it cuts). This is the bow wave, and why a skier bogged in powder wants momentum: it grows with the square of the speed while the sink it multiplies falls away, a HUMP to get over;
- _powder drag_: `powderDrag` = 0.004 of the load per m/s — what compacting fresh snow costs at speed, gone on the groomer, scaled by the pair's sink. Set so the Chamois in a tuck runs about four fifths of its piste speed in powder once planing (92 of 117 km/h).

**The grip** — coefficients on the station's load, each a `tanh` of its sideways slip over `sideRef` = 0.4 m/s, which is how an edge lets go (progressively, then all at once): on packed snow THE EDGE'S hold, `edgePacked` = 0.95, of which a ski lying FLAT keeps `flatShare` = 0.3 and the rest comes with the edge angle, `sin(edge) / sin(edgeMax)`, times the pair's edge factor; in powder THE BASE'S hold, `basePowder` = 0.45, width-scaled; blended by `packed`; on bare ice (`Level.iceAt`, a frozen tarn) each cut to `ice.edge` = `ice.base` = 0.35 of itself. Every sideways grip is multiplied by the arcade's `sideGrip` = 1.3.

## The drive he makes himself (`poles.ts`)

THE ONE PUSH THAT IS NOT GRAVITY, and it is AUTOMATIC: a skier going slowly works for his speed whatever the thumbs say — to gain it and to KEEP it. At a walk — all of it under `poles.strideFrom` = 1.6 m/s and none past `strideTo` = 3 (`strideShare`) — he sets off on his POLES on the flat and down a pitch, the racer's double pole out of the gate, and only up a rise (the view reads the pair's pitch, `gaitOf`) STRIDES, the diagonal stride — the skis parallel, each leg kicking back in turn and gliding the other forward, the opposite arm planting its pole; rolling he SKATES — the skis opened into a V, a leg pushing out off each stride and lifted back in, the poles planted with every push — to `skateFrom` = 5.5 m/s, blending into the DOUBLE POLE by `skateTo` = 9 (both poles planted ahead together, the body folded down over them). The push is POWER-LIMITED, as every human-powered drive is: the lesser of what a plant can press (the spec's `polePush`, 260 N on the Chamois) and `poles.power` = 450 W over the speed — a fit recreational skier's sprint — whole under `poles.speed` = 6 m/s (22 km/h) and gone by `poles.fade` = 9.5 m/s (34 km/h), so a racer skates out of the gate and keeps skating on a flat to hold his speed, and the power law is what makes the last of it little; `powderShare` = 40 % of it in powder, and up to `trench.grip` = 75 % less for a bogged skier. It is for a STRAIGHT: the edge asked past `edgeFrom` = 0.25 of full takes it away by `edgeGone` = 0.6 — a skier works on the flat and down the run-out, not with his skis on edge in a bend. It comes in STRIDES (`SkierState.stride`, counted): a half-sine push over the first `duty` = 45 % of each on a `floor` = 30 % of the mean, the mean of a stride the whole force — and the pose reads the same stride (`gaitOf`), so a leg is seen pushing on the step the snow is pushed. **THE SKATE IS A PUSH AND A LONG GLIDE**, at a skater's own unhurried cadence (`poles.cadence`: 0.85 strides a second at 3.5 m/s rising to 1.3 by 6.3, as measured — a skater lengthens his glide first and quickens only toward his top speed); the poles bite for a quarter of it or so inside the leg's long push (`poleDuty`: no longer than the snow takes to pass under one sweep of the pole, never less than `dutyLeast` = 20 % — measured 0.34 s slow to 0.22 s fast). **THE SKATE GOES WHERE THE SKI POINTS**: a skater rides one arm of the V at a time, so each push sends him off DIAGONALLY along the other ski and the next push diagonally the other way — a zig-zag, never straight up the middle of the V (`glideYaw`, `SkierState.glide`). The V is `skateAngle` (`vee`: 0.36 rad, 21°, each ski off his line at a walk, closing to 0.2 rad, 11°, by `skateTo` — measured skating holds some 14° at 3 m/s), by how much of him is skating (`skateWork`, which needs the drive past `skateDrive` = 0.4: a skier half working under a held brake pushes with his skis together); the glide is carried across from one arm to the other over the push and held through the glide. The skis grip and the drive pushes along that line, the yaw hand holds his nose to it, and the push's SIDEWAYS share turns his way with it each step, its speed kept — the leg pays for the turn; asked of the snow's sideways grip instead, the slip it took scrubbed the flat-ground pace from 24 km/h to 13. His heading stays on the mean line: he faces down the run while his path swings some 0.7–1.2 m across it (the skier lab's `--sheet=path` draws it). **A PLANTED POLE STAYS PLANTED**: double-poling, a push lasts no longer than the snow takes to pass under one sweep of the pole (`poleSweep`: `sweep` = 1.35 m double-poling, `sweepSkate` = 1 m skating — a little short of the pose's own 1.55 and 1.19, so a push ends before the last of the arm's reach), so the faster he goes the quicker he works (`strideRate`, from `cadencePole` = 1.05), up to `cadenceMax` = 2 a second; past that his arms cannot keep up (`poleKeepUp`: whole while a push at that cadence sweeps `keepUp.from` = 85 % of the snow going by, gone by `keepUp.to` = 60 %), and the poles' share of the push goes with it (`driveReach`) — he gives the poles up and tucks, about 22–27 km/h on the flat. The pose holds each basket where it bit for the whole push (`holdPush` in `skier-stroke.ts`). It is read off the SPEED, not the way (a skier sliding sideways at 80 km/h has no business pushing on his skis), and it stops for the skid thrown, a jump being loaded, the air and a skier thrown. A skier stood still with his hands off stays standing; one rolling past `DRIVE_FROM` = 0.4 m/s, or asked to go with the tuck, sets off — and stands up to it: a man working is not folded into a tuck, so the crouch is the tuck less the drive, read from the step he means to work (the drive's target), so a skier sent off at GO with the tuck held does not fold down and stand back up before his first push. On the flat from a shuffle the Chamois is at 10 km/h in a second, 17 at 3 s and 24 at 12 s; nothing but gravity pushes him past that, and nothing here brakes him.

## The jump (`skier.ts`, `TUNING.jump`)

HELD, the jump LOADS: while it is held on the snow the skier sinks onto his legs, the deeper the longer (a crouch of `jump.crouch` = 0.75 at the full load), and the time held is counted to `jump.full` = 2 s — held longer is no higher. LET GO, he SPRINGS: `popMin` = 2.2 m/s for a tap, rising with the load to `popMax` = 6 m/s, straight off the SNOW'S OWN NORMAL (an ollie, not a hop in the world's up), and the `jump` event reports the pop and the load. At the race's flight gravity a tap hops a boot's height (0.16 m) and a full load stands him a metre off the snow for three quarters of a second; a load carried into the air keeps, and one let go there is spent on nothing. The flight that follows is any other (`flight.ts`), counted as air, scored on a tricks run, and landed under the landing's load (below).

## The edge, the carve and the skid (`skier.ts`, `limits.ts`)

**The edge.** `steer` asks for an edge angle — the skis tipped off flat — up to the pair's `edgeMax` at a standstill, fading to two thirds by `steer.fadeSpeed` = 25 m/s (`edgeLockAt`, which the bot reads too: a skier at a schuss stands his skis flatter than one snaking a nursery slope), reached at `steer.edgeRate` = 3.4 rad/s scaled by the pair's edge factor. A dulled edge pulls the line toward its own side (`damage.ts`).

**The carve is the sidecut's geometry.** A ski on `edge` rad bends its sidecut into the snow and runs the arc that makes: a curvature of `tan(edge) / sidecut` (`carveCurvature` — 45° of edge carves about the sidecut radius, a flat ski runs straight), tightened by a lean forward, `1 − steer.tipLoad · lean` (0.25). The tip's line points inside the boot's by that curvature times how far ahead it stands and the tail's outside by the same — the TOE-IN a bent ski has — and each station's edge bites on its own line, so the tip's pulls the tip round, the tail's pushes the tail out, and the six stations slide across nothing only once the body is turning at exactly the curvature the edge asks for. That is the whole of why an edged ski turns without being turned, and it is the bare physics: with the arcade's hand off (`Assist.yaw` = 0) a half edge still carves. On the groomer only — in powder the edge is buried, the ski bends into its float, and THE CARVE IS THE BODY'S: a ski rolled over turns toward the low side, `skier.carve` = 1.2 of its load per radian of roll off the snow's plane, with way on by `carveSpeed` = 4 m/s.

**THE ARCADE'S HAND ON THE YAW** (`steer.yawHold` = 1600 N·m per rad/s) models nothing and says so. The yaw rate is held toward the one the edge's own geometry asks for (the way times the curvature) but no faster than `pathShare` = 0.9 of the corner grip can turn the way itself, and the body is held to the way it is going, `slipHold` = 1100 N·m per rad of slide once faster than `slipFrom` = 3 m/s — the two together no more than `yawHoldMax` = 1000 N·m, on the reference pair and scaled by this one's yaw inertia. It exists because the bare physics spins a skier who is over-edged at speed: a full edge at 60 km/h asks 1.6 g of the edges, the edges hold 1.2, and past what they hold the tanh grip saturates at every station and the tips' pull and the tails' push cancel — the skid has no restoring yaw and the body swaps ends. OPTIONS ▸ ASSIST is this dial.

**THE SCRUB** (`steer.scrub` = 0.12 — this is a FAST game, and a clean carve gathers speed down a red pitch rather than bleeding it): an edge holding a skier round a carve is cutting a groove, and cutting it is paid out of the way — a drag against the travel, at the CoG so it turns nothing, of that share of the bend's own acceleration (the rate the edge asks for, as the yaw hand reads it, times the way), on the packed share of the snow. A bend held on an edge scrubs speed, which is why the carves are measured on the 20° pitch and at the speed he came in with (below).

**THE CORNER** a pair can hold (`cornerGrip`, read by the yaw hold and the bot) is its sideways grip or its TIPPING POINT, whichever comes first — and on the groomer it is the grip: `tipLimit` is the tangent of the inclination a skier holds into a turn, the whole's `rollPacked` plus the angulation his hips hung `hipReach` inside over his CoG height half a tuck low, times the arcade's `hangOff` = 1.4 — 2.7 g on the Chamois, where the edges hold 1.2 (0.95 × 1.3). The bot corners at `cornerShare` = 0.7 of it.

**THE BACK KEY MEANS TWO THINGS, by the order it meets the edge in** (`input-model.ts`'s `backMode`, held for as long as the key is): pressed FIRST it is the BRAKE, and an edge put on after it swings the skis across the way into a hockey stop; pressed with an edge ALREADY ON it is the edge CUT HARDER. On the phone the edge thumb dragged down on the snow is the same key (in the air it is the lean back it always was).

**Cut harder** (`TUNING.carve`, `SkierInput.carve`, `SkierState.carve`): the skis stood `carve.edge` = 45 % further over than the speed's own lock allows (never past the spec's `edgeMax`), the curvature tightened by `carve.tighten` = 35 %, the pressed edge holding `carve.grip` = 30 % more sideways (and the yaw hand and the inclination allowed as much more of the corner), and `carve.scrubSpared` = 60 % of the groove's scrub spared — a tighter line that costs little: a full edge at 80 km/h down the 20° pitch carves a 34 m radius at 1.29 g cut hard, where the same edge not pressed runs 51 m at 0.89 g, at the same speed.

**The skid is the brake.** `brake` pivots the skis across the way (`SkierState.skid`, reached at `steer.skidRate` = 4 rad/s) by up to `skidAngle` = 1.05 rad at a crawl — a snowplough's 60° — narrowing to `skidFast` = 0.5 rad by `skidFadeSpeed` = 20 m/s (a hockey stop at speed is a narrower angle held harder), toward the side the edge is on: both skis one way is a HOCKEY STOP, and with the edge straight a SNOWPLOUGH, which pivots nothing and only scrubs. What it costs: the pivoted edge's sideways grip now works against the way, and the skis pay `skidDrag` = 0.35 of their load per unit of brake for the snow they shove aside — a snowplough alone is 0.35 g, which slows a skier clearly on a 20° groomer and brings him down to a walk on it. A pivoted ski is SCRAPING on its edge rather than cutting a groove with it, and sliding friction is less than the bite: a fully skidding ski keeps `grip.skidHold` = 0.5 of the edge's sideways hold (blended by `skid`), so a hockey stop pulls about 0.7 g where a real one pulls 0.5–0.8 — from 60 km/h, a couple of seconds and under twenty metres. The bot plans on `brakeDecel` less what the fall of the piste gives back (`v² = v_bend² + 2·a·d − 2·g·drop` — a skid down a steep pitch sheds only what it takes beyond the slope's pull), and while its skis are pivoted or still sliding across their line it stands them up no further than `slideEdge` of the edge that catches (`bot.ts`): a skier finishing a hockey stop flattens his skis as the slide dies. A slide the skier asked for is never a caught edge (`crash.catchSkid`, below). A finished skier checks himself to a stop in the arena on half the brake.

**THE SKIER** is nine tenths of the moving mass, and moving him is how skis are skied. His hips move `hipReach` inside the turn (THE ANGULATION, on the groomer only as far as the bend asks for it — full once it pulls `skier.hangG` = 0.35 g, at any pace in powder) and `aftReach` = 0.25 m fore and aft on the lean, both lagging `skier.lag` = 0.16 s; his weight off-centre is a moment on the whole. THE INCLINATION into a carve is the balance of the load the bend actually puts on him — atan(v²κ / g), the way a bicycle leans, off the curvature the edge asks for and never more than the grip holds — capped at `rollPacked` = 0.7 rad (40°, the whole body of a strong carver laid over; a racer goes past 60°), and in powder `rollPowder` = 0.5 rad at full edge, which is the whole turn there; held by `rollStiff` = 5000 N·m per rad with `rollDamp` = 380 N·m·s and never more than `rollMax` = 900 × `hangOff` N·m, giving out past a radian — a skier well over is going over and nothing holds him. THE FORE-AFT BALANCE: his boots and his legs hold the body square to the slope under him — toward `leanPitch` = 0.2 rad tips-up at full lean back — with `pitchStiff` = 4000 N·m per rad, `pitchDamp` = 300 N·m·s and no more than `pitchMax` = 1500 N·m, which is how he sits back into a skid rather than going over the tips (a skid at 0.9 g at the feet is 700 N·m over them). Both hands are stated on the reference pair and scaled by this one's inertia; in the air neither exists.

**THE TUCK.** The body folds toward the crouch the tuck asks for at `skier.crouchRate` = 3 /s, the drag area easing from `cdAUpright` to `cdATuck` (`dragAreaOf`) against the air at `airDensity` = 1.05 kg/m³ (−8 °C, two thousand metres up), and the CoG dropping `crouchDrop`. On the 20° pitch the Chamois settles at 117 km/h tucked and 86 standing tall.

## The air (`flight.ts`)

A skier is airborne when no station and no body point touches the snow; the `air` event fires once he has been up `air.counts` = 0.15 s (anything shorter is a skip over a roller). In the air he has one lever on the pitch, the LEAN (`leanTorque` = 53 N·m at full lean, back = tips up — 2.0 rad/s² on the reference pair's 26 kg·m² of pitch, enough to trim a landing and not to turn a flip), the edge swings the skis across for a little yaw (`steerTorque` = 6 N·m), nothing rolls him and the body levels the roll itself (`rollLevel` = 320 N·m per rad, `rollDamp` = 55 N·m·s, given up past `rollGiveUp` = 1.1 rad — a body thrown onto its side comes down on its side), and the air damps every rate: `damping` = 6 N·m·s about the pitch and the roll, `yawDamping` = 1.8 about the up axis (a skier is a tall body and a short one across: his yaw is a third of his pitch to turn, and the same drag would stop a 360 in half a second). THE ARCADE'S HAND ON THE PITCH: with the lean left alone the body eases the tips toward half the flight path, never more than `pitchAim` = 0.35 rad, at `pitchLevel` = 46 N·m per rad and no more than `pitchLevelMax` = 28 N·m, and over the last `landLook` = 0.8 s before the snow comes back toward THE SLOPE HE WILL LAND ON (`landingAhead`, the ballistic arc traced over the real snow), so both skis meet it along their length. All of it in the body frame; tips up is a negative torque about x. Three things a rigid body would do a skier does not: A SKIER SPINNING A 360 holds himself upright through it, so past `spinLevel` = 2 rad/s of yaw the roll's and the pitch's levelling are let go (a spinning body's tilt reads as pitch, then as roll, then as pitch again once a turn, and a spring on each axis chasing that reading at the spin's own rate — the roll's natural rate IS the spin's — wound every 360 into a tumble; let go, the few degrees he threw the spin with stay). The hips' weight off-centre (`hipRight`, `hipAft`) is a moment only against the snow's push back at the skis, none in the air. And Euler's coupling (ω × Iω) is stepped only on the snow: in the air he holds his shape with his limbs, and a body as thin as his — a yaw inertia a third of the pitch's and the roll's — fed every wobble back through it. HOW HE RIDES A FALL is the figure's alone (`pwa/src/game/skier-flight.ts`, read by nothing in the physics): on a fall clock that runs only while his skis are high over the snow (from 0.6 m, at full rate past 2.2 m), compact and quiet off a kicker, SPOTTING a drop (from 0.1 s: hands forward and wide, chest over the knees, head down on the landing), a measured forward WINDMILL on a cliff (from 0.32 s, a circle a second, never on a body turning a flip or a spin), wound home ahead of him from 0.6 s before the snow, and REACHING for it over the last 0.5 s — the arms forward and down, the legs extended by more the bigger the fall — timed off his flight's own ballistics over the map.

**THE AIR'S PULL is the arcade's too** (`air.gravity` = 1.5, `RunRules.airGravity`; 1 on a tricks run): a skier genuinely flying is pulled down at 1.5 g, because at the real 9.81 a kicker taken at race speed hangs him 6 m up for 2 s and a hang that long on a chase camera reads as slow motion rather than weight. Only a skier who was flying at the end of the last step feels it, so where he leaves the snow is a fact about the shape and the speed and the ground holds him over a roller at exactly g. The bot's ballistics read it through `flightGravity`.

**A landing** is the legs' job and the knees take it — the model of a good one is nothing at all. Past `air.harshSpeed` = 8 m/s of speed INTO the slope on the reference pair (`harshSpeedOf`: the stroke energy the legs store, scaled by the pair's own give — the soft park ski takes the hardest landing whole, the stiff downhill ski the least) the legs have folded to their stop and the skier pays `harshLoss` = 2 % of his way along the slope per m/s over, up to `harshMax` = 12 % (`landingLoss`) — which is why landing on a kicker's downslope is fast and landing flat after overshooting it is not.

**THE LANDING'S LOAD, and how true it must be** (`landingLoad`, `landingTolerance`, `landingOff`, `TUNING.landing`). The snow-park engineers measure a landing by its EQUIVALENT FALL HEIGHT — the speed into the slope as a drop from rest, EFH = v⊥² / 2g; jump designers hold a landing's sweet spot to 0.1–0.9 m and call past 1.5 m a hard one, measured tables run to 3–5 m off the sweet spot, and hard snow lands about a third harder than soft at the same spot. The legs and the snow stop that fall over a STROKE: the hips sinking from the stance into a squat, `landing.stroke` = 0.45 m (less `tuckStroke` = 25 % of it at a full tuck, which has already folded them), plus `give` = 60 % of whatever LOOSE snow lies under the skis (the dial's cover and the new fall, on the unpacked share — 40 cm of powder adds a quarter of a metre, a metre of it most of the fall). So the load is 1 + EFH / stroke g, carried on the `land` event with how far OFF TRUE the skis came down: the worst of the tips into the slope (`tipsDown` = 0.5 rad at the clean limit), the tails first (`tailsDown` = 0.8), the roll across it (`rolled` = 0.6) and the slide sideways to the way (`sideways` = 0.9; landing backwards counts as a quarter turn more), as a share of those. What a load FORGIVES shrinks with it: under `clean` = 6 g the whole of it — more still for a hop, to twice it at a standing load — then down to `tight` = 20 % of it at `buckle` = 14 g, and past the buckle nothing. That measure grades a landing for the score and the HUD (`tricks.ts`'s `landingGrade`, the news's load line); it no longer decides a fall. A PROFESSIONAL RIDES AWAY whatever he comes down ON HIS SKIS for — crooked, rolled onto his edges, slapped down tips-first — and goes down only where the body cannot stand up out of it (the wipeout, below): the legs folding past `crash.legsFold` = 18 g, or the hips, the shoulders or the helmet hitting the snow. Deep soft snow is a long, kind stroke and the groomer and the ice are not. Dropped 1.5 m at 70 km/h onto the groomer, skis true, is 8.9 g and ridden away; rolled 20° it is still ridden; onto his side it is a fall the moment his hip and shoulder land; dropped 8 m onto the groomer is 36 g and the legs fold however true; the same 8 m into a metre of powder is 13.7 g — ridden.

## The body meeting the snow (`chassis.ts`)

Eleven unsprung points — the skis' tips and tails (standing across the snow as the legs' stations do, raised by the tuck), the knees, the hips, the shoulders and the helmet (`hullOf`) — meet the snow when the legs have run out: a tail dragged over a crest, a tip buried in a face met too fast, a skier down on his hip. They are resolved at the VELOCITY level, not with springs (a penalty spring stiff enough to hold ninety kilos off a slope met at eighty kilometres an hour stores the impact and hands it back): each point in the snow has its velocity into the slope taken away by an impulse through the body's effective mass there (the angular term in, so a tip meeting the snow pitches him as well as stopping him), with `hull.restitution` = 0.1 of it given back and a push-out of `pushRate` = 10 /s of its depth, no more than `pushOut` = 1.5 m/s; Coulomb friction takes the slide along the slope up to `hull.friction` = 0.4 of the impulse. The snow under a body point is the powder's FLOOR (`powderFloor`): deep powder does not hold a body up, it is pushed aside by it, down to the base the skier's own skis pressed.

## Trees and the edge (`collision.ts`)

A tree is its trunk, a vertical cylinder of `radius` from its foot to its top (the crown is drawn and is nothing to the physics). The skier is three plan circles of `trees.bodyRadius` = 0.35 m down the skis' length; a circle inside a trunk is pushed out along the line between their centres, the closing speed comes back at `restitution` = 0.15, the speed along the trunk is scrubbed by up to `scrub` = 35 %, and a `hit` is reported at `hitSpeed` = 1.5 m/s closing or more, at most once per `cooldown` = 0.6 s. The map's edge turns a skier back over its last `bounds.soft` = 20 m with a push of `bounds.push` = 12 m/s², and holds him `bounds.margin` = 6 m inside it.

## The wipeout (`crash.ts`)

**A PROFESSIONAL'S RESILIENCE.** The skier goes down only when the body physically cannot stay up — and everything short of that he rides out, the near fall kept as a SAVE (below) for the figure to play. Six ways off, each a threshold on something the step has already measured; the numbers here are a professional's (`SkierState.resilience` = 1, the player's), and THE KNOB blends every one of them down toward a club skier's (`crash.club`, resilience 0 — `crashLimit`) for a skier between: each rival is dealt his own off `RACE.resilienceBand` = 0.35–1 at the start line, on a stream of its own so nothing else the start deals moves, and `createGame({ resilience })` sets the player's (1 when left out — difficulty, later). The bot, over the sim's seeds on every pair as a professional, lands every jump the map gives it and meets no trunk; the one place it goes down is a pre-existing trap on seed 10 where it is battered to a crawl and comes down on its side.

- **a trunk met hard** — a `hit` at `crash.treeSpeed` = 7 m/s (25 km/h) closing or more with the trunk SQUARE IN FRONT OF THE SKIS (the tips' circle): the trunk stops the skis and the skier goes on at `keep` = 85 % of the way he had, until the snow or the trunk itself stops him. A trunk BESIDE HIM, met by the body's or the tails' circle — taken on the shoulder — throws him only at `treeShoulder` = 10 m/s (36 km/h): the blow knocks him aside and round, and his skis are still under him (club: 5 and 6 m/s);
- **a landing over the tips** — a `land` ending a flight of `noseAir` = 0.3 s or more, at `noseImpact` = 5 m/s into the slope or more with the tips too far down against it: `noseDig` = 0.56 rad (32°) in loose snow, where they dig, and `noseAngle` = 0.8 rad (46°) on the groomer, where they slap down flat until they come in steep enough to spear it — the limit blended by the packed share under him (club: 25° and 32°). He goes over them, and the skis are given `skiKick` = 0.35 rad/s of tip-over per m/s of the impact (at most 5). A skip off a touchdown is that landing handing itself back and is never judged;
- **the body down** — the hips, the shoulders or the helmet driven into the snow at `bodySlam` = 1.5 m/s or more (`SkierState.bodyHit`, off the hull's own contacts — the speed a twelve-centimetre drop hits at; club 1 m/s), still going `rollSpeed` = 6 m/s: he has come down on his side, his back or his head. In a landing (or a moment after one) it is the `landing` cause, out of one the `roll`. A hip brushed in a deep carve slides along the snow and goes into it at a fraction of that, and is a save;
- **a fall at speed** — the body lying over on the SNOW (its up under `reset.overUp` = 0.25 of the ground's normal, not the sky's — a skier on a steep face stands well off vertical) for `rollHold` = 0.35 s (`SkierState.rolledFor`, held — not started over — through a bounce off the snow; club 0.12 s) at `rollSpeed` = 6 m/s or more: an edge lost and no hand put down in time. Turning over in the AIR is not a fall — the landing decides — and a slow sit-down he rides out, the reset's own clock standing him up;
- **the legs folded** — a `land` ending a flight of `landing.air` = 0.3 s or more at a load past `legsFold` = 18 g (club 10 g): more than legs can hold, however true he came down. How true the skis came down is NOT judged here: a landing on the skis is the snow's and the legs' to sort out, and one that is not true enough ends on the body;
- **a caught edge**, the high-side — a ski stood over `crash.catchEdge` = 0.85 rad (49°) while the snow slides across it faster than `catchSlip` = 9 m/s (32 km/h; `SkierState.sideSlip`, the fastest any touching station slid across its own line; club 0.7 rad and 6 m/s): the edge bites all at once and the body is thrown over it — unless the skis are in a SKID past `crash.catchSkid` = 0.5 of the brake's pivot, which is the slide he asked for: a hockey stop is exactly a ski stood on its edge and slid across the way. The bot stands its edge down well short of it, from `skier.slipEdge` = 0.75 rad and `slipSpeed` = 6 m/s.

**THE SAVE** (`noteSave`, `SkierState.save`): on a step that did not throw him, how near anything came — a hard or crooked landing (from `landing.clean` g toward `legsFold`, from `saveTip` = 0.3 rad tips-down toward the dig, from `saveRoll` = 0.5 rad rolled toward his side), a trunk (its closing speed over the limit it was met at), the body touching the snow, an edge biting (from `saveEdge` = 0.9 of the catching edge and `saveSlip` = 0.7 of the catching slide — an ordinary skidded turn is short of both) — as a share of the skier's own threshold, kept from `saveFrom` = 0.25 of one, with the side it came from and, for a landing, which way it threw him. A newer save takes the place of one older than `saveHold` = 0.5 s or nearer than what is left of it; a `save` event marks each. The figure plays it (`skier-save.ts`: sunk and lurched with the arms flung out for a landing, the shoulder knocked back and the body rocked away for a trunk, a hand down to the snow for the body, a dying wobble for an edge, followed on a spring of `JOLT_FOLLOW` = 24 rad/s so it comes and goes as a motion), the HUD says the near ones (`SAVED THE LANDING`, `SHRUGGED OFF THE TREE`, `BACK UP!`, `HELD THE EDGE`, from 0.6) and the hands feel a short shudder. Nothing in the physics reads it.

THE SKIER THROWN is a body of his own (`SkierState.thrown`): a RAGDOLL (`ragdoll.ts`) — thirteen points, the hips, the shoulders, the head, the knees, the feet, the elbows and the hands, each with a mass (80 kg in all, the trunk the heaviest) and a radius, moved by Verlet under gravity and held at the skier's own measures (`crash.body`, the same bones `skier-pose.ts`'s `BODY` draws) — Jakobsen's position-based body, with the pose drive of an active ragdoll held to the laws a real body keeps. The trunk and the head are rigid; the limbs swing, but only through what a body allows: a knee bends only forward and an elbow only back (each hinge left alone on a limb all but straight — `STRAIGHT` = 97 % of its reach — where a few degrees past is a locked joint, and a hinge put right only fights the bones' lengths), neither folds tighter than `kneeFold` = 35° or `elbowFold` = 30° between its bones, a thigh goes no further than `hipBack` = 0.35 of its length behind the hip nor `hipUp` = 0.3 above it, a hand never folds nearer its shoulder than `foldArm` = 0.2 m, and no limb comes nearer the line of the spine than `torso` = 0.16 m, so an arm flung across him meets his chest. A JOINT'S LIMIT IS AN INELASTIC STOP: the correction carries no velocity and the relative velocity across the joint that runs into it is taken away, shared by mass; each solver pass holds the joints first and the bones' lengths last, and THE JOINTS GIVE THE BODY NO ENERGY — whatever a pass added to his motion about his centre of mass is taken back off it (`calm`), because a joint put right and then held at its length is a kick of speed out of nowhere, and one threw a body lying still back up off the snow. THE MUSCLES (`crash.tone`): a man falling is not a sack. Every limb is driven toward a pose in the trunk's frame by a damped spring — in the air the PROTECTIVE REFLEX, `brace` = 10 rad/s at `braceDamp` = 0.8 of critical, the arms thrown out ahead and wide at `reach` = 0.85 of their length and the legs spread and a little bent (never tucked: a body that draws itself in spins up like a diver, and a pair of hands chasing the world's down would windmill and spin the trunk the other way), giving way at `relax` = 12 /s once his trunk (or a trunk's weight of him) is down to the slack pose a body lies in, legs out and arms out at his sides, at `lie` = 2 rad/s; a limb planted in the snow yields and is not driven. The muscles are inside the body, so the push and the twist their drive would put on the whole of him are taken straight back off every point — they move the limbs against the trunk and never turn the man; only the snow and the trunks can. He is laid where he stood and sent off at `keep` = 85 % of his way, turning at his speed over `tumbleRadius` = 2 m (no faster than `maxSpin` = 6 rad/s) with `carry` = half his own turning on top — and WHICH WAY HE GOES OVER is the cause's (`crash.over`, against the way he was going): a trunk pitches him on over his stopped skis and away from it, climbing 1.8 m/s; the tips digging pitch him over them, the same; a caught edge flings him on over it the way the snow slid, 1.2 m/s; a fall at speed takes him down onto the side he was lying toward, barely off the snow; and a landing his legs could not hold sits him down back and to the side — each sideways fall turning at least `topple` = 3 rad/s. EVERY POINT MEETS THE SNOW ON ITS OWN: settled `sink` = 0.15 m into powder at the ordinary dial, its way into the snow taken away and its way out no more than `crash.pushOut` = 0.3 m/s (a point put back on the surface from under it is a position corrected, not a launch — a body thrown down in deep powder starts a hand under the surface), Coulomb friction along it on its own weight and its arrival — `frictionPacked` = 0.45 on the groomer, `frictionPowder` = 0.8 in fresh snow — and the PLOUGH, `plough` = 1.2 of its way a second per unit of powder depth. But what touches the snow is a patch of back, side or shoulder, never a point to pivot over: `patch` = 70 % of every point's loss is taken off the body as a whole, so a hand or a head digging in slows him rather than being something to cartwheel over, and once a trunk's weight of him is in the snow it scrubs off `spinDrag` = 3 of his turning a second, more the deeper the powder he is in. So he goes over once or so with his arms out, is dragged flat by the snow, and slides to rest lying on it: about 30 m on the groomer from a 60 km/h landing over the tips, 18 m in powder and a little less in a metre of it. A trunk stops every point too. None of it draws from the stream: a crash is a pure function of the moment it started, and a run replays wipeout for wipeout.

While he is off them the skis go on with the controls let go (the yard sale), take no gate and run none of the automatic reset's clocks; the race clock runs. The reset comes once he has been off `lieMin` = 1.8 s and has lain still (every point under `restSpeed` = 0.6 m/s, on the snow) for `lieStill` = 1 s of it, or at `lieMax` = 6.5 s whatever he is doing — reported `auto`. The still beat is the one the app's death cam rises into the sky over him on, at full speed — a wipeout is never slowed. His own reset stands him up at once, and the HUD lights its press the step he is thrown.

## Bogged in deep powder (`trench.ts`)

A skier BOGGED — poling (the tuck over half) in powder under `trench.creep` = 1 m/s — for `after` = 1 s starts to sink: the hole under the skis (`SkierState.trench`) deepens at `dig` = 0.1 m/s in virgin powder, to `max` = 0.25 m on top of the sink. `skier.ts` adds it to the stations' sink target, so the skis hang in their own hole while the knees, which read the powder's floor, take the load they lost — to the knees, which is what bogged is — and takes up to `grip` = 75 % of the poles' push with it. No push-off out of a powder start gets near it.

ROCKING OUT: every metre the skier's weight moves (`hipAft` fore and aft on the lean, `hipRight` side to side on the edge) packs `rock` = 0.04 m of it back, and skiing out clears `clear` = 0.4 m per metre of way past `creep`. Past `stuckAt` = 0.05 m he is bogged and `stuck` fires once; from its first centimetre the automatic reset waits `holdFor` = 8 s instead of `reset.stuckFor`.

## Damage (`damage.ts`)

Only on a run that asked for it (`createGame({ damage: true })`, OPTIONS ▸ ASSIST ▸ DAMAGE, off by default; a rival never takes any). Three figures, each 0 sound … 1 wrecked: the two skis' edges and the legs. A trunk past `damage.treeFrom` = 4 m/s closing dulls the edge on its side by `treeRate` = 0.06 per m/s over; a landing past the pair's harsh speed hurts the legs by `landRate` = 0.05 per m/s over; a wipeout adds `wipeout` = 0.2 to what its cause reaches. Nothing mends it but a new run.

What it does: a dulled edge pulls the line `skiToe` = 0.06 rad toward its own side at fully dulled and bites `skiGrip` = 40 % less; hurt legs lose up to `springSoft` = 40 % of their rate, `dampSoft` = 50 % of their damping and `harshSoft` = 45 % of the speed into the slope they take whole. Every share is `1 − k·d`, so a sound skier is the same arithmetic to the last bit.

## The body (`body.ts`, `defs/anatomy.ts`)

WHAT HIS BODY TAKES, part by part, on every run and every skier — a readout: nothing in the physics reads it and it draws nothing from the stream. The skier is kept as twenty parts (the head, the neck, the chest, the back, the abdomen, the pelvis, and either side's shoulder, arm, hand, thigh, knee, shin and foot — `BODY_PARTS`), each with the worst injury it carries.

**A BLOW IS A STOP.** A part met at `v` m/s is brought to rest over its own give (`injury.give`: 6 mm at the head and the neck, 3–6 cm at the trunk, 3–8 cm at a limb, whose joints fold under it) and the give of what it met — the snow's (`injury.snow`: `ice` = 1.2 cm, the groomer's `packed` = 2.2 cm, loose snow `soft` = 5 cm and `deep` = 12 cm more per unit of the snow dial, the new fall counted in — `snowGive`) or a trunk's (`tree` = 1 cm, and on the head the helmet's liner crushing, `helmet` = 1.2 cm, which it does against a trunk and not against snow). Its peak is half a sine's over the mean v²/2s (`peak` = π/2): THAT is the blow, in g (`blowOf`). The numbers are fitted to the measured head drops on snow (a 6 kg headform at 6.1 m/s: 51 g on soft snow, 106 g on hard, 170 g on very hard — `tests/body_test.ts` holds the fit), so the same fall is a few g into a deep day's powder, tens on the groomer and a hundred on ice — and that is the whole of how the snow's depth and hardness reach an injury.

WHERE THE BLOWS COME FROM, each off something the step already measured:

- **on the skis** — a trunk (`hit`'s closing speed): SQUARE IN FRONT OF THE SKIS the tips meet it and the skis stop, levering the shins over the boots (`bend`, m/s) and twisting the knees (`noseTwist` = 0.45 of it), the body's own blow coming once it is thrown on into the trunk; BESIDE HIM it takes the shoulder, the arm, the ribs, the flank, the hip, the thigh and a glancing share of the head (`injury.side`). The hips, the shoulders or the helmet into the snow (`SkierState.bodyHit`). A LANDING: its load (`land`'s `g`, the equivalent fall height over the legs' stroke) runs up the legs into the spine (`load`; `folded` = 1.3 times it when the legs folded past `legsFold`), onto the knees as a DRAWER load — `square` = 0.6 of it on a landing square on the skis, more by `backSeat` = 1.2 at the tails as far down as `landing.tailsDown` (the cruciate's phantom foot and the boot's drawer) and `crooked` = 0.6 at a roll as far as `landing.rolled` — and whips the neck (`neck` = 1.6 g per g over standing); a landing that came down on the body leaves the legs `onBody` = 0.4 of it. And the falls that are a mechanism: a caught edge twists the knee of the outside ski at the slide's speed (`twist`, m/s; the other at half), a fall at speed at `rollTwist` = 0.5 of the speed, a fall over the tips levers both shins at the speed;
- **thrown** — every point of the ragdoll meeting the snow or a trunk this step (`Thrown.impacts`, `.struck`: the way it brought into what it was put back out of, a FRESH contact only — a point lying on the snow is not struck again): the head (and `share.neck` = 0.35 of it on the neck), a shoulder or a hip (and `share.trunk` = 0.6 of it on the chest or the abdomen when he comes down on his front, the back and the kidneys on his back, the ribs and the spleen or the liver on his side — read off the ragdoll's own chest against the snow's normal or the trunk's), a knee (and the thigh and the shin), a foot (and the shin), an elbow (and the shoulder), a hand (and the arm and the shoulder — the fall on an outstretched hand);
- **another skier** — the player's shoulder into a rival's (`feelBumps`).

**A DOSE IS A CHANCE.** Every injury on a part's ladder (`INJURIES`, 57 of them, each ranked on the Abbreviated Injury Scale — 1 minor, 2 moderate, 3 serious, 4 severe, 5 critical; 6, unsurvivable, is on no ladder) has the dose of its mechanism at which it is an even chance (`at`), and the chance at a dose `d` is 1 / (1 + (at/d)^`steep`), `steep` = 8 — the log-logistic of the injury-risk curves: three quarters of the dose a chance in eleven, a quarter more six in seven, under `floor` = half of it none. It is drawn off a hash of the step, the part and the map (never `state.rng`), so a run replays injury for injury and no digest moves. The ladder is read worst first; a part takes at most one new injury a step and never one ranked under what it carries; a part already hurt is hurt again more easily (`weaken` = 6 % off every threshold per rank); and of every part's injury drawn in one step the worst `perBlow` = 3 are taken. The ladders lean on the measured tolerances: a concussion an even chance at 95 g (the measured mean 96–103 g), a skull fracture at 230 g in a helmet, broken ribs at 52 g (the chest's 60 g over 3 ms), a compressed vertebra at a landing of 15.5 g and a broken back at 21 (the spine's 20–25 g), the cruciate torn by a drawer of 15 g or a twist at 11.5 m/s, the medial ligament by a twist at 9.5, the skier's thumb at 21 g on the hand; the kidneys only from behind, the spleen from the left, the liver from the right.

THE WHOLE BODY is summed the way a trauma ward sums it: the INJURY SEVERITY SCORE (`severityOf`), the squares of the worst rank in each of the three worst-hurt regions (the head and the neck; the chest and the back; the abdomen; the limbs and the pelvis) — 0 unhurt, 16 and up major trauma, 75 the top. A reset does not mend him; a new run does.

THE G METER (`SkierState.body.impact`): the hardest blow a step dealt to the head or the trunk — a limb's whip is a small mass flung faster than he is and is not billed — from `shown` = 3 g (a landing from `landingShown` = 2 g, after a real flight), held `hold` = 1.6 s, a harder one in that time taking its place; `peak` is the run's hardest.

MEASURED (`make ride`, the professional, chamois): the stock kicker overshot at 75 km/h lands at 8 g and hurts nothing; a 3 m drop onto flat packed snow at 70 km/h is 15 g — whiplash; the drop from twelve metres equivalent is 36 g on the groomer (a spinal cord injury and both cruciates) and 14 g in powder (a compressed vertebra); a caught edge at 45 km/h tears both knees' ligaments; a trunk on the shoulder at 28 km/h is 124 g — broken ribs, a broken collarbone and a broken arm; a trunk dead ahead at 45 km/h, thrown on into it at 63, is 161 g and an injury severity score of 22. The bot over twelve seeds on two pairs is hurt only by a 14 g landing on seed 7 (a strained back and a sprained knee) and on seed 10's battering spot.

## Tricks (`strokes.ts`, `tricks.ts`)

Only on a run whose rules count them (`RunRules.tricks` — the TRICKS mode); on every other run the lean and the edge are the air control above and nothing more. The numbers are `TUNING.tricks` (`defs/tricks.ts`). **THE STROKES** are read off the SHAPE of an input: the lean carried past `flipGate` = 0.8 toward either end of its axis is THE PUMP, one angular impulse of `flip` = 77 N·m·s about the pitch axis over the pair's own inertia — about 2.9 rad/s on the Chamois — back for a backflip, forward for a front flip; the edge carried past `spinGate` = 0.85 is THE TWIRL, `spin` = 38 N·m·s about the up axis, about 4.2 rad/s — the 360; each wound up and paid out along a critically damped rise, out of a flight's budget of `flipCeiling` = 6.5 and `spinCeiling` = 8 rad/s, and only in a flight that left the snow climbing. **THE POSES**: the trick button held in the air takes the body off the controls into a DAFFY (the edge over), a SPREAD (the lean back) or a GRAB (the lean forward, or nothing) (`poseOf`). **THE SCORE** pays the air by the second and by the metre on one curve, the Nth revolution on an axis N × 300 points and N steps of multiplier, a pose 150 and a step, and banks the combo on a landing the legs took whole — `landingGrade` reads the touchdown against the pair's harsh speed; clean at 0.6 of it, perfect at 0.3 — at its base on a sketchy one, and loses it to a wipeout, a reset or a landing taken in a pose. The score is kept on every run and draws nothing.

## The gates (`course.ts`)

A gate is taken by crossing its line, facing down the piste, inside its width and `course.grace` = 2 m either side of it (`course.startGrace` = 10 m more at the start gate, for a field jostling off the line). On a single-piste map every gate spans the piste, and one skied past — its line crossed beside it within `course.missReach` = 25 m of its edge, or the next gate taken while it is owed — costs nothing: the HUD points back at it and lights the reset, and the run owes it until it is taken. On a resort's COURSE (R28) every gate between the start gate and the finish is a SLALOM GATE, set off the line and narrower than the piste (`Checkpoint.offset`, `.span`), and one skied past — beside it anywhere across the piste, or the next one taken first — costs `course.missPenalty` = 3 s on the run's clock (`Progress.penalty` keeps the sum, already in `time`) and the run goes on to the next: an arcade racer's penalty, never a climb back to a gate set in the middle of the snow. The `missed` event carries the penalty, and the news column and the finish plate say what it cost. The bot skis THE GATE LINE (`gateLineAt`): a cosine from each gate's offset to the next's, standing on every gate's centre, crossing the line straight halfway between two set either side and turning hardest round each gate, its curvature added to the bend's when the bot reads how fast it can go.

## The wind tunnels (`wind-tunnel.ts`)

Along the hub at the foot of a resort (R29) run two WIND TUNNELS (R30), `W1` one way across the valley floor and `W2` back: a horizontal lift a skier is blown down without skiing a metre. He is TAKEN IN where he stands inside one's width with his skis within `tunnel.capture` = 0.7 rad of the way it blows — so a racer crossing one on his way to the finish is never carried off — and the engine says so (`{ kind: "tunnel", phase: "in" }`, `SkierState.tunnel` while he rides). Inside, the air moves along the tunnel at its own speed (R30's `tunnel.speed`, 28 m/s), so his drag is against THAT air: it pushes him on while he is slower and costs him nothing once he rides at its pace; the blowers THRUST him along over it, `tunnel.thrust` = 9 m/s² while he is `tunnel.soft` = 4 m/s or more under the wind, easing to nothing at it (and a quarter of it back past it), so a skier stood at the entrance is at the wind's speed in a few seconds; and across it he is CENTRED, `tunnel.centre` = 0.8 m/s² a metre off its line with `tunnel.damp` = 1.5 /s of his sideways way taken out. He is LET GO `tunnel.release` = 2 m past its edge, at its exit with his way kept, or thrown (`phase: "out"`). The player and every rival alike: it is part of the one step a skier takes (`run.ts`), drawing nothing from the stream.

## The reset

A skier's own (`SkierInput.reset`) or the engine's: a body whose up axis has been under `reset.overUp` = 0.25 of vertical for `reset.overFor` = 3 s, or held poling below `stuckSpeed` = 0.6 m/s for `stuckFor` = 3 s — or, once bogged, for `trench.holdFor` = 8 s — or thrown and lain long enough (above), is stood back on the piste `course.resetAhead` = 3 m past the last gate he took (or short of the start gate before he has taken one; on a free ride, at the nearest point of the nearest run of the resort — a piste preferred over a transport lane by 40 m — or of the map's one piste — unless that is within `course.footReach` = 30 m of the foot of a run into the village, the finish and its arena, where nothing is left to ski and the reset is the lift back up to the start line), at rest, facing down it (`course.ts`).
