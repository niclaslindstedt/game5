# Skiing

The skier model (`engine/game/skier.ts`, with `suspension.ts`, `snow.ts`, `poles.ts`, `flight.ts`, `chassis.ts` and `collision.ts`; the shared numbers in `engine/game/defs/tuning.ts` and the pair of skis in `defs/skis.ts`) is a rigid body on the snow — the skier and his skis as one — integrated semi-implicitly at 120 Hz. Every force is summed in `stepSkier`, each from the module that owns its model, and each model says where its numbers come from at the function that implements it. **There is no MODE.** A launch is a skier whose stations have all left the snow; a landing is one whose stations came back; a fall is a body lying on its side. Each is read off the same forces every other step is made of, and the events say so after the fact.

Every number below is quoted with its unit as `TUNING` or `SKIS` states it; the measured ones at the end come from skiing the real engine on the synthetic maps the tests use (`npm run ride`).

The three speed-event pairs are built to their discipline's competition rules at the top level (`docs/disciplines.md`, "The skis"): the Chough at the men's least giant slalom length (1.93 m) and sidecut (30 m) on the most waist (65 mm) under a 98 mm shoulder (at most 103), the Falcon at the men's least super-G length (2.10 m) and sidecut (45 m) on the most waist under a 94 mm shoulder (at most 95) — its shovel short and low, as the class's is, so its sidecut runs 1.97 m between the widest points — the Eagle at the men's least downhill length (2.18 m) and sidecut (50 m) on the most waist under a 90 mm shoulder (at most 95). The Peregrine is built to speed skiing's top class (`docs/disciplines.md` § Speed skiing): the most length its rule allows (2.40 m), under its 10 cm, next to no sidecut, 27 kg of kit with the skis' 15, and the airtight suit and calf fairings' tuck of 0.08 m² (the class measures 0.06–0.09); its `topSpeed` is the terminal speed on the reference pitch, 316 km/h, which no track is long enough to reach — `tests/catalog_test.ts` holds it to the terminal and the physics to three quarters of it half a minute down the strip, still gathering. The Wolverine is built to the ski-cross class (`docs/disciplines.md` § Ski cross), whose rules set no length, width or radius — only a binding plate at most 50 mm high, which its look stands on: a giant slalom ski cut down, 1.88 m on a 24 m sidecut (the class runs some 1.80–1.95 m on 21–27 m), a 68 mm waist under a 105 mm shoulder, softer and a touch rockered at the tip for the rollers and the landings — it lands softer than every other race pair, and round a berm at 65 km/h it holds the hardest bend of the nine (the ski card's BERM). Every pair's widths and sidecut are stated together: the edge between the drawn plan's widest points (a chord c) bows in by the side depth d = ((tip + tail) / 2 − waist) / 2, and the circle through the three, R ≈ c² / 8d, is the spec's sidecut to within a sixth — a race pair's to a few per cent (`tests/topsheet_test.ts`).

Every pair carries a `skierMass` = 80 kg skier in his kit, standing `cogHeight` = 1.0 m over the snow (skier and skis together, the body frame's origin), his boots `stance` = 0.3 m apart, his hips able to hang `hipReach` = 0.35 m inside a turn, a full tuck dropping him `crouchDrop` = 0.3 m, his poles reaching `poleReach` = 0.9 m ahead and pushing `polePush` = 260 N a plant. A racer's tuck is 0.25–0.35 m² of drag area and a recreational skier's half-tuck 0.45–0.6; standing tall on a piste 0.6–0.9.

`topSpeed` is a DOCUMENTED EXPECTATION, not an input: the physics delivers it, `tests/skier_test.ts` and `tests/catalog_test.ts` hold every pair to within a tenth of it, and the bot reads it (`limits.ts`'s `topSpeedOf`) to know what flat out is. It is quoted in a tuck down `TOP_SPEED_PITCH` = π/9 (a 20° groomed schuss — a red piste's steep pitch, the steepest a groomer holds a straight line down for long), and it is where the air's drag on `cdATuck` meets the slope's pull less the base's friction (`terminalSpeed`): `v = √(2·m·g·(sin θ − crr·cos θ) / (ρ·C_dA))`, 117 km/h on the Chamois.

**The Chamois is the reference pair.** Every shared number in `TUNING` was tuned on it, and `footprint.ts` prices every other pair's skis as multipliers on the shared snow model that read exactly 1 for it:

- _the pressure_ is the whole weight over the two skis' length by their waist. The rest sink in powder goes as `(p / p₀)^footprint.floatExp` (0.8) and the planing speed as `√(p / p₀)` — the wide powder ski sinks least and comes up onto the top soonest, the narrow race skis sink most — and the same sink prices the powder drag, the work of compacting the snow (Bekker);
- _the edge_ holds the groomer as `1 + edgeFlex · (flex − flex₀)` (0.5) and `1 − edgeRocker · (rocker − rocker₀)` (0.35): a stiff ski holds its whole length on the edge, a soft one its middle, and a rockered tip is lifted off the snow before the edge starts — the downhill ski bites hardest, the powder ski least;
- _the base_ holds powder as the waist to `baseFloat` (0.8), and a rockered tip floats and steers there, `1 + rockerFloat · (rocker − rocker₀)` (0.3);
- _the roll onto an edge_ goes as the inverse of `(L / L₀) · (W / W₀)^waistRate · (1 + flexRate · (flex − flex₀))` (0.5, 0.4): a short narrow ski is the quickest edge to edge (the slalom ski), a long stiff one the slowest (the downhill ski) — the waist because the boot stands that much further from the edge it is tipped onto;
- _a soft ski lands softer_: the harsh speed goes as `1 + flexHarsh · (flex₀ − flex)` (0.25).

The exponents are tuning values, bounded by the measurements above rather than read off a paper; `make ride ARGS="--skis all"` is what they are judged by.

The inertia is a tall body over a pair of rods (`inertiaOf`): on the Chamois 26 kg·m² in pitch, 9 in yaw and 28 in roll — which is why a skier turns far more readily than he pitches, and why every hand on the body below is stated on the reference pair and scaled by this one's inertia about that axis.

## The rider's weight (`defs/riders.ts`)

The skier picks his BUILD on the DRESS card (`Outfit.weight`): **LIGHT** 60 kg, **MEDIUM** 80 kg (the reference — every pair's row carries him), **SOLID** 95 kg and **HEAVY** 115 kg, each in his kit. `withRider(spec, rider)` restates the row's skier and nothing of the skis, and the medium rider hands the row back untouched, so no digest, no pinned run and no ghost skied before a build could be chosen moves. What it restates, and why:

- _the mass_ (`skierMass`) — which the model already reads everywhere: the sink's load and the footprint's pressure (a heavy rider sinks deeper in powder and planes later), the inertia (he turns and flips a little heavier), the legs' load and every impulse;
- _the drag area_ (`cdAUpright`, `cdATuck`) as `√(m / m₀)` — at one height a body's breadth and depth grow together with its mass — so the terminal speed `√(m / cdA)` climbs as the fourth root of his weight, and `topSpeed` is restated with it: in a tuck down the reference pitch on the Chamois **110.6 km/h light, 116.8 medium, 120.8 solid, 125.6 heavy** (`make ride ARGS="--card --rider <id>"`);
- _the legs_ (`legs.rate`, `bump`, `rebound`) in step with the whole's mass — he stands at the same sag and bobs at the same rate, so the pose is the same;
- _the push_ (`strength`: light 0.85, medium 1, solid 1.12, heavy 1.05 of the reference skier's, in both `polePush` and `poles.power`) — never in step with the mass, so a heavier skier skates up to speed slower: 12 s of skating across the flat reaches 24.6 km/h light, 23.8 medium, 23.9 solid and 21.8 heavy;
- _the legs' hold_ (`hold`: light 1.08, medium 1, solid 0.94, heavy 0.8) — the share of the reference legs' stroke he stops a landing over. Stopping a falling body takes force in step with its mass and a muscle's force grows only as its cross-section, so the same drop is more g to a heavy skier (`landingLoad`'s stroke times `hold`; the landing he takes whole, `harshSpeedOf`, as `√hold`): 1.5 m onto the groomer at 70 km/h is 8.1 g light, 8.7 medium, 9.2 solid and 10.7 heavy, and a 3 m drop onto the flat that a medium skier lands at 15 g folds a heavy one's legs (`crash.legsFold`, 18 g).

AGAINST ANOTHER SKIER the exchange is shared by the riders' weights (`shoulderShare`): two skiers of the field shoulder each other by their `skierMass` (half each, to the bit, between two of one build — the field is always medium), and the crowd's knock-down, the player's own throw off an amateur and the body's blow from a shoulder are stated against the medium rider and scaled by his share — a heavy rider knocks an amateur down at a lower closing speed and is jolted less himself.

## Where the skis meet the snow, and the legs (`suspension.ts`)

SIX STATIONS: each ski's tip, its middle under the boot and its tail (`STATIONS`, at 0.38 of the length ahead of the boot, 0 and 0.32 behind, bearing a quarter, a half and a quarter of the ski's load). Each station is a spring-damper — THE LEG — cast down THE SNOW'S OWN NORMAL from under the hips: along the ski's line by its station (the body's forward axis, so a body pitched over its tips loads them), across the snow by half the stance (the ground's own across, square to the skis), and down from the CoG — the raycast vehicle, whose contact is wherever that ray meets the snow's SUPPORT (`snow.ts`), found by Newton's method along the ray. **The stance stands on the snow and the leg reaches from the hips**: a skier's legs are two, and his skis stay on the snow whatever his body does above them, so the body's roll is its INCLINATION into a turn and never a ski lifted. Cast from a point rolled over with the body along its own axis — the sibling games' sled — a 0.3 m stance under a body laid 30° over was one ski 0.15 m off the snow, and every turn-in a skier falling over his inside ski.

The REST LOAD each station carries comes off the geometry rather than being authored — each ski half the weight, its stations by their shares — and each attachment is placed so its spring sits at exactly its rest sag with the CoG at `cogHeight`. So a skier put down on flat snow stands at the height the spec says (`tests/skier_test.ts`): the Chamois stands 0.988 m over the groomer (1.0 less the corduroy's 1.2 cm of sink) with his knees bent 6 cm, and the six loads sum to his 868 N.

The legs: `legs.rate` = 7500 N/m per ski (the three stations of one ski share it), sagging about 6 cm at rest — about 1.8 Hz in heave — with `bump` = 500 N·s/m folding and `rebound` = 900 straightening (a knee is always softer folding than straightening: a landing taken in one bob), over `travel` = 0.45 m. The damper reads THE COMPRESSION'S OWN CHANGE against the same surface as the spring, so a crease in the snow is a crease in both; over the last `BOTTOM_ZONE` = 30 % of the stroke the bump damping rises to three times its own (the muscle a skier braces a landing with), and past the travel a HYSTERETIC stop at `STOP_RATE` = 12 times the leg's rate loads fully and hands back `STOP_RELEASE` = 20 % of it on the way out — a knee at the end of its bend swallows a slam rather than springing off it. THE TUCK FOLDS THE LEGS: the stations hang `crouchDrop · crouch` higher, and the body is moved down by the change along its own up as it folds (kinematically — a tuck let go at 80 km/h on loaded springs fired the sibling model half a metre into the air), so a crouched skier sits lower on legs that still carry all of him and has less leg left for a landing.

Two FUSES, which are not models: no station may push more than `MAX_LOAD` = 15 times its rest load, and no body rate may exceed `MAX_SPIN` = 25 rad/s. A station is read only while the body's up points at least `PROBE_MIN_DOWN` = 0.25 of the way at the sky — a skier on his side has no legs under him.

## The snow (`snow.ts`)

**The sink** is the planing hull's draft carried straight over. Snow under a moving ski has less time to yield the faster it is crossed, so the support rises with speed:

```
sink = powderSink · depth · scale · exp(−(v / planeSpeed)²) · (1 − packed) + packedSink · packed
```

with `powderSink` = 0.22 m (a pair on 40 cm of fresh snow, buried to the boot tops at rest), `packedSink` = 0.012 m (a groomed piste's centimetre of corduroy), `planeSpeed` = 6 m/s — a third of the rest sink at the planing speed and a few centimetres by 30 km/h, the moment a skier feels the tips come up — `scale` the pair's own `Footprint.sink` (and `endSink` = 0.7 of it at a tip or a tail: the ski is a beam and its ends ride up), the planing speed scaled by `Footprint.plane`, and `packed` the level's `packedAt` under the station. Each station's support follows the sink the speed asks for over `sinkLag` = 0.25 s.

**The snow dial.** A free ride may ask for shallower or deeper powder (`SNOW_DIAL`, 0.25–2.5; the start card's SNOW row asks for it as a depth of loose snow, read back through `snowCoverOf`, `snow.cover` = 0.4 m at the ordinary dial): `GameState.snowDepth` multiplies `powderSink` wherever it is read, and the groomer's cut is left alone.

**DEEP SNOW** (`snow.ts`'s header). Past the ordinary depth — up to a metre of fresh snow, `deep.full` = 2.5 on the dial, a maritime winter's usual by March — the powder is BOTTOMLESS, and everything it adds grows by that share (`bottomlessOf`: none at the ordinary depth, where every race is skied and nothing below changes by a bit): _the snow gives under load_, `(L / L₀)^give` with `deep.give` = 0.8 (Bekker's exponent for fresh snow is near one), read no further than `loadMax` = 2.5 and never past `deep.compact` = 0.72 of the loose layer; _it stays pressed_, a support coming back up only as fast as the ski moves onto snow nobody pressed (`settleShare`, all of it by `deep.settle` = 2 m/s); _it planes later_, the planing speed going as the dial to `deep.plane` = 0.1; _the body ploughs_, the knees each over a shin's `0.12` m paying `deep.belly` = 0.5 of the plough's law on whatever untouched powder stands over them (to `bellyMax` = 0.5 m) — so tips held up out of the snow pay none of it, and leaning back is how a skier climbs onto the top; and _it turns with the weight_: the roll the skier holds loses `skier.deepHold` = 0.7 of itself and THE SOFT SIDE GIVES, a moment of `skier.deepTip` = 2 times his weight at his CoG height per radian of roll off the snow's plane, fully while the skis sit down at their rest sink and `deepPlaning` = half of it planing on top, only once he is moving (over `deepTipFrom` = 1.5 to `deepTipFull` = 4 m/s).

**The new snow.** Where the sky snows (R19), the fall at a moment is `snowAt(level, t)` in `engine/game/snowfall.ts`, a pure function of the map and the clock, and it LAYS SNOW: `GameState.fresh` grows at `freshRate` = 8 cm an hour at a fall of 1, and `freshBury` = 0.18 m of it buries the groomer's feel outright — every station, the hull, a stood skier and a thrown body read the surface through `packedUnder`, and `depthUnder` deepens the dial by it.

**The piste through the day** (`engine/game/piste-day.ts`, `PISTE_DAY` in `defs/piste-day.ts`). On a free ride — the run whose rules have the ski area's machines, `RunRules.groomer` — the runs are as the map's hour, date and sky have left them since the night's grooming, worked out once as the run is stood up (`pisteDayOf`, a pure function of the map: no draw, nothing stepped) and kept as `GameState.piste`. The machines finish their pass at `groomed` = 08:00 solar time, so the first chair at `open` = 09:00 rides the night's corduroy whole. From then the traffic (full until `close` = 16:30, `after` = 0.3 of it later) skis the comb off: `worn` = 1 − exp(−hours / `wear`), `wear` = 4 h — a fifth worn by 10:00, half by noon, four fifths by the last chair. A snowing sky lays its fall on the runs at `freshRate` from the night's last pass, and the traffic skis a share of what lies into the piste (it settles toward the rate times `pack` = 3 h): a steady fall leaves 5–6 cm by the afternoon, a storm 15–17 cm, a flurry under a centimetre — and that loose layer is the new snow the run starts with (`GameState.fresh`), so `packedUnder` buries the groomer by it as by any fall. A high sun warms a groomer over `sun.from` = 15° of elevation (the sine's excess, by the sky's lid: none under a snowing sky, a third under a lid), cooling back over `sun.cool` = 1.5 h; past `sun.soft` it is slush (`soft`) — a spring afternoon's, hardly a midwinter one's and never the far north's — and what was slush and is no longer has FROZEN (`hard`): the evening's glassy groomer. The physics reads it two ways: `loose` — `loose.worn` = 0.22 of a worn groomer's pack and `loose.soft` = 0.35 of a slushed one's (at most `loose.most` = 0.5) reads as loose snow (`packedUnder`'s third argument, and so the sink, the drag, the grip and the hiss), and `ice` = 0.4 of `hard` is the share of bare ice's grip a refrozen groomer stands at (`pisteIce`, through `onIce`). A cell the night's machines have groomed since (`groomed.ts`) is none of it. Nor is the ground the ski area KEEPS through the day (`kept-ground.ts`, `keptAt`): every top station's pad and deck, a chair's unload ramp and the way off, the ramps off a top down onto its runs, a drag's let-go, the load zone and corral round every lift's foot and the transport lanes — flat ground no pitch would carry a rider through, shovelled, milled and ploughed — which is packed through under only what has fallen since the ride began, so a storm's hand of loose snow never stops a rider stepping off a lift (`make lift-tops ARGS="--weather storm"`). The tail of a ski ridden switch is not dug in by it (`tailDug`): heaps a hand high over a hard base are no cover to bury a tail in. A race, a trial and a measurement are dealt none of it, so no digest moves; `CreateGameOptions.piste` asks for it (or not) on any run.

**The resistance**, per station, along its line of travel, faded out below `DRAG_FADE` = 0.3 m/s so a skier at rest is not rocked through zero:

- _the base's friction_: `crrPacked` = 0.035 of the load on the groomer (0.03–0.05 on cold hard snow) or `crrPowder` = 0.07 in fresh snow;
- _the plough_: `plough` = 70 N per metre of ski width per metre of sink per (m/s)², in powder only and only at the TIP (the mid and the tail run in the furrow it cuts). This is the bow wave, and why a skier bogged in powder wants momentum: it grows with the square of the speed while the sink it multiplies falls away, a HUMP to get over;
- _powder drag_: `powderDrag` = 0.004 of the load per m/s — what compacting fresh snow costs at speed, gone on the groomer, scaled by the pair's sink. Set so the Chamois in a tuck runs about four fifths of its piste speed in powder once planing (92 of 117 km/h).

**The grip** — coefficients on the station's load, each a `tanh` of its sideways slip over `sideRef` = 0.4 m/s, which is how an edge lets go (progressively, then all at once): on packed snow THE EDGE'S hold, `edgePacked` = 0.95, of which a ski lying FLAT keeps `flatShare` = 0.3 and the rest comes with the edge angle, `sin(edge) / sin(edgeMax)`, times the pair's edge factor; in powder THE BASE'S hold, `basePowder` = 0.45, width-scaled; blended by `packed`; on bare ice (`Level.iceAt`, a frozen tarn) each cut to `ice.edge` = `ice.base` = 0.35 of itself. Every sideways grip is multiplied by the arcade's `sideGrip` = 1.3. **THE PLATFORM** (`grip.platform`, `snow.ts`'s `platformOf`): past `from` = 0.95 rad (54°) of edge a ski bent into reverse camber has cut a shelf as long as itself and stands on it, and the snow's reaction is square to its base — so what it holds across grows as tan θ, the reaction's share across the slope over its share into it: `share` = 0.6 of tan θ's growth past `from`, as a multiple of the edge's own bite, for a skier whose technique stands on it (`Technique.platform`, below; the free skier's is 0, so every mode but the slalom holds exactly the edge's bite). That share of each station's hold runs up the inclined leg through his hips, so it turns him as any edge does, from where the station stands along the ski, and rolls him no further — the bend's moment a racer carries by his inclination, not his muscles. On the slalom pair cut hard at 70° it holds some 2.9 g where the bite alone holds 1.6: a racer on 65–70° of edge carries 2.5–3 g round a slalom pole (the measured loads are 2.5–3 body weights).

**Standing still** — a `tanh` grip is nothing at no slip and develops over `sideRef` and the drag's fade (0.3–0.4 m/s), so on its own it lets a skier stood on a pitch creep down it at the slip his grip needs to hold him (0.15 m/s on his brake in the start gate), and a stop on a steep pitch settle into a slow side-slip that never ends. So below `grip.stillSpeed` = 0.6 m/s over the snow — twice that slip — a skier on his skis who is not working for his speed, springing off them, bogged or in a wind tunnel is HELD (his way over the snow taken out, his legs left to settle along its normal) wherever his stations hold the slope's pull at no slip. A ski at rest presses the snow under it into a LEDGE level across it, so however steep the pitch the pull across the skis stands on that ledge, and only the pull ALONG their line is the base's and the plough's to hold (on bare ice, which takes no ledge, the edge's own hold is all there is across): each station's share of the pull (its load times the slope's tangent) over what it holds, an ellipse between the two ways, weighted by the loads. The WIND's push is shared out over the stations the same way, by their loads, over a hold at rest `wind.still` = 3 times the sliding one (a waxed base's static friction on cold snow is some three times its sliding friction), so a breeze never starts a skier stood still and a storm's gust at his back does. So it is the skis' line that decides, not the pitch: stood across a 31° face he stays; pointed down it, or in a plough down a groomer steeper than the plough holds, he runs. A stop ends on the edge SET: the skid's scrape (`skidHold`) fades out below `grip.skidBite` = 2 m/s, so a hockey stop on a 29° groomer ends stood rather than side-slipping. UNDER THE LIGHTS he is held in the gate outright (`run.ts`), his poles planted over the wand; and SAT ON A CHAIR (`lift-ride.ts`) he is inclined to nothing, the cross-slope passing under the chair not being under his skis.

## The drive he makes himself (`poles.ts`)

THE ONE PUSH THAT IS NOT GRAVITY, and it is AUTOMATIC: a skier going slowly works for his speed whatever the thumbs say — to gain it and to KEEP it. At a walk — all of it under `poles.strideFrom` = 1.6 m/s and none past `strideTo` = 3 (`strideShare`) — he sets off on his POLES on the flat and down a pitch, the racer's double pole out of the gate, and only up a rise (the view reads the pair's pitch, `gaitOf`) STRIDES, the diagonal stride — the skis parallel, each leg kicking back in turn and gliding the other forward, the opposite arm planting its pole; rolling he SKATES — the skis opened into a V, a leg pushing out off each stride and lifted back in, the poles planted with every push — to `skateFrom` = 5.5 m/s, blending into the DOUBLE POLE by `skateTo` = 9 (both poles planted ahead together, the body folded down over them). The push is POWER-LIMITED, as every human-powered drive is: the lesser of what a plant can press (the spec's `polePush`, 260 N on the Chamois) and `poles.power` = 450 W over the speed — a fit recreational skier's sprint — whole under `poles.speed` = 6 m/s (22 km/h) and gone by `poles.fade` = 9.5 m/s (34 km/h), so a racer skates out of the gate and keeps skating on a flat to hold his speed, and the power law is what makes the last of it little; `powderShare` = 40 % of it in powder, and up to `trench.grip` = 75 % less for a bogged skier. It is not taken away for a bend he can STEP round (below): only past what the drive reaches does the edge asked past `edgeFrom` = 0.25 of full take it away by `edgeGone` = 0.6 — a skier carving at speed is not also pushing. It comes in STRIDES (`SkierState.stride`, counted): a half-sine push over the first `duty` = 45 % of each on a `floor` = 30 % of the mean, the mean of a stride the whole force — and the pose reads the same stride (`gaitOf`), so a leg is seen pushing on the step the snow is pushed. **THE SKATE IS A PUSH AND A LONG GLIDE**, at a skater's own unhurried cadence (`poles.cadence`: 0.85 strides a second at 3.5 m/s rising to 1.3 by 6.3, as measured — a skater lengthens his glide first and quickens only toward his top speed); the poles bite for a quarter of it or so inside the leg's long push (`poleDuty`: no longer than the snow takes to pass under one sweep of the pole, never less than `dutyLeast` = 20 % — measured 0.34 s slow to 0.22 s fast). **THE SKATE GOES WHERE THE SKI POINTS**: a skater rides one arm of the V at a time, so each push sends him off DIAGONALLY along the other ski and the next push diagonally the other way — a zig-zag, never straight up the middle of the V (`glideYaw`, `SkierState.glide`). The V is `skateAngle` (`vee`: 0.36 rad, 21°, each ski off his line at a walk, closing to 0.2 rad, 11°, by `skateTo` — measured skating holds some 14° at 3 m/s), by how much of him is skating (`skateWork`, which needs the drive past `skateDrive` = 0.4: a skier half working under a held brake pushes with his skis together); the glide is carried across from one arm to the other over the push and held through the glide. The skis grip and the drive pushes along that line, the yaw hand holds his nose to it, and the push's SIDEWAYS share turns his way with it each step, its speed kept — the leg pays for the turn; asked of the snow's sideways grip instead, the slip it took scrubbed the flat-ground pace from 24 km/h to 13. His heading stays on the mean line: he faces down the run while his path swings some 0.7–1.2 m across it (the skier lab's `--sheet=path` draws it). **A PLANTED POLE STAYS PLANTED**: double-poling, a push lasts no longer than the snow takes to pass under one sweep of the pole (`poleSweep`: `sweep` = 1.35 m double-poling, `sweepSkate` = 1 m skating — a little short of the pose's own 1.55 and 1.19, so a push ends before the last of the arm's reach), so the faster he goes the quicker he works (`strideRate`, from `cadencePole` = 1.05), up to `cadenceMax` = 2 a second; past that his arms cannot keep up (`poleKeepUp`: whole while a push at that cadence sweeps `keepUp.from` = 85 % of the snow going by, gone by `keepUp.to` = 60 %), and the poles' share of the push goes with it (`driveReach`) — he gives the poles up and tucks, about 22–27 km/h on the flat. The pose holds each basket where it bit for the whole push (`holdPush` in `skier-stroke.ts`). It is read off the SPEED, not the way (a skier sliding sideways at 80 km/h has no business pushing on his skis), and it stops for the skid thrown, a jump being loaded, the air and a skier thrown. A skier stood still with his hands off stays standing; one rolling past `DRIVE_FROM` = 0.4 m/s, or asked to go with the tuck, sets off — and stands up to it: a man working is not folded into a tuck, so the crouch is the tuck less the drive, read from the step he means to work (the drive's target), so a skier sent off at GO with the tuck held does not fold down and stand back up before his first push. On the flat from a shuffle the Chamois is at 10 km/h in a second, 17 at 3 s and 24 at 12 s; nothing but gravity pushes him past that, and nothing here brakes him.

**HE TURNS AT A CRAWL BY STEPPING** (`stepWork`, `stepYaw`, `SkierState.step`; `poles.turn`). A ski's sidecut arc at a walk is fifteen metres and more, and a skier who waited on it barely came round (with the steer held from 11 km/h, 33° in 2 s, and the drive taken away for the bend, so he came out of it slower than he went in). A skier STEPS his skis round instead: the SKATE TURN — skating more to one side, the V turned into the turn, its inside arm stepped out and glided on, the outside ski pushed off and brought in — and at a walk the STEP TURN, short quick steps. The steer key over how much of him can step (`stepWork`: all of the drive the speed leaves him, eased in over its upper half as the skate is) is the step, taken up and let go at `turn.rate` = 3 a second. Each stride turns his heading `turn.step` = 0.7 rad (40°: a skate turn's push-and-step is 30–45° at a crawl) on top of what the edge carves, the way turned with it by the leg (the push pays for the turn, never the snow's sideways grip, and the scrub charges only what the edge carves); the V leads his heading into the turn by `turn.lead` = 0.25 rad (`glideYaw`); at a walk his strides come `turn.quick` = 35 % quicker (`stepQuick`, faded out by `skateFrom`); the skis stand on `turn.edge` = 60 % less of the speed's lock while he can step (some 22° rather than 55°: an edge to push off and to lean the turn's load on); and on his poles at a double pole's pace the turn is SKATED (`skateShare`'s `step`: a double pole's skis stay together and turn nothing). He is pushing all the way round: from a crawl to 27 km/h, with the steer and the tuck held, he comes round 90° in 1.8–2.4 s on a radius of 2.5–8.5 m, within a km/h or two of the speed the same run straight reaches (`make skate-turns`). Past the drive's reach (`fade`) nothing changes: a skier at speed carves. **STOOD STILL WITH ONLY A STEER HELD** (`stoodStill`: under 0.4 m/s, not asked to go with the tuck, not working, braking or loading a jump) he is going nowhere, so he steps his skis round ON THE SPOT (`stepRound`, `poles.pivot`, `SkierState.pivot`): the inside ski's tip lifted and set down `pivot.angle` = 0.44 rad (25°) further round, its tail where it was (the star turn), then the outside ski lifted and brought alongside (`pivotSteps`), `pivot.steps` = 1.2 pairs a second — measured stepping round on skis takes 15–30° a step at one to two steps a second — his body standing between the two skis, his skis flat (a steer stood still is no edge). A pair begun is finished the way it was begun, whatever the thumb does meanwhile, and let go he stands with his skis together: 30° in the first second, 90° by 3 s, 101° once the last pair is down, 0.00 m moved, with poles or without (`make ride SCENARIO=pivot`, the `pivot` rows of `make skate-turns`). The figure steps each ski off its tail (`pivotGait`), the stepping ski lifted and his weight over the standing one, and a skier with empty hands holds them out for his balance rather than hung at his sides (`SkierSpring.stepping`).

**WITHOUT POLES** (`SkierState.poles` off — the player's hard mode, the DRESS card's POLES row on NONE or `createGame`'s `poles: false`; every rival has his, `TUNING.poles.bare`): the arms have nothing to push on. There is no double pole — he SKATES at every speed the drive reaches (`skateShare` is 1, so `poleKeepUp` never takes the push away before the fade) — on `bare.legs` = 60 % of the power and of a plant's press: measured skating, the arms put a third and more of the propulsive power into the snow, and all of a double pole's. Up a rise a push with no basket to brace it slips back (`climbShare`): whole to the pair's pitch `bare.climb.from` = 0.035 rad (2°), down to `least` = 15 % of it by `to` = 0.17 rad (10°). His BALANCE is less: he keeps `bare.balance` = 50 % of his resilience, so every one of the crash's thresholds sits halfway to the club skier's (`crashLimit`; below), and a bog's rocking packs the hole back at `bare.rock` = 45 % of the rate (`trench.ts`). On the flat with the tuck held he reaches 20 km/h where the poled skier reaches 25; up a 4 % rise he makes 58 m in 20 s against 93, and up an 8 % one he crawls at a walk until the reset calls him stuck, where on his poles he skates up it at 13–15 km/h. Downhill nothing changes but the balance: the sim's eight seeds without poles finish 1–4 % slower (`npm run sim -- --no-poles`). The figure is posed for empty hands rather than for grips nobody drew (`skier-bare.ts`): his arms hang at his sides stood still, his hands rest on his knees in the start gate, riding he carries them where any skier does, forward and a little wider than his shoulders, the elbows soft (the outside hand forward and the inside one down toward the snow in a turn); skating he swings them FROM THE SHOULDERS as the free skate is coached — opposite the legs, the arm on the pushing leg's side forward and across with its forearm over the gliding ski and the other back past the hip, bent and close to the body, one smooth swing a push held still through the glide; striding (which is how he sets off on the flat as well as up a rise) like a man walking — no plant anywhere, and no plant heard (`make poleless`).

## The sidestep (`sidestep.ts`, `defs/sidestep.ts`)

**A FACE TOO STEEP TO WALK IS CLIMBED SIDEWAYS.** A skier walks a gentle rise, opens his skis into a herringbone as it steepens (a V of some 17° a ski), and once its edges slip — some 10° and up, and on anything steep or icy — turns side-on and SIDESTEPS: his skis square across the fall line, the uphill ski lifted and set down a step up the hill on its uphill edge, stamped hard so the snow is pressed into a ledge under it, his weight moved onto it, then the downhill ski brought up beside it. The poles brace each step, the uphill one planted short up the hill, the downhill one long below. A sidestep gains 20–40 cm a step at about a step of each ski a second. How steep: a black run's steepest pitch is about 35°, 40° is about the steepest any marked run has and steep skiing begins at 45°. A set edge cuts a ledge on all of it, each step shorter and harder; past some 50° snow sluffs off rather than lies and there is nothing to stamp a platform in — a skier there takes his skis off and kicks steps in his boots, which this game does not do.

**STOOD STILL ACROSS A SLOPE** of `from` = 0.17 rad (10°) or more, his skis within `across` = 0.7 rad (40°) of square to the fall line (`hillSide`), he stands on his PLATFORMS (`SkierState.sidestep`, the side the hill rises on): his skis set into the hill on their uphill edges by `set` = 80 % of the slope, never past `edge` = 0.7 rad (`sidestepEdge`), and the ledges hold him however steep it is (the standstill hold), whether or not he is stepping — a skier who stops half way up a 45° face stands there. Stood still is over the snow: his legs settling along its normal do not take him off them, and in powder the steer on his platforms asks for a step rather than rolling him over (`incline.ts`). **THE STEER TOWARD THE HILL STEPS HIM UP IT** (`stepSide`), a pair at a time like the step turn on the spot (`sideSteps`: the uphill ski over the first part of the pair, the downhill one over the second, each eased at both ends, the body between them carried half of each): `step` = 0.32 m up the snow a pair at `steps` = 0.9 pairs a second. A pair begun is finished. The steer away from the hill steps him round to face down it (`stepRound`); the tuck sends him off across it. Each ski's contacts are laid where it stands (`laySkis`) and the one in the air touches nothing, so the trail map takes a LADDER of ledges, never a smear. **THE STEEPER THE SHORTER** (`sidestepReach`): from `steep` = 0.61 rad (35°) each step is shorter and slower, to `least` = 45 % of it by `most` = 0.79 rad (45°), the slowing shared `pace` = 35 % to the rate and the rest to the length; past `none` = 0.87 rad (50°) there is no platform and he does not try. In loose snow each stamp packs down before it carries him: `powder` = 70 % of the step.

Measured (`make sidestep`, the Chamois): on the groomer 32 cm a pair at 0.90 pairs a second to 35° — 4.5 m of vertical a minute at 15°, 7.4 at 25°, 10.0 at 35° — then 26 cm at 0.81 (8.1 m/min) at 40°, 20 cm at 0.69 (5.8 m/min) at 45° and 11 cm at 0.49 (2.4 m/min) at 48°; nothing at 50°. In powder 22–23 cm a pair to 35° (7.0 m/min there), 14 cm at 45°, and at 48° the loose snow does not hold him. Nothing slides back on any slope he climbs. The figure (`skier-gait.ts`'s `sidestepGait`) lifts each ski in its own window and slaps it down onto its ledge (the stamp a short press of the legs), shifts his weight onto the ski that has just landed, and stands his body over the hill, `HILL.share` = 85 % upright in the world rather than square to the face (`skier-spring.ts`'s `hillLean`); each pole is planted beside its ski and moves up the hill with it (`skier-sidestep.ts`).

## The jump (`skier.ts`, `TUNING.jump`)

HELD, the jump LOADS: while it is held on the snow the skier sinks onto his legs, the deeper the longer (a crouch of `jump.crouch` = 0.75 at the full load), and the time held is counted to `jump.full` = 2 s — held longer is no higher. LET GO, he SPRINGS: `popMin` = 2.2 m/s for a tap, rising with the load to `popMax` = 6 m/s, straight off the SNOW'S OWN NORMAL (an ollie, not a hop in the world's up), and the `jump` event reports the pop and the load. At the race's flight gravity a tap hops a boot's height (0.16 m) and a full load stands him a metre off the snow for three quarters of a second; a load carried into the air keeps, and one let go there is spent on nothing. The flight that follows is any other (`flight.ts`), counted as air, scored on a tricks run, and landed under the landing's load (below).

## The edge, the carve and the skid (`skier.ts`, `limits.ts`)

**The edge.** `steer` asks for an edge angle — the skis tipped off flat — up to the pair's `edgeMax` at a standstill, fading to two thirds by `steer.fadeSpeed` = 25 m/s (`edgeLockAt`, which the bot reads too: a skier at a schuss stands his skis flatter than one snaking a nursery slope), reached at `steer.edgeRate` = 3.4 rad/s scaled by the pair's edge factor. A dulled edge pulls the line toward its own side (`damage.ts`).

**The carve is the sidecut's geometry.** A ski on `edge` rad bends its sidecut into the snow and runs the arc that makes: a curvature of `tan(edge) / sidecut` (`carveCurvature` — 45° of edge carves about the sidecut radius, a flat ski runs straight; that is sin(edge) of the geometric carve, a ski tipped part way being bent part way), tightened by a lean forward, `1 − steer.tipLoad · lean` (0.25), and never past the SIDECUT'S GEOMETRY (`carveMost`): a ski bent until its whole edge lies on the snow carves R_sidecut · cos(edge) — the 13 m slalom pair at 70° ~4.4 m, the 30 m giant slalom pair at 68° ~11 m, the 50 m downhill pair at 55° ~29 m — and nothing a skier does with his weight bends it tighter, so a line turned tighter than that is a skid. The ski's line is turned IN THE SNOW'S PLANE (the skis' heading laid into it, then the toe about the snow's normal), so a body inclined 60° has the same toe-in under it as one stood up. The tip's line points inside the boot's by that curvature times how far ahead it stands and the tail's outside by the same — the TOE-IN a bent ski has — and each station's edge bites on its own line, so the tip's pulls the tip round, the tail's pushes the tail out, and the six stations slide across nothing only once the body is turning at exactly the curvature the edge asks for. That is the whole of why an edged ski turns without being turned, and it is the bare physics: with the arcade's hand off (`Assist.yaw` = 0) a half edge still carves. On the groomer only — in powder the edge is buried, the ski bends into its float, and THE CARVE IS THE BODY'S: a ski rolled over turns toward the low side, `skier.carve` = 1.2 of its load per radian of roll off the snow's plane, with way on by `carveSpeed` = 4 m/s.

**THE ARCADE'S HAND ON THE YAW** (`steer.yawHold` = 1600 N·m per rad/s) models nothing and says so. The yaw rate is held toward the one the edge's own geometry asks for (the way times the curvature) but no faster than `pathShare` = 0.9 of the corner grip can turn the way itself, and the body is held to the way it is going, `slipHold` = 1100 N·m per rad of slide once faster than `slipFrom` = 3 m/s — the two together no more than `yawHoldMax` = 1000 N·m, on the reference pair and scaled by this one's yaw inertia. It exists because the bare physics spins a skier who is over-edged at speed: a full edge at 60 km/h asks 1.6 g of the edges, the edges hold 1.2, and past what they hold the tanh grip saturates at every station and the tips' pull and the tails' push cancel — the skid has no restoring yaw and the body swaps ends. OPTIONS ▸ ASSIST is this dial.

**THE SCRUB** (`steer.scrub` = 0.12 — this is a FAST game, and a clean carve gathers speed down a red pitch rather than bleeding it): an edge holding a skier round a carve is cutting a groove, and cutting it is paid out of the way — a drag against the travel, at the CoG so it turns nothing, of that share of the bend's own acceleration (the rate the edge asks for, as the yaw hand reads it, times the way), on the packed share of the snow. A bend held on an edge scrubs speed, which is why the carves are measured on the 20° pitch and at the speed he came in with (below).

**THE CORNER** a pair can hold (`cornerGrip`, read by the yaw hold and the bot) is its sideways grip or its TIPPING POINT, whichever comes first — and on the groomer it is the grip: `tipLimit` is the tangent of the inclination a skier holds into a turn, the whole's `rollPacked` plus the angulation his hips hung `hipReach` inside over his CoG height half a tuck low, times the arcade's `hangOff` = 1.4 — 2.7 g on the Chamois, where the edges hold 1.2 (0.95 × 1.3). The bot corners at `cornerShare` = 0.7 of it.

**SPEED TAKES THE TURN AWAY.** The yaw an edge asks for is the way times the curvature, but the bend it can hold is a lateral acceleration, v²κ, and a skier can hold only so much of one — so once the grip is what limits the bend the most yaw he can have goes as the grip over the speed, and the radius as the speed squared. At full edge on the flat groomer the Chamois turns fastest at about 60 km/h (some 41°/s at 0.95 g); by 80 km/h the grip holds the bend and it is 32°/s on a 31 m radius, by 100 km/h 24°/s on 52 m, by 120 km/h 19°/s on 81 m. The thumb asks for less edge as well (`edgeLockAt`, above).

**THE CHATTER** (`TUNING.chatter`, `chatterOf`, `chatterHold`): a groomed surface is never smooth — its corduroy, the ruts of the skiers before, its grains — and a ski running over it at v is driven at v / λ: a few hertz at a walk, the ski's own bending (some 10–20 Hz) and torsional (some 50–70 Hz) modes at a racer's pace. Measured on running skis, the vibration climbs steeply with speed and is worst on hard snow under a loaded edge; past the ski's damping the edge skips off the snow for an instant at a time, and a skipping edge holds less than a biting one — which is why a downhiller's skis are long, stiff and heavily damped, and a short soft ski that carves a nursery slope clean is all over the place flat out. So the chatter is the speed's share — nothing under `from` = 14 m/s (50 km/h), the whole by `full` = 36 m/s (130 km/h), a smoothstep between — times the pair's own (`footprint.ts`'s `chatter`, `(L₀ / L)^chatterLength · (1 − chatterFlex · (flex − flex₀))` with 1.5 and 0.6: the Eagle lets through about half the Chamois's, the Hare a fifth more), and at the whole of it the EDGE's sideways hold is `loss` = 0.2 less. On packed snow only — powder cushions the ski, and the base's hold keeps all of it. `cornerGrip` takes the speed, so the yaw hand, the inclination and the bot (which plans a bend's speed at the grip left at that speed) all read the same loss. What the skier FEELS of it is `SkierState.chatter`, 0..1: the speed's share on the packed share of the load, `flat` = 0.35 of it running straight and the rest with the bend's load on the edge (whole by `loadedG` = 0.8 g) — a readout for the view, which shakes each drawn ski on the snow passing under it (`ski-chatter.ts`: a hop up off the snow, the tip flapping about the binding, the edge rocking, the two skis out of step) while the knees take it and the body above rides quiet. With it, the Chamois holds a full edge thrown on at 80 km/h at 0.78 g, the Eagle — which lets through about half as much — at 0.87, the Hare 0.64 (`ride --card`'s `g @80`).

**THE BACK KEY MEANS TWO THINGS, by the order it meets the edge in** (`input-model.ts`'s `backMode`, held for as long as the key is): pressed FIRST it is the BRAKE, and an edge put on after it swings the skis across the way into a hockey stop; pressed with an edge ALREADY ON it is the edge CUT HARDER. On the phone the edge thumb dragged down on the snow is the same key (in the air it is the lean back it always was).

**Cut harder** (`TUNING.carve`, `SkierInput.carve`, `SkierState.carve`): the skis stood `carve.edge` = 45 % further over than the speed's own lock allows (never past the spec's `edgeMax`), the curvature tightened by `carve.tighten` = 35 % (as far as the sidecut's geometry, never past it), the pressed edge holding `carve.grip` = 30 % more sideways (and the yaw hand and the inclination allowed as much more of the corner), and `carve.scrubSpared` = 60 % of the groove's scrub spared — a tighter line that costs little: a full edge at 80 km/h down the 20° pitch carves a 37 m radius at 1.10 g cut hard, where the same edge not pressed runs 60 m at 0.75 g, at much the same speed.

**THE TECHNIQUE** (`defs/technique.ts`, `RunRules.technique`, `CreateGameOptions.technique`): how the skier WORKS the ski, apart from the ski's own numbers — a table of multipliers and floors on the shared model, chosen by the mode (`MODE_RULES`; a lab may ski one course with every row through `createGame({ technique })`). `FREE` is every mode but the race disciplines and is the identity on every number, so those runs are bit-for-bit what the model is without the table. `SLALOM_TECHNIQUE` is the racer's, off the measured numbers of top-level slalom (a turn every 0.8–1.0 s, the edge ~5° at the transition and 65–70° at and after the gate, a skid of 12–15° at most early in the turn): the edge rolled `edgeRate` = 1.6 times the shared rate (~6.5 rad/s on the slalom pair), stood at up to `edgeMax` = 1.22 rad (70°, his discipline's in place of the pair's own), held there to a slalom's pace (`fade` = 4: the lock eases four times as slowly with speed — `edgeLockAt`), the whole PLATFORM stood on (`platform` = 1), and the body let in as far as `incline` = 1.1 rad (63°) over `inclineMost`'s 50° — a slalom apex's 2.5–3 g balanced is 68–72°, the last of it his angulation. HOW THE EDGE CHANGES is a row of its own (`Technique.cross`, a `Crossing`): the free skier's is the CROSS-OVER — his whole body rolled over the skis into the next turn as the old one lets him go, the edge no further over than he is laid plus his angulation (below) — and the slalom racer's is the CROSS-UNDER: his legs tip the skis from edge to edge UNDER a body that has not crossed yet, so his edge may stand at up to `under` = 1.0 rad (57°) whatever his inclination (`incline.ts`'s `edgeReach`) and the new edge bites while his body is still coming over (past it the edge needs the body laid in, as on any turn), and both legs are drawn up together as the skis swing under him (`retract` = 0.11 m: the skis' points hung that much higher on him while he is near upright and rolling through it at 3 rad/s, `retractionOf`, so a body swung upright on legs still bent by the old turn neither digs his tips in nor is sprung off the snow). On the 20° strip a turn every 0.9 s on the slalom pair peaks at 65° of edge on the mean where crossing over it peaked at 59° (the turns 0.86 s apart where they were 0.80 — a short check between full ones — and the worst slide 28° where it was 32°); on the all-mountain pair every turn is made (0.86 s, 69°) where crossing over missed every other one (0.95 s, 64°); and the bot skis a slalom 7 % faster. The giant slalom's row crosses under on the flat (`under` = 0.8 rad, half the retraction) and over on a steep complete turn — past `steep` = 0.33 rad of pitch it gives the cross-under up over the next 0.1 rad (`crossUnderOf`); the super-G's and the downhill's cross over. The giant slalom and super-G rows (`GIANT_SLALOM_TECHNIQUE`, `SUPER_G_TECHNIQUE`) are the same terms off those disciplines' measured turns — slower to roll, stood at their research's most edge (68°, 60°), less of the shelf, let in to 59° and 52° (the apex's balance off each one's measured speed and radius) — each dealt by its mode (R36, R33), and the bot skis both on the downhill's line-follower read closer (`GIANT_SLALOM_STEER`, `SUPER_G_STEER`). The DOWNHILL's (`DOWNHILL_TECHNIQUE`, R32) is dealt by its mode: the edge rolled at 0.48 of the shared rate, stood at up to 60° and HELD at speed — a ~52 m turn carved at 26 m/s asks some 45° of a 50 m sidecut there, so its lock fades two and a half times as slowly (`fade` = 2.5, where the research's first guess of the shared fade left the bot unable to hold a fast bend's line) — half the shelf, the body let in to 53°, crossing over. The bot skis a slalom and a downhill with the racer's technique CUT HARD (`limits.ts`'s `cutEdgeAt` and `cutGrip`, which the par reads too). The SKI CROSS's row (`SKI_CROSS_TECHNIQUE`, R35) is a giant slalom racer's carve on a shorter ski through banked berms of 22–32 m at 50–70 km/h: the edge rolled at the shared rate, stood at up to 64°, held to 70 km/h (`fade` = 2), most of the shelf (0.8), the body let in to 55°, crossing under on the flat (`under` = 0.7 rad, a twentieth of a metre's retraction) and over on the steep past 19° — the berm's bank takes a share of each turn the edge would otherwise hold.

**The skid is the brake.** `brake` pivots the skis across the way (`SkierState.skid`, reached at `steer.skidRate` = 4 rad/s) by up to `skidAngle` = 1.05 rad at a crawl — a snowplough's 60° — narrowing to `skidFast` = 0.5 rad by `skidFadeSpeed` = 20 m/s (a hockey stop at speed is a narrower angle held harder), toward the side the edge is on: both skis one way is a HOCKEY STOP, and with the edge straight a SNOWPLOUGH, which pivots nothing and only scrubs. What it costs: the pivoted edge's sideways grip now works against the way, and the skis pay `skidDrag` = 0.35 of their load per unit of brake for the snow they shove aside — a snowplough alone is 0.35 g, which slows a skier clearly on a 20° groomer and brings him down to a walk on it. A pivoted ski is SCRAPING on its edge rather than cutting a groove with it, and sliding friction is less than the bite: a fully skidding ski keeps `grip.skidHold` = 0.5 of the edge's sideways hold (blended by `skid`), so a hockey stop pulls about 0.7 g where a real one pulls 0.5–0.8 — from 60 km/h, a couple of seconds and under twenty metres. The bot plans on `brakeDecel` less what the fall of the piste gives back (`v² = v_bend² + 2·a·d − 2·g·drop` — a skid down a steep pitch sheds only what it takes beyond the slope's pull), and while its skis are pivoted or still sliding across their line it stands them up no further than `slideEdge` of the edge that catches (`bot.ts`): a skier finishing a hockey stop flattens his skis as the slide dies. A slide the skier asked for is never a caught edge (`crash.catchSkid`, below). A finished skier checks himself to a stop in the arena on half the brake.

**THE SKIER** is nine tenths of the moving mass, and moving him is how skis are skied. His hips move `hipReach` inside the turn (THE ANGULATION, on the groomer only as far as the bend asks for it — full once it pulls `skier.hangG` = 0.35 g, at any pace in powder) and `aftReach` = 0.25 m fore and aft on the lean, both lagging `skier.lag` = 0.16 s; his weight off-centre is a moment on the whole. THE INCLINATION into a carve is THE TURN'S BALANCE (`SkierState.balance`): the lean at which the snow's reaction under his skis — its grip across them over its push along its normal, tan θ = a_lat / g on the level — passes through his centre of mass, the way a bicycle leans, read off the turn he is actually making and eased over `balanceLag` = 0.06 s; a skier on a traverse leans into the hill by the slope's own share, and one in a hockey stop back against the stop. As he commits to a turn he leans `commit` = 0.5 of the way on toward the balance of the bend his edge asks for (atan(v²κ / g), never more than the grip holds) — the cross-over that has his body on its way into the new turn as the skis bite (an arcade dial: at 0 he rolls a turn late, at 1 a skidding skier lies down for a turn he is not getting) — but never off a turn whose load is still on him: against the balance of the turn he is still making he crosses over only as its load falls (`crossLoad` = 1.3 g, an arcade dial: lower and a 0.9 s rhythm misses every other cross-over, unbounded and a downhiller at 90 km/h goes over his edges) — a body thrown into the next turn against a 2 g load is a skier over his edges. THE EDGE IS THE INCLINATION AND THE ANGULATION: on the groomer a ski stands on its edge no further than the body is laid over to that side plus `angulateMost` = 0.6 rad (34°: the ankles, knees and hips — a racer's hip angulation is 10–20° at a slalom apex and his knees add to it), so he rolls his skis over as he lays himself into the turn, and an edge never bites at a racer's 70° under a body still stood up — unless his technique crosses under (`Technique.cross.under`, above), his legs standing the skis on the new edge before his body has come over. A technique that rolls from edge to edge faster (`Technique.edgeRate`, the slalom's 1.6) holds his roll as much stiffer and further (its stiffness and its most times the rate, its damping times the root), none slacker than the free skier. Laid over no further than `inclineMost` = 0.87 rad (50°, a strong free skier's carve at 1.2 g; a technique floors it higher), and in powder `rollPowder` = 0.5 rad at full edge, which is the whole turn there; held by `rollStiff` = 5000 N·m per rad with `rollDamp` = 380 N·m·s and never more than `rollMax` = 900 × `hangOff` N·m, whole to a little past the most he inclines and giving out over the 0.4 rad after it — a skier well over is going over and nothing holds him. The legs are cast down the snow's normal from under his hips, so the stations stand straight under him however far he inclines; a real skier laid θ over stands on feet out to the side of him, where the snow's push along its normal cancels the moment of its grip across up to tan θ of the load. So that much of each station's grip runs through his centre of mass (rolling him nothing) and only a grip past what he is inclined for tips him out of the turn, against his hold. The grip against the stations' own sweep as he rolls (the legs hang from his centre) is no load of the turn's: it damps his roll and is neither balanced nor carried. A body laid over past the turn's balance would fall into it; that his hold alone stands him back up is the simplification (at an edge change the balance swings across in a tenth of a second). `rollPacked` = 0.7 rad is the roll the tipping point (`tipLimit`) is reckoned on. THE FORE-AFT BALANCE: his boots and his legs hold the body square to the slope under him — toward `leanPitch` = 0.2 rad tips-up at full lean back — with `pitchStiff` = 4000 N·m per rad, `pitchDamp` = 300 N·m·s and no more than `pitchMax` = 1500 N·m, which is how he sits back into a skid rather than going over the tips (a skid at 0.9 g at the feet is 700 N·m over them). Both hands are stated on the reference pair and scaled by this one's inertia; in the air neither exists.

**THE TUCK.** The body folds toward the crouch the tuck asks for at `skier.crouchRate` = 3 /s, the drag area easing from `cdAUpright` to `cdATuck` (`air.ts`'s `dragAreaOf`) against the air at `airDensity` = 1.05 kg/m³ (−8 °C, two thousand metres up), and the CoG dropping `crouchDrop`. The air is the air WHERE HE IS, wind and all — see *The wind* below; on the bench, which is skied in still air, it is his own speed alone. On the 20° pitch the Chamois settles at 117 km/h tucked and 86 standing tall.

## The air (`flight.ts`)

A skier is airborne when no station and no body point touches the snow; the `air` event fires once he has been up `air.counts` = 0.15 s (anything shorter is a skip over a roller). In the air he has one lever on the pitch, the LEAN (`leanTorque` = 53 N·m at full lean, back = tips up — 2.0 rad/s² on the reference pair's 26 kg·m² of pitch, enough to trim a landing and not to turn a flip), the edge swings the skis across for a little yaw (`steerTorque` = 6 N·m), nothing rolls him and the body levels the roll itself (`rollLevel` = 320 N·m per rad, `rollDamp` = 55 N·m·s, given up past `rollGiveUp` = 1.1 rad — a body thrown onto its side comes down on its side), and the air damps every rate: `damping` = 6 N·m·s about the pitch and the roll, `yawDamping` = 1.8 about the up axis (a skier is a tall body and a short one across: his yaw is a third of his pitch to turn, and the same drag would stop a 360 in half a second). THE ARCADE'S HAND ON THE PITCH: with the lean left alone the body eases the tips toward half the flight path, never more than `pitchAim` = 0.35 rad, at `pitchLevel` = 46 N·m per rad and no more than `pitchLevelMax` = 28 N·m, and over the last `landLook` = 0.8 s before the snow comes back toward THE SLOPE HE WILL LAND ON (`landingAhead`, the ballistic arc traced over the real snow), so both skis meet it along their length. All of it in the body frame; tips up is a negative torque about x. Three things a rigid body would do a skier does not: A SKIER SPINNING A 360 holds himself upright through it, so past `spinLevel` = 2 rad/s of yaw the roll's and the pitch's levelling are let go (a spinning body's tilt reads as pitch, then as roll, then as pitch again once a turn, and a spring on each axis chasing that reading at the spin's own rate — the roll's natural rate IS the spin's — wound every 360 into a tumble; let go, the few degrees he threw the spin with stay). The hips' weight off-centre (`hipRight`, `hipAft`) is a moment only against the snow's push back at the skis, none in the air. And Euler's coupling (ω × Iω) is stepped only on the snow: in the air he holds his shape with his limbs, and a body as thin as his — a yaw inertia a third of the pitch's and the roll's — fed every wobble back through it. HOW HE RIDES A FALL is the figure's alone (`pwa/src/game/skier-flight.ts`, read by nothing in the physics): on a fall clock that runs only while his skis are high over the snow (from 0.6 m, at full rate past 2.2 m), compact and quiet off a kicker, SPOTTING a drop (from 0.1 s: hands forward and wide, chest over the knees, head down on the landing), a measured forward WINDMILL on a cliff (from 0.32 s, a circle a second traced by the fists IN FRONT of his shoulders — never swung round behind him, where no hand could keep its pole pointing back: a gripped shaft stands 95–150° off the forearm, the wrist's tilt only 20° one way and 35° the other — the poles held back, out and down throughout; never on a body turning a flip or a spin, nor on one COMMITTED to a lean: a lean held past 0.1–0.3 of its travel sets the arms forward and still, because leaning is a line chosen through the air and circling arms are a skier fighting one he did not choose), wound home ahead of him from 0.6 s before the snow, and REACHING for it over the last 0.5 s — the arms forward and down, the legs extended by more the bigger the fall — timed off his flight's own ballistics over the map.

**THE AIR'S PULL is the arcade's too** (`air.gravity` = 1.5, `RunRules.airGravity`; 1 on a tricks run): a skier genuinely flying is pulled down at 1.5 g, because at the real 9.81 a kicker taken at race speed hangs him 6 m up for 2 s and a hang that long on a chase camera reads as slow motion rather than weight. Only a skier who was flying at the end of the last step feels it, so where he leaves the snow is a fact about the shape and the speed and the ground holds him over a roller at exactly g. The bot's ballistics read it through `flightGravity`.

**A landing** is the legs' job and the knees take it — the model of a good one is nothing at all. Past `air.harshSpeed` = 8 m/s of speed INTO the slope on the reference pair (`harshSpeedOf`: the stroke energy the legs store, scaled by the pair's own give — the soft park ski takes the hardest landing whole, the stiff downhill ski the least) the legs have folded to their stop and the skier pays `harshLoss` = 2 % of his way along the slope per m/s over, up to `harshMax` = 12 % (`landingLoss`) — which is why landing on a kicker's downslope is fast and landing flat after overshooting it is not.

**THE LANDING'S LOAD, and how true it must be** (`landingLoad`, `landingTolerance`, `landingOff`, `TUNING.landing`). The snow-park engineers measure a landing by its EQUIVALENT FALL HEIGHT — the speed into the slope as a drop from rest, EFH = v⊥² / 2g; jump designers hold a landing's sweet spot to 0.1–0.9 m and call past 1.5 m a hard one, measured tables run to 3–5 m off the sweet spot, and hard snow lands about a third harder than soft at the same spot. The legs and the snow stop that fall over a STROKE: the hips sinking from the stance into a squat, `landing.stroke` = 0.45 m (less `tuckStroke` = 25 % of it at a full tuck, which has already folded them), plus `give` = 60 % of whatever LOOSE snow lies under the skis (the dial's cover and the new fall, on the unpacked share — 40 cm of powder adds a quarter of a metre, a metre of it most of the fall). So the load is 1 + EFH / stroke g, carried on the `land` event with how far OFF TRUE the skis came down: the worst of the tips into the slope (`tipsDown` = 0.5 rad at the clean limit), the tails first (`tailsDown` = 0.8), the roll across it (`rolled` = 0.6) and the slide sideways to the way (`sideways` = 0.9; landing backwards counts as a quarter turn more — except where the skier may ride switch, when the skis are judged tails first), as a share of those. What a load FORGIVES shrinks with it: under `clean` = 6 g the whole of it — more still for a hop, to twice it at a standing load — then down to `tight` = 20 % of it at `buckle` = 14 g, and past the buckle nothing. That measure grades a landing for the score and the HUD (`tricks.ts`'s `landingGrade`, the news's load line); it no longer decides a fall. A PROFESSIONAL RIDES AWAY whatever he comes down ON HIS SKIS for — crooked, rolled onto his edges, slapped down tips-first — and goes down only where the body cannot stand up out of it (the wipeout, below): the legs folding past `crash.legsFold` = 18 g, or the hips, the shoulders or the helmet hitting the snow. Deep soft snow is a long, kind stroke and the groomer and the ice are not. Dropped 1.5 m at 70 km/h onto the groomer, skis true, is 8.9 g and ridden away; rolled 20° it is still ridden; onto his side it is a fall the moment his hip and shoulder land; dropped 8 m onto the groomer is 36 g and the legs fold however true; the same 8 m into a metre of powder is 13.7 g — ridden.

## The body meeting the snow (`chassis.ts`)

Eleven unsprung points — the skis' tips and tails (standing across the snow as the legs' stations do, raised by the tuck), the knees, the hips, the shoulders and the helmet (`hullOf`) — meet the snow when the legs have run out: a tail dragged over a crest, a tip buried in a face met too fast, a skier down on his hip. They are resolved at the VELOCITY level, not with springs (a penalty spring stiff enough to hold ninety kilos off a slope met at eighty kilometres an hour stores the impact and hands it back): each point in the snow has its velocity into the slope taken away by an impulse through the body's effective mass there (the angular term in, so a tip meeting the snow pitches him as well as stopping him), with `hull.restitution` = 0.1 of it given back and a push-out of `pushRate` = 10 /s of its depth, no more than `pushOut` = 1.5 m/s; Coulomb friction takes the slide along the slope up to `hull.friction` = 0.4 of the impulse. The snow under a body point is the powder's FLOOR (`powderFloor`): deep powder does not hold a body up, it is pushed aside by it, down to the base the skier's own skis pressed.

## Trees, posts, stakes and the edge (`collision.ts`, `posts.ts`, `edge-stakes.ts`)

A tree is its trunk, a vertical cylinder of `radius` from its foot to its top (the crown is drawn and is nothing to the physics). The skier is three plan circles of `trees.bodyRadius` = 0.35 m down the skis' length; a circle inside a trunk is pushed out along the line between their centres, the closing speed comes back at `restitution` = 0.15, the speed along the trunk is scrubbed by up to `scrub` = 35 %, and a `hit` is reported at `hitSpeed` = 1.5 m/s closing or more, at most once per `cooldown` = 0.6 s. The map's edge turns a skier back over its last `bounds.soft` = 20 m with a push of `bounds.push` = 12 m/s², and holds him `bounds.margin` = 6 m inside it.

**THE POSTS** (`posts.ts`) are met exactly as a trunk is: every lift tower's steel column and the post under each bullwheel (`LiftLook.column`, half its width across the flats at the foot — gondola 0.74 m, chair 0.54 m, drag 0.24 m — up to the rope), and every floodlight mast down the runs (`piste-masts.ts`, a pole of `PISTE_MAST.pole.foot` = 0.12 m to its light point). They are one list with the trunks (`solidsOf`: the trunks in their own order, then the posts), which the skier, his body thrown, his skis let go, the snowmobile and both bots' dodges all read; a `hit` on one says `post`, and the wipeout and the save are a trunk's. THE CABINS' WALLS (`cabins.ts`'s `cabinWalls`) join the list after the posts: every wall of a log building beside a run is a row of posts of `CABIN_LAYOUT.wall.radius` = 0.45 m, no more than 0.6 m apart and set in so their outer faces are the wall's, from the snow at its lowest under the plinth to the eaves — met as a post is.

**THE EDGE STAKES** (`edge-stakes.ts`, `TUNING.stakes`) are light: a marker pole every `every` = 25 m down both edges of every run, `out` = 1.5 m past the edge, `height` = 2.2 m. The skier's three circles sweep one over and are never stopped: met under `snap` = 8 m/s (29 km/h) closing a stake BENDS over on its foot and whips back up, a damped spring of `stiff` = 120 /s² and `damp` = 6 /s; met at `snap` or harder it SNAPS and lies on the snow (`broken` = 1.52 rad over) for the rest of the run, ridden across. Each is met once on its way over, for `share` = 3 % of the speed driven into it (at most `loss` = 0.3 m/s), `snapLoss` = 0.35 m/s more to break it, and a twist of `twist` = 2.2 rad/s of yaw per m/s taken at the tips — a stake caught on a tip snatches it back and turns him. A `stake` event marks each (its closing speed, whether it broke); a stake met at `crash.stakeSpeed` = 15 m/s (54 km/h; club 9) throws him (below). Every run keeps its own stakes (`GameState.stakes`, made the first time one is touched), and the app draws them as knocked.

## The wipeout (`crash.ts`)

**A PROFESSIONAL'S RESILIENCE.** The skier goes down only when the body physically cannot stay up — and everything short of that he rides out, the near fall kept as a SAVE (below) for the figure to play. Seven ways off, each a threshold on something the step has already measured; the numbers here are a professional's (`SkierState.resilience` = 1, the player's), and THE KNOB blends every one of them down toward a club skier's (`crash.club`, resilience 0 — `crashLimit`) for a skier between: each rival is dealt his own off `RACE.resilienceBand` = 0.35–1 at the start line, on a stream of its own so nothing else the start deals moves, and `createGame({ resilience })` sets the player's (1 when left out — difficulty, later). A skier WITHOUT POLES keeps only `poles.bare.balance` = 50 % of his: a professional without poles falls where a skier halfway to the club skier does. The bot, over the sim's seeds on every pair as a professional, lands every jump the map gives it and meets no trunk (on seed 7's black it drifts to the edge braking in the powder and clips a floodlight mast at 22 km/h, short of a fall); the one place it goes down is a pre-existing trap on seed 10 where it is battered to a crawl and comes down on its side.

- **a trunk met hard** — a `hit` at `crash.treeSpeed` = 7 m/s (25 km/h) closing or more with the trunk SQUARE IN FRONT OF THE SKIS (the tips' circle): the trunk stops the skis and the skier goes on at `keep` = 85 % of the way he had, until the snow or the trunk itself stops him. A trunk BESIDE HIM, met by the body's or the tails' circle — taken on the shoulder — throws him only at `treeShoulder` = 10 m/s (36 km/h): the blow knocks him aside and round, and his skis are still under him (club: 5 and 6 m/s);
- **a landing over the tips** — a `land` ending a flight of `noseAir` = 0.3 s or more, at `noseImpact` = 5 m/s into the slope or more with the tips too far down against it: `noseDig` = 0.56 rad (32°) in loose snow, where they dig, and `noseAngle` = 0.8 rad (46°) on the groomer, where they slap down flat until they come in steep enough to spear it — the limit blended by the packed share under him (club: 25° and 32°). He goes over them, and the skis are given `skiKick` = 0.35 rad/s of tip-over per m/s of the impact (at most 5). A skip off a touchdown is that landing handing itself back and is never judged;
- **the body down** — the hips, the shoulders or the helmet driven into the snow at `bodySlam` = 1.5 m/s or more (`SkierState.bodyHit`, off the hull's own contacts — the speed a twelve-centimetre drop hits at; club 1 m/s), still going `rollSpeed` = 6 m/s: he has come down on his side, his back or his head. In a landing (or a moment after one) it is the `landing` cause, out of one the `roll`. A hip brushed in a deep carve slides along the snow and goes into it at a fraction of that, and is a save;
- **a fall at speed** — the body lying over on the SNOW (its up under `reset.overUp` = 0.25 of the ground's normal, not the sky's — a skier on a steep face stands well off vertical) for `rollHold` = 0.35 s (`SkierState.rolledFor`, held — not started over — through a bounce off the snow; club 0.12 s) at `rollSpeed` = 6 m/s or more: an edge lost and no hand put down in time. Turning over in the AIR is not a fall — the landing decides — and a slow sit-down he rides out, the reset's own clock standing him up;
- **the legs folded** — a `land` ending a flight of `landing.air` = 0.3 s or more at a load past `legsFold` = 18 g (club 10 g): more than legs can hold, however true he came down. How true the skis came down is NOT judged here: a landing on the skis is the snow's and the legs' to sort out, and one that is not true enough ends on the body;
- **a caught edge**, the high-side — a ski stood over `crash.catchEdge` = 0.85 rad (49°) while the snow slides across it faster than `catchSlip` = 9 m/s (32 km/h; `SkierState.sideSlip`, the fastest any touching station slid across its own line; club 0.7 rad and 6 m/s): the edge bites all at once and the body is thrown over it — unless the skis are in a SKID past `crash.catchSkid` = 0.5 of the brake's pivot, which is the slide he asked for: a hockey stop is exactly a ski stood on its edge and slid across the way. The bot stands its edge down well short of it, from `skier.slipEdge` = 0.75 rad and `slipSpeed` = 6 m/s.
- **an edge stake run into** — a `stake` at `crash.stakeSpeed` = 15 m/s (54 km/h) closing or more (club 9 m/s): light as it is, caught on a tip, a boot or a pole at that pace it snatches the limb back faster than the body follows, and he goes over it and round onto the side it caught (`crash.over.stake`). Short of it, it is a save (`STAYED UP!`, the figure's wobble).

**THE SAVE** (`noteSave`, `SkierState.save`): on a step that did not throw him, how near anything came — a hard or crooked landing (from `landing.clean` g toward `legsFold`, from `saveTip` = 0.3 rad tips-down toward the dig, from `saveRoll` = 0.5 rad rolled toward his side), a trunk (its closing speed over the limit it was met at), the body touching the snow, an edge biting (from `saveEdge` = 0.9 of the catching edge and `saveSlip` = 0.7 of the catching slide — an ordinary skidded turn is short of both) — as a share of the skier's own threshold, kept from `saveFrom` = 0.25 of one, with the side it came from and, for a landing, which way it threw him. A newer save takes the place of one older than `saveHold` = 0.5 s or nearer than what is left of it; a `save` event marks each. The figure plays it (`skier-save.ts`: sunk and lurched with the arms flung out for a landing, the shoulder knocked back and the body rocked away for a trunk, a hand down to the snow for the body, a dying wobble for an edge, followed on a spring of `JOLT_FOLLOW` = 24 rad/s so it comes and goes as a motion), the HUD says the near ones (`SAVED THE LANDING`, `SHRUGGED OFF THE TREE`, `BACK UP!`, `HELD THE EDGE`, from 0.6) and the hands feel a short shudder. Nothing in the physics reads it.

THE SKIER THROWN is a body of his own (`SkierState.thrown`): a RAGDOLL (`ragdoll.ts`) — thirteen points, the hips, the shoulders, the head, the knees, the feet, the elbows and the hands, each with a mass (80 kg in all, the trunk the heaviest) and a radius, moved by Verlet under gravity and held at the skier's own measures (`crash.body`, the same bones `skier-pose.ts`'s `BODY` draws) — Jakobsen's position-based body, with the pose drive of an active ragdoll held to the laws a real body keeps. The trunk and the head are rigid; the limbs swing, but only through what a body allows: a knee bends only forward and an elbow only back (each hinge left alone on a limb all but straight — `STRAIGHT` = 97 % of its reach — where a few degrees past is a locked joint, and a hinge put right only fights the bones' lengths), neither folds tighter than `kneeFold` = 35° or `elbowFold` = 30° between its bones, a thigh goes no further than `hipBack` = 0.35 of its length behind the hip nor `hipUp` = 0.3 above it, a hand never folds nearer its shoulder than `foldArm` = 0.2 m, and no limb comes nearer the line of the spine than `torso` = 0.16 m, so an arm flung across him meets his chest. A JOINT'S LIMIT IS AN INELASTIC STOP: the correction carries no velocity and the relative velocity across the joint that runs into it is taken away, shared by mass; each solver pass holds the joints first and the bones' lengths last, and THE JOINTS GIVE THE BODY NO ENERGY — whatever a pass added to his motion about his centre of mass is taken back off it (`calm`), because a joint put right and then held at its length is a kick of speed out of nowhere, and one threw a body lying still back up off the snow. THE MUSCLES (`crash.tone`): a man falling is not a sack. Every limb is driven toward a pose in the trunk's frame by a damped spring — in the air the PROTECTIVE REFLEX, `brace` = 10 rad/s at `braceDamp` = 0.8 of critical, the arms thrown out ahead and wide at `reach` = 0.85 of their length and the legs spread and a little bent (never tucked: a body that draws itself in spins up like a diver, and a pair of hands chasing the world's down would windmill and spin the trunk the other way), giving way at `relax` = 12 /s once his trunk (or a trunk's weight of him) is down to the slack pose a body lies in, legs out and arms out at his sides, at `lie` = 2 rad/s; a limb planted in the snow yields and is not driven. The muscles are inside the body, so the push and the twist their drive would put on the whole of him are taken straight back off every point — they move the limbs against the trunk and never turn the man; only the snow and the trunks can. He is laid where he stood and sent off at `keep` = 85 % of his way, turning at his speed over `tumbleRadius` = 2 m (no faster than `maxSpin` = 6 rad/s) with `carry` = half his own turning on top — and WHICH WAY HE GOES OVER is the cause's (`crash.over`, against the way he was going): a trunk pitches him on over his stopped skis and away from it, climbing 1.8 m/s; the tips digging pitch him over them, the same; a caught edge flings him on over it the way the snow slid, 1.2 m/s; a fall at speed takes him down onto the side he was lying toward, barely off the snow; and a landing his legs could not hold sits him down back and to the side — each sideways fall turning at least `topple` = 3 rad/s. EVERY POINT MEETS THE SNOW ON ITS OWN: settled `sink` = 0.15 m into powder at the ordinary dial, its way into the snow taken away and its way out no more than `crash.pushOut` = 0.3 m/s (a point put back on the surface from under it is a position corrected, not a launch — a body thrown down in deep powder starts a hand under the surface), Coulomb friction along it on its own weight and its arrival — `frictionPacked` = 0.45 on the groomer, `frictionPowder` = 0.8 in fresh snow — and the PLOUGH, `plough` = 1.2 of its way a second per unit of powder depth. But what touches the snow is a patch of back, side or shoulder, never a point to pivot over: `patch` = 70 % of every point's loss is taken off the body as a whole, so a hand or a head digging in slows him rather than being something to cartwheel over, and once a trunk's weight of him is in the snow it scrubs off `spinDrag` = 3 of his turning a second, more the deeper the powder he is in. So he goes over once or so with his arms out, is dragged flat by the snow, and slides to rest lying on it: about 30 m on the groomer from a 60 km/h landing over the tips, 18 m in powder and a little less in a metre of it. A trunk stops every point too. None of it draws from the stream: a crash is a pure function of the moment it started, and a run replays wipeout for wipeout.

THE SKIS LET GO — the yard sale (`lone-skis.ts`, `crash.skis`). The pair under him is no longer stepped on its legs: each binding lets go on its own and each ski is a body of its own (`Thrown.skis`, a `LoneSki`), a stick the ski's length whose tip and tail meet the snow and the trunks on their own, turned about its length by its `up`. The one under him — on the side he goes down on — is held in its binding `hold` = 0.22 s longer (less in a slow fall), carried wherever his foot goes, and let go with his foot's way no more than `fling` = 1.5 m/s off his body's own; the other releases at once. Each leaves with a wrench of its own off a hash of the moment, never the stream — turned about the snow's up (up to `yaw` = 5 rad/s), popped up (the free one, up to `pop` = 2.2 m/s), spun about its length (up to `spin` = 9 rad/s) and the two thrown apart (`spread` = 1.2 m/s), all at full strength from `kickSpeed` = 15 m/s and less below it — so the two never fly alike. NOTHING BOUNCES: the way into the snow is taken whole and the way out of it kept no faster than `pushOut` = 0.2 m/s. A SKI IS BUILT TO RISE: it never sinks into the snow, no end of it and not its middle over a crest (the app lays it on the loose cover the powder is drawn with), and in powder it only ploughs, `plough` = 2 of its way across a second per unit of depth and a quarter of that along. On the snow it flops flat onto its base at `settle` = 14 /s — unless it came down more than 120° over onto its back (`right` = 0.5), since the binding stands proud of the topsheet and a ski propped on it rolls back over — and slides: `base` = 0.05 along its wax, `top` = 0.3 on its topsheet, `edge` = 0.3 across, and the `brake` = 0.22 added both ways at the tail end of a ski on its base, where the brake's arms drop in once the boot is out. So a ski swings tip first down the fall line and slides away on any pitch over about 9°, and stops in powder or on the flat. While he is off them the skis take no gate and run none of the automatic reset's clocks, the skier's own state follows the middle of the two (`followSkis`: the six stations each ski's tip, middle and tail where it lies, so the trail is where they slid); the race clock runs. A rival's reset comes once he has been off `lieMin` = 1.8 s and has lain still (every point under `restSpeed` = 0.6 m/s, on the snow) for `lieStill` = 1 s of it, or at `lieMax` = 6.5 s whatever he is doing — reported `auto`. THE PLAYER is held down longer: his fall is his to watch — the death cam over him, the HUD's body showing what it did — so no press of his own stands him up for `getUp` = 3 s (a reset pressed inside them is let go, not kept), and the engine stands him up at `lieFor` = 6 s, however he lies (`stepRun`'s `player`). The still beat is the one the app's death cam rises into the sky over him on, at full speed — a wipeout is never slowed. Past `getUp` the HUD lights the reset and asks for the get-up: the tuck key pressed (W), or a tap anywhere on the glass, is the reset while he is down (`input.ts`), and stands him up at once.

## Bogged in deep powder (`trench.ts`)

A skier BOGGED — poling (the tuck over half) in powder under `trench.creep` = 1 m/s — for `after` = 1 s starts to sink: the hole under the skis (`SkierState.trench`) deepens at `dig` = 0.1 m/s in virgin powder, to `max` = 0.25 m on top of the sink. `skier.ts` adds it to the stations' sink target, so the skis hang in their own hole while the knees, which read the powder's floor, take the load they lost — to the knees, which is what bogged is — and takes up to `grip` = 75 % of the poles' push with it. No push-off out of a powder start gets near it.

ROCKING OUT: every metre the skier's weight moves (`hipAft` fore and aft on the lean, `hipRight` side to side on the edge) packs `rock` = 0.04 m of it back, and skiing out clears `clear` = 0.4 m per metre of way past `creep`. Past `stuckAt` = 0.05 m he is bogged and `stuck` fires once; from its first centimetre the automatic reset waits `holdFor` = 8 s instead of `reset.stuckFor`.

## Damage (`damage.ts`)

Only on a run that asked for it (`createGame({ damage: true })`, OPTIONS ▸ ASSIST ▸ DAMAGE, off by default; a rival never takes any). Three figures, each 0 sound … 1 wrecked: the two skis' edges and the legs. A trunk past `damage.treeFrom` = 4 m/s closing dulls the edge on its side by `treeRate` = 0.06 per m/s over; a landing past the pair's harsh speed hurts the legs by `landRate` = 0.05 per m/s over; a wipeout adds `wipeout` = 0.2 to what its cause reaches. Nothing mends it but a new run.

What it does: a dulled edge pulls the line `skiToe` = 0.06 rad toward its own side at fully dulled and bites `skiGrip` = 40 % less; hurt legs lose up to `springSoft` = 40 % of their rate, `dampSoft` = 50 % of their damping and `harshSoft` = 45 % of the speed into the slope they take whole. Every share is `1 − k·d`, so a sound skier is the same arithmetic to the last bit.

## The body (`body.ts`, `defs/anatomy.ts`)

WHAT HIS BODY TAKES, part by part, on every run and every skier — a readout: nothing in the physics reads it and it draws nothing from the stream. The skier is kept as twenty parts (the head, the neck, the chest, the back, the abdomen, the pelvis, and either side's shoulder, arm, hand, thigh, knee, shin and foot — `BODY_PARTS`), each with the worst injury it carries.

**A BLOW IS A STOP.** A part met at `v` m/s is brought to rest over its own give (`injury.give`: 6 mm at the head and the neck, 3–6 cm at the trunk, 3–8 cm at a limb, whose joints fold under it) and the give of what it met — the snow's (`injury.snow`: `ice` = 1.2 cm, the groomer's `packed` = 2.2 cm, loose snow `soft` = 5 cm and `deep` = 12 cm more per unit of the snow dial, the new fall counted in — `snowGive`) or a solid's by what it is made of (`injury.solid`: a trunk's bark and sway `trunk` = 1 cm, a lift tower's or a mast's bare `steel` = 3 mm, a tower's foam `padded` = 15 cm crushed, a cabin's `log` wall 6 mm — `Upright.stuff`, carried on the `hit` event; and on the head the helmet's liner crushing, `helmet` = 1.2 cm, which it does against a solid and not against snow). Its peak is half a sine's over the mean v²/2s (`peak` = π/2): THAT is the blow, in g (`blowOf`). The numbers are fitted to the measured head drops on snow (a 6 kg headform at 6.1 m/s: 51 g on soft snow, 106 g on hard, 170 g on very hard — `tests/body_test.ts` holds the fit), so the same fall is a few g into a deep day's powder, tens on the groomer and a hundred on ice — and that is the whole of how the snow's depth and hardness reach an injury.

WHERE THE BLOWS COME FROM, each off something the step already measured:

- **on the skis** — a trunk or any solid (`hit`'s closing speed): SQUARE IN FRONT OF THE SKIS — ahead of him and within its radius and `front.lane` = 25 cm of his line — the tips meet it and the skis stop, levering the shins over the boots (`bend`, m/s) and twisting the knees (`noseTwist` = 0.45 of it), the body's own blow coming once it is thrown on into the trunk; BESIDE HIM it takes the shoulder, the arm, the ribs, the flank, the hip, the thigh and a glancing share of the head (`injury.side`). The hips, the shoulders or the helmet into the snow (`SkierState.bodyHit`). A LANDING: its load (`land`'s `g`, the equivalent fall height over the legs' stroke) runs up the legs into the spine (`load`; `folded` = 1.3 times it when the legs folded past `legsFold`), loads each leg's BONES from the bottom up — the heel, the tibia's end at the ankle (the pilon), its top in the knee (the plateau), the femur and the hip socket, each at its axial tolerance (6–15 kN in cadaver drop tests of the leg; a leg carries half his weight's stop, about 0.39 kN a g for the medium build, and a heavier build's g is a harder load) — onto the knees as a DRAWER load — `square` = 0.6 of it on a landing square on the skis, more by `backSeat` = 1.2 at the tails as far down as `landing.tailsDown` (the cruciate's phantom foot and the boot's drawer) and `crooked` = 0.6 at a roll as far as `landing.rolled` — and whips the neck (`neck` = 1.6 g per g over standing); a landing that came down on the body leaves the legs `onBody` = 0.4 of it. And the falls that are a mechanism: a caught edge twists the knee of the outside ski at the slide's speed (`twist`, m/s; the other at half), a fall at speed at `rollTwist` = 0.5 of the speed, a fall over the tips levers both shins at the speed;
- **thrown** — every point of the ragdoll meeting the snow or a trunk this step (`Thrown.impacts`, `.struck`: the way it brought into what it was put back out of, a FRESH contact only — a point lying on the snow is not struck again): the head (and `share.neck` = 0.25 of it on the neck, `neckAxial` = 0.35 more as far as the blow runs down his spine — the dive's cervical fracture), a shoulder or a hip (and `share.trunk` = 0.6 of it on the chest or the abdomen when he comes down on his front, the back and the kidneys on his back, and `flank` = 0.9 of it on the ribs and the spleen or the liver on his side — read off the ragdoll's own chest against the snow's normal or the solid's; a hip met with the trunk upright over it loads the spine as `seat` = 0.18 of its blow, the seated fall's compression fracture), a knee (and the thigh and the shin), a foot (and the shin, and — met sole first along the leg's own line, feet first into a wall — `legAxial` = 0.7 of it up the leg as a landing's load: the heel, the pilon, the plateau, the femur and the hip socket), an elbow (and the point of the shoulder), a hand (and the arm and the shoulder — the fall on an outstretched hand);
- **another skier** — the player's shoulder into a rival's (`feelBumps`).

**A DOSE IS A CHANCE.** Every injury on a part's ladder (`INJURIES`, 95 of them, each ranked on the Abbreviated Injury Scale — 1 minor, 2 moderate, 3 serious, 4 severe, 5 critical; 6, unsurvivable, is on no ladder) has the dose of its mechanism at which it is an even chance (`at`), and the chance at a dose `d` is 1 / (1 + (at/d)^`steep`), `steep` = 8 — the log-logistic of the injury-risk curves: three quarters of the dose a chance in eleven, a quarter more six in seven, under `floor` = half of it none. It is drawn off a hash of the step, the part and the map (never `state.rng`), so a run replays injury for injury and no digest moves. The ladder is read worst first; a part takes at most one new injury a step and never one ranked under what it carries; a part already hurt is hurt again more easily (`weaken` = 6 % off every threshold per rank); and of every part's injury drawn in one step the worst `perBlow` = 5 are taken — but every part's on a landing past the legs' buckle (`landing.buckle`), which loads the whole leg at once. The ladders lean on the measured tolerances: a concussion an even chance at 95 g (the measured mean 96–103 g), a skull fracture at 230 g in a helmet, broken ribs at 52 g (the chest's 60 g over 3 ms), a compressed vertebra at a landing of 15.5 g and a broken back at 21 (the spine's 20–25 g), a cracked heel at 17 g, a broken one at 21, the pilon at 25, the plateau at 27, the hip socket at 30 and the femur at 33, the cruciate torn by a drawer of 15 g or a twist at 11.5 m/s, the medial ligament by a twist at 9.5, the skier's thumb at 21 g on the hand; the kidneys only from behind, the spleen from the left, the liver from the right; and a fracture that needs its side (`on`) only from it — the collarbone and the shoulder's separation from the point of the shoulder, the shoulder blade (a high-energy break, 60 and 78 g) from behind, the kneecap from in front.

THE WHOLE BODY is summed the way a trauma ward sums it: the INJURY SEVERITY SCORE (`severityOf`), the squares of the worst rank in each of the three worst-hurt regions (the head and the neck; the chest and the back; the abdomen; the limbs and the pelvis) — 0 unhurt, 16 and up major trauma, 75 the top. A reset MENDS him (`mendBody`): stood back up on the piste, he is sound again, and only the run's hardest blows are kept.

THE BONES (`BONES`, thirty: the skull, the jaw, the cervical, thoracic and lumbar spine, the breastbone, the ribs, the pelvis, and a side's clavicle, shoulder blade, humerus, radius, ulna, hand, femur, kneecap, tibia, fibula and foot): every fracture on a ladder names the bones it reaches (`bones`, on a paired part its own side's) and whether it is a HAIRLINE — a crack that holds, a rank under the break and at four fifths of its dose — or a BREAK, and every bone can take both. A BREAK BREAKS BY ITS ENERGY: every injury keeps the energy that did it over the energy of its even chance (`Injury.energy`, `energyOver` — a blow's or a load's g goes as its energy at the stop it is given, a twist's or a bend's m/s as its square), and every harder blow on the part afterwards raises a fracture's to its own, so a bone already broken and struck again breaks worse. A break under `comminute.wedge` = 1.6 of it is SIMPLE, under `comminute.shatter` = 2.3 a WEDGE — a butterfly fragment knocked out — and past that MULTIFRAGMENTARY, the bone shattered: the AO/OTA's A, B and C in the order of the energy that made them, the 2.3 the pendulum study's (long bones struck at 21 J broke simple and transverse, at 47 J comminuted in 95 of 100; at 60 J the chip at the point of impact twice the size). A trunk at 50 km/h shatters the arm it is met with; at 110 km/h the pelvis, the thoracic spine and a shin go too (`make damage ARGS=--sheet=blows`). `fracturesOf` is each bone's worst (`FRACTURE_GRADE`: sound, hairline, simple, wedge, shatter) and `fractureEnergyOf` the energy behind it; `saidOf` says which injuries are put in words — anything but a fracture, which the HUD shows on the bone (the spinal cord, more than its vertebra, is said).

THE G METER (`SkierState.body.impact`): the hardest blow a step dealt to the head or the trunk — a limb's whip is a small mass flung faster than he is and is not billed — from `shown` = 3 g (a landing from `landingShown` = 2 g, after a real flight), held `hold` = 1.6 s, a harder one in that time taking its place; `peak` is the run's hardest. Only a blow somebody FELL on is shown (`Impact.fall`): he was thrown, or the rival or amateur he shouldered went down — the same step or within `fallWindow` = 0.5 s after it, the shove that put him over a moment later, which restarts its hold. A fall's blow takes the meter from one ridden out, never the other way; `fallPeak` is the hardest of them, the HUD's HARDEST. A landing ridden out or a shoulder both stood up from bills nothing on the HUD.

MEASURED (`make ride`, the professional, chamois): the stock kicker overshot at 75 km/h lands at 8 g and hurts nothing; a 3 m drop onto flat packed snow at 70 km/h is 15 g — whiplash; the drop from twelve metres equivalent is 36 g on the groomer (a spinal cord injury, both heels, both pilons, both plateaus, a femur and the hip socket broken) and 14 g in powder (nothing); a caught edge at 45 km/h tears both knees' ligaments; a trunk on the shoulder at 28 km/h is 124 g — broken ribs, a broken collarbone and a broken arm; a trunk dead ahead at 45 km/h, thrown on into it at 63, is 161 g and an injury severity score of 22. The bot over twelve seeds on two pairs is hurt only by a 14 g landing on seed 7 (a strained back and a sprained knee) and on seed 10's battering spot.

THE INJURY LAB (`make injuries`, `tests/injury_lab_test.ts`) holds all of it to what a body does: some forty moments staged on a flat bench (`tests/support/injury-stage.ts`) — thrown and posed into powder, the groomer or ice, a trunk, a tower's steel or its pad, a log wall; dropped off a cliff onto the flat, landed in the back seat or over the tips, an edge caught, skied into a solid — each drawn 24 times and its rates held to what it should do and must not (`tests/support/injury-scenarios.ts`). Among them: head first from 6 m onto ice is a brain injury and a broken, shattered neck every time; flat on the back from 5 m onto ice the spine and the kidneys; on the left side from 4 m onto ice the ribs and the spleen, on the right the liver; sat down from 4 m onto ice the pelvis and a crushed lumbar vertebra; a cliff of 8 m to the flat cracks a heel or strains the back, one of 20 m breaks the legs and the spine and tears the organs, one of 30 m shatters the lumbar spine and both heels; feet first into a log wall at 40 km/h breaks the legs along their length, and a trunk between the legs the pelvis; the back into a tower's bare steel at 40 km/h tears a kidney, its pad round the same tower spares the chest; and a fall into a deep day's powder breaks nothing.

## Tricks (`strokes.ts`, `tricks.ts`)

THE STROKES are read on a run whose rules let the skier trick the mountain (`RunRules.stunts` — the FREE RIDE and the TRICKS mode); on every race the lean and the edge are the air control above and nothing more. The score is counted on the tricks mode (`RunRules.tricks`). The numbers are `TUNING.tricks` (`defs/tricks.ts`). **THE STROKES** are read off the SHAPE of an input, and each one buys an ANGLE: the edge carried past `spinGate` = 0.6 toward either side is THE TWIRL, HALF A TURN (`spinStep` = π) about the skier's own up axis — once, and he comes down with the skis backward and rides away switch (below); twice, a 360 — and the lean carried past `flipGate` = 0.8 toward either end is THE PUMP, A WHOLE TURN (`flipStep` = 2π) nose over tail, back for a backflip, forward for a front flip. A tap the other way takes one back off what is still owed; a flight owes no more than `spinMost` = `flipMost` = 4π (a 720, a double). What is owed is THROWN, not snapped: the rate gathered at `spinAccel` = 120 / `flipAccel` = 100 rad/s² (on the reference pair, scaled by the root of each pair's inertia against it) to a cruise of `spinRate` = 12 / `flipRate` = 10 rad/s, and taken off again so the body stops dead on the angle — the skis straight backward after a 180, level after a loop — the axis the throw's while anything is owed (the air's hand, the lean's torque and its damping steered over), and a key held on past the stroke it bought the throw's too, not a lean. THE THROW IS PACED TO THE SNOW: the cruise is raised to turn what is owed `finish` = 0.08 s before the flight comes down (`landingAhead`), up to `rateMost` = 18 rad/s, and a stroke that could not be turned in the air left at that rate is not taken — a lean thrown for the landing stays a lean, a hop over a roller a hop. A 180 takes about 0.36 s, a loop 0.5–0.7; off the jump key's own pop on the flat (the free ride flies under the real g, `airGravity` = 1, as the tricks mode does) a full load holds him up 1.2 s and both come round. **THE POSES**: the trick button held in the air takes the body off the controls into a DAFFY (the edge over), a SPREAD (the lean back) or a GRAB (the lean forward, or nothing) (`poseOf`). **THE SCORE** pays the air by the second and by the metre on one curve, the Nth revolution on an axis N × 300 points and N steps of multiplier, half a turn over the whole ones landed (the 180) 150 and a step, a pose 150 and a step, and banks the combo on a landing the legs took whole — `landingGrade` reads the touchdown against the pair's harsh speed; clean at 0.6 of it, perfect at 0.3 — at its base on a sketchy one, and loses it to a wipeout, a reset or a landing taken in a pose. The score is kept on every run and draws nothing.

## Riding switch (`switch.ts`)

On a run that lets him (`RunRules.stunts`: the free ride and tricks — never a race) a skier can go down the hill BACKWARD, tails first: come down off a 180, or turn round on the spot and let the hill have him. `SkierState.switched` is decided on the snow, past `switch.from` = 1 m/s of way backward (and cleared past it forward), and held through a flight. While he is, THE STEER IS READ THE WAY HE IS GOING — tails first an edge to his own right bends his way to the left (the carve asks `way · κ` of the yaw, and the way is negative), so the thumb's right is his left edge and the line still bends the way the player pressed; the yaw hold keeps the TAILS on his line (`heldSlip`); he does not push (a stride along the skis would be back up the hill); the landing is judged tails first (`landingOff`'s `switchOk`, `noseDown`'s leading end) and the chase lens and the minimap both turn to the way he is going. AS DRAWN (`pwa/src/game/skier-switch.ts`) he looks back over a shoulder, as a skier riding switch is taught to: the shoulder picked as he turns round and kept (never flicked side to side), the trunk and hips turned toward it so the head comes round past square with the chin over the shoulder, a little lower on his legs, the hands out wide and the poles trailing toward his tips; the skier lab's `switch` and `switch-turns` moves photograph it. Everything else — the stations, the grip, the sidecut's toe-in, the inclination — already reads the way as signed.

THE TAIL DIGS IN LOOSE SNOW (`tailDug`, the wipeout `nose`, the news's TAILS DUG IN). On the groomer every pair runs backward. Off it, the leading end has to ride over the loose snow (`snow.cover` of the run's depth, by the share of it under him not packed), and a ski's tail rides over `tailRise` of it (`TAIL_RISE`: 0 on the race skis' square tails, 0.3 on the Chamois, 0.5 on the Marmot, 1 on the Hare's twin tips); past `switch.tailDig` = 6 cm of what is left, at `digSpeed` = 5 m/s or more backward — riding, not drifting: one slid back at a walk after a stall ploughs to a stop — the tail dives and throws him. So the race skis dig in 6 cm of powder, the Chamois in 9, the Marmot in 12, and only the park ski rides switch through a powder field.
## The gates (`course.ts`)

A gate is taken by crossing its line, facing down the piste, inside its width and `course.grace` = 2 m either side of it (`course.startGrace` = 10 m more at the start gate, for a field jostling off the line). On a single-piste map every gate spans the piste, and one skied past — its line crossed beside it within `course.missReach` = 25 m of its edge, or the next gate taken while it is owed — costs nothing: the HUD points back at it and lights the reset, and the run owes it until it is taken. On a resort's COURSE (R28) every gate between the start gate and the finish is a SLALOM GATE, set off the line and narrower than the piste (`Checkpoint.offset`, `.span`), and one skied past — beside it anywhere across the piste, or the next one taken first — costs `course.missPenalty` = 3 s on the run's clock (`Progress.penalty` keeps the sum, already in `time`) and the run goes on to the next: an arcade racer's penalty, never a climb back to a gate set in the middle of the snow. The `missed` event carries the penalty, and the news column and the finish plate say what it cost. The bot skis THE GATE LINE (`gateLineAt`): a cosine from each gate's offset to the next's, standing on every gate's centre, crossing the line straight halfway between two set either side and turning hardest round each gate, its curvature added to the bend's when the bot reads how fast it can go.

## The wind (`wind.ts`, `air.ts`)

R19 deals every map a mean wind at the meteorological 10 m over open ground, from a bearing, breathing in gusts (`windAt`). **The wind never blows up the mountain**: a gale straight up the fall line is a wall in a skier's face that all but stops him, and no fun to ski, so a bearing that would carry the air up the slope (against the world's +z) is mirrored across it (`downhillFrom`), the veer folded with the mean. R19's even deal round the whole compass becomes an even deal over the half that blows across or down the mountain — straight down it as likely as any other way. The fold is taken at run time, so no map a seed builds moves; the cloud's drift, the wind-carved crust and the birds all read the folded bearing (`windFromOf`). A sky picked by kind (the free ride's WEATHER row, `?weather=`) keeps the bearing the map's own wind was dealt. What a skier meets is that air where he is (`airAt`, `TUNING.wind`):

- **Down to his body, by the log law.** Over snow of aerodynamic roughness `roughness` = 1 mm (measured fresh snow lies near 0.25 mm, a skied, rutted slope a few mm) the mean wind grows as ln(z / z0), so at his body (`BODY_HEIGHT` = 1 m) it is ln(1 / 0.001) / ln(10 / 0.001) = **0.75** of the 10 m wind.
- **Exposure.** The flow is squeezed over the ridge and lies in the lee of the valley: the 10 m wind runs from `valley` = 0.7 of the dealt mean on the valley floor to `summit` = 1.3 at the top, linearly up the mountain's vertical.
- **The woods' shelter.** The crowns' share of the ground within `shelter.radius` = 30 m (about two trees' heights), off a grid of `cell` = 10 m baked once a map from its trees (a sapling or krummholz counted by its height over 6 m), takes up to `most` = 0.75 of the wind away at `full` = 0.3 cover — R14's woods grow in clumps with lanes between, and at their thickest their crowns cover some 0.3–0.4 of the ground; in an ordinary stretch of wood about a quarter, where some 40 % of the open wind is left. The trunk space of a real closed conifer stand keeps 10–30 % of the wind above it. A piste through the woods keeps most of its wind (its corridor is wide) and a lane between the trees very little.

All of it a pure function of the map, the place and the clock, drawing nothing from the stream: the physics, the sound (`wind-voice.ts`) and the HUD's wind meter meet the same air, and a replay its own.

**The drag is against that air** (`air.ts`'s `airForce`): ½ρ|a| times the drag area times `a`, where `a` is his velocity less the air's — in a wind tunnel the tunnel's air, the weather shut out. TWO AREAS: the air past him is split along his heading and across it, and each part drags on its own area — the frontal one (`dragAreaOf`) along, the SIDE-ON one (`sideAreaOf`) across: `sideUpright` = 0.7 of the frontal stood up (a body shows the wind about two thirds as much of itself side-on) to `sideTuck` = 1.2 of it in a tuck (folded, the frontal area shrinks to a ball but the profile — back, thighs and shins laid along the way — stays long). The vertical takes the frontal. That is the shape wind-tunnel work on skiers finds: the drag barely moves inside 15° of yaw while the side force climbs with it. The force acts at the CoG — a standing body's side area centres within a hand's breadth of it — so a crosswind turns nothing by itself; what it does:

- **A tailwind carries him, and a headwind holds him back.** Down the 20° pitch in a tuck, 12 m/s at 10 m (some 9 at his body) behind him takes his top speed from 117 to 145 km/h; dealt up the pitch, the same wind is folded down it and carries him the same. A headwind is met only faced up or across the mountain — on a traverse, out of a turn — and there it holds him back by as much — the terminal speed moves by about the wind's speed at his body, as the drag says it must. A downhill run modelled against a measured wind loses up to a fifth of its time to a head-on breeze.
- **A crosswind pushes him off his line and he LEANS INTO IT.** The edges hold the push across him as they hold a bend's pull, so the inclination he settles at (`skier.ts`) is the edges' share of the lateral load — the bend's pull less the wind's (`AirForce.side`): stood straight on, he leans into the wind; into a turn the wind blows into, he leans less; out of it, more. Hands off at 70 km/h with 15 m/s blowing from his left, he leans 5° into it and is blown 0.6 m aside in 4 s, his edges holding the rest.
- **Across a turn, it tightens or widens it.** The yaw the hand holds is no faster than the grip can turn his way at — and a wind blowing into the turn bends the way with the edges, one out of it takes from what they can (`reach`). On full edge from 95 km/h with 18 m/s across, he is 47° round in 1.5 s with it blowing into the turn and 36° with it blowing out of it (42° in still air).
- **Stood still**, the wind's push is part of what the standstill's hold weighs (above): a storm at his back (32 m/s) starts him at once and blows him 85 m down the flat in 8 s.

**A race is run in the wind its jury allows** (`jury.ts`'s `juryDay`, `JURY` in `defs/modes.ts`; the research is `docs/disciplines.md`'s *The jury's weather*): the strongest gust at 10 m over the course's start gate is held to 60 km/h on a slalom and 50 km/h on a downhill — a windier day is a race held for a calmer hour, the same sky with its wind eased, its bearing kept — and a downhill is not run in a storm (its fall eased to a steady one). A free ride, a time trial and a tricks run keep whatever the sky deals.

The bot (`sim/bot.ts`) skis in the same wind with no knowledge of it — it reads only what the wind does to him — and its patience for a gate is three times as long while he is still moving at a skater's pace (`stillGoing`), because a gale in the face on a traverse slows a skier to a crawl, not to nowhere. `npm run sim -- --weather storm` rides every map in a storm.

## The wind tunnels (`wind-tunnel.ts`)

Along the hub at the foot of a resort (R29) run two WIND TUNNELS (R30), `W1` one way across the valley floor and `W2` back: a horizontal lift a skier is blown down without skiing a metre. He is TAKEN IN where he stands inside one's width with his skis within `tunnel.capture` = 0.7 rad of the way it blows — so a racer crossing one on his way to the finish is never carried off — and the engine says so (`{ kind: "tunnel", phase: "in" }`, `SkierState.tunnel` while he rides). Inside, the air moves along the tunnel at its own speed (R30's `tunnel.speed`, 28 m/s) — or at his, once he is past it — so his drag is against THAT air: it pushes him on while he is slower and never holds him back. The blowers THRUST him along over it, `tunnel.thrust` = 9 m/s² up to the wind's speed and past it the same POWER (the thrust times the wind's speed over his), so there is NO TOP SPEED: the push falls as 1/v — 6 m/s² at 150 km/h, 2.5 at 360 — and only the snow's friction is left against it. A skier stood at the entrance is at the wind's speed in some 3 s and 45 m, at 150 km/h in some 5 s and 110 m, past 200 km/h by 300 m, and a lane's whole 1–1.7 km lets him go at 300–360 km/h, which the still air outside takes back off him fast. Across it he is CENTRED, `tunnel.centre` = 0.8 m/s² a metre off its line with `tunnel.damp` = 1.5 /s of his sideways way taken out. He is LET GO `tunnel.release` = 2 m past its edge, at its exit with his way kept, or thrown (`phase: "out"`). The player and every rival alike: it is part of the one step a skier takes (`run.ts`), drawing nothing from the stream.

## The buzz (`buzz.ts`, `defs/afterski.ts`)

A free ride's AFTERSKI LODGES (`afterski.ts`, `RunRules.afterski`) pour beers, and each raises the skier's BUZZ (`SkierState.buzz`, 0 sober … 1) by `AFTERSKI.beers.beer` = 0.14; outside it falls by `BUZZ.decay` = 1/600 a second, ten minutes from full to nothing. It is an arcade dial argued against the feel, never a blood-alcohol curve. At 0 nothing below is read: a sober run is the same arithmetic to the last bit, and no digest moves.

What it does to the hands (`drunkInput`, applied to the player's input before the skier reads it): the edge and the lean reach the skis through a first-order lag of `lagSober + lag × buzz` s (0.02 + 0.42); the edge goes on harder or softer than asked by up to `gain` = 55 % at full buzz, swinging between the two over `swing` = 6.7 s; two slow swells of edge (`drift` = 0.30 and 0.16 of its range over 4.3 and 1.7 s) pull him to one side and then the other, so he does not ride straight; the lean wanders by `lean` = 0.32 of its range and the tuck asked for comes and goes by `tuck` = 35 %. Every wave is a sine of the run's clock with phases off a hash of the seed — nothing draws from `state.rng`.

What it does to the falls: every threshold `crashLimit` reads (`crash.ts`) is blended toward `BUZZ.crash` by the buzz — at full, a trunk on the tips at 13 km/h, the legs folding at 7 g, an edge caught at 31° with the snow sliding 14 km/h across it, a hand put down held for a twentieth of a second.

NO RESET: from `BUZZ.fetchFrom` = 0.1 a player thrown on a free ride is not stood back up on the piste. Once the ragdoll comes to rest he GETS UP where he lies (`getUp`, the `fetch` event's `up`), and is on his feet in his boots (`SkierState.fetch`): up over `fetch.rise` = 1.6 s, then walking to each ski the fall threw off at `fetch.walk` = 1.7 m/s on the flat — slower uphill (`climb`, to `slowest` of it), quicker down (`descend`), slower in loose snow (`loose`), weaving `stagger` = 0.3 rad with the buzz, round every trunk, post and wall — bending for it within `reach` = 0.9 m for `pick` = 0.9 s (`ski`) and carrying it on his shoulder, and once he has both stamping back into the bindings over `clip` = 1.4 s (`in`), stood across the fall line to ski on. The player's own edge and tuck walk him; let go for `hands` = 1.2 s the walk goes on by itself. The skis go on sliding while he walks (`lone-skis.ts`'s `slideSkis`). The reset key stays the way out of all of it.

## The reset

A skier's own (`SkierInput.reset`) or the engine's: a body whose up axis has been under `reset.overUp` = 0.25 of vertical for `reset.overFor` = 3 s, or held poling below `stuckSpeed` = 0.6 m/s for `stuckFor` = 3 s — or, once bogged, for `trench.holdFor` = 8 s — or thrown and lain long enough (above), is stood back on the piste `course.resetAhead` = 3 m past the last gate he took (or short of the start gate before he has taken one; on a free ride, which owes no gate, at the nearest point of the nearest run he has SKIED — `Progress.skied`, every run of the resort (or the map's one piste) his skis have been on, noted four times a second and seeded with the run he was stood up on — a piste preferred over a transport lane by 40 m, `skied.ts` — and, off every run, back on the run where he LEFT the runs, at the point of it nearest the last place his skis were seen on it, `Progress.lastOnRun`, forgotten whenever a lift, the helicopter, the snowmobile or a staged moment carries him; a free ride's restart stands him at the head of the last piste he skied), at rest, facing down it (`course.ts`).
