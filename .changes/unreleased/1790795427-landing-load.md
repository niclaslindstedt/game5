title: Landings have a load, and a big one has to come down true
---

Every landing is read as the fall into the slope it amounts to, stopped over the skier's legs and whatever loose snow lies under him: deep powder is a long, soft landing, the groomer a hard one. The bigger the landing, the truer the skis must meet the slope — tips, tails, roll and sideways — and past what the legs can take no landing is true enough and the skier is COMPRESSED. A big landing ridden away shows its load in g.
