The clouds are real layers now. Each weather stands its own clouds at their real heights over the valley: fair-weather heaps at one and a half to two kilometres, the middle sheets at three to five, the high ice at seven to nine, and a grey deck with ragged scud under a snowfall. Each layer drifts with the wind at its own height, faster and turned further aloft, and the clouds grow and fade over a few minutes as you ride. They are lit by the sun through the air to their own height, so at golden hour their sunward edges and bases glow orange and pink, the high ice stays pink after the sun has set on the snow, and their bases catch the light from below just after sunset. Their shadows sweep across the mountain, and the sun dims by however much cloud is between it and you. A veil of high ice rings the sun in a 22° halo, and from above a valley fog you look down on a sea of cloud. LOW draws one layer of each; MEDIUM and up give the heaps depth, with shaded sides, bright rims and dark bases.
