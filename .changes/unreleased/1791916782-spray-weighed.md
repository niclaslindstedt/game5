The snow your skis and board throw is now worked out from the snow itself. A stop or a turn throws however many kilograms of snow its edge actually cuts and shoves, based on how hard the snow is, how heavy the skier is, how far the edge is tipped and how fast it slides. A hockey stop on the groomer throws a dense fan that comes down within a metre or two. Ice gives off a low puff of dust. Spring slush comes off in heavy clumps thrown short. Powder rises over your shoulder and hangs. A carve on hard snow only flicks a little off the tail. Each grain falls at the speed its size and weight allow. A fresh curtain of snow is solid white and thins to a veil as it spreads. The cloud a skid raises follows the fine dust the edges threw, so a stop on the groomer leaves a low mist instead of a big cloud. At a lower SPRAY setting you see the same amount of snow, drawn as fewer and coarser grains.
