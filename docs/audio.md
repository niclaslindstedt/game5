# Audio

Fall Line ships no audio files. Every sound is synthesized at runtime from a handful of authored numbers — TypeScript data under `pwa/src/game/audio/` — played on one small WebAudio instrument. That keeps the app tiny and offline and makes the sound design as diffable as the skis' own spec. The `sound-effects` skill is the working loop; this page is the shape of it.

## The register

A modern arcade racer in the snow — not a chip, and not a sample library. There is no engine: what a skier hears of his own speed is the AIR — the rush past the helmet, the roar it becomes folded into a tuck — and under it the skis on the snow: the bases hissing on the groomer, the steel tearing in a carve, the chatter of an edge on ice, the rasp of a skid, the hush of powder; and the poles' clicks at a crawl. Snow swallows transients, so a landing in powder is a brown thump that swells open under a pink puff rather than a click. The only sharp edges in the bank belong to the things that are not snow: a trunk cracking, a pole's tip on the hardpack, a gate's panel slapped past, two skiers shouldering, the finish's horn and its cowbells.

**What was met, and what it came down into** (`route.ts`'s `Contact`) is what the app knows and the event does not say: the snow the bed last read under the player's skis (a landing or a fall in deep powder is lower, longer and a touch quieter, on ice barer) and, for a `hit`, WHICH trunk — the map's nearest to the event's point (`trunkAt`), its radius and whether it is a dead snag. Left out, every contact is the groomer's and every trunk a middling living one.

`air` makes no sound on purpose: the lip is where the skier stops touching anything, and the wind coming up and the snow going quiet say so. A new sound needs a rung in `route.ts` (or a cue the bed raises) or it can never play, and **no `GameEvent` is ever added for a sound's sake** — what the app can work out from the state, it works out.

## The beds

A continuous sound is never written out of one-shots. It is a LAYER: an oscillator or a looping window onto a pool of noise, a filter, a saturation curve and a gain, built once and then steered every frame toward a target (level, pitch, cutoff, grit, pan) over a glide, on the audio thread. Nothing is booked ahead, so a late frame leaves every layer holding its last value rather than leaving a hole — and so every frame that is not hearing the race (the pause card, a hidden tab, a held screenshot) says `silence()`, or the wind would blow on behind the card.

**The wind** (`wind-voice.ts`), five layers: the RUSH (the broad pink rush past the helmet, climbing with the square of the airspeed, and the one layer that plays as loud in the air as on the snow), the ROAR (the low brown roar the tuck makes of it — the head down behind the hands, the wind breaking over the back — the layer that says TUCK before the speedo does), the WHISTLE (a thin band that comes in past forty and climbs with the speed: the air over the goggles' strap and the poles under the arms), the FLUTTER (the suit flapping, loudest stood up at speed and pulled tight in a tuck) and the BUFFET (a low brown battering that comes in past 90 km/h of air: a gale in the face on top of a schuss shaking the helmet). The tuck is why it sounds like skiing: folding down drops the drag and gathers speed (the physics), and moves the ear down out of the clean air into the roar (this bed). A wind that only followed the speed would say nothing about the skier's own body.

**What the wind bed hears is the APPARENT wind**, never the speedo: the air where he is (`airAt` — the weather's wind brought down to his body, more of it high on the mountain, sheltered by the woods) less the skier's own velocity — `airflowAt` in `engine/game/wind.ts`, read by the bed and by the HUD's wind meter; his drag is against the same air (`air.ts`). A headwind adds to his speed, a tailwind as fast as he is goes quiet, and a storm is heard standing still. Nor is it capped at a schuss: past `WIND_FULL` (40 m/s) the rush climbs on the cube of the airspeed (turbulent air's noise, the sixth power in intensity) up to `WIND_TOP` (70 m/s, some 250 km/h) and the buffet comes up under it — a 100 km/h gale in the face of a skier doing 100 km/h meters some 7 dB over a schuss in still air, at a hard landing's level. A wind ACROSS him lands on the ear rather than streaming past the helmet's nose: the rush is louder for it, the suit flaps harder, and the rush, the whistle and the flutter are panned to the side it comes from (through the input model's one screen flip, and the listener's `side` column).

**The skis on the snow** (`snow-voice.ts`), eight layers — the ski game's answer to the snowmobile game's engine voice, since a skier's own machine is his bases and his edges: the HISS (the bases on the groomer, climbing with the speed), the POWDER (a dark hush with body in it, loudest slow where the skier is sunk and shoving snow aside, thinning as he planes — darker and quieter still in new snow, which swallows sound), the EDGE (the steel tearing a carve on firm snow, with the edge and the speed together — taken down by a skid, since a skidded ski is not carving), the CHATTER (the edge skipping on firm snow at speed), the SKID (the skis pivoted across the way — the hockey stop's rasp, brighter the firmer the snow), the CRUNCH (the wind crust breaking under the skis in plates), the SLUSH (wet spring snow, a heavy swash loudest slow) and the SCRAPE (ice under the steel — a thin screech with the edge set or the skis thrown across, never on a flat base running straight). What lies under the skis is the SIX KINDS OF SNOW as a mix — `snowpack.ts`'s groomed, hard, soft, new, wet and ice, read at the skier's boots off the same snowpack the spray, the cloud and the grooves are (the map's groomer, crust and ice, the new snow the sky has laid and `GameState.fresh`, the thaw under a high sun) — so skiing off the piste is heard as the hiss giving way to the hush, a scoured crest crunches, and an afternoon in the sun goes to slush. And the PAIR colours it, off its own spec (`skiVoiceOf`: its flex, its waist, its length): a soft park ski chatters where a stiff downhill ski holds it down and rings higher, a fat powder ski throws a bigger, darker hush, a long ski's hiss drones lower.

**The poles** are one-shots the bed raises where the figure PLANTS them: the bed reads the same gait the pose draws (`gaitOf`) and plays a plant at the start of each stroke as far as the arms work the poles — none once they are given up for the tuck or cannot keep up with the snow (`Gait.keep`), none stepping round on the spot, none from a skier without poles. What is heard is the snow it goes into (`plantVoice`): the bank's `plant` — a carbide tip's tick — by the packed share under him, and `plantSoft` — the basket's low pat — by the loose rest, its cutoff lowered and its level taken to nothing as the loose snow deepens toward 0.6 m (the run's snow dial and the new fall, `depthUnder`).

**The wind tunnel** (`tunnel-voice.ts`), three layers through the wind's fader, built only on a map with a tunnel on it: the GALE (a broad pink band that opens as the skier goes in and wavers on two slow beats — the air buffeting off the arches), the BODY (the brown pressure of a moving column of air under it) and the FAN (the entrance fan's motor, a low detuned saw that fades a hundred metres down the lane). While the engine carries him (`SkierState.tunnel`) the gale is full; beside a lane (`wind-tunnel-plan.ts`'s `tunnelNear`) it is a murmur, gone thirty metres off. Taken in and let go are the engine's `tunnel` event, routed to two one-shots: `tunnel_in`, a pink sweep opening upward over a brown swell — sucked in — and `tunnel_out`, the sweep falling away.

**The helicopter** (`heli-voice.ts`, scheduled by `heli-bed.ts`), eleven layers through the effects' fader, built only on a run with one (`GameState.heli`, the free ride's) and only once its rotor turns. The ROTOR is a pulse train at its BLADE-PASSAGE rate — three blades at 390 rpm, some 19.5 a second at full speed, times the spool — played as a sawtooth whose every reset is a click, three ways: the WHOP (a resonant bandpass, so each blade is a short ringing thwop, brighter with the collective), the THUMP (a lowpass: the weight in the chest) and the SLAP (a driven highpass: the crack of each blade meeting the last one's tip vortex — blade-vortex interaction — up in a descent into its own wake, a hard turn at speed and at full collective, silent in a level hover, `slapOf`); the SWISH is the blades' broadband noise over it. Winding up from the pad the rate rises from a lone whup a second. The TURBINE is a sine a few kHz up and its second harmonic, its pitch on the square root of the spool (the gas generator runs up ahead of the rotor) and louder while it is running up — the start. The TAIL rotor buzzes at its own blade passage (some 68 a second). The WASH (pink) and its BODY (brown) are the snow blown up off the surface under the disc: none past three and a half rotor diameters of height, louder near the snow and pulling (`washOf`). **Distance is the whole mix** (`heliHeard`): the ear is the player's skier's head, three metres from the hub on the skid and deafening there; past six metres the machine falls off as the inverse of the distance to sixty metres and more gently beyond — a game's choice, so the pilot flying home after the drop is heard down the valley under the skier's own wind — and the air takes the top off it first (the whine's band losing some 0.03 dB a metre, the slap's a third of that, every cutoff lowered, the thump carrying furthest); its pitch moves with the Doppler shift of its closing speed (none while he rides it), and it is panned to the side it is on through the screen's one flip and the seat's `side`. When it crashes the engine stops the rotor dead and the bed hears the blades CHOKE instead, over a third of a second, the whop's rate and the turbine's pitch falling away under the blast; then the wreck BURNS — the FIRE (a brown roar heaving on two slow beats) and the FLAME (a pink flutter) — for the wreck's burn (`HELI.crash.wreck`), with its CRACKLE raised as cues: every fortieth of a second of the engine's clock is a slot a pop may land in (`crackleAt`, a hash of the slot — never a stream — so a replay crackles the same and a pause holds it).

**The jump plane** (`plane-voice.ts`, scheduled by `plane-bed.ts`), sixteen layers through the effects' fader, built only on a run with one (`GameState.plane`, the free ride's) and once its engine turns. A single turboprop is mostly its PROPELLER: each blade throws a pressure pulse as it passes, so it is a set of tones at the BLADE-PASSAGE rate (three blades at 2,200 rpm, 110 Hz) and its multiples, the fundamental strongest and the harmonics falling by order — played as the BOOM (a triangle under a lowpass: the fuselage drummed by the blades, strongest in the cabin), the THRUM (the low harmonics) and the BUZZ (a driven band of the higher ones, thickening as the blades bite — the thrust's share of the static). At full power the tips run near the speed of sound (the tip's speed round the disc and the plane's along its way, `tipMachOf`) and the buzz turns into a hard RASP, the loudest a turboprop is — the take-off. On the snow at idle the governor lets the propeller turn slower (`propRpmOf`), a loping thrum; in the air it holds. The TURBINE is a WHINE a few kHz up with a partial over it and the intake's HOWL; on the start it lights and runs up AHEAD of the propeller (the gas generator leads), louder while it climbs. The EXHAUST is a low roar with the power. With the jump door open the cabin is full of the SLIPSTREAM (pink, on the square of the airspeed, and a brown body under it), louder than the engine in the door. On the snow the wheel-skis RUMBLE (rougher as the legs work) and HISS with the ground speed. The STALL HORN is a reedy sawtooth in a narrow band at 1.65 kHz as the wing comes within a few degrees of its stall (`stallWarnOf`), heard in the cabin only; the FLAPS' motor whirs while they run. **Inside or out** is the listener's `cabin` column, read only with him aboard: from the cockpit and the door the boom is up, the whine and the exhaust behind the firewall, the buzz dulled, the slipstream all round and the horn heard; from the booms the machine's outside — the buzz, the rasp, the whine. **Once he has jumped** the plane flies home without him, heard by the helicopter's law (`heliHeard`) and Doppler-lowered as it goes; a crash chokes the propeller over a third of a second and the wreck BURNS with the helicopter's crackle. While he is aboard the ride bed's wind and skis stand down (`chute-bed.ts`'s `rideShares`): the slipstream through the door is the plane's.

**The skydive** (`chute-voice.ts`, scheduled by `chute-bed.ts`), twelve layers through the effects' fader at the listener's `wind` (it is the skier's own air), built only on a ride with a jump (`GameState.chute`). Out of the door the plane's noise tears away (its own bed's distance) and the exit's BUFFET shakes him for a few seconds. In FREEFALL the air is everything — the ROAR on the square of the airspeed to 90 m/s, the RUMBLE of the body as a bluff plate, a WHISTLE off the helmet, the suit's FLAP — coloured by how his body lies (`postureOf`): on the belly a broad, dark roar with the suit flogging; head down brighter and tighter, the whistle up and the suit pulled flat; in a track the suit flapping hardest. The ride bed's wind stands down in the exit, the freefall and the deployment, so the roar is never heard twice. The OPENING is the pilot chute's flap, line stretch's snap and the open's whump (one-shots) with the canopy FLOGGING between as it snivels and spreads, and the roar falling away. Under the canopy it is QUIET: the fabric's RUSTLE and the lines' HUM (a 1.6 mm line sheds vortices at a fifth of the airspeed over its diameter — 1.5 kHz at the canopy's 12 m/s, `humOf`), the cloth LUFFING deep in the brakes and stalled, the brake line ZIPPING through its guide ring as his hands work the toggles or the risers (its pitch rising with how fast — `pullOf`), the TAIL's soft strained flutter while a toggle or a riser holds the cloth out of its trim, and the ride bed's soft rush of his glide. Cut away, the canopy FLAPS AWAY up the sky, heard by its distance. Caught in a crown or on a lift, what holds him CREAKS as he swings — the wood or the rope's steel, cues off a hash of the clock (`creakAt`).

**The snowmobile** (`sled-voice.ts`, scheduled by `sled-bed.ts`), eight layers through the effects' fader, built only on a run with one (`GameState.sled`, the free ride's) and only once its engine runs: the sibling sled game's engine voice for its 850 two-stroke. The MOTOR (the block's square through a lowpass) and the HUM (a detuned triangle pair folded harder the more work it does) at the firing note — two firings a revolution, a 50 Hz chug at idle and some 280 Hz on the limiter — the OCTAVE carrying it at idle, the RASP (the expansion chamber: a driven sawtooth in a band that climbs with the revs, silent under a third of the band and singing once it is on the pipe), the BASS an octave under, and the INTAKE (pink, opening with the thumb). The BELT is the CVT and the lugs passing the drive — a driven whine at the TRACK's speed over the 3-inch pitch, not the crank's — which is why it sounds like a sled: under full throttle the note barely moves while the whine climbs with the speed. The CHURN (brown) is the paddles chewing loose snow, loud as the belt spins in powder and a hush on the groomer. Off a crest the belt unloads and the crank runs free. A gain (`LOUD`) puts a pinned machine under the rider's boots with the helicopter on its skid rather than under the skier's own wind. Distance (`sledHeard`) is the inverse law past four metres, faded out between 150 and 400 metres and duller far off; panned to its side through the screen's one flip.

**The hot air balloon** (`balloon-voice.ts`, scheduled by `balloon-bed.ts`), six layers through the effects' fader, built only on a run with one (`GameState.balloon`, the free ride's). A balloon in the air is the quietest flying thing there is — it drifts with the wind, so no air rushes past the basket — until the burner fires, and then it is a jet of burning propane a metre over the pilot's head. The ROAR is pink noise in a wide band a few hundred hertz up that opens with the flame; the RUMBLE under it is brown noise under 200 Hz, both heaving a little on two beats of the clock a little apart so the flame tears without pulsing; the JET is the gas leaving the coil, a white hiss that leads the flame in and trails it out (it follows the square root of the flame); the PILOT is a small steady hiss between burns, the one sound a balloon in the air always makes; the VENT is a soft rush out of the crown while the parachute is pulled on an envelope hotter than the air; and the FIRE, the envelope alight, is a deep heaving roar with the burning helicopter's crackle raised over it as pops. The blast valve's edges are one-shots (`balloon_valve`: the lever's clack and the WHOOMP of the gas catching; `balloon_shut`). Every level is the level in the basket, about the helicopter's hover there; out of the basket the ear falls off with the helicopter's law, the top first.

**A ski jump** (`jump-voice.ts`, scheduled by `jump-bed.ts`), nine layers built only on a run with a jumping hill (`GameState.jump`). On the bar and the tracks the skis are the jump bed's, not the snow bed's (`rideShares`): the bases held in the grooves are a narrow pink HISS climbing from 900 Hz with the speed, a brown RUMBLE of the track under it and a thin driven WHIR of the edges ringing in the ice, all through the seat's snow. Off the edge the snow goes and the wind is the jump bed's too: a pink RUSH round the body ∝ the speed squared and opened by the V, a low brown FLUTTER of the tails shaking in it on two beats, a narrow white WHISTLE off the V's edges. Over all of it the stadium's CROWD, heard from the jumper by how far he is from it (a thread from the bar, all round him in the out-run): a MURMUR, and a ROAR with its brown BODY rising as he flies past P and swelling at the touch to `jumpExcitement` of his distance — a cheer short of K, a roar at K, the stands on their feet at HS — held a second and a half and dying away over three; a fall is half of it, darker. The out-run is the ride bed's skis on the snow under it.

**The wood** (`bird-bed.ts`) is not a layer but a scheduler of one-shots: a cry is a CUE off the birds' plan, never a `GameEvent` — the engine has no idea a raven exists. Each frame it walks the flocks within earshot, asks how much of each is in the air (a flock calls more on the wing than at rest, and hardly at all after dark — `activityAt`, the sun the sky is lit by), and draws each quarter second since the last frame off the flock's own scatter (`criesIn`), so a seed cries the same cries on every ride and a replay cries them again. Distance is the plan's distance with the height in it, faded to nothing over the last third of a call's reach; a skein is heard from its leader. The eagle keeps quiet on purpose — its silence over the ridge is the character of it; the chough's ringing whistle off the crags is the alpine's own.

## Where the ear is (`listener.ts`)

One row per camera rung. At the TIPS the snow is an arm's length away — the bases hissing, the edges tearing, the wind full in the face; in the HELMET you are the skier, the wind roaring round the helmet and every plant in your own hands; behind and above (CHASE, the seat the mix is tuned at) it is all in proportion; stood back (FAR) and craned up (HIGH) the skier is a small thing on a big hill, the wind gone; the menu's ORBIT is a thread under a card. Its `side` column says how far a crosswind is panned to the ear it comes from: all the way with the ear on the skier, none from the orbit, which circles him. Its `machine` column leans on the helicopter — on top of its own distance, which is the bed's — loudest in the helmet sat on its skid, thinner from the boom. Its `cabin` column says whether a lens aboard the jump plane is in its cabin (TIPS, the cockpit; HELMET, his own eyes in the door) or outside it (the booms), and its `doorway` column how far that ear is at the jump door — all of it on HELMET, a little up front in the cockpit. A camera-dependent sound is a column in this table, never a branch in a bed.

## The mix

- The helicopter's crash is the loudest thing in the game, a few dB under the limiter's knee; the rotor heard from its skid is the loudest bed, over a gale in the face and under the crash.
- The jump plane heard from its open door sits with the helicopter on its skid (climbing −22 dBFS, the take-off's rasp −21, against the hover's −22), and from the boom outside a couple of dB under; its crash is the helicopter's blast at the same peak with the airframe tearing over it. The freefall's roar on the belly sits a touch over a tuck flat out (−30 against −33) and head down with a gale in the face (−24 against −26); under the canopy it is near silence (−51).
- The hot air balloon's burner heard from its basket sits at about the helicopter's hover from its skid (−21 dBFS on the meter against −22), a deafening thing in life held under the rotor here; the pilot light between burns is a whisper (−50) and its crash under the helicopter's.
- A ski jump's tracks at the edge sit with a flat-out tuck on the piste (−31 dBFS against −32.5), the flight in the V between the air and the tuck (−36), and the crowd's roar climbs from −36 for a jump short of K to −33 at K and −29 past HS, at a soft landing's level and under a hard one; the landing's slap (−26) is the loudest of its one-shots, under `land_hard`.
- The wipeouts, the landings and the trunk are the loudest things in the bank after it (a brush with a trunk well under a hit); the gates' slaps sit under the snow — heard over a race, never instead of one. `tests/audio_test.ts` holds a ceiling on every voice.
- **The faders** (OPTIONS ▸ SOUND): MASTER, WIND (the fader keeps the sibling games' name, `engine`, in the settings) and EFFECTS, under the SOUND switch. `bus.ts` keeps two volume-scaled views of the one synth — the wind's layers play through one (`engineSfx`), the skis, the poles, the birds, the helicopter and every one-shot through the other (`sfx`) — and `mixOf` in `settings.ts` folds the master and the switch into both. A layer reads its view every frame, so a fader moved over the front door is heard at once.
- Under a card the whole bed is ducked (`CARD_DUCK` in `App.tsx`), and the race's events make no sound at all unless the player is skiing (`soundsLive` in `shell.ts`) — a gate the bot takes behind the front door is not news.
- Nothing plays before the player has touched something: `unlockAudio()` hangs off document-wide `pointerdown` and `keydown`, because a context built outside a real gesture is one iOS Safari never resumes.

## Hearing it

```sh
make audition                                                         # previews/audition.html: every sound on a button, the beds under sliders
CHROMIUM_PATH=/opt/pw-browsers/chromium make audition ARGS=--meter     # headless: a level (dBFS) for every bed preset and every sound
make audition ARGS="--meter --seat helmet"                             # ...heard from another camera
```

The page is built by `scripts/audition.mjs`, which inlines the audio modules into one page — the framework's instrument read from its shipped source — and plays them on the synth the game ships — so what is auditioned is what ships. A session that cannot listen reads the meter as a SHAPE: the start gate under the cruise under the tuck flat out, the air with the snow gone, the hockey stop's rasp, the landings at the top of the bank, the gates' slaps at the bottom, nothing within a few dB of the limiter — and the helicopter's presets: spooling up on the pad, on the skid hovering and diving (over the gale), flying home three hundred metres and a kilometre and a half off (well under the cruise, never silent) and beside the burning wreck; the jump plane's (starting in the door, the take-off roll, climbing from the door and from the boom, the jump run, the stall horn, flying home and the wreck) and the skydive's (out of the door, freefall on the belly, head down and tracking, the canopy flogging open, under it, the flare and the cut-away); the crashes at the top of the bank. `tests/plane_audio_test.ts` holds the plane's and the skydive's faults. `tests/heli_audio_test.ts` holds the helicopter's own faults: a rotor whose rate does not follow its spool, a machine as loud far off as on the skid, a whine that outlives a crash, a wreck that does not crackle. `tests/audio_test.ts` guards the faults that are silences rather than crashes: an event nothing answers, a mix that creeps, a bed whose numbers never move, a bed that holds under the pause card, a cutoff past a Bluetooth headset's Nyquist.

There is no music, by design: the game's sound is its effects alone, as in the jet-ski game.
