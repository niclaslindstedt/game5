title: Speed skiing — straight down a track of its own, your speed through a 100 m timing zone the result, on nine tracks
---

A fourth race discipline beside the slalom, the super-G and the downhill, and the fastest thing in the game. A speed track is not a piste: it is cut straight down the face, 30 m wide and graded smooth, its crests rounded so nothing leaves the snow at 200 km/h and its knees eased so nothing slams into it, with a closed margin cleared either side. Out of the start house down the launch, through a 100 m TIMING ZONE marked in red, and down a long run-out to the finish enclosure at the bottom, where the crowd stands. Hold the tuck and keep the skis flat: your result is your speed between the zone's two lines, to the hundredth of a km/h, read finer than a frame. Past the zone you stand up into the wind on your own and skid to a stop beyond the braking line. Two runs, as the sport runs them folded down: a QUALIFYING run from a start lower down the track, then the fastest twenty race the FINAL from the top, slowest first and fastest last, ranked on the final alone. The HUD reads your speed where the clock would be, the finish plate and its board read in km/h, and the record book keeps your best on every track. It has NINE TRACKS OF ITS OWN, from a short fell track won at 185 km/h to a long launch past 225, across the fell, the alpine, the continental and the maritime, all open from the first visit. A speed race is only run in a light wind — its jury's anemometer stands at the top of the timing zone. Links: `?start=speedski`, `?mode=speedski`.
