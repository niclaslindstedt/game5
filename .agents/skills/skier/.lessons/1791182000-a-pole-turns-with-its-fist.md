The windmill's poles first pointed out along the arm: at the top of the
circle they stood out of the fists like antennas. A power grip lays the
shaft obliquely across the palm (some 65° off the hand's line), so it
leaves the little finger's side about 115° off the forearm, and the
wrist's sideways tilt moves that only 20° toward the thumb and 35° toward
the little finger: the shaft lies 95–150° off the forearm, while the
forearm's roll and the shoulder leave the hand nearly free round it.

Two dead ends before the answer. A rigid grip carried round a full
circle points the pole forward over his head. Any field of pole
directions inside that cone round a FULL circle must also point forward
somewhere: a straight arm swung behind him and a little out leaves only
"down and forward" or "up" for the shaft, and the "nearest to the aim"
choice flips when the arm passes near the aim. The arm swinging behind
him was the fault, not the pole: circle the fists in front of the
shoulders (`ARMS.mill`'s circle through home, its phase the arms' turning
angle) and the pole can stay at one aim, back, out and down, the whole
way. `tests/skier_flight_test.ts` holds the fists ahead of the shoulders
and no pole forward through the windmill; a small node sweep of the
candidate field over the circle (rate, forward lean, angle to the arm)
found the dead ends in seconds where a lab shot each took a minute.

A held lean in the air is the player's commitment: the windmill (a skier
fighting a rotation) is cancelled by it, like a spin's.
