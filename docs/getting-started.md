# Getting started

Fall Line runs in any browser with WebGL2 — on a phone held either way up, or on a desktop with a keyboard. This page is what a first run looks like, what every control does, and how to run the game from a checkout.

## Playing it

Open [game5.niclaslindstedt.se](https://game5.niclaslindstedt.se/). To keep it on a phone, install it: iOS Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen; Android Chrome → menu → Install app. The installed app launches fullscreen, plays offline once loaded, and shows a small new-build button in a corner when a newer version has been deployed — press it whenever you like; the build you are skiing keeps working until you do.

## A first run

1. **The attract card.** The publisher's name while the first mountain is built behind the card, then the app's two carved tracks lay themselves over the peak, FALL LINE rises under them, and the card asks for a press. Any key or a tap clears it.
2. **The front door.** One lit tile, **CAMPAIGN** (below), with how far up the mountain you have got and the run it would ski next. Under it, **RACE**: one run from the start gate to the finish line against three skiers, and **TIME TRIAL**: the same piste alone under the lights, against the clock and the record book's best for that mountain and pair. Both ski one of the campaign's pinned maps, picked on a **level card** out of the shelves the campaign has opened — its name is on the tile — so a record is a record down a piste anyone can ski; a link that names `?seed=<n>` skis that seed instead. Each opens the ski card. Then **TRICKS** and **FREE RIDE** (both below). Along the foot: **OPTIONS** (the sound switch and every other setting live there), **GALLERY**, the build. Behind the card a bot is already skiing the mountain and the camera circles him; nothing is paused.
3. **The ski card.** Which pair: the skis on their rack with the skier beside them, an arrow either side to step through the six, best all-round first — **CHAMOIS**, an all-mountain ski (the middle of every band), **SWIFT**, a slalom ski (short and narrow on a tight sidecut: darts from edge to edge, nervous at speed), **CHOUGH**, a giant slalom ski (long, stiff and cut for a wide arc: holds an edge on ice, skids a tight bend), **EAGLE**, a downhill ski (the longest and fastest in a tuck, hates a tight bend), **MARMOT**, a powder ski (wide and rockered: floats where the others bog, vague on the groomer) and **HARE**, a soft twin-tip for the park and the beginner (spins and grabs, lands soft, slow) — its length, waist, sidecut radius, weight and top speed beside it, six bars saying where it stands against the others (TOP SPEED, EDGE HOLD, QUICKNESS, FLOAT, FORGIVENESS, LANDINGS), and its TOPSHEET: four graphics each pair is sold in — the paint, the trim and a pattern — picked on the swatches. The pair and each pair's topsheet are remembered. **SKI** stands the run up; the three rivals are each dealt a pair of their own.
4. **The loading card.** The mountain generated from its seed, the field stood on the start line, the terrain, the forest and the gates built, every shader compiled — a second or two, shown as a bar per phase.
5. **The lights.** You stand four abreast on the start line behind the start gate, a few metres of flat before the face falls away. Three lights, then GO — and the field goes on its own reactions, a rival a fraction of a second late off the gate and none of them in step.
6. **Out of the gate.** Hold TUCK and the skier skates out of the start — the skis opened into a V, a leg pushing off each stride, the poles planted with every push — then double-poles once he is rolling, and the start gate a few metres down opens the run. The mountain is a whole ski area — a peak and a lower shoulder over a village on the valley floor, its LIFTS (a gondola to a mid-station on a bench part-way down, chairs on to the peak and the shoulder, a drag over the nursery slopes) running up it, its runs of every colour coming down it and merging, and narrow transport lanes — cat tracks — winding gently across the face between them and to the foot of each lift. You race ONE course down it, from a top station to the village: a run, then each run it merges into. Every other run on the mountain is groomed too, its edge poles in its colour, and is yours to ski on a free ride. The course is a groomed, packed line graded down the fall line with long traverses across it, narrowing through necks between the trees and opening out again, rollers on it that are jumps, drifts of fresh snow across it (R17) — slower going on a narrow ski, and where a powder ski earns its keep — and the groomer's windrows along both edges. Off the piste is the mountain: bowls and gullies, the woods below the tree line, and the deep powder. At the foot of the mountain every run comes down into the **HUB** — an open band of groomed snow along the valley floor, the valley's lifts standing in it — and every run can be skied again from it without skiing a harder one on the way back up. Along the hub run two **WIND TUNNELS**, one each way: a lane under a row of glowing arches with a fan at its mouth. Stand into the wind at the fan and it blows you down the lane to the lifts at the far end without skiing a metre (the news column says so going in); ski across one and it leaves you be.
7. **The run.** Gates — a pair of poles with a panel between them, red and blue alternating, the next one loud, the rest muted — must be taken in order. Down a resort's course they are SLALOM GATES, narrower than the piste and set alternately left and right of its line, so the fast line weaves through them: ski past one — outside its poles — and **three seconds** go on your clock (the news column says so, and the finish plate says what the missed gates cost in all), and the run goes on to the next. On a map built before the resorts — one piste down one face — the gates span the piste, and one skied past is owed instead: the HUD warns you at once, with an arrow back up at it, the metres to go and the reset press lit, and the next one is not credited until the missed one is taken. The last gate is the **finish line** under the arch, in a fenced arena on the valley floor. The round map under the buttons in the top right turns with you, so ahead is always up: the grey line is the piste, the other runs of the resort lie under it in their colours with the lifts as thin lines, the red bar the gate you owe (a red chevron on the rim points at it when it is off the map), and the coloured dots the other three skiers.
8. **The finish plate.** Your place and time, then the whole field's table, live, as the others cross the line. **RACE AGAIN** (the same mountain from the start line), **NEW MOUNTAIN** (back to the level card), or **MAIN MENU**. A time trial's plate is your time, then the record book's line: **NEW RECORD**, or the best that stood — its pair and the day it was set — and how far off it you were; **SKI AGAIN** is the same mountain from the start line.

**The record book and the ghost.** Every finished run is filed under its mountain's seed, the pair and the mode, and a quicker one replaces the row (a tie does not). In a time trial the HUD's split carries a second chip, **VS BEST**: how far ahead (green) or behind (red) the record was at that same gate. And the run that set the record is kept as the controls that skied it, so the next trial on that mountain and pair is skied beside its **ghost** — a pale, see-through skier on exactly the line the record took, leaving no track in the snow. Beat the time and your run becomes the ghost.

## The campaign

**CAMPAIGN** is four shelves, each a whole SKI AREA, and six of its courses to race on it — the **Rime Woods** (deep snow and rimed firs in the maritime: one blue to warm up on, then the reds), the **High Cirque** (the alpine: reds, then the first two blacks), the **Frost Basin** (a steep continental face: reds giving way to blacks) and the **Cold Crest** (another continental massif: one red, then the hardest blacks on the board). Every level is a red or a black course — a blue only to open the first shelf; the greens and the easier blues are the free ride's — and the blacks are ordered by how hard they really are, not by their colour alone. Each tab carries its area's name, its country and the six levels' grade signs (a green circle, a blue square, a red rectangle, a black diamond), each box its own level's sign. The course is the part of the mountain that counts — its gates, its clock, its points — but the whole area is there to ski: the other runs, the lanes, the lifts and the wind tunnels along the valley floor, and each shelf a race, a time trial, two races, a time trial and a race, every rung asking a little more than the one before. A race rung is skied against the same three rivals, who never lean on you, and pays the podium **3**, **2** and **1** points for the whole field — fourth pays nothing. A time trial rung is skied alone and pays a **medal**: bronze, silver or gold against the times on its box. A podium or a medal opens the next run; clearing every run on a shelf AND standing top of its table opens the next shelf. Every box shows the map's piste, its day (the sky and the hour it starts at) and what you have got out of it; the ring is on the rung the campaign would ski next, and **SKI** in the card's head takes it. Nothing is ever spent: ski a run again whenever you like, and the board keeps your better afternoon. The finish plate on a rung says what it paid and what it opened, and **NEXT RUN** skis the rung after it.

## A tricks run

**TRICKS** on the front door opens the **trick map card**: six maps, each a seed with its **terrain park** laid down the piste (R20) and skied on a day of its own — a clear late-winter noon, a January morning in flurries, an April afternoon, a low December sun, a snow day and a March night under the stars — the box naming the sky, the hour and the date. The park is kickers in three sizes — low, medium and high — at every straight stretch of the piste: the low one a kicker onto snow falling away, the medium and the high ones a lip, a flat deck, and a **landing slope** dug down past it to catch you, so a big jump taken at the right speed comes down on the slope rather than slamming onto the flat. Pick a map (it is remembered) and it opens the ski card; **SKI** stands it up, and after the lights you have two minutes alone on it, the gates counting nothing. The score is the run:

- **The air pays** by the second and by the metre, more the longer you hang.
- **Flips**: carry the lean all the way back off the lip (**↓** / **E** / **Shift**) and the skier throws a **BACKFLIP**; all the way forward (**↑** / **Q** / **Z**) a **FRONT FLIP**. **360s**: throw the edge all the way over (**A** / **D** held to the stop). One full press is one throw; tap it again for more rotation, out of what the flight allows. You cannot start one on the way down. Short of full lean, the lean is the usual air control — how you check a flip for the landing.
- **Grabs**: hold the trick button (**F** / **X** on a keyboard) in the air: with the edge over, a **DAFFY**; with the lean back, a **SPREAD EAGLE**; otherwise a **MUTE GRAB**. While you hold a grab the lean and the edge move you, not the skis — and let go before you land.
- **The combo**: everything turned in one flight, and in flights linked by landing and taking the next kicker within a second or so, is one combo: its points × its multiplier, over the tips while it is in hand, banked when you have been back on the snow a moment. A second revolution in one flight is worth more than two single ones; a flip and a 360 in one flight is a **TWIST**. Land it soft — met on the landing slope, the legs taking it a little at a time — and the landing is its own element: a **CLEAN LANDING**, or a **PERFECT LANDING** for one that barely touched, worth more the softer it was and, beside a trick in the same flight, a step more of multiplier (two for a perfect one). Land it hard (**SKETCHY**) and you are paid the points without the multiplier; land over the tips, get thrown, reset, or come down still in a grab and the combo is lost.

The HUD shows the **SCORE** and the seconds **LEFT** where a race keeps its gates; the finish plate is the score. The record book keeps no tricks scores.

## A free ride

**FREE RIDE** on the front door is the whole mountain: no lights, no gate owed, no clock to beat — the bowls, the kickers out on the face, the deep powder — and the ski area full of people. A few hundred AMATEURS ski its runs and ride its lifts — queueing in the corral at a lift's foot, sat four to a chair, towed up in pairs behind a T-bar, so the lifts are full of people — the greens busiest and the blacks nearly empty: families with the children in a wedge behind a parent, ski schools snaking down in the instructor's track in matching bibs, friends who stop together in the middle of the run, the beginner who traverses wall to wall, the one who stops just below the crest, the bomber straight down the fall line, the powder hound off into the trees, the park rat at every kicker, and the lot coming down from the hut after a long lunch. They fall, they get up, they get in your way: shoulder one and the news column says so; hit one hard and you both go down. It opens the **start card** first:

- **MOUNTAIN** — the seed, typed or stepped, and **ANOTHER MOUNTAIN** to deal a fresh one. Beside it the mountain itself, summit at the top and every run falling down the picture, in two views a chip in the corner turns between. The **PANORAMA** paints the mountain from out over the valley the way a ski area's piste map hangs — the lit snow and its blue shadows, the woods with every run's cut through them, the rock, the village — edge to edge in the plate under a clear blue sky with a few clouds, the summit ridge its skyline, with the whole ski area laid over it: every run in its colour with its number in its grade's sign (the pistes numbered 1 on up without a gap, the same number the sign at the run's head reads; a cat track a thin dashed line), the course the map is raced on drawn widest, the stretches the view cannot see dotted, the lifts as dark lines between their stations, the start line (a green triangle), the finish (a black square) and the kickers. The **PLAN** is the same mountain from above: the face shaded, the woods, the groomed piste in orange from the start line to the finish, the start line and every **kicker** as an arrow the way it throws — red on the piste, violet out on the mountain, which is what a free skier goes looking for. Under it the piste's length, the vertical and the kicker count. **Tap either view to start anywhere on it** (a spot in a trunk is moved onto the nearest point of the piste); **FROM THE START GATE** takes that back.
- **GRADE** — the colour of the run, graded as a piste is by its steepest hundred metres: the mountain's own (**AS DEALT**), or a **GREEN** (gentle and wide, all of it under 16 %), a **BLUE** (under 27 %), a **RED** (under 47 %) or a **BLACK** (steep off the start hut, pitches past 70 %, cliff bands across the piste to drop off, cliffs beside it, the most kickers). The line under the chart carries the sign of the colour that came out, and the piste's edge poles are painted in it.
- **COUNTRY** — what kind of snow country the mountain is raised in, on the same seed: the **ALPINE** (every map's own: steep, the woods low down and the bowls above the tree line, wind crust on the crests and rock breaking through the steep faces), the **FELL** (low rounded country in the far north, half the vertical, birch at a low tree line, a wind crust carved into sastrugi), the **CONTINENTAL** (a tall dry range, cold light snow, the tree line high) or the **MARITIME** (deep heavy snow, rime on the trees). Each is graded in its own light over the whole picture. The chart follows the row.
- **SEASON** — **EARLY WINTER** (mid-December, the lowest sun and the shortest day), **MIDWINTER**, **LATE WINTER** or **SPRING** (April, a high sun and long days): how high the sun climbs and how long the shadows lie. It stands on **AS DEALT**, the mountain's own date, until moved.
- **TIME** — **MORNING**, **DAY**, **EVENING** or **NIGHT**, worked out on that date at that mountain's latitude — a morning in December is late and short, one in April early. Night skis under the moon and the stars, the finish arena lit by its floodlights and every skier by the headlamp on his helmet — a wide pool of light that turns with his head. It stands on **AS DEALT**, the mountain's own hour, until moved. The sun stays where it is for the whole run.
- **WEATHER** — the mountain's own sky, or clear, fair, flurries (a few flakes out of a sunny sky), high cloud, overcast (flat light, the rollers hard to read), a steady snowfall, a storm under black cloud (a few tens of metres to see by) or a valley fog. Where it snows, new snow builds up as you ski — a storm lays a hand's depth in an hour, burying the groomed piste and deepening the powder.
- **SNOW** — how deep the powder lies off the piste: **THIN** (20 cm), **MEDIUM** (40 cm — the snow every race is skied on), **THICK** (70 cm) or **VERY DEEP** (a metre). Past MEDIUM it is bottomless, and skiing it is a skill of its own: stop and you sink to the knees, so keep the speed up and lean back to float the tips (a wide pair floats where a narrow one bogs).

**NEXT** is the ski card, and **SKI** there stands the run up — **ON A CHAIR**: you come onto the mountain seated on the chairlift whose run passes nearest the spot you picked (or the start gate), a span or two below its top, riding up the rope over the last tower — the chair lurches as its grip runs over the sheaves — and slowing into the top station: the terminal under its hood, the operator in the glass booth beside the unload, the orange netting round the wheel, the wind mast. At the unload point you are stood up onto the ramp and slide off it on the diagonal; you are LED round on the level pad and over its lip onto the run you picked — the face falling away under a lens held level, so the drop off the top reads as the drop it is — and from the first touch of a control the skis are yours (**R** on the chair puts you at the top at once). And the lifts run all the time, on every map: ski into a lift's **load zone** slowly, facing up its line, and it takes you up — a **CHAIR** from the blue load line at the head of its roped corral (it scoops you from behind and sits you down; at the top it stands you on the ramp), the **GONDOLA** through the door at the back of its station house (you ride inside a cabin and walk out of the top station onto its pad, facing down the mountain), a **DRAG** from the head of its track (the T-bar behind your thighs, pulled up the track on your skis and let go short of the top). The news column says so as it takes you. The HUD keeps the speed, the edge bar, the air clock (a jump timed once it has lasted half a second) and the minimap, and in place of the race's place, gate count and vertical shows the run's clock, its **BEST AIR** and the distance **SKIED**; the minimap shows the piste without a gate on it, every other run and every lift round it. **R** stands you back on the nearest run — a piste before a transport lane — or, at the foot of the mountain with nothing left to ski, back up on the start line. The pause card's **START AGAIN** skis the same mountain from where you started. Everything on the start card is remembered.

At any point, **Escape** (or the pause mark in the top right, under the minimap) holds the run under the pause card. Under its head a strip bills the run so far — your place and the clock, then the record a time trial is skied against, your best air, the distance skied, the gates taken and the vertical dropped, up to four figures — and under that: **RESUME** (back to the frame you left), **OPTIONS** (a panel with the camera and the sound: the switch and the three faders, applied to the held frame at once), and along the card's foot, in one quiet strip, the three presses that end the run: **RESTART**, **REPLAY** and **MAIN MENU**. The pause card is the only surface that stops the snow; behind every other card the run keeps going.

**The replay.** Every race, time trial and tricks run is recorded as the controls that skied it, and **WATCH REPLAY** — on the finish plate, or on the pause card, where it ends the run — plays it back: the same run rebuilt, the rivals skied again by the same bot, the tracks cut again from clean snow. It opens on the **BROADCAST** camera, which is the chase camera until the run reaches a moment it marked (a flight, a place taken, a trunk or a rival met, a wipeout, the finish); for those it cuts to a lens planted at the edge of the wood beside the kicker, the arch or the spot, before the skier gets there, and runs the moment in slow motion (the bar says **SLOW**). **C** walks the other cameras, **Escape** or **EXIT** leaves for the front door. A free ride keeps no replay.

## Pictures and the gallery

**Enter** takes a picture of the run as it stands — the snow, the skier, the sky and the HUD over them, signed in the corner with the game's mark — and says **PICTURE SAVED** in the news column (**· COPIED** where the browser also put it on the clipboard). **Enter** works while a replay is being watched too — the broadcast camera's frame, without the replay's bar. **H** (or OPTIONS ▸ PICTURE ▸ **HUD**) takes the readouts down — the thumbs and the corner presses stay — and a picture taken then is the snow alone. The desktop app's **View ▸ Take Screenshot** presses the same shutter, and in the store app a screenshot taken with the phone's own buttons is filed here too. Each picture is captioned with the mountain's seed, the gate, the speed and the pair.

**GALLERY**, along the front door's foot, is the roll: the newest picture first, an arrow either side to flip (or the filmstrip under it), and **SHARE** (the phone's own share sheet, where the browser has one), **COPY**, **SAVE** and a two-step **DELETE**. The roll keeps the last forty pictures on this device (`docs/configuration.md`); the oldest falls off.

## The developer page

Hold the front door's title — the mark and the name — for **seven seconds**. Nothing shows while it is held; when it lands a **DEVELOPER** chip appears along the foot. The page behind it is instruments over the picture, each a switch: the **FRAME RATE**, the **FRAME COST** (the engine's steps and the draw cut into its stretches, the draw calls and triangles), **PHYSICS** (every station's load, sink and travel — the tip, the middle and the tail of each ski — the share on packed snow, the edge and the skid), the **TRAIL MAP** (the two maps the snow reads its tracks off, in the corner), the **ENGINE LOG** and a **FREE CAMERA** (I K fly, J L slide, U O sink and climb, shift faster, drag to look). With any of them on, the overlay's foot carries the **REPRO** line — the run on screen as a link; **COPY REPRO LINK** puts it on the clipboard. **UNLOCKS** opens or shuts the campaign's shelves by hand. **BENCHMARK** takes the canvas for thirty seconds of a pinned race through the woods and over a kicker, the whole field skied by the bot and drawn as fast as the machine can; its score is an index where 100 is real time, **COPY DEBUG REPORT** puts the whole breakdown on the clipboard, and **BENCHMARK HISTORY** keeps the last twenty runs. **LOCK** shuts the page again.

## Skiing

The whole game is two grounds under one pair of skis. On the **groomed piste** the edges bite, a carve holds and the skier is quick and sure. In **powder** the skis sink: at walking pace you wallow to the knees, and as speed builds the tips float up onto the top of the snow and you plane — keep the speed up and you float; stop in it and you dig in. Cutting a corner through the powder is a decision with a price. The drive is gravity: the steeper the pitch, the faster you go, and a **tuck** takes the wind off you.

- **Turning.** On the piste the **edge** turns you: tip the skis over and the sidecut bends into the snow and carves an arc — more edge, a tighter arc, until the edge lets go and skids. In powder the skis turn on their bases and the skier's weight does the work.
- **The back key means two things, by the order you press it.** Press it FIRST and it is the **brake**: alone a snowplough, and an edge put on after it swings the skis across the way into a **hockey stop** — the skis turned across the slope, the stop. Press it with an edge ALREADY ON and it **cuts the edge harder**: the skis stood further over and pressed into the groove, a tighter arc that costs you almost nothing — the slalom racer's turn. On the phone, drag the edge thumb DOWN for the back key (in the air it is still the lean back).
- **The tuck.** Fold out of the wind and the speed builds; stand up and it bleeds off.
- **Working for speed.** Below about 40 km/h on a straight the skier works on his own, to gain speed and to keep it: off a standstill he **double-poles** out like a racer leaving the gate (up a rise he **strides** instead — the skis parallel, each leg kicking back in turn, the opposite arm planting its pole), rolling he **skates** (the skis in a V, pushing off each leg's inside edge in turn with both poles planted on every push), and faster he **double-poles** again (both poles planted ahead and the body folded down over them). Under the start lights he waits in the gate crouched over poles planted ahead of his boots, and GO sends him out on them. It is a man's push, strong off a standstill and fading as he gathers speed; braking or loading a jump stops it. Stood still with nothing pressed, he stays put — hold the tuck to set off.
- **The jump.** Hold **SPACE** and the skier sinks onto his legs, the lower the longer; let go and he springs — the longer it was loaded, the higher, up to two seconds' worth. Pop a roller's crest and you fly further.
- **Landings.** Every landing is a load on the legs: the speed into the slope as a fall from rest, stopped over the legs and whatever loose snow is under you. You ski like a professional: anything you come down on your skis for — crooked, rolled onto your edges, slapped down tips-first — you ride away, and the skier shows the save (the arms flung out, the shoulder knocked back off a tree, a hand down to the snow, the wobble of an edge that bit). You go down only when the body cannot stay up: landing on your side, your back or your head, the tips spearing the snow, a trunk square in front of your skis, an edge caught sliding sideways, or a load past what legs can hold (**CRASH LANDING**). Deep powder is a long, soft landing; the groomer and the ice are hard. A big landing ridden away shows its load in g. The rivals are dealt how much each can take — some ride out what throws another.
- **Kickers.** Rollers on the piste (and wind lips on the crests off it) are shaped to throw you. In the air the **lean** pitches you, tips up or down; land on the downslope and you keep your speed; land flat past it and the legs bottom and cost you.
- **Trees** are solid. A clipped trunk spins you; one met square stops you — and met hard, it stops the skis and not you.
- **Wipeouts.** Hit a trunk hard, land over the tips, land a big air crooked (or flat, past what the legs take), fall at speed or catch an edge (an edge stood too far over for the speed — the high-side) and the skier comes off — throwing his hands out to meet the snow, going over the way that fall goes (on over a trunk or the tips, over onto his side off a caught edge, back and down off a landing his legs could not hold) and sliding to rest with the skis going on without him — and a couple of seconds later you are stood back on the piste at the last gate you took. The reset press lights up the moment you are thrown: press it (or R) to be back on your skis at once.
- **Bogged.** Stop in deep powder and you sink to the knees (**BOGGED** on the screen). **Pole out**: hold the tuck for the poles and **rock** — the lean back and forth and the edge side to side — and you climb out.
- **Stuck or down?** **R** stands you back on the piste just past the last gate you took. It also happens on its own after a few seconds lying still or going nowhere (longer once you are bogged, to give you the time to pole out).

## Controls

**Keyboard** (`pwa/src/game/settings-input.ts` is the table):

| Key                | Does                                                                                      |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| W                  | Tuck — and sets off from a standstill; in the air, lean forward                           |
| S                  | First: brake (then A / D: hockey stop). After an edge: cut harder — in the air, lean back |
| Space              | Hold to load a jump, let go to spring (higher the longer, to 2 s)                         |
| A D / ← →          | Edge left / right                                                                         |
| ↓ / E / Shift      | Lean back — in the air, tips up                                                           |
| ↑ / Q / Z          | Lean forward — in the air, tips down                                                      |
| F / X              | Hold in the air on a tricks run: a grab                                                   |
| R                  | Back onto the piste at the last gate taken                                                |
| B                  | Restart the run from the start line                                                       |
| C                  | Next camera                                                                               |
| H                  | HUD on / off                                                                              |
| Enter              | Take a picture (filed in the GALLERY)                                                     |
| Escape             | Pause                                                                                     |
| Arrows, Enter, Esc | Walk a card, press a row, go back                                                         |

W and S lean only when they go down while the skier is IN THE AIR (a tuck held off the lip stays a tuck), S leaning does not brake, and a lean key held over them wins.

**Touch:** the lower-left of the screen is the **edge control** — touch anywhere there and move the thumb: sideways travel tips the skis onto their edge, vertical travel leans — and on the snow a drag DOWN is the back key (down first, then across: the hockey stop; across first, then down: the edge cut harder). The lower-right is the **tuck lever** — the skier is IN A FULL TUCK the moment your thumb lands; slide UP to stand him up, and further up to brake. **Tap it and put the thumb straight back down** to load a jump (the knob rings and swells), and lift to spring. The top-right corner carries three presses: pause, reset, camera.

**Cameras** (C, or the camera press): **tips** (a lens low over the ski tips) and **helmet** (the skier's own eyes) are worn and pitch and roll with him; **chase** (the default), **far** and **high** stand behind on a boom. The game remembers the one you chose.

## Options

**OPTIONS** — along the front door's foot, or a `?menu=options` link — over the same live run, in four groups. **CONTROLS**: **KEYS** opens a page with every action on it — press a row, then the key to put on it (Escape leaves it as it was); a key on two actions says so. On a touchscreen, **TUCK SIDE** swaps the lever and the edge control, **TRAVEL** shortens or lengthens every thumb's throw, and **INVERT LEAN** makes pushing the edge control away the lean back. **ASSIST**: **EDGE HOLD** (the arcade's hand holding the body on the arc the edges ask for and catching a skid) and **AIR BALANCE** (the skier's body keeping the skis level side to side in the air), each FULL, HALF or OFF, and **DAMAGE** (on, a trunk or a hard landing dulls an edge or hurts a knee and you ski it for the rest of the run, with an instrument beside the speed; off by default), all from the next run. **SOUND**: the switch, and faders for everything, the wind (the skier's own noise: the wind in the helmet and the edges on the snow) and the effects. **PICTURE**: the **HUD** switch (H mid-run), a **PRESET** that moves every row at once, then RESOLUTION, DISTANCE, TERRAIN, TRAILS, FOREST, SHADOWS, SPRAY and SMOOTH EDGES one at a time (DISTANCE has a fourth stop, **MAX**: LOW, MEDIUM and HIGH each close a mist over the mountain at their own reach — LOW only a few seconds ahead — and MAX draws the whole mountain to the summit ridge, clear; FOREST also sets how many variants of each kind of tree are drawn — all ten on HIGH, five on MEDIUM, two on LOW, never fewer kinds — and how many forms of each bird and animal (a hen beside the cock, a calf beside the cow: all of them on HIGH, two on MEDIUM, one on LOW, never fewer species) and how near one has to be to be drawn at its finer cut; SHADOWS' first stop, **SKIERS**, casts the field's shadows alone, and every stop but OFF lays the mountain's own shade over the slopes behind its ridges) — each applied at once, so it is judged against the run behind the card (SMOOTH EDGES alone waits for the next visit). PRESET's first stop, **AUTO** (a fresh visit's), times the machine under the front door and sets every row for sixty frames a second, keeping what shows most (`docs/configuration.md`); moving any row makes the picture yours. The caption at the foot says what the row under the pointer does.

## What the game remembers

The camera, the pair, the sound switch and every OPTIONS row (`docs/configuration.md`).

## From a checkout

```sh
npm install
npm run dev          # the game, hot-reloading; ?seed=38 on the URL for a given mountain
make build           # typecheck + production build into pwa/dist/
make test            # the suite
make sim             # the bot skis generated mountains headlessly: the balance table
make level SEED=38   # one mountain from above, every gate numbered
make resort SEED=7   # the whole ski area a seed builds: the runs, the lifts, the courses
make ride            # the skier on the snow in profile, one staged scenario at a time
```

The README's Usage table lists every target; [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) says which one a change owes. The browser-driven labs (`make screenshots`, `make profile`, `make world`, `make audition ARGS=--meter`) need `npm i --no-save playwright-core` and a Chromium (`CHROMIUM_PATH`).

The desktop app (`make tauri`) and the store app (`make native-*`) wrap the same built site — [platforms.md](platforms.md).
