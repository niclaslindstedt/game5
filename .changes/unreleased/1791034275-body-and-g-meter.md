title: The body and the g meter
---

How hard can you take a hit? Every landing, every fall onto the snow and every tree now puts the **g** of the blow up over the skier in big figures, shaking by how hard it was, with what took it from what under it. At the left edge of the HUD stands your **body**, seen from behind: every part painted green while it is sound, yellow, orange and red as it is hurt, the worst injuries under it in plain words (a sprained thumb, a torn ACL, a bruised kidney, a broken collarbone, a concussion…) and the run's hardest blow. The same fall is gentle in deep powder, hard on the groomer and harder on ice; a clean landing hurts nothing, a hard flat one finds the back, a landing in the back seat or a caught edge finds the knees, a hand put down finds the thumb and the wrist, and a tree finds everything. Twenty parts and fifty-seven injuries, each on a risk curve fitted to measured impact tolerances; a moderate injury or worse is news too. Nothing is ever drawn on the skier — only the readouts.
