title: The wind you ski through, heard and read on a wind meter
---

The wind in your ears is now the wind you are actually skiing through: the weather's wind and your own speed together. Ski into a headwind and the two add up — a 100 km/h gale in the face at 100 km/h is a roar of 200 km/h of air, with a low battering under it that a schuss in still air never makes — a tailwind as fast as you are goes quiet, a storm is heard standing still, and a crosswind is louder, flaps the suit and is heard on the side it comes from. A WIND meter beside the speed shows it: a dial turned with you, a faint arrow for the weather's wind and a bold one for the wind you feel, with the felt wind in km/h (red past 150) and the weather's under it.
