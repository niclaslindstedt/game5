title: Fly the jump plane
---

A free ride can now start in the open door of a jump plane: a single-turboprop, high-wing utility plane on wheel-skis, parked on its own strip below the village. Pick PLANE on the start card's START row, or open a `?plane=1` link.

You fly it by hand on a key table of its own, rebindable under OPTIONS ▸ KEYS ▸ PLANE: the stick, the rudder, a power lever that stays where you leave it, the flaps and the brakes. On touch it flies on the helicopter's two pads, with buttons for the flaps. The flight is a full physical model of the airframe, so the plane takes off in a couple of hundred metres, climbs, stalls and drops a wing — and loops, rolls, hammerheads, flies upside down and spins, out of the physics alone. The HUD shows a horizon, the airspeed in knots, the height over the snow, the climb, the power and the flaps, and calls a stall.

ENTER (a double tap on touch) jumps out of the door; stopped on the snow, it steps you off. Once you are out, the pilot flies the plane home and lands it.

- The plane is a modelled airframe: the propeller spins behind a blur disc, the elevator, rudder, ailerons, flaps and door move, and its navigation lights, beacons, strobes and landing light work after dark.
- TIPS is the cockpit: the pilot's seat behind live glass displays, moving sticks, pedals and levers, with the pilot at them. HELMET is your own eyes in the door. CHASE, FAR, HIGH and ORBIT circle the airframe.
- The strip has edge boards, threshold markings, a hangar, a fuel shed, a hut and a windsock, lit after dark, and it is laid where its approach is clear of the lift ropes. Its buildings and the parked plane are solid.
- A crash breaks the plane apart by how it hit — nose in, cartwheeled, on its belly, out of a spin or into the trees — with a fireball and a pool fire sized by the fuel aboard, burning on long after the ride has started again on the strip.
- It is heard: the propeller's buzz and its rasp at full power, the turbine's whine, the slipstream through the open door, the skis on the snow, the stall horn and the crash.

Near the edge of the map, every craft in the air — the plane, the helicopter, the paramotor, the balloon and the parachute — is now blown back in on the wind and turned toward the middle. The wind tunnels no longer catch anything flying more than 8 m over the snow.
