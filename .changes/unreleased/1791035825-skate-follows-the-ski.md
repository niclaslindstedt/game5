title: Skating takes the skier where his skis point — a long, committed stride on a zig-zag
---

When the skier skates at a crawl — out of the start, across a flat, out of a stop — he now rides the ski he has stepped onto. Each push sends him off diagonally along the gliding ski, and the next push sends him diagonally the other way, so his path zig-zags across the run while he keeps facing down it. He no longer slides straight forward between two splayed skis. The stride itself is slower and bigger, after measured skating technique. He skates about one push a second, quickening only as he nears his top speed, and glides longer on each ski. His push leg drives out long off its inside edge, and his weight goes wholly over the gliding ski. He bobs down as he loads it and rises over it. His hips turn toward the line he glides on, and his poles bite quickly inside each push, his arms following through behind him. The V of his skis is wide at a walk and closes as he gathers speed. Rivals leaving the start line push on their own reaction rather than standing through a glide first.
