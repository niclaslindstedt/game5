# Audio

Fall Line ships no audio files. Every sound is synthesized at runtime from a handful of authored numbers — TypeScript data under `pwa/src/game/audio/` — played on one small WebAudio instrument. That keeps the app tiny and offline and makes the sound design as diffable as the skis' own spec. The `sound-effects` skill is the working loop; this page is the shape of it.

## The register

A modern arcade racer in the snow — not a chip, and not a sample library. There is no engine: what a skier hears of his own speed is the AIR — the rush past the helmet, the roar it becomes folded into a tuck — and under it the skis on the snow: the bases hissing on the groomer, the steel tearing in a carve, the chatter of an edge on ice, the rasp of a skid, the hush of powder; and the poles' clicks at a crawl. Snow swallows transients, so a landing in powder is a brown thump that swells open under a pink puff rather than a click. The only sharp edges in the bank belong to the things that are not snow: a trunk cracking, a pole's tip on the hardpack, a gate's panel slapped past, two skiers shouldering, the finish's horn and its cowbells.

## The one-shots (`bank.ts`, `route.ts`)

| Event         | Sound                                                                                                          |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `land`        | `land_soft`, or `land_hard` when the legs could not take it all (`harsh`), scaled by the impact                |
| `hit`         | `hit_tree` — the one crack in the bank, scaled by the closing speed                                            |
| `bump`        | `bump` — two skiers shouldering, skis clacking                                                                 |
| `checkpoint`  | `checkpoint` — a gate's panel slapped past on its pole (not the start gate, whose crossing is the wand's beep) |
| `lap`         | `lap`                                                                                                          |
| `missed`      | `missed`                                                                                                       |
| `reset`       | `reset`                                                                                                        |
| `count`, `go` | The start hut's beeps, and the wand's GO                                                                       |
| `finish`      | The arena's horn, the crowd's cowbells under it and the gate's chime on top                                    |
| —             | `plant`: a pole's tip on the snow, raised by the bed on each plant while the skier is poling (no event)        |

`air` makes no sound on purpose: the lip is where the skier stops touching anything, and the wind coming up and the snow going quiet say so. A new sound needs a rung in `route.ts` (or a cue the bed raises) or it can never play, and **no `GameEvent` is ever added for a sound's sake** — what the app can work out from the state, it works out.

## The beds

A continuous sound is never written out of one-shots. It is a LAYER: an oscillator or a looping window onto a pool of noise, a filter, a saturation curve and a gain, built once and then steered every frame toward a target (level, pitch, cutoff, grit, pan) over a glide, on the audio thread. Nothing is booked ahead, so a late frame leaves every layer holding its last value rather than leaving a hole — and so every frame that is not hearing the race (the pause card, a hidden tab, a held screenshot) says `silence()`, or the wind would blow on behind the card.

**The wind** (`wind-voice.ts`), four layers: the RUSH (the broad pink rush past the helmet, climbing with the square of the airspeed, and the one layer that plays as loud in the air as on the snow), the ROAR (the low brown roar the tuck makes of it — the head down behind the hands, the wind breaking over the back — the layer that says TUCK before the speedo does), the WHISTLE (a thin band that comes in past forty and climbs with the speed: the air over the goggles' strap and the poles under the arms) and the FLUTTER (the suit flapping, loudest stood up at speed and pulled tight in a tuck). The tuck is why it sounds like skiing: folding down drops the drag and gathers speed (the physics), and moves the ear down out of the clean air into the roar (this bed). A wind that only followed the speed would say nothing about the skier's own body.

**The skis on the snow** (`snow-voice.ts`), five layers: the HISS (the bases on packed snow, climbing with the speed), the POWDER (a dark hush with body in it, loudest slow where the skier is sunk and shoving snow aside, thinning as he planes), the EDGE (the steel biting a carve on the hardpack, with the edge and the speed together — and taken down by a skid, since a skidded ski is not carving), the CHATTER (the edge on HARD snow at speed: the ski skipping on the ice in a fast buzz, off the piste's hardness — `level.iceAt` is boilerplate, a groomer firms with the pace the edge is driven at) and the SKID (the skis pivoted across the way scraping the snow off ahead of their edges — the hockey stop's rasp). Packed or powder is one number — `SkierState.packed`, the share of the load on groomed snow — and the hiss and the hush crossfade on it, so skiing off the piste is heard as the hiss giving way before anything else changes.

**The poles** are one-shots the bed raises: while the skier holds the tuck under walking pace on the snow (the engine's own poling band), each rise of `plantPulse` plays the bank's `plant` — a carbide tip's tick and the basket's pat — a touch lower in powder.

**The wood** (`bird-bed.ts`) is not a layer but a scheduler of one-shots: a cry is a CUE off the birds' plan, never a `GameEvent` — the engine has no idea a raven exists. Each frame it walks the flocks within earshot, asks how much of each is in the air (a flock calls more on the wing than at rest, and hardly at all after dark — `activityAt`, the sun the sky is lit by), and draws each quarter second since the last frame off the flock's own scatter (`criesIn`), so a seed cries the same cries on every ride and a replay cries them again. Distance is the plan's distance with the height in it, faded to nothing over the last third of a call's reach; a skein is heard from its leader. The eagle keeps quiet on purpose — its silence over the ridge is the character of it; the chough's ringing whistle off the crags is the alpine's own.

## Where the ear is (`listener.ts`)

One row per camera rung. At the TIPS the snow is an arm's length away — the bases hissing, the edges tearing, the wind full in the face; in the HELMET you are the skier, the wind roaring round the helmet and every plant in your own hands; behind and above (CHASE, the seat the mix is tuned at) it is all in proportion; stood back (FAR) and craned up (HIGH) the skier is a small thing on a big hill, the wind gone; the menu's ORBIT is a thread under a card. A camera-dependent sound is a column in this table, never a branch in a bed.

## The mix

- The landings and the trunk are the loudest things in the bank; the gates' slaps sit under the snow — heard over a race, never instead of one. `tests/audio_test.ts` holds a ceiling on every voice.
- **The faders** (OPTIONS ▸ SOUND): MASTER, WIND (the fader keeps the sibling games' name, `engine`, in the settings) and EFFECTS, under the SOUND switch. `bus.ts` keeps two volume-scaled views of the one synth — the wind's layers play through one (`engineSfx`), the skis, the poles, the birds and every one-shot through the other (`sfx`) — and `mixOf` in `settings.ts` folds the master and the switch into both. A layer reads its view every frame, so a fader moved over the front door is heard at once.
- Under a card the whole bed is ducked (`CARD_DUCK` in `App.tsx`), and the race's events make no sound at all unless the player is skiing (`soundsLive` in `shell.ts`) — a gate the bot takes behind the front door is not news.
- Nothing plays before the player has touched something: `unlockAudio()` hangs off document-wide `pointerdown` and `keydown`, because a context built outside a real gesture is one iOS Safari never resumes.

## Hearing it

```sh
make audition                                                         # previews/audition.html: every sound on a button, the beds under sliders
CHROMIUM_PATH=/opt/pw-browsers/chromium make audition ARGS=--meter     # headless: a level (dBFS) for every bed preset and every sound
make audition ARGS="--meter --seat helmet"                             # ...heard from another camera
```

The page is built by `scripts/audition.mjs`, which inlines the audio modules into one page — the framework's instrument read from its shipped source — and plays them on the synth the game ships — so what is auditioned is what ships. A session that cannot listen reads the meter as a SHAPE: the start gate under the cruise under the tuck flat out, the air with the snow gone, the hockey stop's rasp, the landings at the top of the bank, the gates' slaps at the bottom, nothing within a few dB of the limiter. `tests/audio_test.ts` guards the faults that are silences rather than crashes: an event nothing answers, a mix that creeps, a bed whose numbers never move, a bed that holds under the pause card, a cutoff past a Bluetooth headset's Nyquist.

There is no music, by design: the game's sound is its effects alone, as in the jet-ski game.
