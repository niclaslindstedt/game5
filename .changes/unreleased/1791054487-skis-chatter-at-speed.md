The faster you go, the less your skis can turn. Your edges could always hold only so much sideways force, so a turn flat out was already wider than one at a cruise. Now the snow rushing under the skis also makes them chatter, and a chattering edge grips less. At 120 km/h a full edge now holds about an eighth less than before. How much a pair chatters depends on the ski: the long, stiff downhill pair barely loses grip, while the soft park and powder pairs lose the most. You can see it too. At speed each ski hops off the snow, its tip flaps and its edge rocks, out of step with the other ski, and the skier's knees soak it up while his upper body stays still. The shaking is strongest on a hard-carved edge on groomed snow, and powder cushions it away.
