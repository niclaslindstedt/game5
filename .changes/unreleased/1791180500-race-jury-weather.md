title: Races are run only in the weather a real jury would race in — the wind held, a downhill's storm eased, the course clean at every start
---

A race's jury holds or calls off a start the weather makes unsafe or unfair, and now so does the game's: a slalom is never raced in gusts over 60 km/h at the start, and a downhill never over 50 km/h — a windier day is the race held for a calmer hour, the same sky with its wind eased and its direction kept. A downhill is not run in a blizzard either: its storm is eased to a steady fall you can see the course through. A slalom is still raced in falling snow, as the real ones are. Every race run — a training run, a slalom's first and second, the downhill race — starts on a course groomed hard and slipped clean, and the snow that falls while you ski it is all that lies on it: a few millimetres at most. A free ride, a time trial and a tricks run keep whatever sky they were dealt. The giant slalom, the super-G, the ski cross and speed skiing already carry their limits for when they are built.
