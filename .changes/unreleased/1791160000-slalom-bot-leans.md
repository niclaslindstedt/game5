title: The slalom's racers ski the leaning skier — and the par is the time they can make
---

The slalom bot (the attract run, the labs, and the racer every par is reckoned on) now plans its turns on the skier who leans into them: an edge no further over than his body is laid, a carve no tighter than the sidecut bends, the way turning only once his skis have swung through it, and a check that pivots his skis the way he steers. It finishes every campaign slalom again, a little slower than before (about 30 km/h on the mean, as it leaves room to cross from one edge to the next), and the par — and so the board's times — moves with it. On the open piste the downhill pair no longer runs wide of the gates on the bends its long sidecut cannot carve at speed.
