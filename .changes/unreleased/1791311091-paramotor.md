A PARAMOTOR on the free ride: the start card's RUN row has a new stop before the snowmobile that starts you on the summit, skis on, a small speed wing held up over you and a motor on your back. Ski off until the wing flies, then fly it on your own keys — the tuck the throttle, the skid the brakes and the flare, the edge the toggles, the lean the risers. Touch down and speed ride under it, or press Enter (a double tap on touch) to drop the whole rig and ski on. The wing stalls, surges, swings and turns as a real one does. It flies in the wind, and the wind matters: it blows harder the higher you fly, rises up the slopes it blows onto (ridge lift) and sinks in their lee, and turns rough the harder it blows, near the snow, over the woods and in a storm. Rough air folds a side of the wing or its leading edge (the HUD calls it; pump the brakes to reopen it), a strong wind can blow you backwards, a storm is close to unflyable, and in a tailwind you must ski faster than the wind before the wing will fly. The wing is drawn after real ones: an arced, elliptical wing of 25 pillowed cells with its mouths, stabilizers and three-colour panels, a full cascade of lines down to the risers, and a caged two-blade propeller, engine and tank behind you. Dropped, the propeller runs down and the wing lies crumpled on the snow.
