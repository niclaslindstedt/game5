The downhill rider's knees fold less deeply through compressions, berms and big landings, and in the air his arms reach out to push the bike down instead of hunching his trunk over the bars. When he stops pedalling, the cranks come round to level rather than freezing with one foot at the top of the stroke, so he never coasts into a turn or off a lip with a knee jammed up.
