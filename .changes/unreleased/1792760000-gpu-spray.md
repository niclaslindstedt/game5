The snow a turn throws is flown on the graphics card now, and much more of it hangs in the air for a fraction of the old cost. A hockey stop throws a bright fan of chips with a fine veil over it, and the chips that land lie on the groomer as a scatter of broken snow with their own small shadows before they settle in. A powder turn throws its snow out over the turn's outside and up over the shoulder. The thrown snow is shaded with the slope it flies over, so against a low sun it no longer glows white in the shade. A hard fall out of the air now presses a round crater where you land, wider and deeper the harder you came down, with its rim standing round it before the slide's gouge leads away.
