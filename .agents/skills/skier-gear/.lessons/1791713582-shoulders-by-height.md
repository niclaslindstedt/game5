The rig's shoulder joints stand wider and only ~7 cm under the neck's
base, so a trunk cut to the survey's levels ends in a flat lid at the
deltoids' height and a collar flared out to meet it reads as a cone of a
neck. The jacket's top is laid by HEIGHT over the joints instead
(`shoulderLine`): full breadth to the deltoid's top, then in to a collar
foot a few centimetres above the neck's point, so the trapezius slopes
and the collar stands as a cylinder round a 13 cm gaiter. Its depth must
thin slowly (most of it kept to ~3 cm over the joints) or the sleeve's
cap pokes out behind as the arm comes forward in a pole plant. A
contrasting collar (the body's colour on a dark gaiter) is what makes the
neck read narrower than the helmet from behind.

Every section and every cut is a ring: the trunk's sections every 0.05
levels already put a ring each 2.5 cm, so a quilt's step changed nothing
until the sections were spaced at 0.08. The budget (11,000 triangles) is
tight on the woman's puffer — measure every outfit when adding geometry
(the face's grid cost ~340).
