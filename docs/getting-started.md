# Getting started

Fall Line runs in any browser with WebGL2 — on a phone held either way up, or on a desktop with a keyboard. This page is what a first run looks like, what every control does, and how to run the game from a checkout.

## Playing it

Open [game5.niclaslindstedt.se](https://game5.niclaslindstedt.se/). To keep it on a phone, install it: iOS Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen; Android Chrome → menu → Install app. The installed app launches fullscreen, plays offline once loaded, and shows a small new-build button in a corner when a newer version has been deployed — press it whenever you like; the build you are skiing keeps working until you do.

## A first run

1. **The attract card.** The publisher's name, small on the dark, while the title scene comes in; then the scene's light comes up out of the black — a racer carving past a gate in a low winter sun, his spray backlit, snow falling and spindrift streaming off the crests — the lens pushing slowly in, and over it the logo draws itself and the card asks for a press. A press during the reveal finishes it; a press after it flies the logo up into the front door's header. Any key, a click or a tap.
2. **The front door.** A column of glass to one side of the picture: the lit **PLAY**, then **OPTIONS** (the sound switch and every other setting live there) as big and quieter, and under them, small, **GALLERY** and **STATISTICS** (both below) side by side, with the build at the foot. **PLAY** opens the three ways onto the snow on the same column — **BACK** or Escape returns to the door. First, lit, **RACE**, which opens the **race card**: one box a discipline — **SLALOM**, a race as the sport runs one, TWO RUNS against a field of thirty, one racer on the course at a time, out of a start house through gates of flex poles, on combined time (below); **GIANT SLALOM**, TWO RUNS against a field of thirty on two courses set on one hill, a gate every twenty-odd metres swinging you into long carved turns, on combined time (below); **SUPER-G**, ONE RUN against a field of thirty on a course you have never skied, long fast turns round panelled gates at ninety kilometres an hour and more, over its jumps (below); and **DOWNHILL**, the fastest race there is, ONE RUN down the whole course after a TRAINING run on it, through wide speed gates at well over a hundred kilometres an hour, past a speed trap, between the safety nets (below); **SKI CROSS**, four at once out of one start gate down a course built in the snow — a timed QUALIFYING run, then HEATS of four, the first two through to the final (below); and **SPEED SKIING**, straight down a track of its own, a QUALIFYING run and a FINAL, your speed through a 100 m timing zone the result (below) — each with its format and the map it stands on. Every race skis a PINNED map, picked on a **level card**, so a record is a record down a piste anyone can ski: one of its discipline's own **nine**, mountains chosen because their course makes a good race of it, the gentlest first, every one open from the first visit, each box billing the course's drop and length. A link that names `?seed=<n>` skis that seed instead. Each opens the ski card. Then **TRICKS** and **FREE RIDE** (both below). The keys walk every card: the arrows move a light round the rim of the press it is on, Enter takes it. The first time, the door stands over the title scene, the lens easing aside so the skier stands clear of the column — beside it on a wide screen, above it on a phone held upright; once you have skied a run and come back, a bot is skiing the mountain behind it and the camera circles him; nothing is paused.
3. **The ski card.** Which pair: the skis on their rack with the skier beside them, an arrow either side to step through the nine, best all-round first — **CHAMOIS**, an all-mountain ski (the middle of every band), **SWIFT**, a slalom ski (short and narrow on a tight sidecut: darts from edge to edge, nervous at speed), **CHOUGH**, a giant slalom ski (long, stiff and cut for a wide arc: holds an edge on ice, skids a tight bend), **FALCON**, a super-G ski (long and stiff on a 45 m sidecut: holds the hardest bend at a hundred km/h), **EAGLE**, a downhill ski (long and fast in a tuck, hates a tight bend), **WOLVERINE**, a ski-cross ski (a race ski cut down for berms and jumps: turns hard, lands clean, gives no ground), **PEREGRINE**, a speed ski (two and a half metres and an airtight suit: by far the fastest straight down, and it will not turn), **MARMOT**, a powder ski (wide and rockered: floats where the others bog, vague on the groomer) and **HARE**, a soft twin-tip for the park and the beginner (spins and grabs, lands soft, slow) — its length, waist, sidecut radius, weight and top speed beside it, three bars saying where it stands against the others (SPEED, how fast it runs; CARVE, how hard and how quickly it turns on the groomer; FREESTYLE, how it floats, forgives, lands, takes the bumps and spins), each pair in the one topsheet it is sold in, and **CUSTOMIZE SKIER**: the DRESS card, the skier on the same stand beside seven rows — BODY (a man or a woman), WEIGHT (LIGHT, MEDIUM, SOLID or HEAVY — not only a look: a heavier skier is cut broader — a solid one through the chest, a heavy one with a stomach — runs faster down a pitch, skates up to speed slower, lands a jump harder and shoulders another skier harder, and the ski card's top speed is his; each build keeps its own record book), JACKET (a race shell, a down puffer, a freeride shell, an anorak, a retro one), PANTS (insulated, race softshell, baggy, cargo), HELMET (race, freeride, visor, slalom), GLOVES (gauntlet, mittens, undercuff, race) and POLES (alloy, carbon, speed, powder — or **NONE**, the hard mode: no poles to push on, so no double pole, a rise you can barely climb and less to keep your balance with, which the card says under the rows while it is picked), each stepped with its arrows and each piece sold in its own colours; DONE is the ski card again. The pair and the outfit are remembered — the pair is yours to pick in a slalom too, though every racer of its field is on the slalom ski. **SKI** stands the run up.
4. **The loading card.** The mountain generated from its seed, the slalom, the super-G or the downhill set on it — or a speed track cut down it — the terrain, the forest and the gates built, every shader compiled — a second or two, shown as a bar per phase.
5. **The start house.** A slalom is set on the steepest stretch of the map's piste that drops a slalom hill's 140–220 m (R31) — a red or a black, never an easy hill — netted down both edges from the start to the finish arena, the trees cut back, the kickers levelled, the crowd standing behind the nets on both sides and thickest at the finish. You stand in the doorway of the START HOUSE at the top of it, a tall billboard front with a narrow dark door, your boots just behind the WAND and your poles planted in the snow beyond it, outside its posts. The starter's word is the screen's only count — **READY**, small at the top, then **GO** four seconds later — while the start clock inside the door counts it down, its light turning from red to green. The picture is the start as television shows it: first from high over the door, looking down on you leaning out over the wand, then cut to inside the house behind you, looking out past you and down the course through the doorway — the shot you go on.
6. **Out of the gate.** **Press TUCK** (W, or the tuck lever on touch) and you throw yourself out: the chest out over the wand, both heels kicked back and up, both skis fired through together on one push of the poles — and the snow under the door falls away in a short, steep START DROP, so you are moving within a second. Your shins open the wand, and **the clock starts at the wand**, not at GO: there is no hurry in the house, but you must be out within **ten seconds** of GO or you are disqualified. That one push is all the working you do in a slalom — no skating, no double pole: from the house on you are on the pitch and skiing it. The lens follows you out of the door and flies onto your own camera. The mountain round the course is a whole ski area — a peak and a lower shoulder over a village on the valley floor, its LIFTS (a gondola to a mid-station on a bench part-way down, chairs on to the peak and the shoulder, a drag over the nursery slopes) running up it, its runs of every colour coming down it and merging, and narrow transport lanes — cat tracks — winding gently across the face between them and to the foot of each lift. A race map races ONE course down it, from a top station to the village: a run, then each run it merges into; a slalom is set on the steepest stretch of that course. Every other run on the mountain is groomed too, its edge poles in its colour, and is yours to ski on a free ride. The course is a groomed, packed line graded down the fall line with long traverses across it, narrowing through necks between the trees and opening out again, rollers on it that are jumps, drifts of fresh snow across it (R17) — slower going on a narrow ski, and where a powder ski earns its keep — and the groomer's windrows along both edges. Off the piste is the mountain: bowls and gullies, the woods below the tree line, and the deep powder. At the foot of the mountain every run comes down into the **HUB** — an open band of groomed snow along the valley floor, the valley's lifts standing in it — and every run can be skied again from it without skiing a harder one on the way back up. Along the hub run two **WIND TUNNELS**, one each way: a lane under a row of glowing arches with a fan at its mouth. Stand into the wind at the fan and it blows you down the lane to the lifts at the far end without skiing a metre (the news column says so going in); ski across one and it leaves you be.
7. **The run.** A slalom's gates are POLE GATES — two thin red or blue flex poles and no panel between them, the next one breathing a little light with a marker turning over its turning pole: OPEN gates across the hill, the turning pole set either side of the line in turn, and the combinations — HAIRPINS and VERTICALS of closed gates, one above the other down the fall line, the feet flicking under a still body, and DELAYED gates further on. The rules are strict: **both feet must pass through every gate**, in order. A gate missed, a pole STRADDLED (one ski either side of it) or a gate skipped is **DISQUALIFIED** at once, and a fall that stops you — or **R** pressed — is **DID NOT FINISH**: nobody is stood back on a slalom course. The poles themselves are no wall: your shins, hands and arms knock them over as you clear them, as a racer does, and they spring back up behind you, the knock costing you a little speed (**POLE DOWN** in the news column). The HUD's place is **RUN 1 / 2**, and at the course's two timing points — the gates nearest a third and two thirds of the way down — the split goes up with your gap to the **LEADER** there, green ahead and red behind. The last gate is the **finish line** under the arch, in the fenced finish arena. A course's arcade gates (a tricks run's, the free ride's) are a pair of poles with a panel between them, red and blue alternating, the next one loud, the rest muted, taken in order. Down a resort's course they are SLALOM GATES, narrower than the piste and set alternately left and right of its line, so the fast line weaves through them: ski past one — outside its poles — and **three seconds** go on your clock (the news column says so, and the finish plate says what the missed gates cost in all), and the run goes on to the next. On a map built before the resorts — one piste down one face — the gates span the piste, and one skied past is owed instead: the HUD warns you at once, with an arrow back up at it, the metres to go and the reset press lit, and the next one is not credited until the missed one is taken. Under the speed, **ALT** reads your height above the sea in metres: the valley floor a few tens of metres up, the summit of a ski area past 1400. Bottom left, to the right of the speed, the **WIND** meter reads the air you are skiing through: a dial turned with you (its notch is ahead) with a bold arrow through it for the wind you FEEL — the weather's wind less your own speed, pointing straight down at you when the air streams back past your face — and a small pale-blue marker on its ring for the weather's TRUE wind where you are (less of it in the woods and down in the valley, more of it up high), standing on the side it blows from and pointing the way it blows; the felt wind's number in km/h is beside it, red past 150, and the weather's under it in pale blue. Into a headwind the two add up, and that is the wind you hear. The round map under the buttons in the top right turns with you, so ahead is always up: the grey line is the piste, the other runs of the resort lie under it in their colours with the lifts as thin lines, and the red bar the gate you owe (a red chevron on the rim points at it when it is off the map).
8. **The finish plate.** A slalom's plate says which run it was — **SLALOM · RUN 1** or **SLALOM · FINAL** — your place and time (on the second run the first run's time, this run's and the **TOTAL**, and the place on combined time) and how far you stand from the leader, over the **BOARD**: every racer by his start number (the bib), his time — on the second run both runs and the total — and his gap to the leader, your row lit and the list opened at it; the racers who went out are under everyone home, billed **DSQ** or **DNF** and where. The field has skied before you — only one racer is ever on the course — so the board is theirs and you race it. On the first run you start last, after the seeded racers; under the board is **SECOND RUN**: the top thirty of the first run start it in REVERSE order, the leader last, on a course set afresh on the same hill — with a field of thirty, everyone who finished — and the final is on combined time. A run that went out — in a slalom, a downhill or a super-G — is no finish: its plate says **DISQUALIFIED** or **DID NOT FINISH**, why under it in plain words (_MISSED GATE 12_, _STRADDLED GATE 7_, _LATE OUT OF THE START_, _FELL AT GATE 20_), and **TRY AGAIN**, which skis the run again (**B** too) — nothing else; out of the first run there is no second. **SKI RUN 1 AGAIN** (or **RUN 2**, the second run's board kept — **B** too), **NEW MOUNTAIN** (back to the level card), or **MAIN MENU**. A downhill's plate is the same race read once: **DOWNHILL · TRAINING** or **DOWNHILL**, your place and time, your gap to the leader, the **SPEED TRAP** (your speed through it, the field's best and your place among them), and the board; over a training run skied home its press is **RACE** — the race on the same course with the same field, the training counting for nothing — and **TRAIN AGAIN** or **RACE AGAIN** (**B** too) skis the same run again. A super-G's plate is the downhill's race plate — **SUPER-G**, your place and time, your gap, the speed trap, the board — and **RACE AGAIN** (**B** too).

**The downhill.** A downhill is set on the map's WHOLE piste — the course the ski area builds with the most vertical, a black — from a start house near the summit to the finish arena on the valley floor (R32): the crests shaved so a jump lands on the downslope, the trees cut back, the piste netted both sides with tall A-NETS, the crowd behind them at the jumps and the finish. Its gates are SPEED GATES — two poles with a red panel slung between them, wide and far apart, set on the racer's line every eighty metres or so and never on a jump's lip — and the strict rules hold as on a slalom: both feet through every gate. You ski a **TRAINING** run first (the field's times a touch slower, the run counting for nothing), then the **RACE**: one run, the place on time. The HUD's split is read at **four intermediates**, and a **SPEED TRAP** on the course's fastest long straight reads your speed through it — a chip on the HUD for a few seconds after. Tuck on the straights and hold the line round the bends: a downhill is won by the speed you carry, and a racer who runs wide into the nets is caught by them and is out (**DNF**) — and driven in at any pace he goes down in them (**INTO THE NETS!**), the mesh billowing round him, and stays there tangled at its foot, his skis as often as not left hanging in it. The skis a downhill is billed on are the **EAGLE**; the others will run it, but the line will not hold round a fast bend on a short slalom ski.

**How hard can you take a hit?** The moment you go down — off a landing, onto the snow, into a tree, or a shoulder into another skier that puts either of you on the snow — the **g** of the blow goes up over the skier in big figures, shaking by how hard it was — a jolt barely trembles, a blow past what a body takes whole rattles the number — At the left edge stands your **body** as an anatomy plate shows it, from the front (your right on the left): every part green while it is sound, yellow with a minor injury, orange with a moderate one and red with a serious one, and over it your **skeleton** — every bone grey, shaded as it lies and whole while it is sound, yellow with a crack opened into it when it has a hairline fracture, and red and broken right through when it breaks, its pieces knocked out of line as a broken bone's are. Under it, the word for the whole of you over what the bones cannot show, in plain words (_TORN RIGHT ACL_, _BRUISED KIDNEY_, _CONCUSSION_), and the **HARDEST** blow you have fallen on. A landing you ski away from, or a shoulder you both stay up through, shows no g. The same fall is gentle in deep powder and hard on the groomer, and ice is harder still; a clean landing hurts nothing, a hard flat one finds the back and the knees, a landing in the back seat finds the knees, a caught edge twists them, a hand put down finds the thumb and the wrist, and a tree finds everything. A reset stands you back up on the piste healed.

**The giant slalom.** A giant slalom is set on the super-G's hill from a start LOWERED down it — the drop to the finish 400 metres at the most (R36) — prepared as a super-G is (the crests shaved round, the trees cut back, nets along both sides) and raced on the giant slalom pair. Its gates are two red or blue panels each, a gate every twenty-odd metres, one side of your line and then the other, so the race is a carved turn after another at sixty to eighty kilometres an hour, with no straight to win a wide line back: pass outside every turning pole — inside one is a gate missed and **DSQ** — and a fall is a **DNF**. Two intermediates split the run against the leader. It is TWO RUNS, as a slalom is: the first against the field's board, then — if you are in the first run's best thirty — the **SECOND RUN** press on the plate stands you in the start house of a second course set afresh on the same hill, the thirty in reverse order, the place on combined time; the plate reads **GIANT SLALOM · RUN 1** or **GIANT SLALOM · FINAL**. A link's `?start=gs` boots straight into its first run.

**The super-G.** A super-G is set on the downhill's hill from a start LOWERED down it — the drop to the finish 600 metres at the most (R33) — prepared as a downhill is (the crests shaved round, the trees cut back, the A-nets along both sides), but its gates TURN you: some forty of them, about fifty metres apart, two red or blue panels each, set to one side of your line and then the other, so the race is long carved turns at speed, with a jump or two where the mountain gives one. There is NO TRAINING run: a super-G is skied unseen, one run, the place on time; read the course off the gates and the minimap. Pass outside every turning pole — inside one is a gate missed and **DSQ**. Three intermediates time you against the leader and a speed trap reads your speed late in the course. Billed on the **EAGLE** (the downhill ski meets the super-G's rule). Its level card is its own nine maps — three reds and six blacks across the alpine, the continental and the maritime, one of them at night under the floodlights.

**Ski cross.** Four racers on the course at once. The course is BUILT in the snow down a gentle stretch of the ski area, fenced, its turns banked into BERMS and its straights carrying ROLLERS, JUMPS and STEP-DOWNS, triangular flags marking it: a single flag on the inside of every berm, a pair before every feature — go round the wrong side of one and you are out. First a QUALIFYING run alone, timed against a board of thirty: the best sixteen go into the HEATS, four abreast out of the START GATE — "skiers ready", "attention", and the doors drop when nobody expects it; hold the tuck (**W**, or the lever full) to pull out of the gate. The FIRST TWO over the line go through: quarter-final, semi-final, then the BIG FINAL for the podium (the semi-finals' third and fourth race the small final). Lean on a rival and he leans back; hit one hard from behind and put him down and the jury shows you the RED CARD. The plate after each heat shows the four in their order, who goes through, and the press on to the next round. Raced on the **Wolverine**, the ski-cross pair.

**Speed skiing.** Straight down the fall line as fast as you can go, and the result is a SPEED. The track is cut straight down the face, 30 m wide with a closed margin either side: a LAUNCH from the start house down the steep, a 100 m TIMING ZONE marked in red, and a long RUN-OUT to the finish enclosure at the bottom. Hold the tuck (**W**, or the lever full) and keep the skis flat and straight — every touch of edge costs speed, and your speed between the zone's two red lines, to the hundredth of a km/h, is your result. Past the zone you stand up into the wind on your own and skid to a stop past the braking line. Two runs: a QUALIFYING run from a start lower down the track, then the best twenty race the FINAL from the top, slowest first and fastest last, ranked on the final alone. The HUD reads your speed where the clock would be, and the board reads in km/h. Billed on the **PEREGRINE**, the speed ski. Its level card is its own nine tracks, from a slow fell track won at 185 km/h to a fast one past 225.

**The record book.** Every finished race is filed under its mountain's seed, the pair and the mode, and a quicker one replaces the row (a tie does not); a slalom's second run, on a course of its own, keeps a row of its own beside the first run's; a downhill's training run is filed nowhere. Where the book has a row for the run, the HUD's split carries a second chip, **VS BEST**: how far ahead (green) or behind (red) the record was at that same gate, and the finish plate says **NEW RECORD**, or the best that stood — its pair and the day it was set — and how far off it you were.

## A tricks run

**TRICKS**, behind the front door's PLAY, opens the **tricks card**: the **PARK RUN** (this section), **BIG AIR**, **SLOPESTYLE**, the **HALFPIPE**, **MOGULS** and **AERIALS** (below). Any of them goes on to the **trick map card**: six maps, each a seed with its **terrain park** laid down the piste (R20) and skied on a day of its own — a clear late-winter noon, a January morning in flurries, an April afternoon, a low December sun, a snow day and a March night under the stars — the box naming the sky, the hour and the date. The park is kickers in three sizes — low, medium and high — at every straight stretch of the piste: the low one a kicker onto snow falling away, the medium and the high ones a lip, a flat deck, and a **landing slope** dug down past it to catch you, so a big jump taken at the right speed comes down on the slope rather than slamming onto the flat. Pick a map (it is remembered) and it opens the ski card; **SKI** stands it up, and after the lights you have two minutes alone on it, the gates counting nothing. The score is the run:

- **The air pays** by the second and by the metre, more the longer you hang.
- **Spins and flips** (on TRICKS and the FREE RIDE): in the air, tap the edge (**A** / **D** / **← →**) and the skier throws **half a turn** — once and he lands with the skis backward and rides away **switch**; twice and it is a **360**. Tap the lean (**↓** / **E** / **Shift** back, **↑** / **Q** / **Z** forward, or **S** / **W** pressed in the air) and he throws a whole loop — a **BACKFLIP** or a **FRONT FLIP**; tap again for a double. A tap the other way takes one back. A throw only goes if there is air left to finish it, so jump first — hold **Space** to load a bigger pop — and tap on the way up. On the FREE RIDE a throw only goes in a flight you meant: off a kicker, or in your own pop — a knee or a roller that throws you at speed is a flight you steer, and a tap of the edge or the lean there is steering, never a spin. Short of a full press, the lean is the usual air control.
- **Riding switch**: going backward, the camera swings round behind the way you are going and left and right still steer the way you press. On the groomer any pair rides switch; in loose snow the tails dig in and throw you — unless you are on the **Hare**, whose twin tips ride over it.
- **Grabs**: hold the trick button (**F** / **X** on a keyboard) in the air: with the edge over, a **DAFFY**; with the lean back, a **SPREAD EAGLE**; otherwise a **MUTE GRAB**. While you hold a grab the lean and the edge move you, not the skis — and let go before you land.
- **The combo**: everything turned in one flight, and in flights linked by landing and taking the next kicker within a second or so, is one combo: its points × its multiplier, over the tips while it is in hand, banked when you have been back on the snow a moment. A second revolution in one flight is worth more than two single ones; a flip and a 360 in one flight is a **TWIST**. Land it soft — met on the landing slope, the legs taking it a little at a time — and the landing is its own element: a **CLEAN LANDING**, or a **PERFECT LANDING** for one that barely touched, worth more the softer it was and, beside a trick in the same flight, a step more of multiplier (two for a perfect one). Land it hard (**SKETCHY**) and you are paid the points without the multiplier; land over the tips, get thrown, reset, or come down still in a grab and the combo is lost.

The HUD shows the **SCORE** and the seconds **LEFT** where a race keeps its gates; the finish plate is the score. The record book keeps no tricks scores.

**Big air.** One huge jump cut into the mountain straight down the face (R37) — a start platform, a long drop-in, a kicker, a 16 m table and a steep landing shaped for the speed a tucked skier carries off the lip — and a CONTEST on it. Each jump is one run: after the starter's count, drop in, stay tucked and straight (a check on the in-run lands you short, on the table), throw your trick off the lip with the same taps as on the park run — the ceilings are raised to a 2160 and a quad — hold a grab if you like, and land it. A panel of six judges scores the jump out of 100: how hard the trick was, how clean the landing, the height, and whether you cleared the table; the highest and lowest marks are dropped and the four left averaged. A fall scores low whatever you threw. The **QUALIFICATION** is two jumps, the better counting; the best twelve of twenty-four go to the **FINAL**, three jumps, the best TWO counting — and those two must be DIFFERENT tricks (spun opposite ways, or a flip where the other spun). The plate names your trick as the judges would call it (**SWITCH LEFT DOUBLE CORK 1260 MUTE**), gives the score and the board, and its first press stands you at the top for the next jump. Picking big air opens the ski card on the **Raven**, the big-air pair, and the dress card on the medium build. A link's `?start=bigair` boots straight into its first jump.

**Slopestyle.** A whole course of features in a row (R38): three **RAIL SECTIONS** — a rail and a box side by side on a gentle deck, two lines to pick between — then three **JUMPS**, each bigger than the last, and the finish. Ride a line onto its rail or box: meet it at its end, going along it, and you are on it, sliding. On it, tap the edge (**←** / **→**) to **SWAP** between a 50-50 and a slide across it, hold the lean forward or back to **PRESS** the nose or the tail, and jump (**Space**) to pop off it early; let it run out and you are turned square and ride away. The course gives you the speed — stood up through the rails, tuck down to each jump — and the air is yours, the same strokes and grabs as the park. The judges mark it **section by section**: two judges on what you did on each of the six sections, three on the whole run's flow and variety (both kinds of jib, both stances, different spins, the grabs), 60/40; a fall ends the run and every section after it scores nothing. A **qualification** of two runs, the best counting, the best twelve of thirty to a **final** of three runs, the best counting. The plate shows each section's mark, the two panels and the board, and the **RUN 2** or **ON TO THE FINAL** press. Picking slopestyle opens the ski card on the **Raven** and the dress card on the medium build. A link's `?start=slopestyle` boots straight into the first run.

**Halfpipe.** A 22-foot pipe cut into the mountain (R39): two walls 6.7 m high and 20 m apart, their tops standing nearly vertical, 170 m long down an 18° pitch. Ride across it at an angle onto one wall, up it and straight out of the top, and come back down onto the SAME wall, then across to the other — five or six **HITS** a run, wall to wall. Tuck on the flat and stand up through the curve of the wall to **pump** and keep your height; in the air spin (**←** / **→**), flip (the lean) and grab as anywhere — a spin turned uphill, toward the top of the pipe, is an **ALLEY-OOP**. Each run is judged as one: the height over the top of the wall, the difficulty, the landings, the variety and how well you used the pipe; a landing on the flat or the deck costs, a fall scores low. Two qualification runs, the best counting, then the best twelve of twenty-five to a final of three. Picking the halfpipe opens the ski card on the **Raven** at the medium build. `?start=halfpipe` boots straight into it.

**Moguls.** A course of moguls down a steep pitch with two small jumps (R40): 235 m at 25°, a 10 m line of moguls three and a half metres apart between nine control gates, and two air bumps. Turn on every mogul down the line — the edge from side to side — and keep your speed to what your legs can take: up to about 35 km/h they swallow the moguls, much faster and the moguls throw you. Off each air bump throw a trick (a spin, a flip, a grab), a DIFFERENT one on each. One run is scored out of 100, the way the rules score it: the TURNS out of 60 by five judges (the carve, the legs, the upper body; a stop costs six), the AIR out of 20 (each jump judged and times its degree of difficulty) and the SPEED out of 20 off your time against the pace. A qualification, then FINAL 1 for the best sixteen and FINAL 2 for the best six, nothing carried over. Picking moguls opens the ski card on the **Ibex**, the mogul ski, at the medium build. `?start=moguls` boots straight into it.

**Aerials.** One jump off a steep kicker, DECLARED before you go (R41): pick it on the trick map card's JUMP row — its code (`bLF`, a back layout then a full) and its degree of difficulty — and the site is built with the kicker it needs: a single for one flip, a double for two, a triple for three. The in-run is tucked for you to the kicker's foot. In the air, a tap of the lean back is a flip and a tap of the edge a full twist, turned inside the flip you are in; hold the trick key to tuck a tuck flip, and let it go before the snow. Throw the flips and the twists the jump declares — the wrong number of either is a DID NOT FINISH — and land 2–4 m past the knoll. Five judges score the AIR (0–2), the FORM (0–5) and the LANDING (0–3); the high and the low of each are dropped, the rest summed and multiplied by the DD. A qualification, then a final of twelve and a final of six, one jump each and nothing carried over; the two finals' jumps must differ. Picking aerials opens the ski card on the **Kestrel** — the aerials ski, short, narrow and the lightest pair — at the light build. `?start=aerials` boots straight into it (`&plan=bLFF` for another jump).

## A free ride

**FREE RIDE**, behind the front door's PLAY, is the whole mountain: no lights, no gate owed, no clock to beat — the bowls, the kickers out on the face, the deep powder — and the ski area full of people. A few hundred AMATEURS ski its runs and ride its lifts — queueing in the corral at a lift's foot, sat four to a chair, towed up in pairs behind a T-bar, so the lifts are full of people — the greens busiest and the blacks nearly empty: families with the children in a wedge behind a parent, ski schools snaking down in the instructor's track in matching bibs, friends who stop together in the middle of the run, the beginner who traverses wall to wall, the one who stops just below the crest, the bomber straight down the fall line, the powder hound off into the trees, the park rat at every kicker, and the lot coming down from the hut after a long lunch. They fall, they get up, they get in your way: shoulder one and the news column says so; hit one hard and you both go down. And off the snow the ski area has its PEOPLE ON FOOT: the lift crew sweeping the load line, the patrol at the tops, instructors gathering their classes, guests clumping about in ski boots with their skis on a shoulder, a lunch crowd at the lodge's terrace tables, deck chairs in the sun, mugs of cocoa, children in a snowball fight or round a snowman, and the afterski dancing from mid-afternoon into the night. It opens the **start card** first:

- **MOUNTAIN** — the seed, typed or stepped, and **ANOTHER MOUNTAIN** to step to the next of the free ride's own mountains — a fixed list, the same for every skier, so the card opens on the same first mountain for everyone (the one the front door stands on) and the next one is built in the background while you look at this one; a seed typed or stepped is a mountain of your own. A chart you have seen before is kept, and shows at once. Beside it the mountain itself, summit at the top and every run falling down the picture, in two views a chip in the corner turns between. The **PANORAMA** paints the mountain from out over the valley the way a ski area's piste map hangs — the lit snow and its blue shadows, the woods with every run's cut through them, the rock, the village — edge to edge in the plate under a clear blue sky with a few clouds, the summit ridge its skyline, with the whole ski area laid over it: every run in its colour with its number in its grade's sign (the pistes numbered 1 on up without a gap, the same number the sign at the run's head reads; a cat track a thin dashed line), the course the map is raced on drawn widest, the stretches the view cannot see dotted, the lifts as dark lines between their stations, the finish (a black square), the kickers and — where you will start — a dot in your run's colour inside a white ring, with rings pulsing out of it so it is the first thing you see (on the snowmobile or the helicopter, down at the bottom where the machine waits). The **PLAN** is the same mountain from above: the face shaded, the woods, the groomed piste in orange from the start line to the finish, the same pulsing start mark and every **kicker** as an arrow the way it throws — red on the piste, violet out on the mountain, which is what a free skier goes looking for. Under it the run's grade, its length and vertical, and the mountain's kicker count. **Tap either view to start anywhere on it** (a spot in a trunk is moved onto the nearest point of the piste) — the pulse moves to the spot; **FROM THE LIFT** takes that back.
- **RUN** — which run you ski, by the number its sign and the piste map carry. Picking a **GRADE** brings this row to the first run of that colour on the mountain, and the arrows step through the others of that colour (every run, on AS DEALT or where the mountain has none of the colour). The run is kept for this mountain and country only. Before them all is **PARAMOTOR**: you start on the summit, skis on your feet, a small fast speed wing held up over you on its lines and a motor with its propeller in a round cage on your back. Hold the tuck (the throttle) and ski off down the slope: once you are going fast enough the wing flies, and a couple of seconds later your skis leave the snow. In the air the same keys fly it — the tuck the throttle (full power climbs, idle glides at a speed wing's 50–60 km/h), the skid both brakes (pull them to flare for a landing; held down too long the wing stalls and dives as you let them up), the edge the toggles and your weight in the harness, the lean the risers (forward faster, back slower). Touch down and you are speed riding, skiing with the wing still over you; press **Enter** (or double tap either thumb) to drop the whole rig, wing and motor, and ski on without it. Fly the wing into the snow or a tree and it collapses and is cut away. **R** starts you on the summit again. The HUD shows the height over the snow, the climb, the air through the wing and the throttle. [The paramotor](paramotor.md) explains it all. After it is **BALLOON**: you start standing in the wicker basket of a hot air balloon on the valley floor, the envelope inflated over you and held on its tether. Hold the tuck to fire the burner — it roars, the envelope's air heats, and some seconds later the balloon is light and the tether lets go. There is no steering: the day's wind blows UP the valley toward the summit, faster the higher you go and turning a little with height, so you choose where you go by how high you fly. A burn takes tens of seconds to become a climb, and without heat the envelope cools and the balloon sinks; the skid pulls the parachute valve in the crown to dump heat and come down faster. Let it sink too fast into the snow, or drift it into the rising slope or a tree, and the basket goes over and throws you out. Burning in a strong wind can push the envelope's mouth over the flame and set it ALIGHT — and so can holding the burner until the fabric cooks — and a burning balloon falls. The edge and the lean walk you about the basket (on touch the LEFT thumb is a walking pad, and the right has three presses: **BURN** held, **VENT** held and **JUMP**). The HUD shows the height over the snow and over the sea, the climb, the envelope's temperature against its red line at 120 °C, the propane left and the wind that carries you, with a call when the moment asks for one (BURN, ENVELOPE HOT, SINKING — BURN!, FIRE — JUMP!, LANDED — STEP OUT). The TIPS camera is your own eyes leant out over the rim looking down at the snow below, HELMET your eyes stood in the basket, and the other cameras stand back far enough to hold the whole balloon. **Enter** (or a double tap) takes you over the side wherever you are — the only way out in the air — and the balloon flies on without you; stood still on the snow, it steps you out. **R** starts you in the basket on the valley floor again. [The hot air balloon](hot-air-balloon.md) explains it all. Its **second-last stop is SNOWMOBILE**: you start stood on the running boards of a mountain sled parked at the bottom, your skis and poles in its rack, the engine running, and ride it anywhere on the mountain on your own keys — the tuck is the throttle, the skid the brake, the edge the bars and the lean your weight (on touch the lever is the throttle and the brake, the edge thumb the bars). It floats and climbs in deep powder throwing a roost behind it, lies over onto its side to carve a powder turn, and cuts its tracks into the snow. Press **Enter** (or double tap either thumb) to step off onto your skis; the sled stays where you left it, and stopping beside it and pressing **Enter** again takes it back. It rides out bumps, jumps and drops onto snow; roll it, loop it, land it on its side or its nose, drop it off a cliff, ride it into a rock wall or hit a tree and you are thrown; stood back up, you are back on it. On any free ride it waits at the bottom beside the village, the HUD calling you to it as you come near: stop beside it and press **Enter** to take it. [The snowmobile](snowmobile.md) explains it all. Its **last stop is HELICOPTER**: you start sat on the right skid of the helicopter on its pad on the valley floor, the rotor turning, and fly it yourself, BY HAND, nothing assisting — ↑ ↓ (or Shift Z) the COLLECTIVE, a lever that stays where you leave it; W S A D the CYCLIC, which tilts the rotor disc and leaves it tilted; Q E (or ← →) the PEDALS, needed every time the collective moves the nose (on touch the left thumb is a cyclic pad — up tilts it forward, down back, across banks it — and the right a power pad — up and down the collective, across the pedals; the keys are rebindable under OPTIONS ▸ KEYS ▸ HELICOPTER) — anywhere on the mountain and as high as you like. The HUD shows the **DROP** under the skids, the climb, a horizon and the collective. Land it on snow flat enough and slowly enough and **Enter** steps you off; where it cannot land, **Enter** (or a double tap on either thumb) pushes you off the skid with the helicopter's speed, and the pilot flies it home. Fly it into the snow, a tree or a slope too steep to land on and it goes up in flames, throwing you off; a few seconds later you are back on the pad. On any free ride it waits on its pad, lit up as you come near: stop beside its right skid and press **Enter** to climb on. [The helicopter](helicopter.md) explains it all. After it, on a mountain with an afterski lodge in the valley, is **AFTERSKI**: you start INSIDE the lodge with the party under way, your skis in the rack outside. The beers come round on their own (**Space**, the jump, orders another round sooner), each one raising your **BUZZ**; press **Enter** (or tap the readout) to head out, and you are stood at the door on your skis to ski the mountain with whatever buzz you drank — late, wobbly hands and a fall that leaves you to get up and fetch your skis yourself.
- **GRADE** — the colour of the run, graded as a piste is by its steepest hundred metres: the mountain's own (**AS DEALT**), or a **GREEN** (gentle and wide, all of it under 16 %), a **BLUE** (under 27 %), a **RED** (under 47 %) or a **BLACK** (steep off the start hut, pitches past 70 %, cliff bands across the piste to drop off, cliffs beside it, the most kickers). The line under the chart carries the sign of the colour that came out, and the piste's edge poles are painted in it.
- **COUNTRY** — what kind of snow country the mountain is raised in, on the same seed: the **ALPINE** (every map's own: steep, the woods low down and the bowls above the tree line, wind crust on the crests and rock breaking through the steep faces), the **FELL** (low rounded country in the far north, half the vertical, birch at a low tree line, a wind crust carved into sastrugi), the **CONTINENTAL** (a tall dry range, cold light snow, the tree line high) or the **MARITIME** (deep heavy snow, rime on the trees). Each is graded in its own light over the whole picture. The chart follows the row.
- **SEASON** — **EARLY WINTER** (mid-December, the lowest sun and the shortest day), **MIDWINTER**, **LATE WINTER** or **SPRING** (April, a high sun and long days): how high the sun climbs and how long the shadows lie. It stands on **AS DEALT**, the mountain's own date, until moved.
- **TIME** — **MORNING**, **DAY**, **EVENING** or **NIGHT**, worked out on that date at that mountain's latitude — a morning in December is late and short, one in April early. Night skis under the moon and the stars, the runs lit by the floodlight masts that stand down every one of them (the woods between them left dark), the finish arena by its floodlights and every skier by the headlamp on his helmet — a wide pool of light that turns with his head. It stands on **AS DEALT**, the mountain's own hour, until moved. The sun stays where it is for the whole run.
- **WEATHER** — the mountain's own sky, or clear, fair, flurries (a few flakes out of a sunny sky), high cloud, overcast (flat light, the rollers hard to read), a steady snowfall, a storm under black cloud (a few tens of metres to see by) or a valley fog. Where it snows, new snow builds up as you ski — a storm lays a hand's depth in an hour, burying the groomed piste and deepening the powder.
- **SNOW** — how deep the powder lies off the piste: **THIN** (20 cm), **MEDIUM** (40 cm — the snow every race is skied on), **THICK** (70 cm) or **VERY DEEP** (a metre). Past MEDIUM it is bottomless, and skiing it is a skill of its own: stop and you sink to the knees, so keep the speed up and lean back to float the tips (a wide pair floats where a narrow one bogs).

**AFTERSKI.** Every free ride's mountain has an afterski lodge on the valley floor by the village, and most have a second part way down: a long log hall with a terrace of tables and parasols, string lights and racks of skis below it. The HUD calls it as you come near; stop at the foot of its steps between the racks and press **Enter** (or double tap, or tap the call) to go in. Inside you dance and drink: a beer comes every quarter of a minute or so, and **Space** (the jump) orders the next one sooner. **Enter** again takes you back out, skis on. Each beer raises your **BUZZ** — the glass on the right of the HUD — and it wears off over ten minutes. Buzzed, you ski worse: the skis answer late, turn harder or softer than you asked, drift off a straight line, and you fall far more easily. Through your own eyes (the TIPS and HELMET cameras) the whole picture sways, swims and doubles. And a buzzed fall is not reset: you get up where you landed and walk to your skis yourself — steer to walk, or leave him to stagger there on his own — pick each one up, step back in and ski on. **R** still stands you back on the piste.

**IN TOWN.** Ski onto a street of the village — its streets, the square, the car park — and you stop on your edges, press each heel piece open with your pole and step out, pick the pair up, clap the bases together and swing it up onto your right shoulder, tips forward, poles in your left hand. Then you walk: **W** (the tuck) walks you on, **A D / ← →** turn you, **S** stands you still, and the HUD reads SKIS ON THE SHOULDER. Walk off the streets onto the snow and the pair comes down off your shoulder, is laid on the snow across the hill, and you step back in, one heel snapped shut after the other, and ski on. **R** stands you back on the piste as ever.

**NEXT** is the ski card, and **SKI** there stands the run up. **A spot tapped on the chart is where you start**, stood on the snow there. With no spot picked (or after **FROM THE START GATE**), you come onto the mountain **BY LIFT**, for its last eight seconds, up the lift serving the **RUN** you picked — a drag, the gondola or a chair, whichever this mountain hangs it off — and ski off its top down to that run yourself. Picking another **GRADE** or **COUNTRY** builds another mountain, so it clears the spot and the run. On a chair you ride the last of the rope over the tall last tower and down onto the top station's rail, slowing into it: the terminal under its hood, the operator in the glass booth beside the unload, the wind mast. At the unload point you are stood up onto the ramp, which falls away ahead of you, and slide on down it into the lane past the station house, the lens close behind you — and from there the skis are yours: nothing steers you off a top. The chair you got off runs on behind you over the ramp to the wheel: stop in its way and it sweeps you off your feet. Straight ahead past the end of the lane stands the **PISTE MAP** board, the mountain painted as the start card paints it with **YOU ARE HERE** at the top you are on. Every run off a chair's or a gondola's top starts BELOW it, and a wide groomed RAMP falls from the edge of the pad down onto it — never a climb, and no steeper than a green or a blue for those colours; beside the PISTE MAP board, turned to you as you come off the lift, stand the runs' signs — those whose ramps leave to your left on the board's left, those to your right on its right, each arrow pointing at its ramp — and where the ramp meets its run, a few metres down the slope past the lip, the run's own sign names it again as you come onto it. Follow your run's sign and you slide down its ramp onto it — the face falling away under a lens held level, so the drop off the top reads as the drop it is. (The nursery drag's top has a ramp only where one fits.) **R** on the chair puts you at the top at once. And the lifts run all the time, on every map: ski into a lift's **load zone** slowly, facing up its line, and it takes you up — a **CHAIR** from the blue load line at the head of its roped corral (it scoops you from behind and sits you down; at the top it stands you on the ramp), the **GONDOLA** through the door at the back of its station house (out on the platform beside the wheel your cabin comes round to you at a crawl with its doors open; you rack your skis on its door, step in and sit down, the doors shut and it leaves the station — and at the top you walk out onto its pad, facing down the mountain), a **DRAG** from the head of its track (your own T-bar behind your thighs, pulled up the track on your skis and let go short of the top). Or, easier: every lift's foot has a **BOARDING RING** — a glowing amber circle on the snow just past the open end of its roped corral, where the queue starts, with a column of light standing up out of it so you can find it from up the hill. Ride into the ring at any speed short of a schuss, facing any way, and you skate up the corral beside the queue — shouldering aside anyone in your way — to the load line — turned round first, if you came in facing away, with a stop and a few steps of your skis — where a chair takes you behind a short fade to black, sat on it, and the gondola's door takes you through to its platform to step into your cabin; at a drag you stand on the track and wait for a T-bar to come round, and sit back onto it as it passes. The news column says so as it takes you. Carried, **look round**: drag a finger anywhere on the glass on touch, or hold the mouse button and drag on a desktop, and the lens swings about you — back down the valley, down onto the run under the chair, up the rope to the top; it swings home behind you once the lift lets you go. On touch the thumbs' pads are not drawn while a lift carries you, so a finger on the glass only looks. Once you are carried, **ENTER** (a double tap on touch) lets go of the lift — off the chair, out of the cabin, off the bar — and **holding TUCK** (W, or the tuck lever) for three seconds skips you to the top behind a fade. A free ride is leisure, not a contest, so the HUD carries no run figures at the top left — no clock, no place, no gate count — only the mountain's **SEED** (so any picture of a free ride says which mountain it is); it keeps the speed, the edge bar, the air clock (a jump timed once it has lasted half a second) and the minimap, which shows the piste without a gate on it, every other run and every lift round it (your best air and the distance skied are billed on the pause card). No gate is owed, and none is set: the course's red and blue gates are left off the mountain, while the start, the finish, the edge poles, the signs and the lights stay. the ride remembers every run you have had your skis on, and **R** (the HUD's reset) stands you back on the nearest of THOSE — a piste before a transport lane — never on a run you have not been near. **B** (and the pause card's **RESTART**) takes you to the top of the slope again: stood at the head of the last piste you skied, facing down it. Everything on the start card is remembered.

**Something in the trees.** About one free ride in three, the **GRIMBEAR** is out there too — a bear's bulk on a man's stride. A while into the ride he waits behind a trunk beside the run ahead of you, where you cannot see him, and breaks out of the trees as you come, running you down whatever your speed. Most of the time — four runs in five — he misses: he dives at you, rakes the air a stride beside you and stumbles on past, roaring, and he will try again on a later sighting. When one lands he takes you, once: you go down, he stands over you and then walks back into the woods, and you are stood back up at the top of the slope. After that he is still out there, and the next time he comes out of the trees he only runs after you, roaring, and pulls up short. A restart keeps him on the mountain, and keeps the catch he has had. `?start=free&grimbear=1` sends him after you on a link (`0` keeps him away).

**Doors.** On a free ride every building's door opens: stop in front of one, facing it, and the HUD calls **DOOR** — press **Enter** (or tap the call) and you shuffle to the handle, press the lever, push the door open (or draw it toward you and step back round it) and step through. Its closer swings it shut behind you; from inside it works the same way out, and a press while a door stands open shuts it. A lodge's door taken this way brings you into the afterski.

**The night shift.** On a free ride after dark the ski area's **PISTE MACHINES** are out: up to two big tracked groomers working their runs lane by lane, lamps blazing and an amber beacon turning, fresh corduroy behind them and the day's skied-up piste ahead. Their corduroy is firm under your skis even while new snow falls on the rest. Stop beside a cab and press **Enter** (a double tap on touch) to climb in and drive it: **W** forward, **S** in reverse, **A** and **D** (or the arrows) to pivot it on its tracks, laying corduroy wherever it goes; **Enter** again climbs you out. Ski into one and you are knocked flat, and the body plate shows what twelve tonnes of steel did. `?start=free&groomer=1` puts them out whatever the hour (`0` keeps them in).

At any point, **Escape** (or a press on the minimap in the top right) holds the run under the pause card. Its head names the mountain — on a slalom or a downhill a second line, **FREE RIDE IT · SEED n · COUNTRY · GRADE**, the three answers the free ride's start card raises the same mountain with, so a map you liked can be skied end to end. Under its head a strip bills the run so far — your place and the clock, then the record the run is skied against, your best air, the distance skied, the gates taken and the vertical dropped, up to four figures — and under that: **RESUME** (back to the frame you left), **OPTIONS** (a panel with the camera and the sound: the switch and the three faders, applied to the held frame at once) with **PISTE MAP** beside it on a free ride or a race — on a free ride a panel with the start card's chart and **RUN** row for the mountain you are on: step to another run (or tap the chart to start anywhere) and **RIDE** takes you there, by the lift up to its head or on the machine you picked, the day and the snow as they were; on a race it leaves the run for the race's level card, to pick another of its maps — and along the card's foot, in one quiet strip, the three presses that end the run: **RESTART**, **REPLAY** and **MAIN MENU**. The pause card is the only surface that stops the snow; behind every other card the run keeps going.

**The replay.** Every run you ride — every race, a tricks run, a free ride — is recorded as the controls that skied it, so it can be watched back at any moment from where it started (the last eight minutes of a long free ride). **WATCH REPLAY** on the pause card or the finish plate plays the run so far from its start; **V** mid-run plays the last stretch; and after a wipeout, an injury or a death a **WATCH THAT AGAIN** press comes up for a few seconds (V, or a tap on it) that opens a few seconds before the fall. The same run is rebuilt and ridden again — the tracks cut again from clean snow — and the run itself waits, untouched, until you go **BACK** to it (or to the pause card you came from). The bar at the foot of the screen is a video player's: a scrubber the width of the bar with the run's moments marked on it (a flight, a trunk met, a pass, a wipeout, the flag, and a diamond on the crash it was opened for), dragged and let go to jump there; from the start, back five seconds, play and pause, forward five seconds, and the speed (¼×, ½×, 1×, 2×). On the keys: **Space** or **K** plays and pauses, **← →** (or J L) skip five seconds, **, .** step a frame, **↑ ↓** change the speed, **Home** goes back to the start, **C** walks the cameras and **Escape** goes back. While it plays untouched the bar steps out of the picture and leaves a hairline of progress; any move of the pointer brings it back. It opens on the **BROADCAST** camera, which is the chase camera until the run reaches a moment it marked; for those it cuts to a lens planted at the edge of the wood beside the kicker, the arch or the spot, before the skier gets there, and runs the moment in slow motion (the bar says **SLOW**).

## Skiing

The whole game is two grounds under one pair of skis. On the **groomed piste** the edges bite, a carve holds and the skier is quick and sure. In **powder** the skis sink: at walking pace you wallow to the knees, and as speed builds the tips float up onto the top of the snow and you plane — keep the speed up and you float; stop in it and you dig in. Cutting a corner through the powder is a decision with a price. The drive is gravity: the steeper the pitch, the faster you go, and a **tuck** takes the wind off you.

- **Turning.** On the piste the **edge** turns you: tip the skis over and the sidecut bends into the snow and carves an arc — more edge, a tighter arc, until the edge lets go and skids. In powder the skis turn on their bases and the skier's weight does the work.
- **The back key means two things, by the order you press it.** Press it FIRST and it is the **brake**: alone a snowplough, and an edge put on after it swings the skis across the way into a **hockey stop** — the skis turned across the slope, the stop. Press it with an edge ALREADY ON and it **cuts the edge harder**: the skis stood further over and pressed into the groove, a tighter arc that costs you almost nothing — the slalom racer's turn. On the phone, drag the edge thumb DOWN for the back key (in the air it is still the lean back).
- **The tuck.** Fold out of the wind and the speed builds; stand up and it bleeds off.
- **The wind.** The weather's wind blows on you as well as past you — and never up the mountain: it comes across the slope or down it, from any way in that half, so it is never a gale in your face down the fall line. Into a headwind (faced up or across the slope) your top speed drops by about the wind's own speed, a tailwind carries you faster, and on a traverse into a storm you skate rather than glide. A crosswind pushes you off your line — your edges hold most of it and you lean into it — and it tightens a turn it blows into and widens one it blows out of. The woods take most of it away; the open ridges and the summit get it all. Stand still with a storm at your back and it will blow you along.
- **Working for speed.** Below about 40 km/h on a straight the skier works on his own, to gain speed and to keep it: off a standstill he **double-poles** out like a racer leaving the gate (up a rise he **strides** instead — the skis parallel, each leg kicking back in turn, the opposite arm planting its pole), rolling he **skates** (the skis in a V, pushing off each leg's inside edge in turn with both poles planted on every push), and faster he **double-poles** again (both poles planted ahead and the body folded down over them). Under the start lights he waits in the gate crouched over poles planted ahead of his boots, and GO sends him out on them. Not in a slalom: there the one push out of the start house is all the working he does, and he skis the rest. It is a man's push, strong off a standstill and fading as he gathers speed; braking or loading a jump stops it. Stood still with nothing pressed, he stays put — hold the tuck to set off.
- **The jump.** Hold **SPACE** and the skier sinks onto his legs, the lower the longer; let go and he springs — the longer it was loaded, the higher, up to two seconds' worth. Pop a roller's crest and you fly further.
- **Landings.** Every landing is a load on the legs: the speed into the slope as a fall from rest, stopped over the legs and whatever loose snow is under you. You ski like a professional: anything you come down on your skis for — crooked, rolled onto your edges, slapped down tips-first — you ride away, and the skier shows the save (the arms flung out, the shoulder knocked back off a tree, a hand down to the snow, the wobble of an edge that bit). You go down only when the body cannot stay up: landing on your side, your back or your head, the tips spearing the snow, a trunk square in front of your skis, an edge caught sliding sideways, or a load past what legs can hold (**CRASH LANDING**). Deep powder is a long, soft landing; the groomer and the ice are hard. A big landing ridden away shows its load in g.
- **Kickers.** Rollers on the piste (and wind lips on the crests off it) are shaped to throw you. In the air the **lean** pitches you, tips up or down; land on the downslope and you keep your speed; land flat past it and the legs bottom and cost you.
- **Trees** are solid. A clipped trunk spins you; one met square stops you — and met hard, it stops the skis and not you.
- **Wipeouts.** Hit a trunk hard, land over the tips, land a big air crooked (or flat, past what the legs take), fall at speed or catch an edge (an edge stood too far over for the speed — the high-side) and the skier comes off — throwing his hands out to meet the snow, going over the way that fall goes (on over a trunk or the tips, over onto his side off a caught edge, back and down off a landing his legs could not hold) and sliding to rest with the skis going on without him — and a couple of seconds later you are stood back on the piste at the last gate you took (in a slalom the run is over instead: **DID NOT FINISH**). The reset press lights up the moment you are thrown: press it (or R) to be back on your skis at once.
- **Bogged.** Stop in deep powder and you sink to the knees (**BOGGED** on the screen). **Pole out**: hold the tuck for the poles and **rock** — the lean back and forth and the edge side to side — and you climb out.
- **Stuck or down?** **R** stands you back on the piste just past the last gate you took — except in a race — a slalom, a super-G or a downhill — where a racer stopped is out of it (**DID NOT FINISH**). Thrown off your skis, the first three seconds are the fall's — watch it, and read the body's plate on the left — and after them **R**, **W** or a tap anywhere gets you up (the HUD says so); left alone, you are stood up at six seconds. Going nowhere, it happens on its own after a few seconds (longer once you are bogged, to give you the time to pole out).

## Controls

**Keyboard** (`pwa/src/game/settings-input.ts` is the table):

| Key                | Does                                                                                      |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| W                  | Tuck — sets off from a standstill, throws you out of a start house; in the air, lean fwd  |
| S                  | First: brake (then A / D: hockey stop). After an edge: cut harder — in the air, lean back |
| Space              | Hold to load a jump, let go to spring (higher the longer, to 2 s)                         |
| A D / ← →          | Edge left / right; stood still, step the skis round on the spot — or, stood across a steep slope, toward the hill: sidestep up it |
| ↓ / E / Shift      | Lean back — in the air, tips up                                                           |
| ↑ / Q / Z          | Lean forward — in the air, tips down                                                      |
| F / X              | Hold in the air on a tricks run: a grab                                                   |
| R                  | Back onto the piste at the last gate taken (a race: out of the run, DNF; a free ride: the nearest run you have skied, or where you left the runs if you are off them) |
| B                  | Restart the run from the start line (a race: from the start house; a free ride: the top of the last piste you skied) |
| C                  | Next camera                                                                               |
| H                  | HUD on / off                                                                              |
| Enter              | On a free ride: get on the snowmobile or the helicopter you stand beside, or off again; into an afterski lodge at its door, and out; under the paramotor, drop the rig; in the balloon, jump over the side (or, stood on the snow, step out) |
| V                  | Watch a replay: the crash just taken, or the last stretch of the run                      |
| P                  | Take a picture (filed in the GALLERY)                                                     |
| Escape             | Pause                                                                                     |
| Arrows, Enter, Esc | Walk a card, press a row, go back                                                         |

W and S lean only when they go down while the skier is IN THE AIR (a tuck held off the lip stays a tuck), S leaning does not brake, and a lean key held over them wins.

**Touch:** the lower-left of the screen is the **edge control** — touch anywhere there and move the thumb: sideways travel tips the skis onto their edge, vertical travel leans — and on the snow a drag DOWN is the back key (down first, then across: the hockey stop; across first, then down: the edge cut harder). The dashed ring is the control's reach: a thumb swept past it drags the control along, so swinging back is never further than one throw. On a **slalom** the ring is narrower across, so full edge one way to full edge the other is a short flick from gate to gate. The lower-right is the **tuck lever** — the skier is IN A FULL TUCK the moment your thumb lands; slide UP to stand him up, and further up to brake. **Tap it and put the thumb straight back down** to load a jump (the knob rings and swells), and lift to spring. The top-right corner carries the minimap — press it to pause — and two presses under it: reset and camera. **After dark the HUD dips** with the floodlights, as a dashboard does with its headlamps: the white goes to a soft grey-blue, the plates and the minimap's ground go nearly black (the runs and the lifts left light on it, as a night chart draws its roads), and only the warnings, the flag's red and the gap's green keep their full light.

**Cameras** (C, or the camera press): **tips** (a lens low over the ski tips) and **helmet** (the skier's own eyes) are worn and pitch and roll with him; **chase** (the default), **far** and **high** stand behind on a boom. The game remembers the one you chose.

## Options

**OPTIONS** — the front door's second slab, or a `?menu=options` link — over the same live run, in four groups. **CONTROLS**: **KEYS** opens a page with every action on it — press a row, then the key to put on it (Escape leaves it as it was); a key on two actions says so. On a touchscreen, **TUCK SIDE** swaps the lever and the edge control, **TRAVEL** shortens or lengthens every thumb's throw, and **INVERT LEAN** makes pushing the edge control away the lean back. **ASSIST**: **EDGE HOLD** (the arcade's hand holding the body on the arc the edges ask for and catching a skid) and **AIR BALANCE** (the skier's body keeping the skis level side to side in the air), each FULL, HALF or OFF, and **DAMAGE** (on, a trunk or a hard landing dulls an edge or hurts a knee and you ski it for the rest of the run, with an instrument beside the speed; off by default), all from the next run. **SOUND**: the switch, and faders for everything, the wind (the skier's own noise: the wind in the helmet and the edges on the snow) and the effects. **PICTURE**: the **HUD** switch (H mid-run), the **INJURIES** switch (the body plate at the HUD's left edge and the g meter over the skier — and the gore: limbs torn off, bones out through the skin, run through on a spike, the blood and the red snow, the X-RAY CAM — a fall he will die of seen coming a second before the hit, the run slowed almost to a stop, the lens close on the part hit through a skin dissolving to glass over that second's last half as the bone cracks and the blood pools inside him, panning to every other big bone that goes and every limb torn, then back out to the game's own camera as the skin grows back solid; a body a machine takes apart — under a piste machine, into a helicopter's rotor, blown off its skid — is shot from outside instead, slowed further, the rotor's blades seen one by one as they go through him — and DEATH, the HUD falling apart, DIED, the picture dark and the run started again from the top; off for a younger player — the crashes stay, the injuries go, and the run is SAFE FOR WORK besides: nobody collides with another skier or anyone on foot, the afterski lodges are shut, the helicopter flies on a steadying hand (the stick a speed, the lever a climb with its middle holding the height) and cannot crash, and the hot air balloon never catches fire or crashes; in the store app on an iPhone or iPad with the phone's content restrictions on it starts off, and with Communication Safety on a child's account it is locked off), a **PRESET** that moves every row at once, then RESOLUTION, DISTANCE, TERRAIN, TRAILS, FOREST, SHADOWS, SPRAY, LAMPS and SMOOTH EDGES one at a time (RESOLUTION is the share of the screen's own pixels drawn, in tenths from 50% to 100%, so a phone short of its frame gives up a step rather than a third; LAMPS is how many lamps light the snow at once after dark — six on HIGH, four on MEDIUM, two on LOW, your own headlamp always one of them and the nearest of the rest after it — and LOW also lets the snow in the beams go without its glitter; DISTANCE has a fourth stop, **MAX**: LOW, MEDIUM and HIGH each close a mist over the mountain at their own reach — LOW only a few seconds ahead — and MAX draws the whole mountain to the summit ridge, clear; FOREST also sets how many variants of each kind of tree are drawn — all ten on HIGH, five on MEDIUM, two on LOW, never fewer kinds — and how many forms of each bird and animal (a hen beside the cock, a calf beside the cow: all of them on HIGH, two on MEDIUM, one on LOW, never fewer species) and how near one has to be to be drawn at its finer cut; SHADOWS' first stop, **SKIERS**, casts the field's shadows alone, and every stop but OFF lays the mountain's own shade over the slopes behind its ridges) — each applied at once, so it is judged against the run behind the card (SMOOTH EDGES alone waits for the next visit). PRESET's first stop, **AUTO** (a fresh visit's), times the machine under the front door and sets every row for sixty frames a second, keeping what shows most (`docs/configuration.md`); moving any row makes the picture yours. The caption at the foot says what the row under the pointer does.

## What the game remembers

The camera, the pair, the sound switch and every OPTIONS row (`docs/configuration.md`).

## From a checkout

```sh
npm install
npm run dev          # the game, hot-reloading; ?seed=38 on the URL for a given mountain
make build           # typecheck + production build into pwa/dist/
make test            # the suite
make sim             # the bot skis generated mountains headlessly: the balance table
make level SEED=38   # one mountain from above, every gate numbered
make resort SEED=7   # the whole ski area a seed builds: the runs, the lifts, the courses
make ride            # the skier on the snow in profile, one staged scenario at a time
```

The README's Usage table lists every target; [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) says which one a change owes. The browser-driven labs (`make screenshots`, `make profile`, `make world`, `make audition ARGS=--meter`) need `npm i --no-save playwright-core` and a Chromium (`CHROMIUM_PATH`).

The desktop app (`make tauri`) and the store app (`make native-*`) wrap the same built site — [platforms.md](platforms.md).
