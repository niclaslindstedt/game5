The **SNOW A TURN THROWS** looks like real spray now, off skis and off a snowboard alike: the snow is cut by an edge sliding across its own line, so a hockey stop or a skidded turn throws a fan of streaking grains ahead of the rider and down the hill — and a board's whole long edge throws it as a pair's two do — while a clean carve flicks only a thin tail. The fine mist rises off the same edge and flies the same way, in smaller, lower puffs on the groomer than in powder. The spray is the snow's own: new powder is thrown fine and light with a big cloud, older snow coarser and clumpier, a wind slab or a sun crust breaks into glinting plates, surface hoar sparkles, wet spring snow is thrown short and heavy with no cloud at all, and the deeper the snow lies the more of it flies, and higher. Every grain flies at the fall speed of its kind, so the fines hang while the chips arc out and clumps break where they land.
