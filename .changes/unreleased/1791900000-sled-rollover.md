title: The snowmobile no longer rolls over in an ordinary turn
---

The snowmobile tips over the way a real mountain sled does. Turning on the flat no longer rolls it, whether the bars are held over, flicked from one lock to the other or linked, on the groomer or in powder of any depth. Before, a turn in the deepest snow put it on its side within a second. A flick of the bars now rolls it from one edge to the other at a rider's pace instead of throwing it off the next crest mid-roll. A landing while sliding sideways slides it instead of tripping it over. Snow depth now decides how far it can lean: on firm snow its skis' stance holds it to about 35° off upright, while in deep powder the low ski sinks and it stands on its narrow belt. So a sled that stalls across a steep face of bottomless powder still goes over. When it does go over, the rider falls off its low side as it rolls instead of being flung over the bars, and in deep powder he is no longer shot out of the snow at several times the machine's speed. His skis stay on the snowmobile's rack, instead of flying off his feet as if he had been skiing.
