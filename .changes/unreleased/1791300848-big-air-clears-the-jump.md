title: Big air jumps clear the table, and the board reads cleanly
---

A big air jump now throws a skier well clear of its table. The kicker is taller and steeper: 32°, standing about 4 m over the flat. The landing is shaped for the flatter flight a skier really leaves the lip on. The in-run is ridden tucked, unbraked and centred from the start gate to the lip, whatever is pressed, so holding the back key or leaning back no longer leaves him short on the knuckle. The edge and the jump are still the skier's own. A jump let go just after the lip is left still springs.

A plain jump flies some 25–35 m. One sprung at the lip flies 35–45 m, about 5 m over it. The contest board now shows each skier the jumps taken so far, with BEST or TOTAL beside them, and no longer shows a dash for a jump still to come.
