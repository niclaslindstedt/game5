The X-ray cam's skeleton reads as a body again. The ribs are rounded bars with the costal cartilage and sternum in front instead of a stack of white blocks, the skull is closed with no seams the brain shows through, and every bone and organ is smooth-shaded. The lungs, which used to be a hull pushing out through the ribs, now fill the chest the way real lungs do: against the inside of the ribs and the spine, sitting on the diaphragm, rising over the first rib and hollowed round the heart, so nothing in the chest clips through anything else. The heart, liver, stomach, bowel and brain are each one closed shape. The X-ray lab gains an `anatomy` scene that circles the standing skeleton, and `make xray-body` prints how much of each organ a bone runs through.
