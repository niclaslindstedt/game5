On HIGH and MAX the clouds are now full volumes: fair-weather heaps with puffed, cauliflower tops and soft, ragged edges, decks with lumpy undersides, all lit by the sun's own colour along its real path through them. At golden hour and just after sunset, the sunward edges and the bases glow orange, red and pink, the clouds low on the horizon are the warmest, and the high ice turns pink once the snow is in shade. The volume stays steady while the sun moves and the camera swings fast.

There is also a low cloud deck now. On about half of the overcast days, and on some fair ones, a sea of cloud lies part way down the mountain. From the top you ski in sunshine under a clear sky and look down on it, with the far peaks standing out of it as islands. Skiing down, you ski into it: the view closes to tens of metres and the light goes flat. Below its base you come out under a grey ceiling. At sunset the sea turns pink and mauve under you.
