title: A hot air balloon on the free ride
---

The start card's RUN row has a BALLOON stop: you start in the basket of a hot air balloon tethered on the valley floor. Hold the tuck to fire the burner; once the envelope is light the tether lets go, and the day's wind carries you up the mountain toward the summit — the higher you fly, the faster, and turning a little with height, which is the only steering you have. A burn takes tens of seconds to become a climb, the skid pulls the valve in the crown to come down, and the edge and the lean walk you about the basket. Let it cool and it sinks; meet the snow, the rising slope or a tree too fast and the basket goes over and throws you out. Burning in a strong wind, or until the fabric cooks, sets the envelope alight and it falls. Enter takes you over the side — the only way out in the air — or, stood on the snow, steps you out. The balloon is a sport balloon as you would see one: sixteen lobed gores in one of many colourways, glowing from inside when the burner fires, hung by its wires over a wicker basket with its burner and cylinders, leaning with the wind and laid out on the snow when it comes down. When the burner fires, a long flame roars out of each coil up into the mouth — clear blue at the jets, a yellow-white body, orange tongues tearing off its top — lit with a soft thump and going out from the coil first, laid over by the wind, with the two small blue pilot lights burning under it; after dark the envelope glows like a lantern and the flame lights the basket and the snow orange. When the envelope catches, it browns and smokes where the flame licks it, then the fire climbs the gores from that point, eating the cloth behind it, under a column of black smoke, with embers, burning drips and shreds of cloth falling — and the wreck burns down and smoulders on the snow.
