title: The skis sound like the snow they are on, and a trunk sounds like the trunk
---

The skis' sound now follows the six kinds of snow under them: the groomer's hiss, the wind crust's crunch, the hush of powder (quieter and darker in new snow), the slush of wet spring snow and the scrape of an edge on ice. It also follows the pair on your feet: a soft park ski chatters, a stiff race ski rings higher, a fat powder ski hushes deeper. Collisions have their own sounds too. Brushing a trunk is a swish through the boughs and a knock on the bark, a real hit cracks deeper on a fat trunk than a thin one, and a dead snag knocks dry and hollow. Every way of being thrown (into a tree, over the tips, tumbling, an edge caught) has its own fall, with the skis clattering off afterwards. A landing is a whumpf in deep powder and a slap on ice.
