title: The wind pushes the skier
---

The weather's wind now acts on you, not just around you. Your drag is against the moving air, so a headwind lowers your top speed by about the wind's own speed, a tailwind carries you faster, and in a storm a traverse into the wind is a skate rather than a glide. A crosswind pushes you off your line: your edges hold most of it and you lean into it, and it tightens a turn it blows into and widens one it blows out of. The air is what reaches you where you are: about three quarters of the reported wind at body height, less in the valley and more on the ridges, and most of it taken away in the woods. A breeze never moves a skier standing still; a storm at your back blows you along. The wind meter and the wind you hear read that same air.
