title: The super-G — one run, never skied before it, its gates turning you into long fast carves, on nine maps of its own
---

A third race discipline beside the slalom and the downhill. A super-G is set on the downhill's hill from a start lowered down it — the drop to the finish 600 metres at the most — prepared as a downhill is (the crests shaved, the trees cut back, the safety nets down both sides), but its gates turn you: some forty of them, about fifty metres apart, each two red or blue panels set to one side of the line and then the other, so the race is long carved turns at ninety kilometres an hour and more, with a jump where the mountain gives one. There is no training run: one run against a field of thirty, the place on time, split at three intermediates, a speed trap late in the course, and a gate passed inside its turning pole a disqualification. It has NINE MAPS OF ITS OWN — three reds and six blacks across the alpine, the continental and the maritime, one at night under the floodlights — all open from the first visit, picked on its level card. The front door's two race tiles are now ONE RACE tile that opens a race card: the slalom, the super-G and the downhill, the disciplines still to come beside them.
