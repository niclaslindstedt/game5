title: The slalom racer crosses under — the new edge bites before his body comes over
---

A slalom racer now changes edges the way a real one does: his legs tip the skis from one edge to the next under a body that stays quiet, so the new edge bites while he is still coming over, and the skis are drawn up under him as they swing across. A turn every 0.9 s now comes round every time where before every other one was lost, and a slalom is skied some 7 % faster — the field's times and the course's par move with it. The line round the poles may now bend as tight as 8 m (it was 10), so a course swings further across the hill.
