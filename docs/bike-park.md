# Bike parks and downhill courses

This page holds the research for how a ski area's summer trails are BUILT: the lift-served bike park and the downhill race course. It covers grades, gradients, widths, how a trail comes down a mountain, the features on it, their sizes by grade, the order they come in, the signs, the way the trails leave a lift's top, and how a race course differs from a park trail. It ends with the rules the generator should build to.

Sources are named by kind: the international trail difficulty rating system (IMBA, 2004), and the national and regional trail standards built on it. Those are a national seven-level adaptation, a regional bicycle-trail standard, a city bike-park plan, and a national forestry datasheet for downhill and four-cross tracks. Then come trail builders' published guides, a GPS study of elite downhill courses, and reference photographs of working bike parks. Bands that are our own synthesis say so.

## Grades

Bike parks grade their trails by colour much as ski areas grade pistes, with the same green, blue, red and black. The international system grades by tread width, the average and steepest grade, and the tallest obstacle a rider must cross:

| Grade | Tread width | Average grade | Steepest | Tallest obstacle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| White (easiest) | 1.8 m or more | under 5% | 10% | none |
| Green | 0.9 m or more | 5% or less | 15% | 5 cm |
| Blue | 0.6 m or more | 10% or less | 15% or more | 20 cm |
| Black | 0.3 m or more | 15% or less | 15% or more | 38 cm |
| Double black | 0.15 m or more | 20% or less | 15% or more | larger |

The national seven-level adaptation adds features. Width is the target with its tolerance; obstacles are "unavoidable / with a ride-around":

| Level | Width | Average | Steepest | Obstacles | Tabletops up to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Very easy | 2.1 ± 0.9 m | ≤7% | 15% | none | — |
| Easy | 0.9 ± 0.3 m | ≤7% | 15% | 10 cm | — |
| Easy / intermediate | 0.75 ± 0.2 m | ≤7% | 20% | 20 cm | — |
| Intermediate | 0.6 ± 0.3 m | ≤10% | 20% | 35 cm / 60 cm | 1.5 m |
| Intermediate / difficult | 0.6 ± 0.3 m | ≤20% | 30% | 40 cm / 1.0 m | 2.0 m |
| Difficult | 0.3 ± 0.15 m | ≤20% | 30% | 50 cm / 1.2 m | 2.5 m |

A regional bicycle-trail standard adds turn radii and clearing:

| Level | Tread | Sustained grade | Steepest | Tightest turn radius | Obstacles |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Easiest | 0.6–0.9 m | 5% | 10% over 30 m | 2.4 m | 10 cm |
| More difficult | 0.3–0.6 m | 10% | 15–22% | 1.8 m | 20 cm |
| Most difficult | 0.3 m | 15% | 25% over 90 m | 1.2 m | 30 cm |

Every level is cleared 2.4 m high.

These are TRAIL-NETWORK standards, written for hand-built singletrack climbed as well as descended. A LIFT-SERVED park differs, as the photographs and the downhill datasheet show:

- **Width.** Its flow and jump trails are MACHINE-BUILT with a small excavator. The tread is 1.2–2.5 m wide, of pale compacted gravel and fines, so they read from a distance as broad pale ribbons. Only the technical red and black trails come down to 0.5–1.2 m of natural dirt between roots and rocks.
- **Gradient.** Its average grades run higher, because nobody climbs them: 6–12% on a flow trail, and 15–30% on a downhill line.

## Gradients that last

A trail lasts only if water leaves it:

- **The half rule.** A trail's grade should be no more than half the grade of the hillside it crosses. Steeper than that, it becomes the fall line's gutter.
- **Average grade** 10% or less on a sustainable trail. A short pitch can be steeper, up to 15–25% depending on the soil and the standard, but over 20% the trail is hard to keep.
- **Grade reversals** are short rises that dump the water off. They come every 6–16 m, as dips, rollers or the low side of a berm.
- **Outslope.** The tread tips about 5% toward the downhill side.

A downhill RACE track breaks these rules on purpose. It runs nearer the fall line, and it is fixed by hand every season.

## How a trail comes down a mountain

- **Flow trails (green and blue)** traverse ACROSS the fall line and turn back in BERMS. They zig-zag down the slope in long legs of 30–150 m joined by banked turns, like a road's switchbacks with the hairpins banked. At an average of 7% a trail runs 14 m for each metre down, so a green trail off a 500 m lift is about 6–8 km long. In the photograph taken from the air over a lift-served park, three or four trails interleave down one face under the chairlift, each a pale ribbon of tight berms, crossing each other and the service road at a few points.
- **Jump lines (blue, red)** run steeper, at 8–15%. Their tables and rollers come in rhythm on the straights, with berms between.
- **Technical trails (red, black)** follow the ground. They drop straighter down the slope through the woods, over roots, rock gardens, steps and drops, with fewer berms and more natural turns.
- **Downhill race tracks** run nearest the fall line of all, with a mean grade of 20–30% and 1.5–3.5 km off 400–700 m of vertical. They come down in a mix of steep straight shots, natural off-camber turns, rock and root sections, built jumps and a few big features, then end in a finish straight on the valley floor. On the measured courses the steepest shot reached about 50 km/h.

Trails stay in the woods where they can, for shade, roots and soil that holds, and cross the open pistes in a few long traverses. A trail never runs straight down a grassed ski run: the bare scar would wash out.

## Features by grade

These bands are our synthesis of the standards above, a builders' tabletop guide and a city bike-park plan's feature limits. A jump has a lip that kicks up; a drop leaves off a flat or a down-sloping edge.

| Feature | Green | Blue | Red | Black |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Roller (a smooth hump, no air needed) | 0.3–0.6 m tall, 4–6 m long | 0.4–0.8 m | — | — |
| Tabletop: height | 0.5–0.9 m | 0.9–1.5 m | 1.5–2.0 m | 1.8–2.5 m |
| Tabletop: take-off angle | 15–25° | 25–35° | 30–40° | 30–45° |
| Tabletop: table length | 2–3 m | 3–5 m | 5–8 m | 8–12 m |
| Tabletop: speed to clear it | 13–19 km/h | 19–29 km/h | 26–35 km/h | 30–45 km/h |
| Gap jump (open between lip and landing) | — | — | 4–8 m | 8–15 m |
| Drop: height | ≤0.3 m, rollable | 0.3–0.6 m | 0.6–1.2 m | 1.2–3 m |
| Drop: width ÷ height | 2 or more | 1 or more | 0.5 or more | 0.5 |
| Step-down (the landing below the lip) | — | 0.3–0.5 m | 0.5–1.5 m | 1–3 m |
| Berm: radius | 4–8 m | 3–6 m | 2.5–5 m | 2–4 m |
| Berm: bank | 20–35° | 30–45° | 40–55° | 45–60° |
| Berm: height | 0.3–0.6 m | 0.5–1.0 m | 0.8–1.5 m | 1–2 m |
| Rock garden / roots (tallest rock) | — | 10–20 cm | 20–40 cm | 40 cm and over |
| Elevated deck (bridge, skinny) | width ≥ height, ≤0.6 m tall | width ≥ height/2, ≤1.2 m | width ≥ height/4, ≤1.8 m | — |
| Wall ride | — | — | 2–3 m tall, 60–80° | 3–4 m, near vertical |

The take-off speed comes from the projectile `d = v² sin 2α / g`, with d the gap from lip to the landing's knuckle (see [the downhill bike](downhill-bike.md)). A landing's slope matches the flight's descent angle: 20–35°, and as long as the take-off's arc needs, at least 1–1.5 times the table's length.

## Sequencing

From the trail builders' guides and the city bike-park plan's risk rules:

- **A filter at the entrance.** Within the first 30–100 m, a trail puts the hardest kind of feature it carries, at the trail's grade, where a rider can still turn back. A ride-around (a "B-line") has a sign at the split.
- **No surprises.** Every feature is visible from its stopping distance at the trail's speed: 15–25 m on a flow trail, 30–50 m on a fast downhill line. A blind crest has nothing hard behind it.
- **Rhythm.** Jumps in a line come 15–30 m apart, lip to lip, so the rider lands, takes one pump and is set up for the next. All the jumps in a line are sized for the SAME speed. The set-up straight before the first is 20–50 m with no turn.
- **Run-out.** After the last jump or a big drop, a straight run-out of 2–3 times the jump's length before the next turn.
- **Fall zones.** 1–2 m clear either side of a jump or drop: no stump, rock or tree, and a padded tree where one has to stand.
- **Turn after feature.** A berm comes where the speed is highest, at the foot of a straight. A flat turn never follows a jump.
- **Grade by the hardest feature.** A trail is as hard as its worst unavoidable feature, never its average.
- **Choke points.** Where riders bunch (junctions, crossings, the bottom of a fast section) the trail is widened and flattened.

## Signs

- **At each trail head:**
  - a post or panel in the grade's colour and shape (circle, square, diamond, double diamond, the same symbols as the pistes');
  - the trail's number and name;
  - its length;
  - often its features as icons (jumps, drops, rocks).
- **At each junction:** a small arrow sign in the trail's colour.
- **Before a feature:** a warning sign 10–20 m ahead, or a split sign for the A-line and B-line.
- **At service-road and hiking-path crossings:** signs on both trails, with the bike trail's crossing flattened and widened.
- **At a lift's top:** the summer trail-map board, the rules board, and a grade-coloured post at every trail head round the staging area.
- **Race tracks:** taped and netted along both edges on race days, with gates at the start and orange tape marking the line.

## From the lift's top

A lift's top station in summer has a STAGING AREA: a flat apron of 20–40 m on the pad, with the map board, benches and bike racks. The trails leave it in a fan, each from its own signed head, easiest nearest the lift and hardest furthest away. The green trail often leaves first and runs out across the open slope on a gentle traverse; the blacks drop off the far edge into the woods.

The trails meet again lower down at a few junctions on the mountain, often at a mid-station or a service road, where a rider can change trails. At the base they all funnel into one or two FINISH STRAIGHTS that end beside the bottom station's bike wash and the load line. A park's trails cover 300–800 m of a lift's vertical. The highest ground, above the tree line on rock and turf, carries only a few natural reds and blacks, or nothing.

Hikers have their own paths and the service roads. Bike trails cross them only at signed, flattened crossings.

## How a race course differs

A downhill race course is ONE trail, built for one rider at a time against the clock:

- **Line and grade:** nearer the fall line, with a mean grade of 20–30% (the measured courses: 23% and 29%), against 6–12% for a park's flow trail.
- **Length and time:** 1.5–3.5 km off 400–700 m of vertical, in 2–5 minutes (top-level courses 1.6–2.4 km).
- **Width:** 2–3 m on the fast sections, down to 0.8 m in tight technical ones, with taped edges set wider than the line.
- **Mixed on purpose:** natural ground (roots, rock, off-camber turns, steep chutes) with a few BUILT features (a big jump or two, a road gap, the finish jump).
- **Start and finish:** a start HUT on a ramp with a timing beam; a finish ARENA on the flat, with a big last jump, a timing line and a crowd.
- **Gates:** none. The track is held by tape and nets, and a racer who leaves the tape is out.
- **Lift:** served by the gondola or chairlift with bike racks, and walked the day before (the course walk). Training runs, a seeding run, then one final run.
- **Four-cross** (a different race, four riders at once) is about 6 m wide and under 1 km, built like a ski-cross course with berms, tables and rollers.

And for the park as a whole:

- **Where it starts.** Trails start from a staging apron (20–40 m) at a lift's top, each from its own signed head, easiest nearest the lift. They end in one or two finish straights beside the bottom station's load line.
- **How much of the mountain.** A park uses 300–800 m of the lift's vertical, and every trail stays below the highest rock band.
- **What a park carries.** At least one green, one blue and one red per park; a black where the ground allows. Green trails are 4–8 km off a 500 m lift; the rest are shorter.
- **Junctions.** At least one mid-mountain junction where a rider can switch trails. Every junction is widened and flattened (a choke point).
- **Crossings.** No bike trail runs along a hiking path or a service road. They cross only at right angles, flattened, at a signed crossing.
- **A RACE COURSE.** At most one downhill course per map, on the face with the most vertical:
  - 1.5–3.5 km off 400–700 m, mean grade 20–30%;
  - width 2–3 m fast, 0.8 m tight;
  - a start hut at the top, a finish arena on the flat, a big jump within the last 200 m;
  - taped edges, which judge "out" as the race gates do in winter.
- **A FOUR-CROSS** course, where wanted: ~6 m wide, under 1 km, built as the ski cross's course is.

## What is built

The rules above are built as R44 (the park) and R45 (the race line), stated once in `engine/mapgen/bike-park-rules.ts` (`BIKE_PARK_RULES`, its prose mirrored in [`level-generator.md`](level-generator.md)). `setBikePark(level)` lays the park over a built map on a summer free ride (`createGame` with `summer: true`, `mode: "free"`); no other run carries one.

- **Off its own stream.** The park draws from a stream of its own (`park.salt`) and reads the map without writing it. The map it is laid on is kept whole as `park.base`, and `levelDigest` of a summer map is its winter one's. The cabins are read off the base, so none moves.
- **Which lifts.** Bikes ride the chairs and gondolas with at least 120 m of vertical. The three lowest lifts within 820 m carry them, or the lowest one when none is that low. The grades are dealt per lift, lowest first: green, blue and red off the first; blue, red and black off the second; red and black off the third. A park of one lift gets one of each. There are at most nine trails, plus the race line.
- **The walk** (`bike-trail-walk.ts`). Each trail leaves its head on the staging apron's rim, 30 m from the top station's middle. On a leg it holds the heading at which the ground falls at the grade's target, eased up or down so its mean comes out at the target. It turns through the fall line at the end of a dealt leg, at its corridor's edge, or where the ground ahead is blocked. Blocking is water, a station, a tower, a cabin, the map's edge, ground past 38°, or another trail or run running the same way within 9 m. A turn is made only where its whole arc is clear. Where it can neither go on nor turn, it skirts along the contour for up to 300 m. Near its end, a ring 40 m up the slope from the bottom station, it is drawn straight home. A head whose first metres meet something is moved 12° round the rim. Each slot tries six lines and keeps the best: the most in the woods (a red's and a black's thrice over), the least cut, and the mean nearest its target.
- **The bench** (`bike-trail-shape.ts`). This is the tread's height down the line: the ground eased over 16 m and held never to rise and never to fall past the grade's steepest. Across the tread it is outsloped 5 % toward the downhill side, and it eases into the ground over a shoulder 1 m wide plus 1.2 m for every metre of cut or fill, 4 m at most. A trail cut or filled more than 3.5 m at its centre line is refused.
- **The features** (`bike-features.ts`), each in its grade's band. Berms bank the turns. Rollers come every 8–16 m down a green's and a blue's legs, and dips every 12–25 m down a red's. Tabletops come in lines, with gaps on a red and a black. Drops, step-downs and rock gardens come from a blue up, and root sections on a red, a black and the race line. Every feature is kept clear of the trail's first and last metres, of the next turn and of the next feature's run-out.
- **The race line (R45).** It is laid first, off the lift with the most vertical, or the next one where it cannot be laid. It is walked at 25 % to the bottom station, with its start 700 m of height above the finish. It is taped down both edges and carries a black's technical features and a finish jump. It is kept only if its mean lies within 18–32 %, its length within 1.5–3.5 km and its drop within 400–700 m.
- **The crossings levelled** (`bike-crossings.ts`). Where two trails' treads overlap in plan — one crossing another, or running across it for a few metres — each bench was cut to its own line's height, so they met as a step or a wall (a metre or two where a trail crosses another a switchback lower). Now both are brought to one height there, the way builders grade a crossing. Each bench is moved part of the way toward the other's and eased on and off along its own line. The easing is at most 4 % of grade, and never more than half the trail's own mean, over at least 6 m. A move that would break R44's pitch, rise or cut is undone, and the other bench comes the rest of the way. A crossing far apart in height also scores a trail's try down while it is laid (`crossingMiss`), so a trail is laid another way where it can be. Whatever is left (a few decimetres where neither bench may move) is blended by the surface.
- **The surface: a layered analytic one** (`bike-surface.ts`'s `withBikeSurface`). This is the halfpipe's answer, not a regraded grid. A tread is 0.5–2.5 m wide, and a table's lip, a drop's ledge, a rock or a root is decimetres. The map's 2 m grid cannot hold any of that, and regrading it would smear every trail into a ramp and move what the winter builds on. So inside a trail's footprint, `groundAt` and `normalAt` answer off the trail's own shape: the bench, its outslope, the features, and the shoulder eased into the ground. Everywhere else they answer off the base map. The grid under each footprint is cut 12 cm below that surface, so the terrain mesh never pokes through. The renderer lays the true surface over it as a ribbon (`bike-trail-plan.ts`, `bike-trails-view.ts`). Trees on a tread or in its fall zone are felled, and a tree on a shoulder is stood on it.
- **The treads to a tyre.** The summer ground (`summer-ground.ts`) asks `bikeTreadAt` first and reports the trail kinds with surfaces of their own (`defs/summer-ground.ts`):
  - `flow` — a flow trail's crushed-stone fines;
  - `trail` — a technical trail's and the race line's loam;
  - `rocks` — on a rock;
  - `roots` — on a root;
  - `dirt` — the raw earth of a shoulder.
- **The questions** (`bike-park-query.ts`, exported through `engine/game/bike-park-api.ts`):
  - `bikeTrailAt` — which trail a point is on, at what arc, how far off its centre and how much on its surface;
  - `bikeTreadAt` — the tread there;
  - `nearestBikeTrailPoint` — the nearest point of any trail;
  - `bikeTrailHeads` — the heads off a lift's top;
  - `bikeTrailById` and `featuresAt`.
- **Held by** `engine/analysis/bike-park.ts` (`analyzeBikePark`). It reads only what the park publishes and checks:
  - every trail's mean, steepest, rise, cut and width;
  - each feature's size, place and run-out;
  - that no trunk stands on a tread or in its fall zone;
  - that the ground is the bench away from features;
  - that every head is on a carrying lift's apron and every end is beside a bottom station.

  A park with no green, blue or red is a warning, not an error. `tests/bike_park_test.ts` and `bike_park_regions_test.ts` hold it on generated mountains, every region and two real faces.
- **As drawn.** The ribbon is pale gravel or dark loam, with the tyre lines worn darker. The shoulders are raw earth fading into the grass. The rocks and roots are pieces of their own, and the race line is staked and taped red and white. Each head has a sign with the grade's mark and a trail name (`bike-sign-plan.ts`, drawn by `run-signs.ts`). The trails are also on the run's minimap in their grades' colours.
- **Labs.** `make bike-park` is the plan, the profiles, the table and the sweep, pure Node. `make bike-look` is the park through the game's renderer at named views.

**Still open:**

- **Real faces.** They are steeper and rougher than dealt ones, so a green or a blue does not fit on every face (two of the three tried carry a park).
- **Woods.** The woods share is scored, never required.
- **Race furniture.** No start hut on its ramp, no finish arena and no timing yet.
- **Junctions and crossings.** They are found and published (`park.junctions`), and two trails' crossings are levelled to one height, but they are not widened or signed. Neither are the crossings of a piste or a road. A few crossings keep a step of 0.3–1.2 m where neither bench may move without breaking R44 (on seed 38 two of B1's, on seed 7 one of B1's over B3).
- **Four-cross.** There is no four-cross.
- **The start card's chart.** It does not draw the trails, because the chart is baked off the winter map in a worker, and laying the park there costs seconds.
- **The bike.** It is built in the engine ([the downhill bike](downhill-bike.md)); its drawing, lens and HUD are still to come.
