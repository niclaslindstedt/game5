The snow reads right under every light. A moonlit night is now a night: the snow under a full moon is about a fifth as bright as at noon and darker than the dusk before it, so the floodlit runs and the headlamp's pool stand out and the woods away from the lamps go dark. Under an overcast the snow keeps a faint modelling up close — swells, tracks and clods rimmed a shade darker — while staying honestly flat. Under a low sun the crystals glint gold rather than white. A body's crater in deep snow is a cool shade instead of an electric blue, its walls slump into the hole within a few frames, and a ski's groove edge no longer saw-tooths along the grid. Under the skis at the lower TRAILS settings the snow is drawn as the ski's own narrow pressed groove instead of a steep notch, and a crater costs about a third of the processor time it did.
