type: Changed
title: Skis, snowboards and snowmobiles leave tracks of their own
---

A track now says what cut it. Skis leave two lines a stance apart. A snowboard leaves one line as wide as its deck: on its edge it cuts a wedge that is deep under the edge and piles the snow on the outside of the turn, it floats higher in powder, and when it skids it scrapes a band as long as the board. A snowmobile leaves a flat-bottomed trench, packed hard and square-walled, with the paddles' imprints printed across its floor in staggered rows, between two ski grooves that each have a keel line down the middle. A sled track ridden over the corduroy breaks the comb, and a piste machine wipes the sled track away again. None of this keeps any extra memory: it uses the same four bytes per texel as before.
