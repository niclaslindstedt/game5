type: Changed
title: Real mountainsides are raised at their real size
---

A free ride raised on a real mountainside now stands it as it really is, metre for metre. Its height from the summit to the valley floor is the real one: a fell is a few hundred metres of low, rounded hill and a big alpine face rises well over a thousand. Before, every face was stretched to the same height as a generated mountain. Its spurs, gullies and knolls are laid at their real size across the whole ski area instead of being smoothed out. The peak's station stands on the brow of the real summit. The height over the sea read on the ground is the real height, and the tree line stands at its real height in metres. Some high faces whose valley lies above the trees are therefore bare. Where the real mountain is gentler or rougher than a generated one, the ski area bends to it on real faces only: runs are skated over a flat valley floor, the way between lifts is cut through the real ground, and the way home may be down the next colour up. Building a real face no longer holds on to a refused attempt while it tries the next, so it needs less memory.
