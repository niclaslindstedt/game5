title: The skier leans into the turn he is making, and a carve turns as tight as its sidecut and no tighter
---

The skier now lays himself over by the balance of the turn he is actually making — the lean at which the snow's push under his skis passes through him, a strong free skier's carve at some 40–50°, a slalom racer's apex at 55–65° — committing his body into the next turn as he changes edges, and leaning into the hill on a traverse and back against a hockey stop. His edge follows his body: a ski stands no further over than he is laid over plus what his knees and hips angulate, so the drawn racer is laid over where he used to stand nearly upright. A carved turn now turns no tighter than the ski's sidecut allows at its edge (a slalom ski at 70° some 4.5 m, a downhill ski at 55° some 26 m) — anything tighter is a skid — and the giant slalom, super-G and downhill techniques stand their skis at their disciplines' measured edge angles. Free carves lean noticeably further than before; race times move by a few tenths.
