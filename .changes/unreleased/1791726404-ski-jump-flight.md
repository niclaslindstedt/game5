title: A ski jump is jumped — the bar, the in-run, the take-off, the flight and the landing
---

A ski jumper now sits on the bar of the jury's gate and pushes off on the tuck. He runs down the in-run's tracks under gravity, the ice's friction and his crouch's drag, and reaches the hill's design speed at the edge. The jump key starts the take-off: a push that lasts a little over a quarter of a second, worth its whole speed only when it ends at the edge. Pressed early or late it is worth less, and never pressed it is worth nothing. In the air he flies on lift and drag that follow his skis' angle to the air. A head wind carries him further and a tail wind shortens him, about five metres for every metre a second. He lays out and opens the V, and the lean, the edge and the assist's hand hold his attitude and roll; too sat-up or turned over, he stalls or falls. He comes down in a telemark on the trick key, or on both feet, and the distance is credited to the half metre. On time from the jury's gate in calm air he lands near the K point. `make skijump-flight` is the lab.
