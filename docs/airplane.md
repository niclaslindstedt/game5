# The jump plane

A free ride can start in the open door of a jump plane. Pick **PLANE** on the start card's START row (the stop after the helicopter), or follow a `?start=free&plane=1` link. You stand on your skis in the sliding door of a single-turboprop utility plane parked on its own strip below the village, the rig of a sport skydiver on your back, the engine running — and the plane is yours to fly, by hand, nothing assisting. Climb as high as you like, loop it, roll it, and press **Enter** to jump out of the door ([the skydive](skydive.md) takes it from there, or [the wingsuit](wingsuit.md) in one). The pilot then flies it home and lands it.

The engine side is `engine/game/plane.ts` (the step: the flight, the skis on the snow, boarding, the jump, the crash and the restart), `plane-aero.ts` (the air on every surface), `plane-state.ts` (its state, controls and events), `plane-pilot.ts` (the bot's hands), `airstrip.ts` (where the strip lies), `airstrip-room.ts` (the room the search reads), `airstrip-corridor.ts` (its approach and climb-out), `airstrip-site.ts` (its markers and buildings), `airstrip-solids.ts` (what a skier meets there), `defs/plane.ts` (`PLANE`, every number), `plane-wings.ts` (the wings: scraped, battered and torn off; the torn ones flown down), `plane-hand.ts` (the take-off hand), `defs/plane-wings.ts` (`PLANE_WINGS`, `TAKEOFF_HAND`), `defs/plane-wreck.ts` (`PLANE_WRECK`, whether it burns and the fire) and `defs/air-bounds.ts` (`AIR_BOUNDS`, the edge every craft in the air shares). All of it is exported through `plane-api.ts`. The app side is `pwa/src/game/plane-view.ts` (the model posed, its lamps), `plane-standin.ts` (the code's stand-in), `plane-scene.ts` (hung on the rider's line, the crash lens, the wreck), `plane-cockpit*.ts` (the cockpit), `plane-wing-cut.ts` (a torn wing put away and drawn loose), `plane-shatter.ts` and `plane-wreck-view.ts` (the crash), `strip-*.ts` (the strip as drawn), `camera-plane.ts` (the ladder), `input-plane.ts` and `settings-plane-keys.ts` (the hands and the keys), `plane-hud.ts` / `hud-plane.tsx` (the strip on the HUD), `audio/plane-*.ts` (the sound) and `strings-plane.ts`.

## The machine

The class mountain drop zones and glacier operators fly: a single turboprop of about 410 kW (550 shp, flat-rated so it keeps its power high), a strut-braced constant-chord **high wing** of 15.87 m and 30.15 m² (aspect ratio 8.35), a long nose over the engine, a conventional tail, a tailwheel, **wheel-skis** on a 3 m track, a three-blade 2.56 m propeller at 2,200 rpm, and big **sliding cabin doors**, the jumpers' on the right. 11.0 m long; 2,450 kg working weight (empty about 1,290 kg, the pilot, the fuel for two lifts and a load of jumpers) plus the skier in the door; 200 kg of fuel in the wing tanks.

Every number and every point of the geometry is in `PLANE`, in the skier's frame (x right, y up, z forward, the origin the snow under the main skis with the fuselage held level). The Blender model is built off that table whole, never numbers of its own. The engine's right is the picture's mirror image (three.js's view from behind mirrors the map, as the helicopter's cockpit plan says), so the jump door — on the right as a pilot sees it — and the left seat are stated at the engine's −x and +x.

## The flight

A real six-degree-of-freedom rigid body at 120 Hz, nothing scripted. Every loop, roll, stall and spin comes out of the forces:

- **The wing** is four sections a side, each with its own angle of attack off the body's velocity and rotation. The lift slope is Helmbold's for the aspect ratio (4.96 a radian), cambered to zero lift at −0.052 rad, stalling 0.335 rad past it (C_Lmax ≈ 1.67 clean, ≈ 2.08 with full flap) into a flat plate's 1.05·sin 2α over 0.07 rad, its drag rising to 1.25·sin²α. The tip is washed out 2°, so the root stalls first; the inboard flaps shift their sections' zero lift and stall.
- **The tail and fin** are surfaces of their own, in the wing's downwash and in 0.6 of the propeller's slipstream; the fuselage, the gear and the struts are drag areas along the body's axes behind the CoG.
- **The engine and propeller**: thrust is the shaft power times the propeller's efficiency over the airspeed, capped by the static thrust off momentum theory (10.5 kN), the power following the lever with a turbine's 1.1 s spool. Its **torque** rolls the airframe and its **swirl** yaws the fin. The plane is **rigged** against both as a single is: the ailerons set a touch apart and the fin offset (`aero.rig`) so they cancel at 50 m/s on three-quarter power — slow on full power the left-turning pull comes back, about 30° of heading over a loop's top with no rudder.
- **The trim**: the tail's incidence trims it at 45 m/s on climb power with the stick centred, the trim a pilot winds in for the jump run and for aerobatics, which a held stick then pulls and pushes about.
- **On the snow** the main skis and the tail ski are springs and dampers (`PLANE.ground`) with a ski's friction along their run (0.07 packed, 0.18 loose) and much more across; the tail ski steers with the rudder; the brakes are a ski's claws and the propeller's reverse.

## The controls

The plane is flown on a key table of its own (`settings-plane-keys.ts`, rebound under OPTIONS ▸ KEYS ▸ PLANE): the helicopter's hands, so a pilot of one flies the other.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| W S | The STICK fore and aft: forward the nose down, back the nose up. |
| A D | The STICK side to side: the ailerons. |
| Q E (or ← →) | The RUDDER pedals; on the snow they steer the tail ski. |
| ↑ ↓ (or Shift Z) | The POWER lever: it moves while held and stays where it is left. |
| F V | The FLAP lever down a notch, up a notch. |
| Space | The brakes on the snow. |
| Enter (a double tap) | Out of the door in the air (the jump); off it onto the snow when stopped. |

On touch the left thumb is the stick pad and the right the power and the rudder (the helicopter's `StickZone`s), with ▲ / ▼ for the flaps.

The player's hand (`input-plane.ts`'s `samplePlane`) is the honest stick a keyboard can give: a held key eases the stick over rather than snapping it, and its **reach** scales with the airspeed — less travel fast, full travel slow — so a held key is a pull, not a snap into the stall. The ailerons reach all the way up to 60 m/s and less with the speed past it, never under 0.6 of their throw. **The hand pulls to the buffet**: the back stick eases off while more than 8 % of the wing is stalled, pushed or pulled; back stick with full rudder is a deliberate spin entry and pulls through. **It eases off against g too**: a held key's pull or push is let off past 5 g up and 1.9 g down (the limit load with a little over it, short of the 7.1 g and 2.85 g that tear a wing off), so a held loop or a push onto its back never folds a wing; the thumb's stick is not eased. So a key held back from 120 kt at full power flies a whole loop over the top at 50–60 kt, a held roll is an aileron roll of about 62°/s, and the hammerhead, inverted flight and a three-turn spin with its recovery are all there.

**THE TAKE-OFF HAND** (`plane-hand.ts`, `TAKEOFF_HAND`) is a flying school's hands on the player's controls on the snow and the first tens of metres off it — all of it on the snow, none by 40 m, scaled by the run's `Assist` (the heading by its `yaw`, the rest by its `air`). It holds the heading on the roll while the pedals are let go (against the torque's and the propeller's swing), levels the wings near the snow while the stick is not over, keeps the tail from coming up past a few degrees nose-up off the snow's lean (so the propeller's tips clear a bump and a held push never noses it over), and eases a held pull forward short of the stall (6 of stick a radian past 0.17 rad of attack, read a quarter second ahead, on the snow only from 17 m/s and held on up to 150 m, so a climb-out hauled back on does not stall as the rest lets go). WHY: a tailwheel single taken off by holding full power and the stick back left the snow at the stall in its three-point attitude, over-rotated, dropped a wing and cartwheeled on it — every naive take-off crashed at about 30 m/s, and stick forward nosed it over at 9 m/s. Now the same keys fly it off at 1.1–1.2 times the stall: off after 192 m on the flat, 0.2 m off the line, 128 m up 35 s on (seed 38's strip: 216 m, 6.6 m off the line, 118 m up).

## The strip, the jump and the pilot

**The strip** (`airstrip.ts`'s `airstripOf`, cached) lies on the valley floor near the village, along the valley where it can. It is 20 m wide and as long as the plane needs: its own measured take-off roll at the working weight with the skier aboard (225 m on a generated map's strip) times 1.5, plus a 60 m overrun — about 400 m. It is searched as a rectangle no steeper than 3 % along and 4 % across, no bump over 1.2 m off a straight line, with its circuit inside `PLANE.pilot.edge` of the map's edge, and **off everything a strip may not be laid over or beside**: one distance field a map (`airstrip-room.ts`'s `stripRoomAt`, the exact Euclidean transform on a 4 m grid) measures every spot's room from the nearest building by its own footprint (22 m past it — a real face's long halls and flats as much as a log hut), every run, lane and ski route by its own width (12 m past its edge), the hub, every lift's line (18 m) and station (40 m), the wind tunnels, the village's streets, lots and areas (a real face's town streets reach past the village's own ground), the helipad, the sled's spot, the balloon's site, and every lake, pond, river and stream. The search walks the floor on a 20 m grid nearest the village first, tries 36 bearings at each spot, and scores what it finds — the distance from the village, the height over it, the turn off the valley's axis, the trees it would fell and how far its approach intrudes — stopping once no spot further out could beat the best. Every bearing is scored against an **obstacle-clearance surface** off each end (`airstrip-corridor.ts`: 900 m out, 45 m half-wide growing 12 % a metre, 40 m under the 6° glide path) checked against every lift rope (3 m clear, every 15 m) and the helipad; the landing is laid the clearer way. Where the floor has no such spot it eases the rules (`Airstrip.tier`: `strict`, then `eased` with twice the slope and half the gaps, then `loose`), and only on a map with no room anywhere falls back onto the village (`village`). WHY: the old search kept 14 m off the trees and each thing by one gap from its middle, so on a real face — wooded valleys, long buildings, wide pistes — it found nothing and fell back onto the town, the runway through the houses and over the slopes; and it searched in seconds. Every real face now lays a strict or eased strip off its runs and buildings, in tens to hundreds of milliseconds (`make airstrip`).

**The trees on it are felled.** A strip is cleared through a wood rather than kept out of one: the trees on the runway and its edges, inside its buildings' walls, and standing up into the air off either end (`STRIP_AIR`: a surface rising one in twenty off the end, as wide as the corridor, read 600 m out — a wood left there took the wings off the bot's plane climbing out at 15 m) (`airstrip-site.ts`'s `stripFelled`) are gone from the map for everything that reads trees (`felled.ts`'s `felledTrees`: the collisions, the woods as drawn, the minimap, the tree wells), on every map with a ski area and no race course set on it (`stripLaid`), so no discipline's map moves.

**NOTHING ELSE AVOIDS THE STRIP.** The cabins, the village, the lifts and every other placement are dealt before it and never read it. Adding the strip to any keep-off list would move every map's digest, so the strip fits round the world, never the other way. The same holds for its site (`airstrip-site.ts`): the edge boards every 50 m 1.2 m outside the packed edge with a bough between each pair, a row of six threshold boards at each end, a hangar (22 × 15 m, its door 18 × 4.8 m — or a lean-to on a tight floor), a fuel shed, a hut and a windsock abeam the landing threshold. All of it is a pure function of the level, cached.

**What a skier meets there** (`airstrip-solids.ts`): the buildings, the mast and the parked or grounded plane (oriented boxes off `planePoint`) push him out and reflect his speed as a trunk does; a hard run is a wipeout. Free ride only, so no race moves.

**Boarding.** Stopped within 5 m of the parked plane's door at under 4 m/s, the machine press puts him aboard (`planeWithin`).

**The jump** is the machine press in the air (`jump()`): he leaves the door with the plane's velocity plus a push of 1.4 m/s out and 0.4 m/s up, and the plane's mode goes to `home`. Stopped on the snow, the same press steps him off.

**The pilot** (`plane-pilot.ts`): `planeInput` is the bot in the door — the take-off (the tail let fly at 15 m/s, lifted off at 28), the climb straight ahead to 60 m, a climbing circuit at 40 m/s, the jump run at 37 m/s with a quarter flap at 2,000 m over the summit (2,500–2,800 m over the snow under the door — 40–50 s of freefall, ten and a half minutes of climb). It never jumps by itself, so a pre-roll stays aboard. `homeControls` flies home after the jump: a stadium circuit on the side with more room, the final on a 6° path aimed 80 m short, the flare at 5 m and the rollout on the centre line; it holds its circuit over rope tops. It recovers a stall, reads the angle of attack ahead on the pitch rate and never pulls past a 57° bank. `planeHold(run, want)` is a steady hand for the labs and the suite.

## The airborne edge

**Every craft in the air** — the plane, the helicopter, the paramotor, the balloon, the skydiver on his body and under his canopy — is turned back from the map's edge by one rule (`collision.ts`'s `airBounds` / `airBoundsAt`, the numbers `AIR_BOUNDS`). Inside a band along the edge a **wind** blows it back in (16 m/s at the edge, growing as the square of the depth, and on past the edge), and its pilot's hand is turned toward the map's middle. Nothing is a wall. It fades in between 40 and 140 m over the snow, so a strip or a pad near the edge is never touched by it.

| Craft | Band, m |
| --- | --- |
| Plane | 900 — a turn's diameter at 50 m/s and a 30° bank |
| Helicopter | 160, plus 6 s × the speed carried outward |
| Paramotor | 120 |
| Balloon | 160 |
| Skydiver on his body | 320 |
| Canopy | 100 |

The plane's hand is full within about 60° of straight out, along the edge only past 60 % of the band, never when heading back in, only upright (fading out past 80° of roll or 52° of pitch), and it never weakens the player's own pull — so a loop near the edge is still the player's loop. The skier on his skis and every race keep `keepInBounds`, untouched. The wind tunnels, likewise, take a skier only within `TUNING.tunnel.roof` (8 m) of the snow, so a canopy flown over one is never caught.

## The wings

The research (`defs/plane-wings.ts`), restated: a normal-category wing is built to a **limit** load of 3.8 g up and 1.52 g down and to hold an **ultimate** 1.5 times that (5.7 g and 2.28 g); a built wing breaks somewhat past its ultimate, and we take a quarter — **7.1 g up, 2.85 g down**, each side its own lift against half the weight (88.8 kN and 35.5 kN). On the snow a wingtip is a light fairing on a long lever: a wing dropped on the take-off or landing roll scrapes it and is flown on (accident reports of such strikes are mostly minor damage); what tears a wing off is a tip dug in hard at speed, a ground loop driving it in, or a trunk.

- **A tip on the snow** (`plane-wings.ts`'s `tipContacts`) is held up by it: a spring of 40 kN/m and a crush of 1.5 kN·s/m under each of its four strike points, the snow's friction along it (0.35) and a **plough** once dug in (½ρ of 400 kg/m³ packed snow against a 0.3 m tip, times the depth and the speed squared). The plane is slewed toward it and lifted off again. Past 600 N a touch is a **scrape** (the `scrape` event): its health wears by the square of the force over the tearing force, and by 0.004 a metre slid.
- **Torn off** (the `tear` event, `tearWing`): a tip pressed past **32.4 kN** — the ultimate bending moment at the root (5.7 g of half the weight about the lift's centre at 0.42 of the semi-span) over the tip's lever — up and down, twice that fore and aft (the spar is deep in its chord), and down to half of it as a battered wing's health runs out; a **trunk** met faster than 5 m/s, a crown's thick limbs (inside 0.3 of its spread) faster than 15 m/s (its twigs only batter it); or **its own lift** past the ultimate in the air. A take-off's wing drop at a 3.5 rad/s roll scrapes at about 12 kN and loses 2 % of its health; a tip dug in landing at 45 m/s tears.
- **Flown on with one**: the torn side's sections are gone from the air (`plane-aero.ts`), so it rolls in toward that side; on the snow or near it that is a cartwheel, high up it is a plane falling away rolling. The torn wing takes its **tank** (the fuel is in the wings: half of what is left goes with each), and is flown down on its own (`stepLooseWings`: 110 kg, a tumbling plate of 7 m², bounced, slid and fallen flat on the snow) where the drawing shows it — the airframe's own wing triangles cut out of it once (`plane-wing-cut.ts`) and drawn there, the tip's lamps with them. **A torn wing never burns**: its fuel streams out behind and away from the engine, onto snow far below jet fuel's 38 °C flash point.

## Engine failure

With OPTIONS ▸ THINGS GO WRONG on, a take-off may be dealt a stoppage (`PlaneState.engine`, `engine/game/mishaps.ts`): 25 to 200 s aloft, never under 150 m over the snow, three seconds of coughing (`cough`, the power surging and dying) and then nothing (`engine`, the horn) — the propeller windmilling down to a third of its turn. Flown at the best glide she comes down at about nine to one, so a field within some nine times the height is a dead-stick landing.

## The crash

Into the snow too hard (a sink past 3.6 m/s, a leg pressed past 0.42 m, a sideways skid past 7 m/s), the nose, the belly, the tail or the propeller's disc into the snow, or a tree: `struck()` names what hit and `crash()` files a rich `PlaneWreck` — where, how fast, its attitude and rates, its power and fuel, and its **kind**: `nose`, `cart` (a wing torn off by its tip or a trunk in the three seconds before, the rest rolled in after it), `belly` (and a ground loop: the gear folded or slid out from under it is never a spin), `spin` or `tree`. A wingtip in the snow is the wings' (above), never a crash on its own. The skier in the door is flung clear by the blast (6 m/s out, 5 up; the `plane` crash cause) as the helicopter's rider is. Ten seconds later the ride begins again in the door on the strip.

**Whether it burns** (`wreckIgnites`, `PLANE_WRECK.ignite`) — REALISM: most light-plane crashes do NOT burn. A survey of light-aircraft accidents found post-crash fire in about three in ten fatal ones and a few in a hundred of all, rising with how hard the impact was; crash-resistant fuel systems cut fires in survivable crashes by about two thirds. A fire wants two things: a **tank torn open** and an **ignition source** at the spill — the turbine's hot section (several hundred °C, far over jet fuel's ≈210 °C auto-ignition), its exhaust, sparks, the electrics. So a wreck burns when its tear share (smoothstep from the kind's own speed over 15 m/s: nose 15, spin 16, cartwheel 18, tree 20, belly 26 m/s) times how far its kind brings the spill onto the engine (nose 1, spin 0.85, cartwheel 0.6, tree 0.55, belly 0.45) times the engine's heat (running or just stopped) reaches 0.4, with 2 kg of fuel aboard at least. A nose-in lights from 21.5 m/s, a spin from 23.5, a cartwheel from 27.5, a tree from 30 and a belly slide from 38 m/s; a crash at a landing's speed on the belly, a ground loop, a slow nose-over and a wing torn off alone lie cold — an unlit wreck (`PlaneWreck.fire` false, the crash event's `lit`) burns nothing, chars nothing, scorches nothing and is heard as the airframe's crunch and tearing with no blast (`plane_wreck`). The old plane burnt every wreck, a pool fire always and a ball past 14 m/s: far too often. The thresholds are our own fit to that pattern; no source gives them as numbers.

**The fire** (`PLANE_WRECK`), when it lights: the fuel burns in the air at 70 kg/h idle to 200 kg/h full. A share of it (0.3, ramped in from 14 m/s to all of it at 45 m/s, at least 6 kg) goes up as a fireball sized by the helicopter's law, D = 5.8·m^⅓; how open each kind tears the tanks (`torn`: nose and cartwheel 1, tree 0.9, spin 0.85, belly 0.35) sets the rest; the rest is a **pool fire** of 45 m² burning 0.04 kg/m²·s with Heskestad's flame height, slower in slush, smouldering 120 s. At 200 kg aboard and 50 m/s a nose-in throws a 22.7 m ball for 1.76 s and leaves a pool 3.7 m across burning 103 s under 10.6 m flames; a slow crash only whoomps.

**As drawn**: `plane-shatter.ts` cuts the model into its pieces (the door, the engine, the prop's blades in three pieces each, the tail, the wings in and out, the struts, the gear, the cabin in panels) and breaks them by the kind's plan — folded, torn, thrown or dropped, the blades bent back at idle and curled forward under power — the thrown pieces flown, bounced and slid to rest, the hulk laid into the snow. Its materials char in three heat sets over 14 s to soot, then ash — only when it lit; a wing torn off before the crash is left out of its pieces (it lies where it came down) — hidden at the burst, never the airframe cut again. **Nothing is made at the crash**: the pieces are cut and the ONE fire (`createExplosion`) is made under the loading card (`prime`), and each wreck only resizes that fire (`Explosion.resize`) — a fire made at the crash added its own light to the scene, and a light added has three compile every lit program in the scene again, seconds on a phone with the picture stopped. `plane-wreck-view.ts` lays, when it lit, the burst, the fireball, the pool fire licking up from its middle, the gouge, the smoke leaning with the wind and an orange flickering lamp, and outlives the restart until the fire is out. The crash lens (`camera-crash.ts`, shared with the helicopter) is cut to on the impact and planted well back, clear of trunks, out of where the ball went up and turned out of a low sun's glare.

Its lettering is backlit after dark. `plane-scene.ts`'s `seen(eye)` decides inside off the settled eye, keeps the falling snow out and shows the cockpit within 60 m.

**The cameras** (`camera-plane.ts`, three-free):

**The strip as drawn** (`strip-ground.ts`, `strip-build.ts`, `strip-view.ts`): the runway and apron packed and combed into the ground's own textures with windrows broken at the apron, dyed threshold bars, the boards and boughs, the hangar (clad, an orange band, four door leaves, one ajar on a dark hall), the fuel shed with its drums and pump, the hut with its stovepipe, the windsock posed off the wind (full at 15 kt), edge lamps after dark (white along, green at the ends) and the hangar's floods. The minimap and the start card's chart bake the runway grey with its roofs.

**The HUD** (`plane-hud.ts`, `hud-plane.tsx`): a horizon, the airspeed in knots, the height over the snow with the climb and the altitude, the power, the flaps, the load past 2.2 g, a stall chip and the next press. The skier's own speed, edge bar and wind meter are put away.

**The sound** (`audio/plane-voice.ts`, `plane-bed.ts`, `plane-bank.ts`): sixteen layers over the one instrument — the propeller's tones at the blade-passage rate (110 Hz at full rpm) and its harmonics, the rasp as the tips near Mach 0.9 on the take-off, the swish, the turbine's whine leading on the start, the exhaust, the slipstream through the open door (a third as loud in the cockpit as in the door — the listener's `doorway` column), the skis' rumble and hiss, the stall horn, the flap motor and the wreck's fire, heard at its own distance until it is out. One-shots for boarding and the start, the lift-off, the landing, the stall's buffet, the jump, stepping off, a wingtip's scrape, a wing torn off (a crack, a rip and the fuel's hiss — no blast) and the crash (a cold one only the crunch and the tearing).

## Determinism

Nothing in the plane, the strip or the wreck draws from `state.rng`. The plane is an option of a free ride (`CreateGameOptions.plane`, `GameState.plane`), never a `RunRule`, so no race and no `make sim` digest can see it.

## Measuring it

- `make plane-flight` (pure Node, seconds): the class's numbers off the engine, each beside its band — the take-off roll (219.6 m on the flat, 200–300), the climb (6.2 m/s), the top speed low (113.9 kt) and high (122.0 kt at 2,500 m, 115–125), the stall clean (53.6 kt, 52–58) and with flaps (49.4 kt) and its recovery, a loop, the roll rate (61.5°/s), the glide (9.8), the map's edge turned back with 115.6 m to spare — and the **aerobatic rows** flown on the player's own hand (`samplePlane`): `p-loop` on the flat and over seed 38's strip low and high (360° each), `p-aroll` (7.7 s), `p-barrel`, `p-hammer` (≈180° reversal), `p-invert` (10 s on its back), `p-spin` (three turns and the recovery) and the **naive take-off** — full power and the stick held back from the strip's start, let go 60 m up — on the flat (`p-takeoff`, and `p-takeoff-p` pulled only past 25 m/s) and on seed 38's strip (`p-takeoff-38`): the height 35 s on (> 60 m), where it lifted off and how far off the line; every hand row notes a wing lost. `SEED=` flies the roll on a map's own strip; `ARGS=--json=FILE` before a change and `--compare=FILE` after.
- `make plane` (browser, its own bundle from `pwa/plane-preview.html`): one contact sheet a group in `previews/plane-<group>.png` — `model` (parked from eight sides, by day and after dark), `strip` (the take-off roll and lift-off), `flight` (on the pilot's hands from six sides, the surfaces held over), `door` (the skier crouched in the door), `lenses` (every rung aloft), `cockpit` (the eye on the strip, climbing, banked, over a loop's top and at night; the panel, the displays, the quadrant, the overhead, the pedals, the controls thrown, the pilot from outside), `crash` (the fireball and the wreck burning on at 5, 30 and 90 s, day and night), `crash-kinds` (each kind frame by frame through the crash's lens; `--cols=4`), `wings` (a wingtip scuffed on the take-off roll and flown off, a wing torn off by a trunk and landing, the plane flying on with one wing and the other tumbling away, the torn wing on the snow, a crash that does not burn beside one that does) and `airstrip` (the strip from the approach, overhead and the village, down the runway, the apron, the buildings and the sock, day and night; `--face=<id>` on a real face). `ARGS=--sheet=model,door` a few; `SEED=` / `REGION=` another map.
- `make airstrip` (pure Node): where each map's strip lies — the piste map from above round it (the runs in their colours, the lifts, every building, the streets, the woods, the water), the room the search reads tinted red where a strip's centre line may not run, the strip, its ends and its site — one picture a map in `previews/airstrip-<map>.png` (up to twelve), all of them on `previews/airstrip-sheet.png`, and a table in `previews/airstrip.txt` (the tier, the search's time, the distance from the village, the turn off the valley's axis, the grade, the least room along it). `ARGS="--faces all"` every real face, `--faces alpine-9,fell-23` some, `--seeds 1-8` generated mountains.
- `make build`, then `make screenshots ARGS="--surface plane-strip,plane,plane-door"` (`--timeout 150` on a cold browser).
- The suite: `tests/plane_test.ts`, `input_plane_test.ts`, `airstrip_test.ts`, `air_bounds_test.ts`, `camera_plane_test.ts`, `plane_cockpit_test.ts`, `plane_hud_test.ts`, `plane_card_test.ts`, `plane_wreck_test.ts`, `plane_audio_test.ts`, `facade_test.ts` and `minimap_test.ts`.

## Known gaps

- The spin is steep and fast — airspeed builds to about 130 kt at an angle of attack near 20°, more a spiral than a flat spin; honest for a spin-resistant utility single, but a real developed spin is near 60 kt.
- A barrel roll on a naive held stick comes out nose-low; a pilot modulates it.
- The 900 m plane band covers most strips; the strip's own approach is under the bounds' fade height.
- Seed 3 keeps a little corridor intrusion (no clear room on that valley floor).
- The loose skis, the crowd, the civilians and the other machines do not meet the strip's solids; only the skier and his ragdoll do.
- The cockpit's coaming is flat-shaded low poly.
- The crash lens's planted shots on a nose-in still show the sky's blue in the shadows.
- A wing torn off takes its strut's head with it in the drawing but leaves the strut on the fuselage; the cut is a straight line at the struts' feet, not a torn edge.
- The take-off hand fades out by 150 m: a stick held back for ever past it still stalls, which is the player's own doing.
- A torn wing on the snow is not solid to a skier; the ride begun again keeps it drawn only while the wreck it came off is.
- The ignition thresholds (`PLANE_WRECK.ignite`) and the trunk and limb speeds that tear a wing are our own fit to the accident pattern; no source states them as numbers.
