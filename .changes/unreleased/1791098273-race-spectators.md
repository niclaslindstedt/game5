title: Spectators
---

Every race, time trial and tricks run is now watched by a crowd of thousands, standing where a real race's crowd stands: most of them in the finish arena — grandstands either side of the line, terraces behind the finish circle with its padded boards, exit gate and leader's platform, a video wall over them showing the run's clock, and the finish slope lined rows deep behind the spectator net — and on the mountain at the jumps, on the inside of the hard turns, on the steep pitches and in knots down the bottom of the hill. Every fan is his own (children among them, every build, bright jackets, bobble hats, hoods, scarves, packs) and brings something: a cowbell, a flag, a board, a horn or a phone. They fidget in the cold between racers, then turn to follow each racer as he comes — clapping, waving, ringing, sweeping their flags, jumping, filming — the excitement rippling down the bank with him; the arena roars at a finish and runs a wave along its stands now and then. The free ride's ski area keeps its own people and no audience.
