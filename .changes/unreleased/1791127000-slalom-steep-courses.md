title: The steepest slaloms are skiable — a hill combed smooth, a rounder line on the steep, no tuck between the poles
---

The steepest slaloms of the campaign (the black faces of HIGH CIRQUE and COLD CREST) could not be finished: on their 60–70 % pitches no skid could hold a racer to the speed the line allowed, braking took the edge he needed for the next gate, and two of them were set on a field of lips that threw him off the snow every few metres. A slalom hill is now combed smooth under the course before the gates go in; the line a racer takes is rounded out on a steep pitch — wider of the poles, each turn finished across the hill — and the par is reckoned on that line, so it moved on the steep courses; the bot that skis a slalom (the attract run, the labs) checks its speed with a short skid held until its skis are back on their line, stands its edge down only when a slide is really running away, and skis in a racing stance rather than a tuck. Every slalom rung and every generated slalom now finishes, within 5 % of its par. A racer airborne over a gate is judged by where his feet are, not where they last touched the snow.
