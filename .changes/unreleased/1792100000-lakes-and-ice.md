title: Lakes and streams that freeze and thaw with the season
---

Maps can now carry lakes, ponds, rivers and streams, and each one is open, freezing, frozen or breaking up depending on the map's date, latitude and real altitude. A small pond freezes before a big lake, running water freezes last, and a large low lake may never freeze at all. As water freezes, ice reaches out from the shore before the whole surface closes over. New ice is black with white cracks until snow whitens it, and the wind scours bare patches into a thin dusting. In spring the ice goes grey and rotten, meltwater pools on it, and an open moat forms along the shore before the middle breaks up. Open water ripples in the wind and mirrors the sky and the mountain. All of it is drawn in a single draw call, and a lake buried under snow costs nothing to draw. The real mountains will get their lakes from map data in a follow-up.
