title: A slalom race day — the board, two runs and the start house
---

A slalom now runs the way a race day does. The board lists every racer by bib with the run's time, the gap to the leader and, in the second run, the first run's time and the total, with the disqualified and those who did not finish last and why. A run that misses or straddles a gate, starts late or ends in a fall says so on its own plate instead of a place. The countdown is a small READY and GO over the start house's own clock, the splits at two timing points read green ahead of the leader and red behind, and the best thirty of the first run go again in reverse order, the final ranked on the combined time. The racer drops out of the house the way a slalom racer does — a crouch behind the wand, the chest thrown out over it, the heels kicked and the skis fired through together — onto a steep start cut below the door. A slalom is only ever set on a red or a black whose stretch is a real slalom hill, and the front door's SLALOM tile names the disciplines still to come.
