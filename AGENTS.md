# Agent guidance for Fall Line (game5)

This file is the canonical source of truth for AI coding agents working in this repo. `CLAUDE.md`, `.cursorrules`, `.windsurfrules`, `GEMINI.md`, and `.github/copilot-instructions.md` are symlinks to it (`tests/symlinks_test.ts` holds them).

**This file is the ROUTER, not the manual.** It says how work is done here, where things live at one level of detail, and which skill owns the rest. The procedures — every loop, every lab, every craft rule — live in `.agents/skills/`. Load the skill that owns the task's SUBJECT before starting; do not re-derive from this file what a skill already states.

This repository conforms to [`OSS_GAME_SPEC.md`](OSS_GAME_SPEC.md) — the committed copy IS the spec, self-contained, with no upstream document to fetch and no validator to call; it is a verbatim copy of the one the sibling games carry, one spec for all of them, and amending it is a reviewed PR that propagates the new mandate into the tree. When in doubt about layout, naming, or workflow conventions, the spec is the tie-breaker. Where the repo knowingly falls short of it, [`docs/spec-conformance.md`](docs/spec-conformance.md) is the ledger — one row per chapter, with the verdict, the evidence and what closing the gap would take; the `sync-game-spec` skill walks it.

Around THAT, both platform shells are built: `tauri/`, the desktop app, and `native/`, the store app. **NOT BUILT, and with no placeholder file to build into:** other modes — every race discipline NAMED in `DISCIPLINES` (`engine/game/defs/modes.ts`) is built now, each a mode and a course rule (R31–R36), and a discipline added later is a row there, a mode and a rule of its own. **There is no music, by design:** the game's sound is its effects alone, as in the sibling games — no score is planned. **The slalom is built too:** the race is a real discipline now, the SLALOM (R31) — the old line-start race against three rivals is `fieldRules`, what a run naming no mode is dealt (the suite, the labs, the benchmark), and no mode the player picks. A slalom is SET over a built map (`engine/mapgen/slalom.ts`'s `setSlalom`, its rule in `discipline-rules.ts`, so no digest moves): the steepest stretch of the piste that drops 140–220 m, netted, its kickers levelled and its trees cleared, a START HOUSE over its top with a START DROP out of the door, POLE GATES down it (open gates, hairpins, verticals, delays — flex poles a racer knocks down and that spring back, `gate-poles.ts`) and the finish arena on its run-out; a seed of its own is built to a red. ONE RACER IS EVER ON THE COURSE: the field of 29 is never skied but DEALT as a board off the seed about the course's par (`field.ts`, `par.ts`), and the player races its times. The start as television shows it (`camera-start.ts`: overhead, then behind him in the house), the starter's READY and GO, the start push — the tuck throws him out through the wand in one push (`start-push.ts`, the clip `slalom-start.ts`), and no skating after — the clock started at the wand and a ten-second window after GO; the STRICT gates (`strict.ts`): both feet through every gate, a miss, a straddle or a skipped gate a DSQ, a fall or a reset a DNF (`Progress.out`); the HUD's RUN and its splits against the leader at two timing points, the BOARD on the plate (`slalom-board.ts`, `hud-board.tsx`), and the SECOND RUN — set afresh, the first run's top thirty in reverse order, the final on combined time (`slalom-heat.ts`, `CreateGameOptions.heat`); the audience behind the nets on both sides, thickest at the finish (`spectator-slalom.ts`) (`engine-system`, `collision`, `hud-and-menus`, `campaign`, `crowd`).

**The downhill is built too:** the second race discipline, the DOWNHILL (R32) — the fastest race there is, raced on the Eagle at well over a hundred kilometres an hour. A downhill is SET over a built map (`engine/mapgen/downhill.ts`'s `setDownhill`, its rule in `discipline-rules.ts`, so no digest moves) on the WHOLE piste of the ski area's course with the most vertical (`downhillCourseOf`), over the preparation the slalom shares (`course-prep.ts`'s `prepareCourse`: the start drop, the stretch combed and groomed, the trees cleared — and the CRESTS SHAVED so a jump lands on the downslope); a RACING LINE relaxed to the least curvature inside the course's corridor (`speed-course.ts`'s `speedLineAt`, its signed bend), SPEED GATES on it every ~80 m (`Checkpoint.panels` — two poles and a panel, wide, never on a jump's lip) held by the same strict gates, A-NETS down both sides that catch a racer and put him out (`nets.ts`, a DNF `net`) — driven in hard, he goes down INTO them (the `net` wipeout): the mesh billows round him, drops him to its foot and keeps him, and his skis hook in it more often than not (`catchInNets`, the drawn net bulging round them — `net-bulge.ts`), a SPEED TRAP on the fastest long straight (`speed-trap.ts`, `Progress.trap`) and four intermediates. ONE RUN, after a TRAINING run that counts for nothing (`Field.training`, `CreateGameOptions.training`, `downhill-run.ts`); the field of 29 dealt about the course's par (`field.ts`'s `DOWNHILL_FIELD`, `par.ts`'s `downhillPar`: a forward pass of the speed down the line, capped by what the pair can hold round each bend), a speed through the trap dealt to each; the bot follows the line (`sim/downhill-steer.ts`) on the downhill technique, cut hard. The HUD's race chip, splits and plate are the slalom's, shared as `snapshot.ts`'s `RaceHud` (`raceOf`) with the trap beside them; `mapgen/race-course.ts`'s `raceCourseOf` is the one question "which race is set on this map" the nets, the start house and the audience ask (`collision`, `engine-system`, `hud-and-menus`, `campaign`).

**The super-G is built too:** the third race discipline, the SUPER-G (R33) — the speed event whose gates TURN the racer, one run skied unseen (no training: the course is inspected, never skied), on the Falcon (the super-G pair, built to the class's rule: 210 cm, 45 m, 94/65/79 mm). A super-G is SET over a built map (`engine/mapgen/super-g.ts`'s `setSuperG`, its rule in `discipline-rules.ts`, so no digest moves) on the downhill's hill — the ski area's course with the most vertical (`superGCourseOf`) — from a START LOWERED down it until the drop to the finish is 600 m at the most (`superGStart`), prepared as a downhill's (`prepareCourse`, its crests shaved for a super-G's speed); its JUMPS its drops and the crests a racer leaves the snow on; ~42 gates on 600 m (7 % of the vertical, ~49 m apart, a rhythm dealt off the seed), each two panelled pairs red and blue in turn (`Checkpoint.panels` with a `turn`), its turning pole inside the apex of a RACING LINE that is the downhill's least-bending line SWUNG to each gate's side in turn; the A-nets, a speed trap and three intermediates. What it shares with the downhill is one module: `mapgen/speed-course.ts` (`speedCourseOf` — the one question "which speed course is on this map" the nets, the trap, the bot and the par ask — `racingLine`, `speedLineAt`, `trapArc`, `gateArcs`). The field of 29 is dealt about par (`field.ts`'s `SUPER_G_FIELD`: wider apart and oftener out than a downhill's; `par.ts`'s `superGPar`, the downhill's forward pass under the super-G technique); the bot is the downhill's line-follower read closer (`SUPER_G_STEER`) on the super-G technique (`SUPER_G_TECHNIQUE`, its edge held at speed). Its NINE PINNED MAPS are its own (`pwa/src/game/race-maps.ts`'s `RACE_MAPS.superG`, off the campaign, all open, held by `tests/race_maps_superg_test.ts`), picked on its level card off the RACE CARD (`menu-races.tsx`) the front door's one RACE tile opens (`collision`, `engine-system`, `bot-improvement`, `hud-and-menus`, `menu-system`, `campaign`).

**The giant slalom is built too:** the sixth race discipline, the GIANT SLALOM (R36) — the technical race between the slalom and the speed events, ~20 m turns at 60–80 km/h, TWO RUNS on two courses set on the same hill, raced on the Chough (the giant slalom pair, built to the class's rule: 1.93 m on a 30 m sidecut). A giant slalom is SET over a built map (`engine/mapgen/giant-slalom.ts`'s `setGiantSlalom(level, run)`, its rule in `discipline-rules.ts`, so no digest moves) on the super-G's hill — the ski area's course with the most vertical (`giantSlalomCourseOf`) — from a START LOWERED down it until the drop to the finish is 400 m at the most, inside the top level's 250–450 m (`giantSlalomStart`), prepared as a super-G's; its gates ~13 % of the vertical in direction changes, 18–42 m apart down the hill (27 aimed at, a rhythm dealt off the seed), each two panelled pairs 6 m wide red and blue in turn, the line passing 3 m outside the turning pole and swung 2.5–4.5 m either side of the least-bending line, never tighter than a 22 m bend; nets, two intermediates and no speed trap. It is the super-G's course with a technical race's numbers: both are set by ONE module, `mapgen/turn-course.ts` (`mostVerticalCourseOf`, `loweredStart`, `turnCourse` — the start house, the preparation, the line swung half a cosine from apex to apex round the gates, the nets), a discipline's salt its only difference, and `super-g.ts` is thin over it; the giant slalom's row alone carries `towers`, which holds the line 4 m off any lift tower standing on the piste, eased over 30 m (so no super-G moved). The SECOND RUN is set afresh on the same stretch off a salt of the run's own (`Level.giantSlalom`'s `run`), skied by the first run's best thirty in reverse order, the final on combined time, as the slalom's (`slalom-heat.ts`'s `twoRunMode`). The field of 29 is dealt about par (`field.ts`'s `GIANT_SLALOM_FIELD`: closer than a slalom's, out of a run a tenth to a quarter of the time; `par.ts`'s `giantSlalomPar`, the downhill's forward pass down the swung line under the giant slalom technique, scaled 1.11 by the bot's own median); the bot is the super-G's line-follower read closer still (`GIANT_SLALOM_STEER`, `steerOf`) on `GIANT_SLALOM_TECHNIQUE`, taking each turn at 0.85 of the grip it could cut. Its NINE PINNED MAPS are its own (`RACE_MAPS.giantSlalom`, all open, held by `tests/race_maps_giantslalom_test.ts`), picked on its level card off the race card; `?start=gs` boots straight into its first run, `?run=2` its second; its words are `strings-giantslalom.ts` (`collision`, `engine-system`, `mapgen-improvement`, `bot-improvement`, `hud-and-menus`, `campaign`).

**Speed skiing is built too:** the fourth race discipline, SPEED SKIING (R34) — straight down the fall line as fast as a body can go, the result a SPEED. Its course is not on the piste: a TRACK OF ITS OWN cut straight down the face (`engine/mapgen/speed-ski.ts`'s `setSpeedSki`, its rule in `discipline-rules.ts`, so no digest moves) — a line searched over every column of the face and a few bearings either side of the fall line, its profile combed, never rising, its crests cut and its knees filled round so nothing at 200 km/h leaves the snow or is slammed into it, graded 30 m wide with a 25 m safety margin cleared either side; a launch of 300–900 m, a 100 m TIMING ZONE, a run-out sized to stop a racer stood up into the wind (its BRAKING LINE, then a skid) and a finish enclosure at its foot (`speedSkiLines`), the speed each track is built for dealt off its seed in the top class's band. Raced on the PEREGRINE (the speed ski: 2.40 m, under 10 cm wide, next to no sidecut, under a racer in an airtight suit and calf fairings — a tuck of 0.08 m², `defs/skis.ts`) with the speed skier's technique (`SPEED_SKI_TECHNIQUE`, 20° of edge at most). TWO RUNS: a QUALIFICATION from a start lowered 12 km/h down the track, then the FINAL from the top for the best 20, slowest first, ranked on the final alone (`field.ts`'s `SPEED_SKI_FIELD`, `slalom-heat.ts`'s `twoRunMode`). The clock runs between the zone's two lines only, each crossing read to the fraction of a step (`strict.ts`, `course.ts`'s `crossingShare`) — the speed is the zone over that time, and the HUD, the plate and the board read every time as a speed (`speed-ski-run.ts`); par is the clean run itself, SKIED in a full tuck in the race's weather (`par.ts`'s `speedSkiPar`); past the zone the racer UNTUCKS, rides the wind and skids only past the braking line (`run.ts`'s `runOut`); the bot holds the middle (`sim/speed-ski-steer.ts`); the jury's anemometer stands at the zone (`jury.ts`). Its NINE RACE MAPS (`RACE_MAPS.speedSki`, 187–225 km/h finals, `tests/race_maps_speedski_test.ts`) (`collision`, `engine-system`, `ski-physics`, `bot-improvement`, `hud-and-menus`, `campaign`).

**The ski cross is built too:** the fifth race discipline, the SKI CROSS (R35) — four racers on the course at once, out of one start gate and down a course BUILT in the snow. Its course is its own line weaving down the gentlest long stretch of the piste (`engine/mapgen/ski-cross.ts`'s `setSkiCross`, its rule in `discipline-rules.ts`, so no digest moves; a seed of its own takes the ski area's course `skiCrossCourseOf` picks): a start straight, legs swung either side of the piste's line, every corner rounded into a turn and the big ones BANKED into BERMS, ROLLERS, table JUMPS and STEP-DOWNS built on the straights, a start platform with a START RAMP below four DOORS (`Level.grid` the lanes, the first turn's inside chosen first), a FENCE along both edges (`netsOf`, caught like a downhill's A-nets) and triangular GATE FLAGS — a turning gate on every berm's inside, a corridor gate before every feature (`Checkpoint.flags`, judged by `strict.ts`, a gate missed a DNF). Raced on the WOLVERINE (the ski-cross pair: a giant-slalom-type ski cut down, 1.88 m on a 24 m sidecut, `defs/skis.ts`) with the ski-cross technique (`SKI_CROSS_TECHNIQUE`). THE FORMAT (`engine/game/cross-bracket.ts`, pure): a timed QUALIFICATION alone against a board dealt about par (`field.ts`'s `SKI_CROSS_FIELD`, `par.ts`'s `skiCrossPar` — the bot's own clean run), then a BRACKET of heats of four — quarter-finals seeded 1-8-9-16, semi-finals, the small final and the big final — the first two of each heat through, a heat the player is not in dealt off the race's seed, the final ranking the bracket's. A HEAT is SKIED (`engine/game/cross-heat.ts`): "skiers ready", "attention", the doors dropping at a moment dealt 1–4 s later (`crossCountdown`, `RunRules.start` "gate"), the pull out of the gate (`start-push.ts`), three of the start list beside the player in their lanes, their paces their skills, each holding and passing in a lane of his own (`crossLane`), a share of his drag taken off tucked in behind a rival (`stepDrafts`, `SkierState.draft`, read by `air.ts`); CONTACT is judged (`RunRules.knock`, `judgeContact`): a shoulder hard enough puts a racer down, and a knock-down from behind is a RED CARD for the racer behind (`RunOut.why` "contact"). The race rides the run as `GameState.bracket` (`CreateGameOptions.bracket`); the app's side is `ski-cross-run.ts` (the HUD's round and the gate's commands, the plate's next heat, `nextBracket`), `hud-cross.tsx` (the plate) and `strings-skicross.ts` (`collision`, `engine-system`, `mapgen-improvement`, `bot-improvement`, `hud-and-menus`, `campaign`, `crowd`).

**Piste grades are built too:** every map the current generator (v2) builds is built to a PISTE GRADE (R23, `engine/mapgen/grades.ts`) — GREEN, BLUE, RED or BLACK, asked for (the free ride's GRADE row, `?grade=`, a campaign map's own) or dealt off the seed — its steepest hundred metres held inside the colour's band (a green's 16 %, a blue's 27 %, a red's 47 %, a black past that), the mountain's vertical, fall line, width, kickers, cliffs and grooming the grade's own; a BLACK drops off the start hut onto its pitch and carries DROPS across the piste (R24, `drops.ts`) and cliffs beside it; the face is turned to the sun (R15). The grade is signed the way a ski area signs it (a green circle, a blue square, a red rectangle, a black diamond — `grade-look.ts`, `grade-mark.tsx`) on the cards, the HUD and the piste's own edge poles (`mapgen-improvement`, `campaign`).

**The campaign is built too:** the front door's lit tile opens FOUR SHELVES, each one SEED's ski area in a kind of country and three to five of its courses (RIME WOODS in the maritime, HIGH CIRQUE in the alpine, FROST BASIN and COLD CREST on two continental massifs — reds and blacks, sixteen in all), each a seed on a generator VERSION with a digest the suite holds it to; a rung is a SLALOM — only on a red or a black whose slalom stretch is a real slalom hill — or a DOWNHILL — on a black, two of them — skied for points against the field (a podium at a slalom's first-run flag or a downhill's race opens the next map; the shelf's table opens the next shelf). A SLALOM and a DOWNHILL are raced on their discipline's own NINE RACE MAPS (`race-maps.ts`), chosen because their course makes a good race of it, all open — so a record is a record down a piste anyone can ski; a seed of one's own is the free ride's and a link's, and the pause card names a measured run's seed, country and grade so its mountain can be free-ridden (`campaign`, `level-rating`).

**Crashes are built too:** a trunk met hard, a landing over the tips, a fall at speed or an EDGE CAUGHT (the sideways slip at a contact past what an edge that far over can hold — the high-side) throws the skier off onto a body of his own — a ragdoll, jointed like a body, that goes over, is dragged flat by the snow (at once in deep powder) and lies in it, the skis carrying on down the slope without him — until the reset stands him back up; a skier stopped in deep powder sinks to the knees, BOGGED, and is poled and rocked back out (the tuck, the lean and the edge); in deep powder every trunk stands in a TREE WELL — a funnel of loose snow, deepest at the trunk and opening downhill — that a skier who comes too close slides into and is held in; and, behind an OPTIONS switch that is off by default, a blow dulls an edge or hurts the legs and the skier rides it for the rest of the run, read off a HUD instrument (`crash`).

**Gear is built too:** the skier is DRESSED — an OUTFIT off a catalog (`outfit.ts`): a BODY (a man or a woman, each the anthropometric survey's mean measure on the one skeleton every pose and the ragdoll share, a woman's braid out from under her helmet), a WEIGHT (four builds — light, medium, solid, heavy — the one row the ENGINE skis too, `engine/game/defs/riders.ts`'s `withRider`: a heavier skier faster in a tuck, slower to skate up to speed, landing harder and shouldering harder, cut to the build by the dress — broader, a solid one through the chest and a heavy one with a stomach; the medium build is every pair's own row, handed back to the bit), a JACKET (a race shell, a down puffer, a freeride shell cut long, an anorak, a retro one), PANTS (insulated, a race softshell, baggy, cargo), a HELMET (race, freeride, visor, slalom with its chin guard), GLOVES (gauntlet, mittens, undercuff, race) and POLES (alloy, carbon, a downhill racer's bent pair, powder baskets), every piece SOLD IN ITS OWN COLOURS and picked on the DRESS card behind the ski card's CUSTOMIZE SKIER press; each rival wears his own, his jacket in his slot's colour. Every piece is CUT IN CODE onto the rig in its bind pose and weighted across its bones (`dress-loft.ts`), so the cloth bends with every pose as one skin and nothing is drawn under it; `make gear` is the lab (`skier-gear`).

**The body is built too:** every blow a skier takes — a landing, the hips, the shoulders or the helmet on the snow, every point of the ragdoll meeting the snow or a trunk, a rival's shoulder — is a STOP over the body's own give and the snow's (ice, the groomer, loose snow by its depth — fitted to measured head drops on snow), its peak in g; twenty parts each carry a LADDER of injuries ranked on the Abbreviated Injury Scale (a sprained thumb, a torn cruciate, a bruised kidney, a broken collarbone, a concussion…), each an even chance at a dose of its own mechanism (a blow, a landing's load, the back seat's drawer, a caught edge's twist, the boot-top lever) on a log-logistic risk curve drawn off a hash, never the stream; the whole body summed as an INJURY SEVERITY SCORE; every fracture names the BONES it cracks (a HAIRLINE) or breaks, thirty of them from the skull to the feet. A readout — nothing in the physics reads it — and a RESET MENDS IT, except with the INJURIES switch on, where he is stood back up as hurt as he lay, where he left the piste and facing down it, and SKIS HURT (`hurt.ts`, `defs/hurt.ts`: the leg on the outside of a turn standing the skis over less and tipping them slower, each ski's edge holding by its own leg, the turn-in slowed by the head, the drive by the arms, the tuck and the landings by the legs and the trunk — each a share of a part's worst AIS, never below a floor) until the injuries he keeps adding up kill him and the run starts again from the top. ## The framework

The generic half of this game lives in [`@niclaslindstedt/oss-game-framework`](https://github.com/niclaslindstedt/oss-game-framework) — a git dependency on a release TAG (`package.json`), shared with the sibling games and extracted from them. From it come: the engine-safe pool (`core/*`: the PRNG, the math and `hypot`, noise, heightfields, quaternions, the sun and the moon, the output sink) and the racing primitives (`racing/*`: the control tape a ghost and a replay are kept on, the record book's policy, the field's standings); the synthesized instrument (`audio/*`: the vocabulary, the one WebAudio synth, `playDef`, the rack, the fader views); the screenshot roll, store, thumbnails, sharing, stamp and HUD raster (`shots/*`); the update watch (`pwa/*`); the canvas and viewport sizes (`display/*`); the press, the thumb guard and the cursor (`input/*`); the race clock's formatting and the counter (`hud/*`); the fixed-step run clock (`loop/*`); and the lab shelf and release plumbing (`tooling/*`, the `ogf-*` bins). What is THIS game's (the snow, the skis, the mountain, the ghost's axes, which modes keep a book) stays here, over those modules.

**A bug in one of those modules is fixed in the framework**, released there, and pulled in by moving the tag here — never patched by a local copy, which is the drift the framework exists to end. **THE ENGINE LINE:** `engine/` may import `@niclaslindstedt/oss-game-framework/core/*` and `/racing/*` (file or module subpaths) and nothing else from any package, never `core/clock` (the wall clock); `tests/imports_test.ts` holds it, and holds the framework's `core/` and `racing/` source to the engine's own hygiene (no `Math.random`, no clock, no console). The determinism digests are cut under that code, so a framework release that moves one is a breaking one.

## Build and test commands

```sh
npm install       # everything resolves from the public npm registry
make build        # typecheck (both programs) + production build (pwa/dist/)
make test         # vitest over the engine and the DOM-free app modules (SHARD=i/N slices it; CI runs six)
make lint         # eslint + typecheck, zero warnings
make fmt          # prettier in place over the code and config (never Markdown — .prettierignore); fmt-check is what CI runs
make hooks        # install pre-commit + commit-msg hooks
make icons        # regenerate icons/favicon from the app mark
make tauri-test   # the desktop shell's decision layer (Rust; needs no GUI libraries)
make native-typecheck  # the store shell's own tsc (its tree is installed on its own)
```

That is the everyday set. **NEITHER PLATFORM SHELL IS ON THE ROOT SUITE'S PATH** — `make test` and `make lint` stop at `tauri/`'s and `native/`'s edges, and each tree is checked by its own peers: `make tauri-test` / `tauri-lint` / `tauri-fmt` (`.github/workflows/desktop-tauri.yml` runs them on every push that touches the tree) and `make native-typecheck` (`native.yml` runs it before an EAS build, and eslint ignores `native/**`). What the root suite does hold is what each shell RESTATES and cannot import: `tests/tauri_test.ts` reads the Rust as text, and `tests/shell_test.ts` / `tests/rumble_test.ts` read the store shell's four import-free modules directly. **The full list is the README's Usage table, and the `Makefile` is the authority.** The table below says which of them a given change OWES.

**Scope the linter, never the typechecker, and leave the suite to CI.** `npx eslint <changed files>` is seconds where the whole repo is tens; a typecheck must stay whole-program, because it checks a PROGRAM (naming files makes it ignore `tsconfig.json`) and because a changed signature breaks its CALLERS. **And "whole" is TWO programs: `npx tsc --noEmit` at the root does not read `pwa/src` at all** — `npm run typecheck:only` runs both and is what `make build` gates on.

**`make fmt` is the Make target to run; `make test` is CI's.** The suite builds whole mountains and skis the bot down them at 120 Hz, and CI fans it out on every push — so locally, run the FILES that cover the change (`npx vitest run tests/<topic>_test.ts`) and let the PR find the rest. The Make target is still the definition of green.

## How work is done here

Rules that apply to every task, before any subject skill has a say. They are restated here from the skills that own them because a session that gets them wrong gets them wrong from its first tool call:

- **LOOK AT THE SIBLING REPOS BEFORE BUILDING ANYTHING.** This game was built by porting the non-sled parts of [`niclaslindstedt/game4`](https://github.com/niclaslindstedt/game4) (the snowmobile racer — the closest sibling: the same snow, the same layering, the same app shell, HUD, audio, shells, labs and skills, retyped for a skier), which was itself ported from [`niclaslindstedt/game3`](https://github.com/niclaslindstedt/game3) (the jet-ski racer) with land-vehicle ideas from [`niclaslindstedt/game2`](https://github.com/niclaslindstedt/game2) (the rally game); all four share [`OSS_GAME_SPEC.md`](OSS_GAME_SPEC.md). **Read their answer first and adapt it; do not reinvent one.** They are public: `git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/niclaslindstedt/game4` (or `game3`, `game2`), read-only; nothing in this tree may import from any of them — what comes across is the DESIGN, retyped in our vocabulary: a sled, a craft or a car is a pair of skis and the skier on them, a basin or a shore is a mountain, a closed loop is an open piste, a checkpoint or a buoy is a gate, a lap is the run, a tread's throttle is the tuck and its brake the skid.
  - **What is ours alone:** the skier's answer to the snow (the edge, the skid, the tuck, the legs, the poles — `ski-physics`), the mountain and its piste (`mapgen-improvement`), the skis and the skier as drawn (`ski-design`, `skier`). The snowmobile game's snow — the sink, the plough, the float, the six kinds of snow, the trail map — came across as the IDEA and was restated for a pair of skis: a tread's traction limit or a planing hull's draft ported into any of it is how this game stops being about skiing — take the idea and state it for an edge.
  - **What comes across nearly unchanged:** tooling and labs, the menu and shell furniture, the maintenance and release plumbing, store and platform work, the weather, the wildlife, the models pipeline, and the procedure half of any skill. Adapting a sibling skill is a real port — its subject must exist HERE first, and it lands with its name and routing added to this file, `.agents/skills/README.md` and the `maintenance` registry, which `tests/skills_test.ts` holds.
- **Lint, typecheck and format ONCE, at the gate — not after every edit.** `make fmt` and `make lint` are the commit's gate (the `commit` skill owns the split). Mid-loop, if a specific answer is genuinely needed, check only the files you touched (`npx eslint <paths>`, `npx tsc --noEmit`) — never a whole-repo pass, and never `prettier`, whose every finding `make fmt` fixes at the end for free.
- **TEST WHAT YOU WROTE; THE PR TESTS THE REST.** Run the suites that cover the change and the ones it plausibly reaches, by file, and push — a red PR is a normal state and a follow-up commit costs nothing. Be honest about reach: a change to `TUNING`, `skis.ts`, `snow.ts` or the generator reaches tests three directories away (a sink retune moves `skier_test`, `flight_test`, `simulation_test` and `determinism_test`'s digests at once; a rules change moves `mapgen_test`, `analysis_test`, `docs_rules_test` and the sim), so name the topics generously for those; and a red PR is work NOW, not something to leave sitting.
- **THIS IS AN OPEN-SOURCE REPOSITORY — ANYONE CAN READ EVERY LINE OF IT.** Write everything that enters the tree for a stranger who will read it: the code, every comment, the docs, the skills and their lessons, the changeset fragments, the commit messages and the PR text. That means nothing private and nothing you would not publish under the project's name — no remark about a person, no internal note or complaint, no half-thought addressed to one reader, no context that only makes sense inside one conversation. Nothing that is someone else's to publish goes in either: research is restated in our own words as the facts and numbers it gave (a rulebook, an article, a paper's prose is never pasted, and a third party's picture, model, sound or font is never committed without a licence that allows it), and a source is named only where naming it breaks none of the rules below. A comment explains the code to the next reader, whoever they are.
- **THIS REPOSITORY IS PUBLIC — no personal details go in it.** Team ids, account names, tokens, keys, device ids, e-mail addresses, absolute paths under a home directory: none of them are committed, not even the ones that are identifiers rather than secrets, and not as a "default" a contributor can override. Everything of that kind is read from the ENVIRONMENT — a gitignored `.env` beside the tree that needs it (with the committed `.env.example` documenting the shape), and GitHub repository **secrets** for credentials or **variables** for identifiers on CI. The published app's own identifiers (`APP_NAME`, `SITE_URL`, `REPO_URL`) are the deliberate exception — they live in `pwa/src/identity.ts`.
- **NAME NO REAL PRODUCT, PLACE OR EVENT — not a game, not a ski, not a resort, not a race.** Nothing in this tree names another game, a games console, a ski or boot maker or one of its models, a resort, a named piste, a named mountain or range, a named race or its course, or any other real brand: not in prose, not in a doc, not in a comment, not in a skill or a lesson, and least of all in anything the player reads. **The feel reference is the arcade winter racers of the late 90s** — named as a GENRE, never as a title. Where a real class's numbers are the honest source for a proportion or a tuning value, keep the NUMBER and the band it sits in and drop the badge ("a giant slalom ski is 27–30 m of sidecut at 1.88 m", never a make and model; "a downhill course is 3–4.5 km over 850–1030 m of vertical", never its name). The generic words — skis, a pair, the skier, a piste, the alpine, a fell — are descriptions and stay; so do the sibling repos `game2`, `game3` and `game4`, which are ours. A cited scientific model is not a brand either (Coulomb, Bekker, Kasten–Young are named on purpose).
- **Every work session ends by committing its work with the `commit` skill.** Once the requested change and its gates are complete, load and follow that skill; when working in a worktree, follow its required sync step afterward.

## Architecture summary

Three layers, one direction of dependency (details: [docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md); enforced by `tests/imports_test.ts`):

- **`engine/`** — the whole game as a framework-free, renderer-free TypeScript module that imports nothing but itself and the shared framework's engine-safe half (`core/*` and `racing/*` — see *The framework* above). Fixed 120 Hz `step(state, input)` (`TUNING.physicsHz`; the bot decides on every step too), deterministic per seed (no `Math.random` at runtime — everything draws from the seeded RNG in state, `state.rng`). Contains the world generator (`mapgen/`), the skier on the snow (`game/skier.ts` summing `suspension.ts` — the legs — `snow.ts`, `poles.ts`, `flight.ts`, `chassis.ts`), the trees and the edge (`game/collision.ts`), the course (`game/course.ts`), the race and the field (`game/step.ts`, `run.ts`, `rivals.ts`), the bot skier + headless simulator (`sim/`), the generator's scoreboard (`analysis/` — dev-time AND the generator's accept gate), and the data (`game/defs/`). `engine/index.ts` is the one public surface; `@engine` is how every host spells it.
- **`pwa/`** — the browser shell: Preact app, three.js renderer (reads `GameState`, never steps physics — the terrain is the engine's own heightfield, and the trail map is stamped from the engine's own contacts), input, HUD, the audio (`game/audio/` — every sound synthesized over one WebAudio instrument, the framework's `audio/synth`; nothing is a file), PWA plumbing (hand-rolled service worker via `pwa-plugin.ts` + the framework's update watch, read through `lib/pwa-update.ts`).
- **`tests/` + `scripts/`** — root-level vitest suites over the engine and the DOM-free app modules, and Node tooling (the sim CLI, the level map, the analyzer, the ride lab, the world lab, the audition page, screenshots, the profiler, icons, release plumbing; every tool parses its flags through the framework's `tooling/cli`, which gives it `--help` and a non-zero exit on an unknown flag).

Beside them, OUTSIDE the npm workspace and outside the root suite's path, the two shells that wrap the built site, both BUILT: **`tauri/`** (the desktop app) is two Rust crates, `shell/` for every decision and `src-tauri/` for every effect, with its own `make tauri*` targets, its own workflow and a packaging matrix in `release.yml`; **`native/`** (the App Store / Play Store app) is an Expo/React Native WebView over a copy of the site packed inside it, with its own `make native-*` targets and a dispatch-only EAS workflow. **Nothing in `engine/` may learn either exists, and the ONE file of `pwa/` that does is `pwa/src/shell-host.ts`** (the frozen `__SH_SHELL__` global and the `sh-` events around it). A feature a shell needs is a feature the website needs first, and a menu row may only press a button the game already has.

The root suite may import from a shell in exactly one place: the pure, import-free seam modules named in `tests/imports_test.ts`'s `SHELL_SEAM` (`native/src/injected.ts`, `navigation.ts`, `rumble.ts`, `cloud-ask.ts`). A seam module that grows an import fails that test. The desktop shell's Rust is held the other way, as TEXT (`tests/tauri_test.ts`).

**Hard rules:** the engine never imports three.js, Preact, `node:` or anything from `pwa/` or `scripts/`; the renderer never mutates `GameState`; engine randomness only via `state.rng` (`tests/determinism_test.ts` + `tests/imports_test.ts` enforce it); source files stay under 1000 lines (`tests/file_size_test.ts`, §20.5's marker for the honest exceptions); the engine prints only through the framework's `core/output`. **Every hill, every tree, every bird and animal, every mark of the course and every sound is code** — the trees built procedurally by `tree-shapes.ts` (chunky, faceted, ten variants a kind at three levels of detail, each trunk drawn to its tree's age), the wildlife by `bird-shapes.ts` and `beast-shapes.ts` and the gates, the hut and the arch by `mark-shapes.ts`, all on `tree-mesh.ts`'s bench; **the skier is DRESSED in code** — his outfit (`outfit.ts`) cut on the loom (`dress-loft.ts`, `dress-garments.ts`, `dress-head.ts`) and skinned on the rig (`skier-dress.ts`), since the gear is a catalog to mix; **the skis are MODELS** — glTFs made in Blender off the game's own data by `make models`, committed in `pwa/models/` with a stamp of their sources (`tests/models_test.ts` fails a model older than what it is made from — `make models` and commit), packed by every build (`pwa/models-plugin.ts`) and drawn by `skier-models.ts` in place of the code's drawn parts, posed off the same readings. The code-built ones are still built and are one switch away: `VITE_MODEL_SKIS=0` (the environment, the root `.env`, or — for every CI build, no commit — `make ci-models MODELS=off`).

### The role map, and what is generated

The spec (§23) names roles, not directories: `engine/` is the **simulation core** (§23.1) with `engine/index.ts` as its one public entry surface; `pwa/` is the **presentation shell** (§23.2); `tauri/` and `native/` are **platform shells** (§23.3); `scripts/` is **tooling** (§23.6) and may import anything while nothing imports it. There is no session service — the game is single-player (§34 does not apply). The core imports nothing from a shell or a script; a shell imports the core, never another shell; the suite reaches the core through `@engine` like a host does.

**Content (§23.5) is the deliberate deviation**: the maps are GENERATED from a seed rather than authored, and the small fixed catalogs (`engine/game/defs/skis.ts`, `tuning.ts`, `modes.ts`; `mapgen/rules.ts`) are TypeScript consts rather than schema-validated data files. `docs/spec-conformance.md` carries the reasoning — do not start converting a catalog to data on the strength of §24 alone.

### Stated once — never restate these

Each of these is the one place an answer is written down. Anything that needs it ASKS; a second copy is a bug the day one of them moves.

- **WHAT A MAP IS** — `generateLevel(seed, opts?)` in `engine/mapgen/generate.ts`, returning the `Level` in `engine/mapgen/types.ts`: the heightfield and its three queries (`groundAt`, `normalAt`, `packedAt` — bilinear samples of grids baked once), the OPEN `track` (the piste: points every 2 m with `s`, heading, width, height, `closed: false`, its first point the start line's row), the `checkpoints` (index 0 the START GATE a few metres down from the start line, the last the FINISH LINE at `s = length`), the `spawn` and the `grid` (four abreast, the player's slot first), the `trees`, the `sun`, `laps` (always 1), and — optional in the type so a synthetic map need not invent them — the packed field, the `kickers`, the `mountain` (its summit, its base and the vertical between) and the attempt. A map is a pure function of its seed: each attempt draws everything off `subSeed(seed, attempt)` in a fixed order and is accepted only if `analyzeLevel` finds it clean. Extend the shape; never rename a field without moving every reader.
- **WHAT THE RULES ARE** — `engine/mapgen/rules.ts`: R1–R24 stated once in the header as prose and every number in `LEVEL_RULES`. The generator BUILDS to them, `engine/analysis/` HOLDS the finished map to them, the tests assert them, and `docs/level-generator.md` carries the prose verbatim.
- **WHICH GENERATOR BUILT A MAP** — `engine/mapgen/versions.ts`. `CURRENT_GENERATOR_VERSION` is the rules in this tree and is what everything but a campaign map gets; a campaign map names its own (`CampaignLevel.version`, written out on every map on purpose) and carries the DIGEST of the map that came out (`levelDigest`), which `tests/generator_version_test.ts` rebuilds and compares. A change that moves what a seed builds owes a version row with the old behaviour kept as a trait — never a regenerated digest, which is the silent re-roll the scheme exists to prevent.
- **WHICH MAP A MEASURED RUN IS ON** — `pinnedFor` in `pwa/src/game/campaign.ts`: a SLALOM, a SUPER-G and a DOWNHILL race their discipline's race map the level card picked (`Settings.raceMap`, `race-maps.ts`), a campaign rung its own map, a FREE RIDE its own seed, and a link's `?seed=` takes the pinned map off for that visit. A campaign RUNG is booked on its board by `campaign-run.ts`'s rig and filed in the record book like any other measured run.
- **WHERE ON THE PISTE A POINT IS** — `nearestTrackPoint(level, x, z)` in `engine/mapgen/query.ts` (off a lazily built spatial hash), with `trackPointAt`, `arcAhead` and `arcBetween` beside it — none of them wrapping, because the piste is open: `trackPointAt` clamps to `[0, length]`, `arcAhead(a, b)` is `b − a` and may be negative. The start, the forest, the analysis, the physics, the course, the bot and the renderer all ask it; nothing walks the piste a second way.
- **WHAT THE SNOW DOES TO A STATION** — `engine/game/snow.ts`: `sinkTarget` (the support under the untouched surface, rising with speed as a ski floats up onto the powder), `powderFloor`, `snowDrag`, `gripAt` (what an edge holds and a flat base does not). The packed share it blends by is the level's `packedAt` and nothing else (under the run's new snow and, on a free ride, the day's skied-up share — `packedUnder`) — the physics' grip, the renderer's groomed look and the audio's hiss-to-whoosh crossfade all read that one field (the last through `SkierState.packed`).
- **WHAT THE SKIS LEAVE IN THE SNOW** — `SkierState.contacts` (`SnowContact`: every station's footprint on the surface — `station: tip | mid | tail` of its ski — its `sink`, width, compression, load, `touching`), written by the engine every step; `drawnDepth` in `pwa/src/game/trail-stamp.ts` is the ONE place the drawn groove is decided — the physics' sink or the powder's own furrow, whichever is deeper, never shallower, cut narrower and deeper the more edge the ski stands on — and `trail-map.ts`'s `trailAt` the one decoder the terrain reads it back with.
- **What the speedo reads** — `SkierState.speed`: `|v|`, vertical included, written once at the end of `stepSkier`. The HUD, the bot and the sim all read it and none restates it; "how fast forwards" is `way`.
- **WHAT A BLOW DOES TO THE BODY** — `engine/game/body.ts`: `blowOf` (a stop over a give, in g), `snowGive` (the snow's half of it), `riskOf` (the dose to a chance), `severityOf` (the injury severity score); the catalog every number is in is `defs/anatomy.ts`. The HUD's tile (`body-tile.ts`), the news line and the ride lab all read it; nothing restates a give or a threshold.
- **What a skier CAN do** — `engine/game/limits.ts` (`topSpeedOf`, `terminalSpeed`, `edgeLockAt`, `lockAt`, `carveCurvature`, `carveMost`, `carveSpeedOf`, `brakeDecel`, `cornerGrip`, `chatterOf`, `chatterHold`, `harshSpeedOf`, `tipLimit`), read by the physics AND `sim/bot.ts`. Never restate a ceiling. `SKIS.topSpeed` is an EXPECTATION the physics is held to (`tests/skier_test.ts` — flat out in a tuck on the reference pitch), not an input.
- **Every shared number** — `TUNING` in `engine/game/defs/tuning.ts`, each with its unit, the timestep derived from `physicsHz` (`dt = 1 / physicsHz`) rather than restated; a pair's own numbers are `SKIS` (the reference pair) and `SKI_CATALOG` in `defs/skis.ts`; the field's on a start line are `RACE` and the slalom's `SLALOM` in `defs/modes.ts`. The renderer never reads `TUNING`.
- **The sign conventions** — heading 0 = +z, clockwise from above; pitch TIPS-UP positive; roll RIGHT-SIDE-DOWN positive; body rates right-handed about right / up / forward, so a tips-up pitch rate is a NEGATIVE `wx`; `steer` and `edge` positive to the right. The framework's `core/quat` `fromEuler` / `toEuler` own the flip. The one place the SCREEN's axes become the engine's signs is `pwa/src/game/input-model.ts`.
- **The heading to the owed gate** — `bearingToNext` in `engine/game/course.ts`, for the HUD's arrow and the bot alike. **Where a reset stands the skier** — `resetPose` beside it: on the piste past the last gate TAKEN, facing down it — on a free ride, the nearest point of the piste; `standSkier` is how anything puts a skier down at rest.
- **Where a skier stands in the race** — `raceProgress` / `racePlace` / `fieldOrder` in `engine/game/rivals.ts`, each run reduced to a standing the framework's `racing/standings` orders: the finish, then gates taken, then distance to the next gate — and on a slalom, whose field is a dealt board, `field.ts`'s `fieldPlace` / `fieldOrderOf` (home by combined time, the racers out of it after). The HUD, the finish plate and the sim read it.
- **The sun over a run** — `sunAtRun(level)` / `moonAtRun(level)` in `engine/game/clock.ts` (the sun stands at the map's hour for the whole run), over the framework's generic `core/solar`. `pwa/src/game/sky.ts`'s `skyLookAt` turns it into the one `SkyLook` every frame; the lights, the dome, the haze and the snow's glitter all read that one look, and nothing picks its own sun.
- **The weather** — `Level.weather` (R19, `engine/mapgen/weather.ts`), always read through `weatherOf` (a hand-built map is under `CLEAR_WEATHER`); a sky or an hour chosen by hand goes through `withSky` (`GenerateOptions.sky`, `CreateGameOptions.sky`, `?weather=` / `?hour=`) and moves nothing the map builds. The wind is `windAt(level, t)` — a pure function at 10 m over open ground, drawing nothing from `state.rng`, which carries the picture's snow and cloud; brought down to a skier's body where he is (`airAt`: the log law, the mountain's exposure, the woods' shelter — `TUNING.wind`) it is the air his drag is against (`air.ts`), what he hears and what the HUD's wind meter reads (`airflowAt`) — one air for all three. The fall at a moment is `snowAt(level, t)` beside it (`engine/game/snowfall.ts`), pure the same way; the other thing a sky does to the physics is the new snow that fall lays, `GameState.fresh`, read only through `packedUnder` / `depthUnder` in `snow.ts`. A RACE is run only in the weather its jury allows: `createGame` eases a race's map through `juryDay` (`engine/game/jury.ts`) to its discipline's `JURY` row — the strongest gust at the course's start, a speed race's fall — a sky picked by hand included. `skyLookAt` is the one place the weather and the night become a picture: the flat light, the glitter, the floodlights and the lamps, the fall, the fog all come off that `SkyLook`.
- **The haze** — `SKY_GLSL`'s `skyColour` in `pwa/src/game/haze.ts`: the dome paints it and every world material fades into it along its own direction through `hazeMaterial`. Three's own fog is never used in the world.
- **The ONE clock** — `state.t` advances by `TUNING.dt` per step and is the only time the engine knows. Nothing in `engine/` reads a wall clock.
- **Which surface is up** — `Shell` in `pwa/src/game/shell.ts` (`splash | menu | loading | pause | run | replay | bench`), and the predicates beside it (`watching` is the replay's). **The snow never stops behind a card — except the pause card**, the one card `simulates` says no to; the `bench` surface is the other no, because the benchmark's pump turns the snow itself (`appDraws`).
- **The app's clock** — the framework's `loop/run-clock`: `MAX_FRAME_SECONDS` is the clamp, stated there and nowhere in this tree; the time beyond it is dropped, never paid down.
- **What the player presses** — `pwa/src/game/settings-input.ts`: every action and its keys (`DEFAULT_KEYS`: the tuck, the brake, left and right, the leans, the grab, the reset, the machine key — ENTER, on and off the snowmobile or the helicopter — the restart, the camera, the HUD, the shutter on P, pause), whether it is held or taken on the press; `HeldAction` is `keyof KeysHeld`, never restated.
- **What the game remembers** — `pwa/src/game/settings.ts` (the camera, the skis picked, the outfit worn, the sound switch, and every OPTIONS row: the faders, the picture, the keys, the thumbs, the assist), and `mergeSettings` is the one place a stored blob becomes settings this build offers — the record book (`records.ts`), the ghosts (`ghost.ts`), the campaign's board (`campaign.ts`), the benchmark's history (`benchmark-history.ts`) and the STATISTICS (`stats.ts`) are the only other things kept in `localStorage`, each under its own key and each checked the same way on the way in (plus the cloud save's one stamp, `cloud-save.ts`'s `CLOUD_KEY` — when this device last moved a carried setting); the SCREENSHOT ROLL is IndexedDB (the framework's `shots/shot-store`, policy in `shots/shot-roll`) and never load-bearing, and so are the start card's charts (`seed-store.ts`, one database per generator version and build). `mixOf` is the one place the master and the switch fold into the two faders the bus is handed.
- **What the picture costs** — `pwa/src/game/settings-video.ts`: every row's ladder and what each stop buys (the pixel share, the ground's clipmap — `terrainLook`, every stop reaching the DISTANCE row's view — the trail maps, the forest's bands, the draw distance and the mist that closes on it, `mistFor` (LOW to HIGH; MAX alone reaches `TERRAIN_REACH`, unmisted), the shadow map, the spray share). `renderer.setVideo` is the one place a row becomes a draw call, and `tests/video_test.ts` holds the ladder; a lab's `?video=<tier>` (and `?picture=row:stop,…`) is this visit's and never stored. **What each stop is WORTH and costs a frame** — `PICTURE_PRICES` in `pwa/src/game/picture-fit.ts`, and `fitPicture` the one place a machine's timed frame becomes a picture (PRESET ▸ AUTO); a stop added to a ladder owes a price there, measured with `make bench ARGS="--gpu --costs"`.
- **How much the skis help** — `GameState.assist` (`Assist` in `engine/game/defs/modes.ts`): the yaw hold and the air's roll-levelling, each 0..1, read and never written during a run, drawing nothing from the stream. Every hand is on unless `createGame` is asked otherwise, and the field always skis with every hand on. **What the URL says** — `pwa/src/game/url-params.ts`, and `scripts/screenshot.mjs` drives the built site through exactly that.
- **WHAT A TRICK IS WORTH** — `engine/game/tricks.ts` (and `TUNING.tricks`): the air by the second and by the metre on one curve (tied at `lengthKnee`), the Nth revolution on an axis N × its points and N steps of multiplier, the twist, the grabs, the air's own rung sold only beside a trick, and the combo banked on a clean landing, at its base on a sketchy one, and lost to a wipeout, a reset or a landing in a grab. What a stroke turns is `strokes.ts`'s: all or nothing at the top of an axis, from a per-flight budget, never opened on the way down. No WORD for any of it is in the engine — `TRICK_WORDS` / `comboLine` in `strings.ts`. The score is kept on every run (the sim reads it) and worked for only where `rules.tricks` is on.
- **What a run is playing by** — `GameState.rules` (`RunRules`), dealt by `createGame` from its mode's bundle in `MODE_RULES` (`engine/game/defs/modes.ts`). A mode is a NAME the app uses to pick a card and a row of the record book; nothing below the app branches on it.
- **WHAT A RUN WAS SKIED ON, TO THE FIGURE** — `snapInput` in `pwa/src/game/ghost.ts`, applied where the player's input is made (`input-model.ts`'s `sampleInput`) and again where `App.tsx` hands the engine any input at all: every axis on the tape's grid (1/127 of edge and lean, 1/255 of the tuck and the brake), so the figure the engine is skied on IS the figure a ghost records and the ghost is the run again rather than nearly it. `ControlTape`, `createControlRecorder` and `readControls` beside it are the only way a tape is written and read here, over the framework's `racing/tape` codec (whose byte format is pinned there).
- **WHAT A REPLAY CUTS TO, AND HOW SLOWLY IT RUNS** — `pwa/src/game/replay-shots.ts`. Nothing else slows the run but the X-ray cam (`xray-shots.ts`, INJURIES on): the death cam (`camera-death.ts`) takes the lens and never the clock. The collector watches the player's own `state.events` and files a flight BACK-DATED to its take-off, a pass, a trunk, a rival, a wipeout and the finish; `planShots` ranks and spaces them; `directAt(plan, step)` is the whole edit, a pure function of how far into the tape the picture is — the moment holding the frame and the TIME RATE. Slow motion is fewer steps per frame and nothing else. A replay is the ghost's `ControlTape` (never a second encoder) plus the afternoon read off the run at its first step (`recipeOf` in `replay.ts`); `camera-tv.ts` decides where the lens stands and restates none of it.
- **The record book** — `pwa/src/game/records.ts`: what names a row (`recordId` — seed, skis, mode, laps, and a slalom's second run), what beats one (`beats` — lower, never a tie), the gap at a crossing (`splitGap`). The front door's tile, the HUD's VS BEST and the finish plate all read it; `ghost-run.ts` is the one place a run is filed.
- **Every word the player reads** — `pwa/src/game/strings.ts`; no component carries a literal.
- **WHERE A LIFT'S CARRIERS ARE** — `carrierAt(plan, k, t)` in `engine/game/lift-line.ts`: every chair, cabin and T-bar of every lift round its loop at the rope's speed, a pure function of the engine's clock, never stepped — THE LIFTS ALWAYS RUN, on every map in every mode. `lifts.ts` hangs them by it; a rider ever seated on one reads the same function. The rope they hang from is `planLift` / `ropeAt` beside it (`liftPlans` once a map), which the lift ride (`lift-ride.ts`) and the drawing both read.
- **Where a bird or an animal is** — `birdPose` in `pwa/src/game/bird-plan.ts` and `beastPose` in `beast-plan.ts`: pure functions of the plan and the engine's clock, never stepped, never stored. The only memories are the caller's, each decided by one rule: `flushAt` (a covey put up by any skier — `birds.ts` and `audio/bird-bed.ts` both keep it) and `spookAt` (a group run off by a skier — `beasts.ts`). Both plans are dealt off `level.seed` on generators of their own and read the map without writing it, so the wildlife moves no digest and draws nothing from `state.rng`.
- **Where the ear is** — `LISTENERS` in `pwa/src/game/audio/listener.ts`, one row per camera rung; nothing else branches on the camera to decide how loud something is.
- **What is felt** — `pwa/src/game/rumble.ts` (which moment is worth a pulse, how big), and `haptics.ts` the one motor.
- **App identity** — `pwa/src/identity.ts` (`APP_NAME`, `SITE_URL`, `REPO_URL`, `PALETTE`, `BRAND_COLOR`).
- **Which shell the page is in** — `pwa/src/shell-host.ts`: the frozen `__SH_SHELL__` global and the `sh-` events (`SHELL_RUMBLE`, `SHELL_COMMAND` and its word list, the fullscreen pair, the cloud's ask and answer `SHELL_CLOUD` / `SHELL_CLOUD_EVENT`). The ONE line of `pwa/` that knows a shell exists.

- **Physics tests stage the skier on the SYNTHETIC maps** in `tests/support/synthetic.ts` — `syntheticLevel()` (the SLOPE: a packed piste down a face, a kicker, rollers, a lone tree) and `flatLevel()` (a drag strip on a grade of one's own, all packed or all powder), nothing the generator built — and stand him at a moment with `placeRun` (`engine/game/place.ts`), then script inputs step by step. That is the §23.8 sequel test kept honest: the rule suite passes with the generator deleted. The `test-scenario` skill owns staging an exact situation.
- **A file that asserts a dozen rules over the same spread of seeds takes its maps from `tests/support/levels.ts`** (`LEVEL_SEEDS`, `levelFor`, `analysisFor`) rather than generating them per `it`. Generating a map is the most expensive thing the engine does and it is deterministic, so the second build can only return the first one's answer. What comes back is SHARED and read-only.
- **Sharding splits at FILE granularity, so the slowest single file is the floor under `make test` on CI.** Keep a file under a minute: share the corpus, and split a file whose subject is really two. The slices are cut by each file's measured time (`SHARD_WEIGHTS` in `tests/support/shards.ts`), so a file that gets much heavier, or a heavy one split, owes its row there.
- **The governance tests are tests too**, and they run in the same suite: `imports_test` (the dependency direction, §23.7), `file_size_test` (§20.5), `symlinks_test` (§7.1, §21.2), `identity_test` (§35.6), `app_mark_test`, `changeset_test` (§8.5), `skills_test` (§21), `docs_rules_test` (the rule book's mirror), `determinism_test` (§25), `tauri_test` and `shell_test` (§23.3). A structural rule this file states and no test holds is a rule that will not survive a deadline.
- **Running them:** `make test` is the whole suite (`SHARD=i/N` runs one slice); `npx vitest run tests/<topic>_test.ts` runs one file.
- No extra test dependencies; everything runs on plain Node.

## Parity and cross-cutting rules

Places where one idea is deliberately written in two files that cannot import each other. Each is a live trap: change one, change both.

- `pwa/src/identity.ts` is the identity source of truth; `pwa/public/icons/icon.svg`, `scripts/generate-icons.mjs` and `pwa/src/game/app-mark.ts` encode the same mark geometry (the two carved tracks over the peak, the gate flag) and the same palette. None can import either of the others, so change one and change all three, then `make icons`. `tests/identity_test.ts` holds the generator's palette to `PALETTE`, and `tests/app_mark_test.ts` holds the SVG to `app-mark.ts`.
- `pwa/index.html` restates the name and `BRAND_COLOR` (a static head cannot import); `pwa/public/CNAME` restates the domain. **THE SITE IS DELIBERATELY NOT INDEXED** — the head carries `noindex` and no description, canonical, Open Graph, Twitter card or JSON-LD, the body prerenders no copy, `robots.txt` is `Disallow: /`, and `sitemap.xml`, `llms.txt` and `og.png` are not shipped. `tests/identity_test.ts` holds all of it from both sides; `docs/configuration.md` § *Discoverability* is the description.
- The service worker contract (the cache id, the emitted files) is shared between `pwa/pwa-plugin.ts` and `pwa/src/app-pwa.ts` (`cacheIdForBase`) — keep them agreeing.
- **The rule book has a mirror.** `engine/mapgen/rules.ts` states R1–R24 once in its header; `docs/level-generator.md` carries the same prose VERBATIM. `tests/docs_rules_test.ts` reads the ids off the code, so a new rule fails the test until its mirror lands.
- **The generator and the analyzer read one rule book and must not share anything else.** The search builds to `LEVEL_RULES`; `analyzeLevel` re-checks the FINISHED map against the same numbers reading only what the `Level` publishes, never the plan. A check that could read the plan checks the plan.
- **The skis are drawn off the physics' spec and their class's traced look.** `pwa/src/game/skis-body.ts` (the skis, the bindings, the boots) and `ski-gear.ts` (the poles and what moves) build each pair in the engine's body frame from its OWN spec (the length, the waist, the tip and the tail, the sidecut, the rocker, the stance, the CoG) and the traced profiles in `ski-looks.ts`, which `lookFrame` pins to the spec's length and mount — and the spec's tip and tail are read off the same trace, so a trace moved moves `defs/skis.ts` with it (`tests/topsheet_test.ts` holds the stretch to 1) — and the ski card's sheet (`ski-stats.ts`) bills it off the same row and the engine's own `limits.ts` / `footprint.ts`; and `skier-pose.ts`'s `MOUNTS` are where the skier's boots and hands are — the figure is carried by the bindings' place on the ski and the pole grips' reach from `MOUNTS.grip`. Move the spec and the drawing follows.
- **THE DESKTOP SHELL RESTATES THE IDENTITY IN RUST.** `pwa/src/shell-host.ts` states the `__SH_SHELL__` global, both fullscreen events and the menu's word list; `tauri/shell/src/config.rs` and `menu.rs` spell every one of them again, and `identity.ts`'s name, description, `SITE_URL` and `BRAND_COLOR` are restated in `tauri/src-tauri/tauri.conf.json` and `config.rs`. `tests/tauri_test.ts` holds all of it, reading the Rust as TEXT. A word added to one side alone is a menu row that silently does nothing. **The store app restates the same global in JavaScript** — `NATIVE_FLAG` in `native/src/injected.ts`, injected before the page's own scripts — and `tests/shell_test.ts` holds it; `native/app.config.js` READS its name and palette off `identity.ts` by regex, the shape `tests/identity_test.ts` holds.
- **A MODEL'S MATERIALS ARE NAMED TWICE.** `scripts/blender/skis.py` and `skier.py` name every material (`paint`, `white`, `base`, `edge`, `boot`, `jacket`, `pants`, `helmet`, `goggle`, …) and `dressOf` in `pwa/src/game/skier-models.ts` dresses them by those names; neither can import the other, so `tests/models_test.ts` reads the builders as text. A material renamed on one side alone is a model drawn in Blender's red whatever the topsheet. (The skier's names dress only a lab's Blender skier now — the game's is dressed in code.)
- **THE SHUTTER'S WORD IS NAMED FOUR TIMES.** `shot` is in `SHELL_COMMANDS` (`pwa/src/shell-host.ts`), a row of `tauri/shell/src/menu.rs` (View ▸ Take Screenshot) and the `SHOT_COMMAND` script the store app injects when the phone takes a screenshot (`native/src/injected.ts`, fired from `native/src/screen-capture.ts`); `tests/tauri_test.ts` and `tests/shell_test.ts` hold them, and `run-actions.ts` lands it on the P key's line.
- **THE CLOUD SAVE IS NAMED THREE TIMES.** The ask and its answer (`SHELL_CLOUD` / `SHELL_CLOUD_EVENT` in `pwa/src/shell-host.ts`), the listener that posts an ask (`CLOUD_BRIDGE` in `native/src/injected.ts`) and the parser and the answer's script (`parseCloudAsk` / `cloudReply` in the import-free `native/src/cloud-ask.ts`) cannot import each other; `tests/shell_test.ts` holds all of it by running the scripts. WHAT is saved and how two devices merge is the website's (`pwa/src/game/cloud-save.ts`, every rule the game's own — `beats`, the board's better afternoon — imported, never restated); the shell moves one opaque string.
- **A PULSE IS NAMED THREE TIMES.** The event (`SHELL_RUMBLE` in `pwa/src/shell-host.ts`), the listener that posts it (`RUMBLE_BRIDGE` in `native/src/injected.ts`) and the parser that reads it (`parseRumble` in `native/src/rumble.ts`) cannot import each other; `tests/rumble_test.ts` holds all three. The WEBSITE owns the feature: what is felt is `rumble.ts`, the motor is `haptics.ts`, and the shell only plays what it is handed.
- **The audition page links by CONCATENATION.** `scripts/audition.mjs` inlines the audio modules into one page in dependency order — the instrument read from the framework's shipped SOURCE (`node_modules/@niclaslindstedt/oss-game-framework/src/audio/`), the beds from `pwa/`; a bed's table that is not exported, or a module a bed calls that is not listed before it, is a page that builds clean and throws on the first button.
- **Linear light, and every hand-written shader closes the loop.** `sky.ts` authors linear RGB; a `ShaderMaterial` that writes straight to `gl_FragColor` skips the output conversion and hands out a picture about half as bright as authored — every one ends with `#include <colorspace_fragment>`.
- The deployed site IS the product (§11.2-as-webapp): there is no separate `website/` tree and no marketing copy. Keep what is left (the title, the theme colour, the manifest, the privacy and support pages) in sync with `identity.ts` (`update-website` owns the sweep).
- The engine's determinism is a contract three suites hold from three sides — `determinism_test` (a run replays), `simulation_test` (the bot finishes what the generator builds), `imports_test` (no clock, no `Math.random`, nothing external). The only draws a race makes are the rivals' paces and pairs at the start line, and their starts (reaction and stride phase) off a stream of the start's own (`rivals.ts`' `START_SALT`) — a slalom's board and its gates draw off streams of their own off the map's seed (`field.ts`, `mapgen/slalom.ts`), never `state.rng`; adding a draw anywhere in `step`, or into the generator's fixed order, changes every digest — say so in the PR.

## Skills

Skills live in `.agents/skills/` (`.claude/skills` and `.gemini/skills` symlink there) — each a `SKILL.md` playbook with an empty `.lessons/` directory `skill-reflection` fills. Load the one that owns the task's SUBJECT, plus the workflow ones its steps name. This file is the router; the procedures live in the skills. `tests/skills_test.ts` holds this list to the directory.

**Session workflow** (every task):

- **`start-work`** — the preflight: clean tree, sync with `origin/main`, the deliver-by-default contract.
- **`write-code`** — how code is written here: comments and the comment-pruning pass, the edit loop, the 1000-line cap, test conventions, the generic pools and aliases. Load beside the subject skill on any code change.
- **`skill-reflection`** — read each loaded skill's lessons at the start (`npx ogf-skill-lessons <skill>`), record/prune/promote at the end.
- **`changelog`** → **`commit`** — the fragment-or-label call, then gates, push, PR. **`conflict`** whenever a branch moves onto another.

**Craft** (the subject owners):

- **`game-feel`** — how the game FEELS: a skier on two grounds, the groomer and the powder; the reference (the arcade winter racers with a modern look); the camera ladder; the cross-system levers. Load it whenever the acceptance test is "does it feel like skiing".
- **`ski-physics`** — the skier's answer to the snow: the six stations and the legs, the sink and the float, the edge and the carve, the skid, the tuck, the poles, the hips, the body, the fall, flight and landings; `make ride`.
- **`ski-tuning`** — a pair's own numbers and the expectations a test holds the physics to; the field's pace; the day a roster lands.
- **`ski-design`** — how the skis LOOK: the builder in the body frame, each pair's one topsheet; `make skis`, `make world`.
- **`blender-assets`** — a game asset MODELLED in Blender off the game's own data (the skis, and the skier for the labs' comparison, rigged and animated): `make blender`, the render and game-budget passes with LODs, the asset sheet beside the game's own, headless Blender on macOS and Linux, references kept local and unnamed, and what shipping a model would take — and the models IN THE GAME: the skis, committed in `pwa/models/` by `make models`, drawn by every build, switched back with `VITE_MODEL_SKIS=0` (`make ci-models MODELS=off` for CI). The skier is dressed in code (`skier-gear`); the wildlife and the course's marks are built in code (`nature`, `collision`).
- **`skier-gear`** — what the skier WEARS: the catalog (`outfit.ts` — two bodies, the jackets, pants, helmets, gloves and poles, each sold in its own colours), the loom that cuts a piece onto the rig in its bind pose and weights it across the bones, the dressed skin posed by the game's pose, the DRESS card, and `make gear` (every piece, every outfit, the moves, the game's pixels, the triangles, a Blender skier and local reference photographs beside them) — and why the gear is procedural, not Blender.
- **`skier`** — the figure on the skis: the stance from the engine's readings, the limbs solved to the bindings and the pole grips; judged from behind at chase range.
- **`skier-improvement`** — making the skier MORE REALISTIC as a loop: `make skier-metrics` (the game's pose measured off the real engine against a skier's bands, the share of frames at fault), the skier lab's `closeup`, `detail`, `game` (the game's own pixels) and `stretch` (the skin off its bind) sheets, the model rebuilt and published, the honest before, and the lessons of the first pass.
- **`collision`** — the skier meeting what is not snow (trunks, rivals, the edge of the map) and the course counting (gates, misses, the finish, the reset); what each event means.
- **`crash`** — the skier past saving and off his skis: the wipeout (a trunk, a landing over the tips, a fall at speed, an edge caught), his body tumbling until the reset, bogged in the powder and poling out, and damage when it is on; `make ride`'s crash scenarios.
- **`engine-system`** — adding or changing a gameplay system, engine-first.
- **`mapgen-improvement`** — the world generator (the search, the mountain, the piste's descent, the gates, the kickers, the start, the forest and the tree line, the day), the R-rules, the piste-and-terrain craft, and the analyze → fix → `make level` loop.
- **`add-region`** — a NEW KIND OF SNOW COUNTRY end to end, in order (R21): the engine's row, the surface the physics reads, the look and the grade, the card's word, the suite, the labs and the docs — with the alpine row kept all ones so no seed moves.
- **`lift-areas`** — where a lift ends: the pad, the unload ramp and the way off, every run off a top starting UNDER it where a ramp has room, the ramps down onto the runs, the signs at their heads, the piste map board painted with the start card's panorama — and the rule that nothing takes the controls at a top: a rider who follows the sign gets to his run downhill; `make lift-tops`.
- **`crowd`** — the free ride's amateurs: who is out on the ski area and in what groups, each one's knobs, how they ski the runs and ride the lifts, the player meeting them, and the figures (eight bodies at three cuts, posed off the player's own pose, dressed per instance); `make crowd` — and a race's SPECTATORS: where the audience stands, what each fan wears and carries, how he moves as a racer passes, the finish arena; `make audience`.
- **`buildings`** — every building on the mountain but the log cabins (lift stations, the start house and hut, the finish arena, the wind tunnels, anything new): real references first, low-poly geometry from the facade kit (`facade-kit.ts`) carrying materials PAINTED IN CODE (`facade-paint.ts`), one mesh a kind, iterated in `make buildings` until it reads; also the way to port the cabins.
- **`nature`** — the snow-loaded woods (where the generator stands them, the tree line, how they are drawn), the mountain as a landscape, the ground's clipmap, the wildlife by region.
- **`atmosphere`** — the clear winter sky: the sun at the run's own hour, the colour model, the blue in the shadows, the haze, the shadow box; the weather and the night.
- **`snow-look`** — the snow as DRAWN: the shader, the glitter, the groomed piste and its corduroy, the trail map and the grooves it lowers.
- **`visual-effects`** — what the skis throw and leave and what the skier feels: the spray, the tracks' stamping, the vibration table; event → effect, presentation only.
- **`platform-shells`** — the desktop app (`tauri/`: two crates, decisions and effects) and the store app (`native/`: the WebView, the local server, the haptics bridge), the `__SH_SHELL__` seam and the names stated twice.
- **`perf-optimization`** — making the game run faster and steadier, a round at a time: the cost map of a frame (the engine, the renderer's CPU phases, the GPU's passes), priorities off the price list, `make cpu-cost` (the processor's half timed in Node with a hash of what it filled), which meter needs a real GPU, the proofs a speed-up owes, and the ledger of what each round bought and what is still open.
- **`picture-pricing`** — what every stop of every PICTURE row COSTS a frame (measured, `make bench ARGS="--gpu --costs"`, from the race and the vista) and is WORTH to the look (argued), the price list `picture-fit.ts` fits PRESET ▸ AUTO by, and the rule that a stop added to a ladder owes a price.
- **`lab-tooling`** — how a lab or a script is built here: the framework's `tooling/*` shelf and `scripts/lib/`, pure-Node versus browser-driven, the harness page, the URL contract, and registering a tool. Load it BEFORE writing a one-off script.
- **`hud-and-menus`** — the HUD's readouts, the three presses, the edge thumb and the tuck lever, the keys — what is drawn over a RUN.
- **`menu-system`** — the shell around a run: the attract card, the front door, OPTIONS and its KEYS page, the first-visit probe, the loading card, the pause card, the settings and the URL.
- **`ui-review`** — the fit-and-finish sweep at the reference viewports (1280×720, 390×844, and 844×390 — the phone on its side).
- **`playtest`** — looking at the real game: `make world`'s views and `make screenshots`' moments.
- **`test-scenario`** — exact situations: the synthetic slope, `placeRun`, scripted inputs, the ride lab's scenarios.
- **`debug-tools`** — the developer page behind the title's hold: the instruments over the picture, the free camera, the REPRO line and the URL that reads it back, UNLOCKS, and the BENCHMARK — its plan, its report, its history, `make bench`.
- **`debug-game`** — deterministic repros, classifying by layer, the failing test first.
- **`simulate-run`** — `make sim`: the `RunReport` table, its columns, which movements are regressions.
- **`level-rating`** — whether a generated mountain is any GOOD and how HARD: `engine/rating/`'s eight axes and the index, the bands calibrated off a sweep (`make rate ARGS=--stats`), the ladder scorer, and `make difficulty`'s schematic.
- **`campaign`** — the pinned maps and the ladder they make (`campaign-levels.ts`, `campaign.ts`, the cards): curating a rung with `make rate CAMPAIGN=1` and the bot, the generator-version contract a pinned map stands on, the points and the locks, and which map a measured run is on.
- **`bot-improvement`** — the piste-reading bot in `engine/sim/bot.ts` — the player's stand-in and every rival — measured with `make sim`.
- **`sound-effects`** — every sound, synthesized: the vocabulary and the instrument, the bank and the route, the beds (the wind, the edges and the snow, the poles) and the listener; `make audition`, and `--meter` for a session that cannot listen.

**Maintenance** (each with a `.last-updated` baseline):

- **`maintenance`** — the umbrella: dispatches every `update-*` skill in registry order after big merges or on a cadence.
- **`update-docs`** / **`update-readme`** / **`update-website`** — re-sync `docs/*.md`, README.md and the identity shell against their sources of truth. (There is no `prompts/` tree, so no `update-prompts`.)
- **`sync-game-spec`** — walk OSS_GAME_SPEC.md chapter by chapter against the repo, offline, and re-date `docs/spec-conformance.md`; the closing step of a full sweep.

Run the specific skill when you know what drifted; run `maintenance` when you don't.

**Reserved, not written** (see `.agents/skills/README.md`): tricks, replay, store-listing, store-shots. A lesson about one of those subjects waits, scoped, in the nearest existing skill until its subject is built — and the sibling repos' skill of the same name is where it starts.
