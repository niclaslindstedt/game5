type: Changed
title: The helicopter's camera flies between views, and its crash tears it apart
---

Changing the camera while flying the helicopter now flies the lens to the new view, as it does on skis. It swings round the machine rather than through it. The lens is also flown onto the helicopter as you sit on its skid, and back to you after the drop. The two close views, which used to look out from the rider's helmet, now look out from the helicopter's nose. One is bolted under the chin and moves with the machine. The other looks down at the snow ahead, for landings and drops.

A crash is now a real wreck. The airframe tears into its pieces: cabin panels, the nose, the skids, the tail boom and the broken rotor blades. The pieces are flung out, tumble, burn and throw sparks off the snow. The fuel goes up as a lobed fireball sized and timed from real fuel fireballs. It rolls into a black mushroom over a pool fire about ten metres tall. The snow around it is scorched. The blast throws the rider tens of metres clear, and they lie where they land until the wreck burns out. The camera pulls back from the view you were on when it struck, so the whole crash is in frame.
