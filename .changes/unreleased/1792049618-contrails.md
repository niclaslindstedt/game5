title: Contrails in the sky
---

Airliners now cross the sky, about one a minute, and leave contrails where the air up there is cold enough. In dry air a trail is a short line gone in seconds. In humid air it lasts, waves, breaks into puffs and spreads into a band of cirrus that drifts on the wind aloft, building up into a cross-hatch under a high sky. A plane glints at the head of a fresh trail. The trails glow pink after sunset, are gone on a dark night (at most a faint line under a bright moon) and hide behind the clouds and the mountains. A clear, dry day shows one or two at a time. The DISTANCE row decides how many are drawn, and `make contrails` is their lab.
