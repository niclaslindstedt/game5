Printed step by step on the 20° strip (a turn every 0.9 s, the slalom technique): the yaw hand turns the skis at the carve's rate faster than the grip turns the path, so the skis end each turn 20–30° inside it, and after the release the skid's grip across those slewed skis keeps pushing the old way. The balance — and with it the lean target — stays on the old side, the new edge is capped at `angulateMost` (34°) by `edgeWithin`, and 34° cannot swing the skis round: crossing over, every other turn of the rhythm was lost (`make technique`'s TURNS sheet: 12–21 m at 34° between the 3 m turns). A technique row's `cross.under` (the edge the legs stand the skis on whatever the body, 57° on the slalom) breaks it; letting the body commit across early instead (skipping `crossLoad`) threw the racer — keep that gate. `cross.retract` lifts only the skis' own hull points while he is near upright and rolling: it changes no contact count on seed 38 and the variants without it lost a run or two more in thirty — within the sweep's chaos, so it stays as the pose draws it (`technique-pose.ts`'s transition). Two things this left: the end-of-turn slew (the max skid stays ~40°, the research's 10–16°) and a lower inclination at the line's tightest point, because the new edge now bites before the body has come over. Every `Crossing` number is a floor or a multiple: `max(a + b, 0)` and `x * 1` keep the free row and `make sim` byte-identical.
