type: Fixed
title: Both skis stay on the snow through turns and stops
---

Turning hard or stopping no longer lifts the skier's outside ski into the air. He now leans into a turn over both skis on the snow: his inside leg folds and his outside leg stays long, and his hips tilt so neither knee folds further than a real skier's would. In a hockey stop both skis lie flat on the snow, side by side across the slope. The outside ski carries most of his weight — nearly all of it at a crawl, about three quarters at a cruise and two thirds in a fast carve — and the spray and snow cloud now come off that ski.
