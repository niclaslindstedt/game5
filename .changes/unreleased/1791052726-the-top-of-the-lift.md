Getting off a chairlift now flows. The top of every chair and gondola is wider, with room to turn round, and no longer flat. The strip along the lift is level and the snow falls away to either side, so you pick up speed as you slide off toward your run. Where the mountain allows it, a wide groomed ramp carries you on down to the run. Where the run lies far below, the ramp stays gentle and then rolls over an edge onto the steep part. Nothing blocks the way off the ramp any more. The stop gate and the orange netting are gone. The run signs at the top now point only at runs you can actually ski onto from there, so a sign no longer points at a lane that leaves from above the station. They're also the same weathered wooden boards with burned lettering as the signs at the head of each run, each board cut into an arrow. On the top and down the ramp, the camera sits low and level behind you, so the edge reads like a cliff whatever the run's colour, then tips down after you as you drop over it. A chairlift no longer drags you up through the hillside on its last stretch: the ground under the end of every lift now falls away beneath the chairs. The operator's booth no longer flickers on its side.
